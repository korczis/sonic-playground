## Genre and Mood

**Apocalyptica** play **Battery** without a guitar, a bass guitar or a singer: four cellos stand in for a thrash metal band. The result sits between neoclassical music and symphonic metal, intense once it gets going and haunting in its first bars.

## Song Summary

Battery is the opening track of Metallica's 1986 album Master of Puppets, written by James Hetfield and Lars Ulrich. Its lyric is read as a song about controlling anger, but it is also about the San Francisco thrash scene of the 1980s and the Old Waldorf club on Battery Street, a scene defending itself as a family. Apocalyptica's instrumental version was not on their 1996 debut. It was one of three never-before-released recordings added to the album's 20th-anniversary remastered edition, issued on 22 July 2016; Battery itself came out first, as a single on 10 May 2016, with an official video two days later.

## Musical Breakdown

Only Louder's review describes this recording in any detail; what follows sets it against the original's structure.

### Intro

Metallica open Battery on acoustic guitars, a chord progression with a melody on top, before distorted electric guitars crash in. Apocalyptica's version, Stephen Dalton wrote, emerges in a fog of ghostly twangs and reverb.

### Main Section

Then it galvanises into what sounds like crunchy guitar riffs but, Dalton noted, could be a furiously strummed cello. In the original, a very fast thrash riff carries the rest of the song; no source describes how the cellos divide it among themselves, so listen for which instrument takes the low line and which the attack.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} can be faithful in notes and radical in sound. Apocalyptica keep Metallica's song and change everything that made it metal: the instruments and the voice, which is simply gone.

The heart of Battery is its {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }}, the short, repeated figure Hetfield improvised while relaxing in London. Played on cellos, it keeps enough bite that Dalton could not be sure whether he was hearing a guitar or a furiously strummed cello.

The opening relies on {{ <concept_link name="reverb" /> }}, the sound of a space, to blur those first twangs into a haze before the riff arrives.

Finally, {{ <concept_link name="remastering" /> }}: streaming services list this track as Remastered because it belongs to the remastered album, but it was a new recording, not a remaster of an old one.

## Artistic Context

Apocalyptica formed in 1993 at the Sibelius Academy in Helsinki, four cellists who began as a classical-style Metallica tribute band. Plays Metallica by Four Cellos, released by Mercury in 1996, held eight covers and went platinum in Finland. The 2016 reissue marked its 20th anniversary, followed in 2017 to 2019 by tours on which the band played it again as a cello quartet.

## Impact and Reception

Louder called the three bonus tracks worthy inclusions; Reflections of Darkness said the cello Battery really gets you head-banging and that the remastered album sounds cleaner. Put on Metallica's version first, then this one.
