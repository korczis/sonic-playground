## Genre and Mood

**Boney M.**'s **"Sunny"** is disco pop and a cover, and it wears all three easily. It is upbeat and feel-good on purpose: a 1960s song about looking at the bright side, set for the discothèques of 1976.

## Song Summary

Bobby Hebb wrote "Sunny" in 1963, after his older brother Harold was stabbed to death outside a Nashville nightclub; he described it as a wish for a sunny disposition and a brighter day. His own 1966 recording reached number two on the US pop chart. Ten years later Frank Farian produced it for Boney M., arranged by Stefan Klinkhammer, with Liz Mitchell on lead vocals.

## Musical Breakdown

None of the sources we used describes this arrangement section by section, so this breakdown stays with what they document: who played and sang, and how the track was made.

### The Backing Track

The record was cut at Union Studios in Munich and Europasound Studios in Frankfurt by the album's session players, credited as The Rhythm Machine: Gary Unwin on bass, Keith Forsey on drums, Nick Woodland on guitar and Thor Baldursson on keyboards.

### The Voice

Liz Mitchell, a Jamaican singer who had sung with the Les Humphries Singers, carries the melody. On Boney M.'s records the singing came from her, Marcia Barrett and Farian himself.

### The Other Language

Farian reused the same backing track for Gilla, another of his artists, who recorded "Sunny" in German.

## Concepts in Focus

"Sunny" is a {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} that became a hit in its own right: Hebb's song is a soul-jazz standard with hundreds of versions, and Boney M.'s took it to number one in West Germany, Austria, Belgium and the Netherlands. Compare the deep house of {{ <track_link source="trauma-sunny-extended-mix.md" /> }} to hear the same melody in a third setting.

Boney M. were also a {{ <concept_link name="stage persona" /> }} as much as a band. Bobby Farrell danced on stage but never sang on the records, and Maizie Williams's voice was not used in the studio either; all four sang live.

The recording has lived on as a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }}: reissued in new mixes in 1988, 1999, 2000 and 2015, and sampled by Mark Ronson for his 2003 song Ooh Wee.

## Artistic Context

Farian put Take the Heat Off Me together quickly to capitalise on the success of "Daddy Cool", using cover versions and recordings he already had. After Boney M. performed "Daddy Cool" on Radio Bremen's Musikladen in September 1976, it went to number one in West Germany. "Sunny", released as a single in November 1976, gave them their second.

## Impact and Reception

"Sunny" was a top-ten hit across Europe and reached number three in the UK. It has turned up in the Soviet film Repentance, Boogie Nights and The Umbrella Academy. Play it next to Hebb's 1966 original and hear how far one song can travel.
