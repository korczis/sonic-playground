## Genre and Mood

**Lykke Li**'s **Breaking It Up** is indie pop at its brightest: a pop song about ending something, sung with exuberance rather than tears. It is defiant and a little bittersweet, and its pleasure is that the singer is never alone: reviewers heard a choir around her.

## Song Summary

Breaking It Up is the tenth track of Youth Novels and its third single, released on 5 May 2008. Li wrote the lyric, and the music is credited to Li and Björn Yttling. What makes it memorable is the crowd around the singer. The Skinny's Neal Parsons pointed to "the chorus of vocals and handclaps that drives Breaking It Up" and expected it to become a break-up anthem. Paste's Stephen M. Deusner wrote: "She sounds particularly exuberant on upbeat numbers like 'Breaking It Up,' on which she's joined by an otherworldly choir."

### Main Section

The liner notes list what you are hearing. Yttling plays piano, synth and bass, sings backing vocals and arranged the strings, played by Andreas Forsman and Erik Arvinder on violin, Erik Holm on viola and Henrik Söderquist on cello. Lars Skoglund plays hi-hat and Lasse Mårtén percussion, and the backing voices are Mapei, Lissy Trullie and The Suzan with Yttling. Treble's Jeff Terich heard "more handclaps, more upbeat choir vocals, and hooks, glorious, hooks; it's one damn great pop song."

### Outro

No source describes the ending as such. Parsons felt the vocals and handclaps wear thin towards the end; Treble's review raises no such complaint.

## Concepts in Focus

Breaking It Up shows {{ <concept_link name="vocal harmony" /> }} used for weight rather than sweetness. Backing vocals credited to Yttling, Mapei, Lissy Trullie and The Suzan surround Li, and the effect Paste and Treble describe is a choir rather than a duet.

It is also a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }}. A string quartet, a hi-hat and percussion sit beside piano, synth and bass, so what sounds like a crowd singing along has been carefully assembled.

That assembly is the work of a {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }}. Yttling, of Peter Bjorn and John, produced all of Youth Novels with Mårtén co-producing; compare the whistled hook Yttling plays on {{ <track_link source="peter-bjorn-and-john-young-folks.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Lykke Li, born in Ystad in 1986, released Youth Novels on 30 January 2008 on her own label LL Recordings, distributed by EMI, and internationally that summer through Atlantic and Warner. It was recorded in Stockholm at the Atlantis, Decibel and Högalid studios.

## Impact and Reception

Youth Novels reached number 3 in Sweden, scored 75 on Metacritic and earned five Grammis nominations, and Li won P3 Guld's newcomer award. Play Breaking It Up and count the voices in the chorus.
