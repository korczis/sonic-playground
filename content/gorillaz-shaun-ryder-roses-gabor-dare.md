## Genre and Mood

**Gorillaz** made **DARE** with two guests, **Shaun Ryder** and **Roses Gabor**, and it slips between funk, disco, electro-pop and trip-hop. On Demon Days, a dark album about one long night, Billboard heard it as a rare burst of playfulness.

## Song Summary

DARE is built for the dancefloor and for the cartoon. Noodle, the band's guitarist, sings lead through Roses Gabor, born Rosie Wilson, who took the character's voice over from Miho Hatori; Albarn sings backing vocals. Shaun Ryder of Happy Mondays and Black Grape walks in and out of the song, which Stereogum calls slithering dance-pop with Ryder as a sort of hype man, propelling it to earworm immortality. The one detail everyone remembers is the title, and the story behind it is disputed.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through DARE part by part, so this page follows its credits and the reviews instead of a timed map.

### The Groove

Albarn plays the synthesizers; James Dring plays drums and programs them with Jason Cox and Danger Mouse, who adds sampled loops. Wikipedia files the result under funk, electropop, disco and trip hop, all at once.

### The Voices

Gabor carries the melody as Noodle; Ryder's lines, by his own account improvised in the headphones, come in around hers. No source describes either delivery in more detail.

### The Video

The official video runs longer than the 4:05 album version: 4:47 on the DVD single, which also carried an animatic of it.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} usually brings a name and a voice; Ryder brought an attitude. He told NME that Albarn put on a track with no lyrics and that he improvised in the headphones. A story told at the 2006 Brit Awards held that the song was first called "It's There" and renamed after Ryder's Mancunian accent; in 2019 Ryder called that an urban myth and blamed his dyslexia and texting habits for the spelling.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Jamie Hewlett and Pete Candeland, puts Ryder's giant disembodied head in Noodle's wardrobe, kept alive by machinery, while Russel reads a paper on the toilet below and 2-D listens through the floor. It borrows from The Exorcist, The Birds and The Brain That Wouldn't Die.

The single also came with a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} by Soulwax, lasting 5:48, and one by DFA that runs to 12:14, three times the length of the song.

## Artistic Context

DARE was written by Albarn, Hewlett and Danger Mouse (Brian Burton), recorded at Sarm West in London and released by Parlophone as the second single from Demon Days, on CD on 29 August 2005. Danger Mouse, then known for The Grey Album, helped Albarn give the album a more muscular, intricate sound.

## Impact and Reception

On 4 September 2005 DARE became Gorillaz' first and only UK number one; it is certified double platinum there. In the US it reached number 87 on the Hot 100 but number four on the dance club chart. It turned up in Just Dance in 2009 and on EastEnders in 2017. Turn it up and listen for Ryder.
