## Genre and Mood

**Tubbe**, the German duo of singer Henri Jakobs and producer Klaus Scheuermann, deliver bright, driving synth-pop with "In Berlin". The duo began in 2010 in Munich's queer rave scene, moved to Berlin in 2013 and describe their music as electropunk and electropop; this track blends modern electronic production with 1980s synth colours. The mood is energetic and euphoric on the surface, with a sharp, ironic eye underneath.

## Song Summary

"In Berlin" is about arriving in the German capital with a head full of dreams. The text that accompanies the official video tells the story: growing up in a village where you dream of the endless big city, where the lights are too bright to sleep; then arriving in Berlin and finding your dreams sold off for 50 cents in a 1-euro shop, everyone else saving the world and wearing more tattoos than you, confetti and noise everywhere, until you open a corner pub called "Bei Mutti" because home is where you find peace. The music is all driving beats and shimmering synthesizers, which makes the disillusion land with a grin.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro: Setting the Scene

The track opens with a pulsing synth bass and crisp electronic drums that immediately set the song's energetic pace.

### Verse 1: Building the Narrative

The vocal enters clear and direct over a relatively sparse arrangement, letting the words take centre stage while the beat keeps the momentum.

### Pre-Chorus: Tension and Release

Additional synth layers come in and the energy rises, setting up the chorus.

### Chorus: Euphoric Release

The chorus opens up with bright, 1980s-flavoured synthesizers around the title, the song's most celebratory moment, whether you take the celebration straight or not.

### Bridge: Atmospheric Interlude

A short breakdown strips back the beats before the final push.

### Final Chorus: Peak Energy

All the elements come together for the last chorus, the track at its fullest.

### Outro: The Night Continues

The elements are gradually stripped away while the core beat carries on to the end.

## Concepts in Focus

Tubbe are heirs to {{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }}, the scene that fused 1980s electro and synth-pop with punk attitude and a taste for performance, and which grew in part around Munich, the city where the duo was founded. Discogs calls them a "queer electro punk duo", and the description fits: cheap-sounding synths used with confidence, a provocative image, and lyrics that poke at their surroundings. Compare {{ <track_link source="chicks-on-speed-euro-trash-girl.md" /> }}, a Munich electroclash act of the generation before.

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} is the song's main instrument: Scheuermann's electronics supply the bass, the beat and the bright chords. Synth-pop's sound has long been the sound of nightlife, which is exactly why it suits a song that both celebrates and mocks the city's party image.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, made by the studio Modest Department and featuring Katrin Kaspar as a "flower girl", came out on Audiolith's channel in March 2015, the week the album was released. Its accompanying text turns the song into a little story of small-town dreams and big-city letdown, a framing that makes the irony in the chorus easier to hear.

## Lyrical Themes

The song treats Berlin as a destination of longing and a place of disappointment at once: the city of brightness and reinvention that, up close, turns out to be crowded, noisy and full of people who seem to be doing better than you. The return to something small and familiar, a corner pub with a homely name, is the punchline.

## Production Techniques

Klaus Scheuermann handles the electronics and production in Tubbe, with Henri Jakobs on vocals, guitar and bass. The production pairs vintage-sounding synthesizers with crisp, contemporary drum programming, so the track sounds both retro and current.

## Cultural Impact

"In Berlin" comes from Keine Arbeit lieber Tanzen ("no work, rather dance"), Tubbe's second album, released on 13 March 2015 by the Hamburg label Audiolith, with guests including Torsun of Egotronic and the jazz pianist Joachim Kühn. German Wikipedia describes it as much more socially critical than their debut Eiscafe Ravetto, while staying danceable. The duo announced a split in 2020, then revealed in 2023 that it had been staged and returned with a third album, Bankrott in Utopia.
