## Genre and Mood

**D12**'s **Bitch** is hip-hop and rap at its most deliberately provocative and irreverent. The title is the subject, and the reviews of 2004 split along that line: the craft could earn praise, the content mostly did not.

## Song Summary

Bitch is the thirteenth track of D12 World and runs 4:56. Eminem produced it, with Luis Resto as additional producer, and mixed it with Steve King. The writing is credited to six members of the Detroit crew, Marshall Mathers, Denaun Porter, Von Carlisle, Ondre Moore, Rufus Johnson and DeShaun Holton, together with Resto, and the official credits name one additional vocalist, Deanna Johnson. Plugged In called it a misogynist anthem in which women are called a string of names.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="rapping" /> }} is usually judged on control, yet the verse the Chicago Maroon singled out works by losing control on purpose: it breaks down into nonsense and snaps back. Listen for the moment the words stop making sense and the rhythm still holds.

A {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} shapes the sound a group raps over. Eminem built this one, with Luis Resto, a keyboardist from the Detroit suburbs who has worked closely with him since The Eminem Show, credited as additional producer, the same pairing behind {{ <track_link source="d12-6-in-the-morning.md" /> }}.

A {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} can change the colour of a track with a single part. Here the credits list Deanna Johnson as the one voice from outside the crew, a sung contrast to the MCs.

## Artistic Context

D12 formed in Detroit in 1996; its lineup was Eminem, Proof, Bizarre, Denaun Porter, Kuniva and Swifty McVay. D12 World, their second album, came out on 27 April 2004 on Shady Records and Interscope. The Indiana Daily Student heard Bitch as a rerun: in its view the song was better the first time, when it was called Superman, Eminem's 2002 song with his frequent collaborator Dina Rae about the rocky relationships and promiscuity of the women in his life.

## Impact and Reception

Bitch was never a single. D12 World debuted at number one on the Billboard 200 with about 544,000 copies in its first week and went double platinum, while Metacritic's score of 58 marked mixed reviews. Listen for the craft, and decide for yourself what it is in service of.
