## Genre and Mood

**Nick Monaco** and **David Marston** saw **Pussy** filed under electronic music, nu-disco and house, but their label described it in the vocabulary of rock. It is provocative by intent, sultry in its delivery and playful about both: the label called it an anthem of lust, tinged with controversy and playful eroticism.

## Song Summary

Wolf + Lamb released the single in October 2016 with a statement of purpose. It called the song the pair's newest musical manifesto and an open letter dedicated to feminine divinity, and said they wrote it during a stay in the Blue Mountains of Jamaica, overlooking Kingston. The music draws on bohemian glam rock. Monaco sings, his vocals sensually raw and explicitly direct; Marston plays guitar, with layered harmonies and a penetrating solo. The cover is a commissioned piece by the Toronto artist Fucci.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="guitar solo" /> }} is a rock and blues tradition: the guitar steps out of the accompaniment and carries the melody alone. House music seldom makes room for one, which is why the label's description of a penetrating solo stands out.

The single also came with a {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed and filmed by Joseph Holton and published by Wolf + Lamb in December 2016. No source describes its images.

Behind both sits a piece of {{ <concept_link name="club culture" /> }}. Monaco was, Mixmag wrote in 2014, the youngest member of Crew Love, the squad of artists brought together by the house veterans Soul Clap and Wolf + Lamb; Soul Clap's label had released his breakout track, Set The Table. Compare {{ <track_link source="feathered-sun-saubohnen.md" /> }}, house from a Berlin collective that also brings live instruments to the club.

## Artistic Context

Pussy appeared on 28 October 2016 as a digital single on Wolf + Lamb Records, and as a limited-edition 7-inch lathe cut; some stores print the title as P***y. Discogs lists a 7-inch on Wolf + Lamb Music, catalogue WLM54, pairing it with Cherry Juice, another Monaco and Marston song, and files it under nu-disco and house.

No chart run or press review of Pussy is documented in the sources found; its public life was the label's release, the video and the lathe-cut 7-inch. Play it loud, and wait for the solo.
