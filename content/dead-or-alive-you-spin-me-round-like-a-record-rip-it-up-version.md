## Genre and Mood

**Dead or Alive**'s **You Spin Me Round (Like a Record)** in its **Rip It Up Version** is the Hi-NRG anthem once more: synth-pop and disco driven by machines, energetic, bold and theatrical. Here it is one link in a chain, the fifth of eight hits run together into one continuous stretch of dance music.

## Song Summary

The You Spin Me Round Rip It Up Version is the band's own remix. Mike Stock's list of the song's versions gives it 4:27 and names Dead or Alive as its mixers, where the 7-inch (3:19) and the Murder Mix (8:02) were mixed by Phil Harding. The credits of the US CD of Rip It Up, as Discogs lists them, likewise give the remix to the band, with Stock Aitken Waterman still credited as producers. The song is the same pursuit, warning and confession.

On the album it follows Lover Come Back to Me, each track segueing into the next.

### The Song

The verses, the warning and the title hook of the Stock Aitken Waterman production, at 4:27: more than a minute longer than the 7-inch and about half the length of the Murder Mix.

### Going Out

It hands over to I'll Save You All My Kisses.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} is a new version made by reworking a recording's parts. Usually an outside producer does it; here the band remixed their own number one more than two years after it topped the chart. Set it beside the {{ <track_link source="dead-or-alive-you-spin-me-round-like-a-record-murder-mix.md" /> }}, the club-length version made inside the production team in 1984.

The new setting is a {{ <concept_link name="dj mix" /> }}. Wikipedia describes Rip It Up as eight singles from Youthquake and Mad, Bad and Dangerous to Know segued together much like a DJ mix: tracks blended so that one flows into the next, the way a club DJ would play them.

The album is also a {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }}, a greatest-hits record of previously released material, as the US CD's notes put it. This one turned the singles into a continuous programme.

## Artistic Context

Rip It Up came out on Epic on 6 July 1987, after Youthquake (1985) and Mad, Bad and Dangerous to Know (1986) and before Nude (1988). The US CD credits Pete Burns on vocals, Mike Percy on bass, Tim Lever on keyboards and saxophone and Steve Coy on percussion. A concert video, Rip It Up Live, followed in 1988, and a Japanese edition, Rip It Up +1, added a bonus track in 2004.

## Impact and Reception

Rip It Up did not chart in the UK, but it reached number 4 in Japan, number 130 in Australia and number 195 in the United States, and AllMusic rated it four stars out of five.
