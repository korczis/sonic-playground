## Genre and Mood

**Solange** found her own voice on **Losing You**, a track that mixes R&B, indie pop and eighties-tinged synth-pop. Its mood is bittersweet, groovy and full of longing: a breakup song that somehow makes you want to move. It sounds effortless, but every detail is placed with care.

## Song Summary

The song is about a relationship slipping away and the painful clarity of knowing it is over. Solange's voice is light and intimate, almost conversational, as she asks what she is still holding on to. The production is airy and skittering, with crisp drums, bright synths and a bouncing bass, and that upbeat energy against the sad subject is the song's genius: it is the sound of dancing through heartbreak. The production feels handmade rather than polished to a high gloss, and that slight looseness, the drums a little dry, the synths a little woozy, gives the song its intimacy.

## Musical Breakdown

The track moves with the grace of a classic pop single, building its groove patiently.

### Intro

Skittering percussion and shimmering synths open the song, setting an airy, mid-tempo groove.

### Verse 1

Solange's voice enters soft and close, describing the slow end of the relationship with quiet honesty.

### Chorus

The hook brightens with layered vocals and a fuller beat, the repeated question about losing you becoming catchy and aching at once.

### Bridge

The arrangement opens into a more reflective moment, the synths and voice floating before the groove returns.

### Outro

The track rides its groove out with ad-libs and layered vocals, fading gently.

## Artistic Context

"Losing You" was released in 2012 as the lead single from *True*, an EP Solange made with Dev Hynes, the songwriter and producer behind Blood Orange, and released on the indie label Terrible Records. It marked a turn away from mainstream R&B toward a more independent, eighties-influenced sound. It also signalled a move toward making music on her own terms, outside the expectations that came with her family name. Its video, directed by Melina Matsoukas, was shot in Cape Town and celebrates the flamboyant fashion of the sapeurs. The pairing with Hynes was important: his loose, eighties-inflected production gave her a space between indie and R&B where her voice sounded completely at home.

## Impact and Reception

The song was widely praised by critics and is often seen as the moment Solange stepped out as an artist with her own distinct identity, a path that led to her acclaimed later albums. It is here for its rare ability to make sadness feel light. Let it play and dance anyway.
