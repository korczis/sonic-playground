## Genre and Mood

**Dada Life** built **Everything Is Free** the way their fans expected in 2012: a singer, a huge bassline and a drop. It is electro house at its most direct, energetic and euphoric, and Elektro Daily heard in it an especially strong melody and overtly party-starting lyrics, which makes it the most celebratory track on its album.

## Song Summary

Everything Is Free is track nine of The Rules of Dada, 4:17 long, written by Olle Corneér and Stefan Engblom with Anthony Mills and produced by the duo. Your EDM heard it as close to the duo's modern sound and predicted a club hit; Magnetic thought it combined their older sound with their newer style of electro house, with a melody somewhat like Happy Violence and a much heavier, fatter bassline.

## Musical Breakdown

There is no timed guide; this walk-through follows Your EDM's account in order.

### Intro

The track opens with a bang, a female vocalist singing over a monster bassline. Dancing Astronaut heard the start differently, as slow grinding synth sounds before a familiar Dada-style drop.

### Verse

After a short break the first verse begins. The bassline drives the groove, and a high-end lead hits in tandem with it, so bass and lead act as one riff.

### Groove

Pitch bends change the feel, and percussion is added piece by piece to push the groove along.

### Breakdown

The arrangement builds to a final breakdown, where the vocals come back for one more go-around.

### Outro

Another gigantic build-up leads to a towering final drop, and then the duo takes the track home.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} is the song's engine. Rather than sitting under the melody, it is the riff, and the lead plays with it rather than above it. Compare {{ <track_link source="dada-life-bass-don-t-cry.md" /> }}, the track just before it, where the bass carries everything without a singer.

The {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }} appears twice: once into the main groove and once, after the last vocal, as what Your EDM called a gigantic rise.

That release is the {{ <concept_link name="drop" /> }}. Dancing Astronaut heard the track move from grinding synths into a familiar Dada-style drop and found the progression logical, fitting the vocal perfectly; the final drop is the climax the whole arrangement is built for.

## Artistic Context

Dada Life are Olle Corneér and Stefan Engblom, a Stockholm duo named in homage to the Dada art movement and known for humour on stage. The Rules of Dada, their second album, came out on 16 October 2012 on their own So Much Dada and Universal. They told Dancing Astronaut they like to treat their vocalists like instruments, part of the track rather than a big vocal on top.

## Impact and Reception

Everything Is Free was not a single, but Stefan Engblom named it his favourite track on the album, and Magnetic called it an amazing vocal track. Your EDM expected to hear it at shows and festivals for the rest of the year. The album reached number four on the US Dance/Electronic Albums chart. Listen for the moment the bass and the lead lock together.
