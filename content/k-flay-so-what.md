## Genre and Mood

**K.Flay** was known as a rapper when she made **So What**, but this track leans towards alternative rock, with hip-hop still in the voice. The East Bay Express, reviewing the EP it comes from, called it more of a rock song, anchored by guitar riffs and churning organ. The mood that sound suggests is gritty and defiant, a heavier, rougher setting than a beat alone would give.

## Song Summary

So What is the fourth of five tracks on What If It Is, the EP RCA Records released on 6 August 2013. K.Flay described the EP's recurring theme as being an outsider: feeling disconnected from yourself, from the people you love or from the world around you. The review heard socially conscious rhymes about confusion and uncertainty across the record. No source discusses the lyric of So What on its own, so this page leaves its words to your own listening.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }} is a short instrumental figure repeated until it becomes the song's backbone. The review's word "anchored" is the point: in rap, that job usually falls to a drum pattern or a sample, and here it falls to the guitar.

{{ <concept_link name="rapping" text="Rapping" /> }} is rhythmic speech over a beat, and K.Flay began doing it at Stanford University. On this track her voice sits in a rock setting, which is why the review called it more of a rock song.

The {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} shapes how a recording sounds. The credits RCA supplied with the track name Alex Fitts as producer and as co-writer with Kristine Flaherty, with Chris Carmouche as mixing engineer, while the EP's announcement said she had written and produced the tracks herself. Credits and press releases do not always agree, and the label's metadata is the more specific of the two.

## Artistic Context

K.Flay, born Kristine Flaherty in Wilmette, Illinois, in 1985, began rapping at Stanford and signed to RCA in 2012. She wrote the EP after moving to Brooklyn, she told the East Bay Express, and that summer she was supporting Icona Pop on tour. She left RCA in 2013 and released Life as a Dog independently in 2014; her rock breakthrough came later with {{ <track_link source="k-flay-blood-in-the-cut.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

The East Bay Express found each track of the EP distinct and tightly produced, and wrote that it pushed her image as a white female MC further past novelty and into artistic territory. Listen for the guitar, and hear where her music was heading.
