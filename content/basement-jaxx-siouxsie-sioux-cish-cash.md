## Genre and Mood

**Basement Jaxx** gave **Cish Cash** to **Siouxsie Sioux**, and the result surprised some of the duo's own listeners. Wikipedia calls it punk-influenced, Spin called it a synth rocker and PopMatters heard it tip its hat to electroclash. It is intense and provocative, and with a punk voice over dance-floor machinery, playful too.

## Song Summary

Cish Cash is the tenth track on Kish Kash, the duo's third album, released on 20 October 2003 by XL Recordings. Felix Buxton told the New Zealand Press Association that he and Simon Ratcliffe wrote the track first and then felt it needed a vocalist like Siouxsie Sioux; after listening to other singers' tapes, someone suggested asking her, and she was keen. The writing is credited to Buxton, Ratcliffe and Sioux. Nathan Curran plays drums, and Shelley Thraves is among the other credited singers.

## Musical Breakdown

### Main Section

No timed analysis maps the track's parts, so this page does not invent intro, verse and chorus. What reviewers describe is its texture: in the New Zealand Herald, Russell Baillie heard Sioux's deadpan vocal over fuzzy breakbeats and called it possibly the maddest-sounding thing on the album. PopMatters' John Davidson compared her to Martina Topley-Bird jacked up rather than stoned out.

### The Voice

Deadpan is Baillie's word for her delivery. Listen for how the voice holds back while the fuzzy breakbeats run underneath it.

## Concepts in Focus

As a {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}, Siouxsie Sioux was not added to a finished song but chosen for one the producers had already written. Buxton's account shows the order: music first, then a search for the right voice.

The {{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }} connection is the critics' reading. That early-2000s scene joined synths and punk attitude with deadpan vocals, and PopMatters found Cish Cash borrowing from it without becoming a fashion victim; Spin's Will Hermes thought it tried a bit too hard. Compare the deadpan of {{ <track_link source="felix-da-house-cat-madame-hollywood.md" /> }}.

A {{ <concept_link name="breakbeat" /> }} places kick and snare off the even grid of house. Here it comes with the fuzz Baillie heard, and it may be part of why Buxton said you have to dance to this track in a different way.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} is credited on the duo's channel to Pleix; Wikipedia notes that it was made without Siouxsie and that the track was never released as a single.

## Artistic Context

Siouxsie Sioux fronted Siouxsie and the Banshees, one of punk's pivotal bands. Kish Kash also featured Dizzee Rascal, JC Chasez and Meshell Ndegeocello, and its opener {{ <track_link source="basement-jaxx-good-luck.md" /> }} gave the BellRays' Lisa Kekaula a rock-leaning song too.

## Impact and Reception

Buxton said some listeners asked whether this was really Basement Jaxx, and he took that as a good sign. Kish Kash reached number 17 in the UK, was nominated for the 2004 Mercury Prize and won the first Grammy for Best Dance/Electronic Album. Play Cish Cash after Good Luck and hear how far one album stretched.
