## Genre and Mood

**D12**'s **American Psycho** is hip-hop and rap played as a horror film. It is dark, eerie and deliberately provocative: a Sputnikmusic reviewer singled it out as the best horror song on Devil's Night, not the best song on the album but the clearest example of how to make a truly twisted one work.

## Song Summary

Three members of the Detroit crew take turns on it: Eminem, Bizarre and Kon Artis, the name Denaun Porter raps under. The imagery is a serial killer's, and the same reviewer heard the three MCs trading in Silence of the Lambs territory. Eminem produced it, with Jeff Bass credited as an additional producer; the official credits add DJ Head as drum programmer. The writing is shared by Mathers, Porter, Rufus Johnson (Bizarre) and Bass, and the album version runs 4:37.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the track section by section; this follows what its reviewers singled out, in the order the verses come.

### The Beat

The Sputnikmusic review calls the beat foreboding, menacing and scary, and contrasts it with the more in-your-face beat of Ain't Nuttin' But Music two tracks earlier.

### Eminem's Verse

Eminem opens. RapReviews found the opening lyrically good, and the Sputnikmusic reviewer said he rips the song up at the start.

### Bizarre's Verse

For RapReviews, this is where Eminem is upstaged: Bizarre's verse is the disturbing one. The Sputnikmusic reviewer agreed that he is in his element, which in his words means you will love him or hate him.

### Kon Artis's Verse

The track ends with Denaun Porter, whom RapReviews heard as the smoothest voice in the group, and the Sputnikmusic review credits him with some of his best rapping on the album.

## Concepts in Focus

American Psycho is a {{ <concept_link name="posse cut" /> }}: one beat, several MCs, each with his own verse. It is a contest as much as a collaboration: the most famous member opens, yet two reviewers remembered the others. Compare a whole crew on a slower beat in {{ <track_link source="d12-40-oz.md" /> }}.

{{ <concept_link name="rapping" /> }} in a horror song depends on voice as much as on words. Bizarre's deep, slow-flowing style, as RapReviews described it, and Kon Artis's smoother delivery give the same subject three different temperatures.

A {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} on a group album can be one of its members. Eminem built this beat; Jeff Bass, who co-produced Lose Yourself with him and shared a Grammy with him for The Slim Shady LP, is credited alongside, and DJ Head programmed the drums.

## Artistic Context

Devil's Night was D12's debut album, released on 19 June 2001 by Shady Records and Interscope Records. Eminem and Bass produced much of it, with Dr. Dre and Denaun Porter behind other tracks. The group returned to the theme on their second album, D12 World (2004), with American Psycho II, a Dr. Dre and Mike Elizondo production featuring B-Real of Cypress Hill.

## Impact and Reception

Devil's Night entered the Billboard 200 at number one and was certified double platinum in the United States, though Metacritic's aggregate of 58 marks mixed reviews. American Psycho was never a single. Listen with the lights on.
