## Genre and Mood

**Dada Life** made their name with electro house that refuses to take itself seriously, and **Feed The Dada** is that formula at full size: a big-room dance track built to be energetic, playful and euphoric in front of a crowd. Elektro Daily called it a hands-in-the-air, big-room track that is hard not to love.

## Song Summary

The track is a study in contrast. Dancing Astronaut heard it as one of the best tracks on The Rules of Dada for balancing the "sugary sweet" with harder craziness: a softer vocal section is set against a banging drop, and by the time the album came out the drop had already proven a crowd favourite. The title reads as the joke: an order to the audience to feed the duo.

## Musical Breakdown

### Opening

On the album, Elektro Daily noted, the track flows straight out of the opener, Kick Out the Epic Motherfucker, the two songs' vocals nearly overlapping.

### Vocal Section

The soft part comes first in Dancing Astronaut's account: a sweeter, gentler vocal passage that lowers the energy before it is released.

### Drop

Then the hard part: the drop the reviewers singled out. No review we found describes the closing bars.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="drop" /> }} is the point of the track, and it works because of what comes before it. A drop is a release, and a release needs tension. Here the tension comes from softness rather than noise: the sugary vocal section makes the hard return feel bigger than it would after another loud passage.

That design belongs to {{ <concept_link name="club culture" /> }} at its largest scale. Big-room tracks are judged by what thousands of people do at the drop, and Dancing Astronaut's measure of success was exactly that, a crowd favourite before the album was out.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Daniel Jigenstedt and released with the single in August 2012, carried the duo's humour beyond the stage; Live for Live Music called it hysterical, in typical Dada Life fashion. For an instrumental from the same album that critics heard very differently, try {{ <track_link source="dada-life-arrive-beautiful-leave-ugly.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Olle Cornéer and Stefan Engblom formed Dada Life in Stockholm in 2006 and named it after the Dada art movement; bananas, champagne and their Dada Land events are running jokes of their shows. Feed The Dada, released on 27 August 2012, was the second single from their second album, where it is track two. The pair wrote it with Anton Hård and Niclas Lundin and produced it themselves; The Rules of Dada followed on So Much Dada and Universal on 16 October 2012.

## Impact and Reception

The single reached number 32 in Sweden, and a remix release followed on 10 September 2012. Magnetic Magazine described the album's first two tracks as previously released singles that had topped dance charts, and Live for Live Music reported that the duo were promising "the biggest drop ever" with the new album. Play it loud, wait through the sweet part, and judge the drop yourself.
