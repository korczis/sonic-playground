## Genre and Mood

**Fischerspooner**'s **Ersatz** is electroclash without a singer: electro and synth-pop machinery doing all the talking. The feelings pull two ways. Dafydd McKaharay, writing in 2002, heard a brooding 1980s anthem, which is where dark comes from; Gullbuy's reviewer heard a showy piece that keeps changing its mind, which is where playful comes from. Repetition holds the two together.

## Song Summary

Ersatz is one of two instrumentals on #1, the duo's debut, which first came out in 2001 in a limited run on International DeeJay Gigolo Records. It was track seven of nine on the original album and moved to eleventh place on the longer reissue, ahead of two bonus tracks. It runs 3:56. Wikipedia credits all the music to Fischerspooner, with Warren Fischer as composer, and the production to the duo with Nicolas Vernhes. The memorable detail is its restlessness: Gullbuy heard it switch patterns and styles so often that it becomes a Reader's Digest tour of electro, past and present.

## Musical Breakdown

No source gives times, so this guide follows the order two reviews described.

### Intro

Silent Uproar heard the track begin with mellow tone-arpeggios, notes of a chord circling calmly.

### Main Section

Then the switching starts. McKaharay heard stops and repeated notes that only modern digital effects could make, inside something that still sounds like the 1980s.

### Outro

By the end, in Silent Uproar's description, the arpeggios have morphed into a cut-up keyboard demo.

## Concepts in Focus

An {{ <concept_link name="instrumental" /> }} on an album built around Casey Spooner's performance hands everything to Warren Fischer's machines. Compare it with the album's other instrumental, {{ <track_link source="fischerspooner-fucker.md" /> }}, which holds one groove and changes the effects; Ersatz changes the patterns themselves.

The {{ <concept_link name="arpeggio" /> }} is the track's starting material: a chord played one note at a time. What happens to it, from mellow to chopped up, is the track's whole story.

{{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }} revived early-1980s electro and synth-pop with a knowing wink. McKaharay heard Ersatz as a cross between OMD and Autechre, the British synth-pop band and the British electronic duo, and wrote that it revels in the sense of artifice; ersatz means a substitute or imitation.

## Artistic Context

Fischer, a classically trained musician, and Spooner, a video artist and experimental theatre performer, formed Fischerspooner in New York in 1998; the group grew into a troupe of more than twenty performers. Wikipedia lists Gary Numan, New Order and Orchestral Manoeuvres in the Dark among their influences. Gullbuy's reviewer wondered whether the title nodded to Adult's label Ersatz Audio, and left the question to the band.

## Impact and Reception

Opinions split. McKaharay's review, run by the Phoenix New Times and the Cleveland Scene, called Ersatz the best track on the album. Silent Uproar, unimpressed by most of the album, counted it among the few highlights of its second half. Alex Wisgard of Drowned in Sound heard it among three tracks of slow dance boredom. Listen for the moment it stops being calm, then pick a side.
