## Genre and Mood

**Apocalyptica** play **Sad But True** as four cellists and nothing else: no guitars, no drum kit, no singer. It sits where neoclassical chamber music meets symphonic metal, a cover that keeps the original's intense, dark weight and has to find it without a single amplifier.

## Song Summary

Sad But True was the fifth and final single from Metallica's 1991 self-titled album, written by James Hetfield and Lars Ulrich, with Hetfield's lyric; Ulrich dates its first idea to 1986. Metallica wrote and demoed it in E, then tuned down to D, and their producer Bob Rock recalled that this was when the riff really became huge. Apocalyptica recorded it as the fifth of eight covers on their 1996 debut, where it runs 4:48 against the 5:24 of Metallica's version. The recording on this page is that 1996 performance, remastered for the album's twentieth-anniversary edition of 22 July 2016.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} can keep every note and change every sound. Sad But True tests that hard, because much of its weight came from a studio decision rather than from the notes.

That decision concerned the {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }}. Tuning down to D lowered every note, and in Rock's account that is what made the riff huge. A cello quartet has no amplifier to lean on; the weight has to come from the players.

As an {{ <concept_link name="instrumental" /> }}, the cover drops the words and keeps the shape of the melody.

Finally, {{ <concept_link name="remastering" /> }}: the 2016 edition gave the 1996 recordings a new master, which Reflections of Darkness heard as cleaner, its details brought forward. {{ <track_link source="apocalyptica-battery.md" /> }}, added to the same edition, was a new recording instead.

## Artistic Context

Apocalyptica formed in 1993 at Helsinki's Sibelius Academy: Eicca Toppinen, Paavo Lötjönen, Max Lilja and Antero Manninen, a cello quartet that began as a tribute to Metallica. Mercury released Plays Metallica by Four Cellos on 10 May 1996, produced by Pekka Ritualuoto. It went platinum in Finland, where Metallica's Sad But True had reached number one, and gold in Germany and Poland.

## Impact and Reception

Opinions on this track are mixed. The Green Man Review counted it with Enter Sandman and Harvester of Sorrow among the songs not quite as moving on cello, and a Sputnikmusic reviewer felt it was handled competently but that better-suited Metallica songs exist. On stage it changed: at New York's Town Hall in 2017, Superconductor heard it played very fast, bringing the thick Metallica crunch. Hear Metallica's single first, then this, and listen for where the weight went.
