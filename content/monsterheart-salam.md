## Genre and Mood

**Monsterheart** made **Salam** the quietest kind of statement. It is dream pop and synth-pop in the form of a ballad: FM4 heard a minimal, pulsing song and called it eerie and wonderful, and The Gap found the title track more serious, darker and more thoughtful than the rest of its album, almost spiritual. Those are the feelings named here: haunting, introspective, spiritual.

## Song Summary

Salam is one of the Arabic words for peace and a common greeting, and Anna Attar told The Gap she simply finds the word and its meaning beautiful. FM4 noted that the title invites a political reading. The song itself, she said, is about a woman who asks her partner to let go; at the time she had been asking herself why women join the Islamic State. The Gap heard images of drowning people, burned bridges and weapons, sung in a soft timbre over reverb and an earworm tremolo. At 2:08 it is the third of eight tracks on the album that shares its name.

### Sound Breakdown

The bed is minimal: FM4 heard a pulsing ballad that recalls the tender moments of Suicide, the New York electronic punk duo. The Gap describes the album's palette as minimal beats, Moog cadences and layered synthesizers, with reverb and tremolo giving Salam its shimmer.

### Main Section

Attar's voice stays soft. For this record, she said, she slowed down considerably; the songs are very gentle, held back and, somehow, very feminine.

## Concepts in Focus

Salam is {{ <concept_link name="lo-fi" /> }} by method rather than by pose. Attar recorded the album mostly in her own flat in Vienna, most songs in a single take, and told The Gap she just records, with little regard for technique. The intimacy you hear is partly the room and the first take.

The Gap names two effects. {{ <concept_link name="reverb" /> }} puts space around a sparse arrangement so that a few sounds fill it, and {{ <concept_link name="tremolo" /> }}, a pulsing up and down of volume, makes a held sound waver. The review calls that tremolo an earworm. FM4's comparison points to {{ <track_link source="martin-rev-sparks.md" /> }}, a solo track by Suicide's Martin Rev.

## Artistic Context

Monsterheart is the solo project of Anna Attar, born in Vienna in 1986, an art student and musician who calls her music graveyard pop; she was nominated for the FM4 Award in 2015. After the albums Monsterheart in 2011 and W in 2014, both on Seayou Records, she released Salam on the same Viennese label on 8 December 2017. The Gap premiered the video on 6 November; Attar made it herself, with Lukas Schnee on camera. The songs had been finished for two years, she said, and she was still flying solo.

## Impact and Reception

Salam was a small independent release, and its reception lives in Austrian music media: FM4 welcomed new music from Monsterheart, and The Gap heard a winter lullaby. She has kept going since, with the album The New in 2021. Play it in a quiet room and let the tremolo do its work.
