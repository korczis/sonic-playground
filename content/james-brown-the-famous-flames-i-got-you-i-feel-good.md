## Genre and Mood

**James Brown & The Famous Flames** made **I Got You (I Feel Good)** at the point where soul and rhythm and blues were turning into funk. It is energetic and joyful, under three minutes of a man announcing his happiness at the top of his voice; Cash Box heard a rhythmic, funky song about a very lucky man.

## Song Summary

The title says it: he feels good because he has someone. Underneath is a twelve-bar blues, dressed in a brass-heavy arrangement close to Papa's Got a Brand New Bag, Brown's hit from earlier that year. The band puts its weight on the first beat of each bar, and Brown's vocal is punctuated by screams and shouts. Maceo Parker takes an alto saxophone solo. Brown wrote and produced the record himself.

## Musical Breakdown

The sources describe the song's form and parts, not how it opens or ends; listen for that yourself.

### Main Section

Each verse runs the twelve-bar pattern while the horns, three trumpets, a trombone and five saxophones, fill the arrangement around the vocal. Jimmy Nolen's guitar, David Williams's bass and Melvin Parker's drums carry the beat, with the emphasis on the one.

### Solo

In Maceo Parker's alto saxophone solo a horn, not Brown's voice, leads.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="blues" /> }} gives the song its frame. A twelve-bar blues repeats a cycle of three four-bar phrases; here it carries a horn section and a dance beat instead of a single guitar.

The {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} is where the future is. The song stresses "the one", the first beat of the bar, an emphasis linked to Brown's developing funk style; he later named that approach The One. Listen for the whole band landing together on that beat.

Brown has been called the world's most sampled recording artist, and {{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} gave this song a second life: its screams turn up in hip-hop and dance tracks, and Yung Gravy sampled it in Supa Good!!! for the film Dog Man. For another Brown record reused in the 1990s, hear {{ <track_link source="smoke-city-underwater-love.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

James Brown, the Godfather of Soul, rose as lead singer of the Famous Flames, Bobby Byrd's vocal group. This song began as I Found You, which Yvonne Fair recorded in 1962 to little success; a 1964 version for Smash, I Got You, was withdrawn as a single after a court order in Brown's contract dispute with King. The hit was cut on 6 May 1965 in Miami and issued by King that October, credited to James Brown and the Famous Flames. The recording here is that 1965 single as collected on The 50th Anniversary Collection, a fifty-track anthology spanning 1956 to 1988, released by Universal in 2003.

## Impact and Reception

It reached number three on the Billboard Hot 100, the highest of Brown's 91 entries, topped the R&B chart for six weeks and reached number 29 in the UK. It entered the Grammy Hall of Fame in 2013 and stood at 78 on Rolling Stone's 500 Greatest Songs of All Time in 2004. Sports arenas still play it. Turn it up and wait for the scream.
