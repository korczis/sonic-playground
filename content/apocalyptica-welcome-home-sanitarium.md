## Genre and Mood

**Apocalyptica** close their first album with **Welcome Home (Sanitarium)**, played by four cellists and nothing else, where neoclassical chamber music meets symphonic metal. Sputnikmusic heard the song as perfectly suited to the mournful feel of the cello, and that is its mood: melancholic first, then haunting.

## Song Summary

Metallica recorded Welcome Home (Sanitarium) for Master of Puppets in 1986, credited to James Hetfield, Lars Ulrich and Kirk Hammett. Drawing on Ken Kesey's novel One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest, the lyric gives the thoughts of a patient unjustly caged in a mental institution. It climbs from a clean arpeggiated riff, alternating bars of four and six beats, through quiet verses and distorted choruses to an aggressive finale. Apocalyptica recorded it as the eighth and last of their 1996 covers; this page plays that performance in the 2016 remaster.

### Intro

Metallica open on clean single notes and harmonics. No source says how the cellos begin; listen for which of the four takes those first notes.

### Main Section

Sputnikmusic describes Eicca Toppinen's method for the whole album: three cellos recreate the band and the fourth plays the vocal line. Applied here, one cello carries Hetfield's verses while the others share the riff. Teen Ink found the result very melodic.

### Outro

Sputnikmusic heard the cover's outro as one of the few moments on the album that hint at what came next on Inquisition Symphony, their 1998 album.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} keeps a song's notes and can change everything about its sound. Welcome Home is a hard test: the original tells its story in clean guitars and distortion.

That makes the cover a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }}. Without a distortion pedal, the turn from quiet to heavy must come from the bow and from how the parts are shared. Listen for the moment the single singing cello gives way to all four driving together.

The song turns on an {{ <concept_link name="arpeggio" /> }}, a chord played one note at a time rather than struck at once. Broken chords are home ground for bowed strings, which may be part of why this transfer works.

The 2016 edition is an exercise in {{ <concept_link name="remastering" /> }}: a new master of the 1996 recording, now followed by three bonus tracks. Compare {{ <track_link source="apocalyptica-the-unforgiven.md" /> }}, another Metallica ballad on the same record.

## Artistic Context

Apocalyptica formed in 1993 at Helsinki's Sibelius Academy: Eicca Toppinen, Paavo Lötjönen, Max Lilja and Antero Manninen. After a 1995 show, a label employee invited them to record these songs. Produced by Pekka Ritualuoto, the album came out in 1996, drawing mostly on Master of Puppets and Metallica's self-titled record. The remaster followed on 22 July 2016 from Harmageddon Records, under licence to OMN Label Services.

## Impact and Reception

Sputnikmusic's reviewer named this one of the few songs that make the transition fantastically, with Master of Puppets and The Unforgiven. The band kept it: it appears on Plays Metallica by Four Cellos - A Live Performance, released in 2018. Hear Metallica's version, then this one, and listen for where the madness goes when nobody sings.
