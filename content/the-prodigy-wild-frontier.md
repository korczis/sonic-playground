## Genre and Mood

**The Prodigy**'s **Wild Frontier** is the sunniest corner of a record built for violence. It is electronic, big beat with breakbeats, and euphoric, futuristic and playful: Metal Hammer called it a real departure by Liam Howlett's standards and an absolute gem, a euphoric, futuristic odyssey that feels like jumping headfirst into a Mario Kart game, which it meant as praise.

## Song Summary

Howlett wrote it with Joe Erskine and Luca Gulotta, who work as KillSonik and also co-produced it, and with Tim Hutton and Nick Halkes. Maxim is the only credited vocalist. The embed above is the album recording, uploaded by the band; the stop-motion video, released the same day as the single, is not embedded here.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the track in order or gives times, and none describes Maxim's part or the lyrics. Two reviews describe what sets its sound apart.

### The Melody

The List, otherwise unimpressed by the album, picked out a melody line on Wild Frontier that evokes eight-bit computer music.

### The Beats

Sonicabuse called it the first track on the album that allows the beats to breathe without layers of processed guitars.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="chiptune" /> }} is music made with, or imitating, the sound chips of old consoles and home computers. No source calls Wild Frontier chiptune, but The List heard its melody borrowing that eight-bit colour, and Metal Hammer's video-game comparison points the same way.

A {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} shapes how a track sounds; on a Prodigy record that is usually Howlett alone. Here KillSonik share the writing and production, and that outside hand may be part of why Metal Hammer heard a departure. Asked about the track's different vibe, the band told Metal Hammer that you should be able to bring in a few different influences but retain what you are about.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, devised and directed by the Dutch filmmaker Mascha Halberstad with animation by Elmer Kaan and produced by Superfelix, is in stop motion. Howlett had seen Halberstad's animation Munya in mij on television by chance. A biker camping in the woods meets two hunters who have killed a rabbit and a boar; the fox from {{ <track_link source="the-prodigy-nasty.md" /> }} returns, joining a moose and a cat to fight back, and the dead animals come back with the hunters' heads.

## Artistic Context

Wild Frontier came out on 23 February 2015 as the second single from The Day Is My Enemy, the band's sixth album, released on 30 March by Take Me to the Hospital and Cooking Vinyl. It sits sixth of fourteen tracks, just before Rok-Weiler. The expanded edition added remixes by KillSonik, Wilkinson, Jesse and the Wolf, and a Shadow Child VIP.

## Impact and Reception

The album debuted at number one in the UK. Not everyone heard a departure: Drowned in Sound put Wild Frontier with the heard-it-all-before tracks, The Line of Best Fit heard it copying Skrillex, Dirtyphonics and Noisia, and The New Zealand Herald called it generic throwback filler. Play it after Nasty and hear the change of air.
