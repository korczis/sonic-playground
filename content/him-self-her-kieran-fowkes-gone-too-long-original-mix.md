## Genre and Mood

**Him_Self_Her** and **Kieran Fowkes** make **Gone Too Long (Original Mix)** the kind of deep house record that hits hardest at the end of the night. It is house music with a soulful, aching centre, rolling and steady, and its feelings are longing, melancholy and a hypnotic, slow-burning pull.

## Song Summary

The song is about absence, the ache of someone who has been away for too long and the hope that they will come back. Fowkes sings it with a warm, yearning voice that sits right in the middle of the groove, while the production surrounds him with a deep, rounded bassline, crisp, restrained drums and dusky chords. At over seven minutes, the original mix takes its time, letting the emotion build slowly instead of forcing it.

## Musical Breakdown

The track follows the long, gradual arc of a classic deep house record.

### Intro

A steady kick and shuffling percussion open the mix, with the bassline sliding in low and smooth.

### Build-up

Moody chords and subtle melodic touches gather above the groove, and the energy rises gently, bar by bar.

### Verse 1

Fowkes enters with a soft, soulful tone, his voice drifting over the rhythm and setting the song's longing mood.

### Chorus

The title line becomes the emotional hook, repeated with growing feeling as the chords swell beneath it.

### Bridge

A breakdown strips the beat away, leaving the voice and chords suspended in the air, the most vulnerable part of the track.

### Climax

The kick returns and the full groove lands, the bass heaviest and the vocal most yearning, a moment built for raised hands and closed eyes.

### Outro

The layers ease away and the groove rolls out, ready to hand over to the next record.

## Artistic Context

Him_Self_Her is the duo of Claire Spooner and Leon Baggotts, who started working together in Leicester and describe their sound as house music with feeling. "Gone Too Long" first appeared in 2012 on the Mexican label Cream Couture, before Damian Lazarus's Crosstown Rebels reissued it in September 2013 as CRM116, with new remixes from Compuphonic and a rework by Yousef.

## Impact and Reception

The Crosstown Rebels release brought the track to a much wider audience, and the original mix became a favourite of DJs who like their deep house warm and emotional. It is here because it captures that late-night feeling of missing someone while the music keeps you moving. Play it when the night is almost over and you are not ready to go home.
