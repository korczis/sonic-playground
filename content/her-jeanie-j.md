## Genre and Mood

**Her** were a French duo who sang soul and R&B in English, and **Jeanie J** comes from the middle of their second tape. PAPER called them "France's go-to answer [to] alt-RnB" and the tape's press release quoted NYLON hearing sensual R&B "dripping with lust, sweat, and passion". The feelings named here, intimate and soulful, are this page's reading; no review describes Jeanie J on its own.

## Song Summary

Jeanie J is track five of six on Her Tape #2, released on 7 April 2017, between Interlude and Swim. Victor Solf and Simon Carpentier wrote it, produced it with Michael Declerck, and sang it with five credited background vocalists. The tape continued their exploration of women, love, relationships and feminism, from maternal love between a mother and child to physical love between partners. Who Jeanie J is, the sources do not say.

## Concepts in Focus

Her's earlier singles rested on {{ <concept_link name="vocal harmony" /> }}, and Jeanie J has more voices than most. Here the credits name Maëva Atuvasa, Marion Mayer, Ellie James, Catherine "Joby Smith" Vignon and Brice Pihan as background vocalists: seven voices in all. Qobuz singled out the voice as the element that makes the project more human and gives it real sincerity.

The credits also show how much care went into {{ <concept_link name="mixing" /> }}: Michael Declerck and Maxime LeGuil are both listed as mixing engineers, and Antoine Thibaudeau mastered the track. With seven voices to place, the mix decides how close each one feels.

## Artistic Context

Solf and Carpentier met as teenagers in Rennes, studied classical music and formed Her in 2015; {{ <track_link source="her-five-minutes.md" /> }} and Quite Like introduced them, and {{ <track_link source="her-her.md" /> }} named Tape #1's manifesto after the duo. Qobuz called Tape #2 a literary record, citing Jacques Lacan and Peter Handke's poem for Wim Wenders' Wings of Desire. Carpentier died of cancer in August 2017, four months after it came out.

## Impact and Reception

Qobuz found "chansons réellement impressionnantes", really impressive songs, on the tape, though the only song it showed was Queens. Jeanie J was not on the 2018 album, so this tape is where it lives. Listen late, quietly, and count the voices.
