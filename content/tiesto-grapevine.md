## Genre and Mood

**Tiësto**'s **Grapevine** is a short, high-energy festival record. The French site Guettapen filed it under electro house, YourEDM called it the Dutch DJ's own bold breed of bass house, Wikipedia lists it as Brazilian bass, and Dancing Astronaut called it an infectious guilty pleasure. It is energetic and catchy by design: one famous vocal line, a heavy low end, and it is over in two and a half minutes.

## Song Summary

The vocal comes from I Heard It Through the Grapevine, written by Norman Whitfield and Barrett Strong for Motown in 1966 and a number one for Marvin Gaye in 1968. Musical Freedom, Tiësto's label, describes the vocal as a cover of Gaye's classic, while Dancing Astronaut and YourEDM write of a Marvin Gaye sample. Either way, Whitfield and Strong share the writing credit with Tijs Verwest, Tiësto's own name. Around the voice, We Rave You heard an almost ominous bass-ridden atmosphere, emphatic synths and thumping percussion.

## Musical Breakdown

No source times the track; the reviews describe its parts.

### Vocal

The borrowed line about hearing the news through the grapevine carries the track; in YourEDM's hearing, the vocals take the lead.

### Build-up

Dancing Astronaut noted that percussion replaces the synth progressions a festival build usually leans on, which it heard as a move away from formula.

### Drop

Guettapen was unimpressed by the drop: it found the drop strangely like the one in Tiësto's BOOM and the vocal too generic to lift it.

### Outro

At 2:30 the track ends quickly, in YourEDM's words a burst of flames and then gone. An extended mix runs 3:27.

## Concepts in Focus

Grapevine is a lesson in the difference between {{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} and {{ <concept_link name="interpolation" /> }}. A sample copies a piece of an existing recording; an interpolation sings or plays the borrowed melody again. The label's own word, cover, points to the second; two outlets heard the first. Both routes give Whitfield and Strong their credit, but only a sample would put Marvin Gaye himself on the record. Method Man and Mary J. Blige took the sung route with another Gaye song in {{ <track_link source="method-man-mary-j-blige-i-ll-be-there-for-you-you-re-all-i-need-to-get-by-puff-daddy-mix.md" /> }}.

The {{ <concept_link name="drop" /> }} is where a dance track releases its tension, and the critics' comparison shows how drops become a producer's signature, for better or worse. The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, published on 12 January 2019, was directed and animated by Thomas Dutton, with dancers including Matt Steffanina and footage from a public-domain archive and NASA.

## Artistic Context

Tiësto, born Tijs Verwest, released Grapevine on 26 October 2018 on Musical Freedom, after premiering it at Ultra Music Festival in Miami earlier that year. The label says it was a highlight of his 2018 festival sets, Tomorrowland's main stage included. Remixes by Tujamo, John Christian and Carta followed.

## Impact and Reception

The single reached number 34 on Billboard's Hot Dance/Electronic Songs and number 14 on the Wallonia dance chart. Its reception split between YourEDM's live crowd-pleaser and Guettapen's charge of a cruel lack of originality. Decide for yourself; it takes two and a half minutes.
