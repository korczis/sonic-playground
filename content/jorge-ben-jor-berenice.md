## Genre and Mood

**Jorge Ben Jor**'s **Berenice** is samba rock from the man Billboard called its father, a short Brazilian love song that sits on samba's swing and plays with its own words. It is playful in its wordplay and groovy in its feel, and it is over in about two and a half minutes.

## Song Summary

Berenice comes from A Banda do Zé Pretinho, Ben's 1978 album and his first for Som Livre, produced by Paulinho Tapajós and recorded at the Sigla studios between December 1977 and January 1978. The lyric is a small comedy: the singer warns Berenice that her beauty-queen looks and her sweetness will make him do something foolish, then Doralice tells him not to bother, because Berenice is hooked on a certain Tom Mix. The refrain is simply her name, its first syllable repeated before the whole word arrives.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes the arrangement or gives times, so this breakdown follows the lyric and the critics.

### Chorus

The hook is one word. "Berê" comes again and again before "Berenice" completes it: the refrain is nothing but the name.

### Verses

The verses tell the story in quick rhymes. Monkeybuzz's Carlos Eduardo Lima called it one of the most ingenious constructions in Ben's lyrics, because every rhyme ends in -ice, even when that takes an unexpected word.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="rhyme" /> }} is the song's game. Lima pointed to two words in particular: Tom Mix, the name of the American cowboy film star, which the song makes rhyme with Berenice, and the English word service, used for the Portuguese serviço. The rhyme holds even when the vocabulary has to cross a language.

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} works the same way: the name broken into a syllable and repeated until it sticks.

The {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }} is where this copy comes from. Atração's Samba Rock Vol.1, released in 2007, closes with Berenice at 2:32. Deezer gives that copy and the Som Livre album track the same ISRC, BRSGL7800009, so it is the 1978 recording, a few seconds shorter than the 2:40 album version. ## Artistic Context

Ben, born in Rio de Janeiro in 1939, moved to Som Livre after África Brasil and Tropical, and replaced his band Admiral Jorge V with the Banda do Zé Pretinho, after whom the album is named. Lima describes the new sound as more pop, more swinging and more electric, and places Berenice in a run of three overlooked classics with Amante Amado and Denize Rei.

## Impact and Reception

The album reached number four in Brazil on Cashbox's chart, and Portuguese Wikipedia lists a French single, Bérénice, issued by Disques Festival in 1979. Lima, writing in 2014, lamented that the album had not been reissued since its 1994 CD; it returned on vinyl in 2018, for its 40th anniversary. Sing the refrain once and you have learned the song.
