## Genre and Mood

**Klangkuenstler** and **Alice Phoebe Lou** wrote the song, but **Man On the Moon (Miguel Campbell Remix)** belongs to the remixer's warm, rolling take. It sits between deep house, indie dance and classic house, and its mood is dreamy, groovy and quietly hypnotic: a track that glides rather than pounds, built for sunset sets and long drives home.

## Song Summary

At its core is Alice Phoebe Lou's voice, soft and slightly wistful, singing about longing for someone as distant as the man on the moon. Miguel Campbell keeps that intimacy intact and wraps it in a nu-disco groove, adding a supple bassline, shuffling percussion and bright chords. The result is a remix that feels both cosy and propulsive, the vocal floating above a groove that never stops gently nudging you forward. It is a remix that respects the song rather than overpowering it.

## Musical Breakdown

Across nearly six minutes, the remix unfolds patiently, letting the groove establish itself before the vocal takes the spotlight.

### Intro

Crisp hi-hats and a steady kick open the track, soon joined by a round, funky bassline that sets the unhurried, swinging tempo.

### Build-up

Chords and percussion layers arrive one at a time, filtering in and out so the groove gains warmth without ever getting crowded.

### Main Section

Alice Phoebe Lou's vocal drifts in, airy and close, and the track settles into its best stretch: voice, bass and chords locked in a mellow, sunlit loop.

### Bridge

A short breakdown pulls the drums back, leaving the voice and soft chords suspended for a moment, a dreamy pause before the beat returns.

### Outro

The groove rolls on with the vocal fragments fading, the bassline carrying the track gently out the way it came in.

## Artistic Context

Klangkuenstler is a Berlin producer best known at the time for his releases on Oliver Koletzki's Stil vor Talent. The single "Man on the Moon" came out on Sunday Best Recordings, with South African-born singer Alice Phoebe Lou, then a fixture of Berlin's busking scene, on vocals. Miguel Campbell, a British producer associated with Hot Creations, delivered this remix, which also appears as a bonus track on Klangkuenstler's album *That's Me*.

## Impact and Reception

The original single caught on online, and Campbell's version gave it a second life in DJ sets, with reviewers singling out the vocal as the standout. It is here for its gentle, glowing groove and for the way it turns a melancholy lyric into something you can sway to. Let it play as the light fades.
