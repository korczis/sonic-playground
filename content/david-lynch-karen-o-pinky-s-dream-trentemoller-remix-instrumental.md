## Genre and Mood

**David Lynch** wrote **Pinky's Dream** as a nocturnal rock song with **Karen O** on vocals, and in this **Trentemøller** remix instrumental the voice is gone and only the nightmare remains. Electronic production, darkwave atmosphere and an industrial edge meet in a mood that is haunting, dark and eerie, pure Lynch even without a single word.

## Song Summary

The original is a feverish, nocturnal song; the remix turns it into a throbbing, sinister synth pulse. Without Karen O's vocal, the instrumental lays bare Trentemøller's work: pulsing bass, tense, distorted textures and layers of ominous synths, all moving forward like headlights on an empty highway. The memorable detail is how cinematic it feels. Every passage seems to be scoring a scene you cannot quite see.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

A low, pulsing synth emerges from the dark, steady and menacing, setting a pace that feels like an engine idling before a long drive.

### Build-up

Drums and bass lock together and the tension climbs, distorted textures creeping in around the edges like shapes in the rear-view mirror.

### Main Section

The full groove takes hold, driving and relentless, with brooding melodic lines in the space where the vocal used to be. The absence of a voice makes it even more unsettling.

### Climax

The arrangement swells into its most intense passage, noise and melody tangled together with a raw, industrial force, the drive at full speed.

### Outro

The layers drop away until only the pulse is left, fading into the dark like a car disappearing over a hill.

## Artistic Context

The film director David Lynch released his debut album *Crazy Clown Time* in 2011 on Sunday Best, and "Pinky's Dream", sung by Karen O of the Yeah Yeah Yeahs, opened it. In 2012 the Danish producer Anders Trentemøller remixed the track for the *Pinky's Dream* remix release, alongside a Visionquest remix, issued digitally and on vinyl by Sunday Best. The embedded video is the official upload of the vocal version of this remix.

## Impact and Reception

Trentemøller's remix was widely praised for turning the original into an even more sinister synth throb, while others heard in it the ruptured anguish of industrial rock. The instrumental version is for anyone who wants that mood without words, and it works beautifully as a soundtrack to whatever film is playing in your own head. It belongs on this playground as a night drive in sound. Turn the lights off and listen.
