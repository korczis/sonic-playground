## Genre and Mood

**Marc Moulin**'s **Into the Dark** is nu-jazz in the literal sense: jazz instruments and a jazz singer laid over a rhythm built with electronics, downtempo in its ease and upbeat in its step. One critic called it "the peppy opening cut" of its album and the album "a nostalgic-yet-modern gem", which is exactly the double feeling it gives.

## Song Summary

Into the Dark opens Top Secret, the album Moulin released on Blue Note in 2001. It is built from the bottom up: in the New Times review, "the subtle thud of electronic bass and the propulsive tap of a brushed high-hat cymbal underpin cool-jazz vibraphone, earthy organ, and sultry soul-diva vocals." The credits name Christa on vocals, Bert Joris on trumpet, Philip Catherine on guitar and Moulin himself on keyboards and as producer and composer.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes the track section by section, so the breakdown follows its layers rather than a timeline.

### Intro

As the opening track of the album, it sets the record's terms: electronics for the rhythm, jazz for the colour.

### Main Section

The groove sits on electronic bass and brushed hi-hat, a jazz drummer's touch played against a machine-like pulse. Above it, vibraphone and organ carry the cool-jazz harmony, and the soul vocal floats over the top.

### Outro

At six and a half minutes the track is long for an opener; notice how it ends and hands over to the rest of the album.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} is the meeting point. A brushed hi-hat belongs to small-group jazz, an electronic bass thud to dance music; together they make a rhythm that is relaxed and still propulsive. Listen for how the two lock together and how little the other instruments need to do once they have.

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} is part of the album's method: BBC Music Magazine lists Moulin on piano, keyboards and samples, and the New Times critic heard soulful sampled vocals on Top Secret beside its live players. Compare the sampled jazz voice at the heart of {{ <track_link source="alif-tree_forgotten-places.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Marc Moulin, born in Ixelles in 1942, led the jazz-rock group Placebo with Philip Catherine in the 1970s, joined Aksak Maboul and in 1978 founded the synth-disco trio Telex, who represented Belgium at Eurovision in 1980. He was also a radio producer and writer. Top Secret returned him to jazz with everything he had learned since; BBC Music Magazine heard "a refreshing new album that reflects jazz in the 21st century rather than the 20th." He died in 2008. For a later Belgian take on jazz through samples, hear {{ <track_link source="boogie-belgique-forever-and-ever.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Top Secret was praised in Europe and the United States, the New Times review ending "Definitely a secret worth sharing." Into the Dark travelled further than the album: it appeared on chill-out compilations and on Blue Note's own survey The Finest in Jazz From 1939 to 2003, and a later expanded vinyl edition of Top Secret added remixes of it by Karma Fever and La Malice. Put it on after dark.
