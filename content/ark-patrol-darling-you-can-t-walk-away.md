## Genre and Mood

**Ark Patrol** made **Darling, You Can't Walk Away** as a piece of indie electronic music that refuses to sit still. EARMILK counted it among the album's bouncy cuts and heard an eccentric feel in it; Saint Audio heard words that sound negative over an almost bubbly instrumental. That gap is the mood: bittersweet in what it says, quirky in how it moves.

## Song Summary

The track came out as a single on 7 February 2019, two weeks before the self-titled album it belongs to, under exclusive licence to Nettwerk. Brandon Gomez, who records as Ark Patrol, is credited as its producer, engineer and mixer. The voice is not his: he told Splice that the vocals on Darling and on Fiend came from Future Pop & Vocals, a sample pack by Audentity Records. So the song is a producer's construction around a borrowed voice, and it runs a little over three minutes.

## Musical Breakdown

No review walks through the track from start to finish, so this breakdown stays with the parts critics named.

### Main Section

Two synths do the work, according to EARMILK's Lindsey Oh: a groovy square-wave lead on top and a solid, rumbling saw bass beneath. Between them sits the sampled vocal with its title line. How the track opens and how it ends, no source says; listen and decide where it breathes.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" text="Sampling" /> }} usually means lifting a piece of an older record. Here it means something more modern: a sample pack, a library of sounds recorded to be licensed and sold to producers through services such as Splice. Gomez found the voice he needed there and built the song around it, much as earlier producers built tracks around a soul record's chorus.

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} parts are named after their waveforms. A square wave sounds hollow and buzzy, like an old video game; a sawtooth is brighter and rougher, which is why it suits a bass that has to rumble. Listen for the two shapes against each other.

## Artistic Context

The Line of Best Fit describes Gomez as Hawaiian-born and Seattle-based, a producer who found success in his late teens and early twenties with glitchy music mixing 8-bit nostalgia and chillwave, and whose Let Go later went viral on TikTok. He told Splice the 2019 album had to be honest: he had been diagnosed with cancer in 2018, and some of the record was written in a hospital room. He worked in Ableton with Sylenth1 and Synplant, and dedicated the album to his younger brother. Compare how an unhappy lyric rides a busy beat in {{ <track_link source="caribou-odessa.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Saint Audio called this the album's second single and pointed to its contrast as what makes the record dichotomous, suffering and rebirth side by side. A year later, The Line of Best Fit reported, Gomez sang on his own track for the first time, so this borrowed voice belongs to the time before he sang himself. Play it loud and listen to the bass under the bounce.
