## Genre and Mood

**James Newton Howard** and **Jennifer Lawrence** gave the world a haunting film song, and **The Hanging Tree (Rebel Remix)** turns it into a pounding anthem. The track mixes folk roots with electronic production and the logic of a remix, and its mood is haunting, defiant and intense. It is a curious object: part film tie-in, part dance-floor experiment, and a lesson in how far a remix can move a song from where it was born.

## Song Summary

The song, sung by Katniss Everdeen in *The Hunger Games: Mockingjay - Part 1*, is an eerie folk ballad about a man hanged for murder who calls his lover to meet him at the tree. In the film it becomes a song of rebellion. The Rebel Remix keeps Jennifer Lawrence's unaccompanied, slightly fragile voice at the start, then layers in strings, heavy drums and an uptempo pulse, turning a lonely chant into a marching call to arms. The remix makes literal what the film suggests: a quiet song spreading from one voice to a crowd. The haunting melody, simple enough to sing after one listen, survives every layer added on top of it.

## Musical Breakdown

The remix is short and driven, building from almost nothing to a full, thundering arrangement.

### Intro

Lawrence's voice opens alone, unadorned and haunting, singing the first verse as in the film.

### Build-up

Drums and strings enter, the rhythm gathering pace as the melody repeats, the tension rising with each line.

### Main Section

The full remix takes over, an uptempo, drum-heavy pulse driving the song forward while her voice rides above it.

### Climax

The arrangement peaks with pounding percussion and soaring strings, the song transformed into a defiant anthem.

### Outro

The music pulls back sharply, leaving a last echo of the melody.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} starts from the original recording rather than from the song. Michael Gazzo kept Lawrence's vocal take, the voice of the scene in the film, and rebuilt everything around it; that is why the remix still sounds like the same performance even though it lasts barely two and a half minutes and moves at a march. Compare the cover by Blankets, {{ <track_link source="blankets-the-hanging-tree.md" /> }}, where a new singer re-records the melody and only the song, not the recording, is shared.

The remix is essentially one long {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }}. It begins with one unaccompanied voice and adds drums, strings and rhythm with each pass of the melody, so the intensity rises while the tune stays the same. In the film the song spreads from Katniss to a crowd of rebels; the arrangement turns that spread into sound.

Finally, it shows what happens to a {{ <concept_link name="soundtrack" /> }} song once it leaves the cinema. The film version, produced by James Newton Howard, reached number 12 on the Billboard Hot 100; the Rebel Remix was its promotional second life, aimed at radio and clubs rather than at the scene it was written for.

## Artistic Context

"The Hanging Tree" features lyrics by Suzanne Collins, author of the *Hunger Games* novels, with music by The Lumineers' Jeremiah Fraites and Wesley Schultz and the film's composer James Newton Howard. The film recording, with Lawrence on lead vocals, was produced by James Newton Howard and Sven Faulconer and first appeared on the score album in November 2014; Republic Records issued it as a single on 9 December. The Rebel Remix by the producer Michael Gazzo followed on 15 December 2014 to promote the song. Gazzo told Yahoo! Music that despite the song's dark tone he imagined it lifting a whole group of people to rise up and unite.

## Impact and Reception

The original became a surprise chart hit around the world, topping charts in Germany, Austria and Hungary and going double platinum in the United States. Reactions to the remix were mixed: Polygon's Petrana Radulovic argued that the club remix and the film's marketing focused on glitz and glamour instead of the sheer horror of the story. It is here precisely because it is a strange, bold transformation, folk ballad turned stomping anthem. Listen and decide for yourself.
