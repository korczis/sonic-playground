## Genre and Mood

**James Newton Howard** and **Jennifer Lawrence** gave the world a haunting film song, and **The Hanging Tree (Rebel Remix)** turns it into a pounding anthem. The track mixes folk roots with electronic production and the logic of a remix, and its mood is haunting, defiant and intense. It is a curious object: part film tie-in, part dance-floor experiment, entirely unforgettable once heard.

## Song Summary

The song, sung by Katniss Everdeen in *The Hunger Games: Mockingjay - Part 1*, is an eerie folk ballad about a man hanged for murder who calls his lover to meet him at the tree. In the film it becomes a song of rebellion. The Rebel Remix keeps Jennifer Lawrence's unaccompanied, slightly fragile voice at the start, then layers in strings, heavy drums and an uptempo pulse, turning a lonely chant into a marching call to arms. The remix makes literal what the film suggests: a quiet song spreading from one voice to a crowd. The haunting melody, simple enough to sing after one listen, survives every layer added on top of it.

## Musical Breakdown

The remix is short and driven, building from almost nothing to a full, thundering arrangement.

### Intro

Lawrence's voice opens alone, unadorned and haunting, singing the first verse as in the film.

### Build-up

Drums and strings enter, the rhythm gathering pace as the melody repeats, the tension rising with each line.

### Main Section

The full remix takes over, an uptempo, drum-heavy pulse driving the song forward while her voice rides above it.

### Climax

The arrangement peaks with pounding percussion and soaring strings, the song transformed into a defiant anthem.

### Outro

The music pulls back sharply, leaving a last echo of the melody.

## Artistic Context

"The Hanging Tree" features lyrics by Suzanne Collins, author of the *Hunger Games* novels, with music by The Lumineers' Jeremiah Fraites and Wesley Schultz and the film's composer James Newton Howard. The Rebel Remix was released on 15 December 2014, a few weeks after the soundtrack and the film, to give the song a more radio-friendly form. Sources disagree on exactly who produced it, but its purpose was clear: to take a song people had heard in a cinema and put it into playlists and clubs.

## Impact and Reception

The original became a surprise chart hit around the world, while reactions to the remix were mixed, with some critics arguing it stripped away the song's meaning. It is here precisely because it is a strange, bold transformation, folk ballad turned stomping anthem. Listen and decide for yourself.
