## Genre and Mood

**Flume** and **Chet Faker**'s **Drop the Game** is electronic soul at a downtempo crawl. It is sultry, soulful and hypnotic: Renowned for Sound called it "a slow-moving, head-bopping, thump of an electronic anthem" and confessed to playing it on repeat for hours, and the word it settled on was "spellbinding".

## Song Summary

The two Australians had already met on Left Alone, from Flume's 2012 debut album. In September 2013 Flume announced that they had made an EP together, and said the result "doesn't sound particularly like Flume or specifically like... a Chet Faker record", but like a hybrid of both. Drop the Game was its first single, released on 18 November 2013 by Future Classic, four days before the three-track Lockjaw EP.

## Musical Breakdown

No source times this recording, so the guide follows the elements reviewers described. The video runs longer than the 3:41 EP version.

### The Vocal

Renowned for Sound heard Faker's "sultry cool voice" laid over the production; Planet Notion praised his "luscious vocal prowess".

### The Synths

Planet Notion's phrase for Flume's part was his "distinctive swishing synths"; Renowned for Sound heard "high-pitched frequencies".

### The Bass

Under both sit what the same review called "deep drones of bass", the weight of that slow thump.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} can be a melody instrument or a source of pure texture, and the reviews hear Flume's in the second role: Planet Notion called his swishing synths distinctive, his own sound. On Drop the Game they sit at the extremes, high-pitched frequencies above and deep drones of bass below, and Renowned for Sound heard Faker's voice laid in front of both. Listen for how the soul of the song comes from that voice while the synths set the space around it.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Lorin Askill and released with the single, is one dancer and one street. Storyboard P, a Brooklyn street dancer known for flex and for his own fractured style called mutation, dances on the pavement outside a collision repair shop in Brooklyn. By January 2021 the video had passed 183 million views, a reach far beyond the charts.

## Artistic Context

Flume is Harley Streten from Sydney, signed by Future Classic in 2011 after an original-artists competition; his self-titled debut topped the ARIA chart, and its breakthrough was {{ <track_link source="flume-holdin-on.md" /> }}. Chet Faker is Nick Murphy from Melbourne, whose name is a homage to Chet Baker; his debut album Built on Glass followed in April 2014 and gave him {{ <track_link source="chet-faker-talk-is-cheap.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Drop the Game reached number 18 on the ARIA Singles Chart and went platinum, came fifth in Triple J's Hottest 100 of 2013 and was nominated for Best Dance Release at the 2014 ARIA Awards, losing to Peking Duk's High. Reviews of Lockjaw were warm too, and two critics hoped the pair would make a full album. Turn it up enough to feel the bass.
