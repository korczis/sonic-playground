## Genre and Mood

**filous** and **LissA** take **Feel Good Inc.**, one of the most recognisable songs of the 2000s, and slow it into a chillout daydream. It is electronic and it is a cover, but it is above all a mood: dreamy, relaxed and quietly nostalgic, the sound of a famous tune remembered on a lazy afternoon.

## Song Summary

The Gorillaz original is twitchy and sardonic, a song about numbness and manufactured happiness with that cackling laugh and a De La Soul rap at its centre. This version drops the rap sections and the menace. What remains is the melody, carried by LissA's soft, airy voice over plucked guitar and an easy, padded beat. The irony of the lyric survives, but it now sounds wistful rather than bitter.

## Musical Breakdown

The arrangement grows slowly from guitar to full electronic groove and back again.

### Intro

A gently picked guitar figure hints at the familiar melody before the beat appears, so the song creeps up on you instead of announcing itself.

### Verse 1

LissA sings the verse close and calm, almost a whisper, while soft kicks and finger-snap textures settle in beneath her.

### Chorus

The famous hook arrives without any of its original swagger. It floats, cushioned by warm synth pads, and the "feel good" line suddenly sounds sincere.

### Main Section

filous lets the instrumental breathe between vocal passages, layering guitar loops and gentle electronic swells into a tropical-tinged, sun-hazed groove.

### Outro

The beat slips away and the guitar is left alone again, closing the circle the intro opened.

## Artistic Context

filous is a producer and guitarist who built his following on reworks and covers that pair live guitar with soft electronic production. He has called this his favourite Gorillaz song, and he released it with LissA in 2016 to kick off a US tour. The source is the 2005 Gorillaz single from Demon Days, which makes the choice a bold one: few songs of that decade are as instantly identifiable.

## Impact and Reception

The cover found its audience fast on streaming and blogs; Earmilk welcomed its chill spin on the classic, and Cover Me Songs later picked it among its favourite takes on the song, praising how dreamy it becomes without the rap. It belongs here as proof that a great melody can wear a completely different mood. Play it after the original and hear how much one song can hold.
