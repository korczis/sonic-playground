## Genre and Mood

**Flume**'s **Holdin On** is two and a half minutes of electronic music that sounds like it is smiling. It sits where hip-hop beat-making meets the lush, wobbling chords that would soon be called future bass, and the feeling is euphoric, energetic and vibrant, a head-nod that keeps turning into a grin.

## Song Summary

The track is built around a chopped, pitch-shifted soul vocal, credited to Otis Redding, repeating the title, cut into stuttering fragments and set against bright, detuned synth chords that swell and sag like they are breathing. Underneath, the drums swing with a loose, hip-hop gait, all crunchy snares and off-kilter hits. It is short, dense and endlessly replayable, the kind of track where the hook is a texture as much as a melody.

## Musical Breakdown

There are no verses in the usual sense; the song is a sequence of variations on its vocal hook.

### Intro

The vocal sample appears first, filtered and fragmented, a glimpse of the hook before the beat drops in.

### Build-up

Synth chords rise underneath, warm and wobbly, and a few drum hits hint at the groove to come.

### Main Section

The beat lands with a lurching swing, and the chopped vocal starts bouncing between the chords. Every few bars Flume reshuffles the pieces, so the loop never quite repeats.

### Bridge

The drums pull back for a moment and the chords hang in the air, a short breath of space that makes the return hit harder.

### Climax

Everything comes back at once, the sample chopped tighter, the synths fattest, the most joyous stretch of the track.

### Outro

The beat cuts out quickly and the song ends almost abruptly, which is why you hit replay.

## Concepts in Focus

Holdin On is a study in {{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} as composition. The credits list Otis Redding as lyricist next to Harley Streten as composer, because the vocal hook is a fragment of a Redding recording. Flume does not loop it whole; he cuts it into syllables, shifts its pitch and rearranges the pieces against his own chords, so a voice from 1960s soul becomes a stuttering electronic instrument. Reviews at the time singled out exactly that pairing of a catchy Redding sample with classic drum breaks and synth melodies.

The chords are the other half of the sound. Flume's {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} parts are detuned and modulated so that they swell, bend and sag in pitch, which is why they seem to wobble. That bright, woozy chord sound, layered over hip-hop drums, became one of the foundations of what was soon called future bass, and producers still recreate it in tutorials today.

The drums supply the {{ <concept_link name="swing" /> }}. Rather than snapping every hit to a strict grid, the beat lurches and lags in the manner of hip-hop beat-makers, crunchy snares landing a fraction late. That loose feel keeps the bright synths from sounding mechanical. For a slower, more soulful use of the same Sydney toolkit, try {{ <track_link source="chet-faker-talk-is-cheap.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Flume is the Sydney producer Harley Streten. Holdin On was released on 9 November 2012 as the third single from his self-titled debut album, which came out on Future Classic the same day, topped the Australian chart, won the J Award for Australian Album of the Year and turned him from bedroom producer into an international name. The deluxe edition a year later added a remix of Holdin On with verses by the rapper Freddie Gibbs. Its music video, directed by Joe Nappa and built around live and tour footage, followed in March 2013 and was nominated for the ARIA Award for Best Video.

## Impact and Reception

"Holdin On" came fourth in triple j's Hottest 100 of 2012, reached number 17 on the ARIA singles chart and number 22 in New Zealand, and went double platinum in Australia and triple platinum in New Zealand. It was nominated for Song of the Year at the 2013 ARIA Awards and won Best Independent Dance/Electronica Single at the Australian Independent Record Label Awards. More than that, it helped define a sound that a generation of producers would chase. Put on good headphones and let the chords wobble through you.
