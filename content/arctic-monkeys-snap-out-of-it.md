## Genre and Mood

**Arctic Monkeys** deliver **Snap Out Of It** as a sleek piece of indie rock with pop-rock polish. It is longing at heart, but it is also catchy and playful, a song that pleads and flirts at the same time, carried by one of the band's most effortless grooves. Where much of AM broods and smoulders, this one winks, and its lightness is a big part of its charm.

## Song Summary

The song is a plea to someone caught up with the wrong person: Alex Turner wants them to snap out of it and come back. What makes it memorable is the contrast between that wounded message and the strutting rhythm underneath, which The Quietus heard as a song of clicks, claps and catchy hooks. Matt Helders' drumming is crisp and economical, Nick O'Malley's bass walks with a little swagger, producer James Ford adds keyboards, and the guitars mostly stay out of the way, which gives Turner's voice room to sound both wounded and amused.

## Musical Breakdown

Short and tightly arranged at a little over three minutes, the song moves from a sparse shuffle to a full chorus without ever losing its swing.

### Intro

Drums, claps and a light keyboard figure set a mid-tempo shuffle, with a confident swing that hints at the song's sly charm.

### Verse 1

Turner's vocal is close and conversational, almost amused, as he sketches the situation and his own frustration with it.

### Chorus

The title hook lands as a command, with Helders and O'Malley's high backing vocals above it, sweet and insistent, turning a desperate request into an earworm.

### Bridge

In the middle section the lyric describes the lover as under a spell, hypnotised, and asks how they can be so blind. The groove stays put; the urgency comes from the words and Turner's push.

### Outro

The final chorus repeats with the full band before the song ends neatly, as if the conversation has simply been cut short.

## Concepts in Focus

Snap Out Of It is built around its {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }}. The chorus is three words, an everyday command, and the song puts it where the ear expects a melody. Short phrases that sound like speech are among the most durable hooks in pop, because a listener can carry them away after one play.

The backing vocals are a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="vocal harmony" /> }} as character. Throughout AM, Helders and O'Malley sing high above Turner, and here they turn his plea into something closer to a girl-group chorus. Compare the way the same voices sit under the riff of {{ <track_link source="arctic-monkeys-do-i-wanna-know.md" /> }}, the album's slow, heavy opener.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Focus Creeps and released on 16 June 2014, adds a story. Stephanie Sigman plays a woman watching studio footage of the band who becomes fixated on Turner; near the end she appears in the footage herself, suggesting the two were a couple who have split. The video does not illustrate the lyric; it reverses it, showing the person who might be the one under a spell.

## Artistic Context

"Snap Out Of It" comes from AM, the band's fifth album, released by Domino in September 2013 and recorded at Sage & Sound in Los Angeles and Rancho De La Luna in Joshua Tree. Turner said the album should sound like a Dr. Dre beat, and it draws on R&B and hip-hop as well as heavy rock. The song is credited to all four members, with lyrics by Turner, and was produced by James Ford with Ross Orton and mixed by Tchad Blake. It was the album's sixth single, sent to UK radio on 9 June 2014.

## Impact and Reception

As a single it reached number 82 in the UK and number 6 on the UK Indie chart, number 19 on Canada's rock chart and number 37 on US Hot Rock & Alternative Songs. Its long life came through streaming: it is certified double platinum in the UK and Canada and platinum in the US. NME's review of AM heard an orchestral intensity in it that would suit Turner's side project the Last Shadow Puppets. It earns its place here for that easy, swaggering groove. Play it when you need a reminder that heartbreak can still have a sense of rhythm.
