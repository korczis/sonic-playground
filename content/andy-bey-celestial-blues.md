## Genre and Mood

**Andy Bey**'s **Celestial Blues** sits where soul, jazz and funk meet. Jazz Journal hears a hard-rocking piece with blues flavours in a soulful jazz-rock style; the writer Bradford Bailey, on the album it opens, hears music at an uncomfortable crossroads of jazz, soul and funk, "straight up funky and weird". The title promises something heavenly and the blues keeps it on the ground: soulful first, spiritual in its reach.

## Song Summary

Celestial Blues is the first track of Experience and Judgment, the album Bey made for Atlantic in 1974, and it is his own song. The recording around his voice is a full studio band: Wilbur Bascomb on bass guitar, George Davis on guitar, Buddy Williams on drums, Selwart Clarke on violin and viola, and Bill Fischer, the album's producer, on electric piano, organ, harpsichord, synthesizer and percussion. The detail that stays is the colour, strings and a harpsichord on a funk record.

## Musical Breakdown

### The Voice

Bey had a baritone with a four-octave range. No source walks through the track bar by bar, so listen for how the melody uses that range: where it climbs and where it settles back into the blues.

### The Band

Jazz Journal singles out Bascomb's bass guitar as the sound that stands out across the album; listen for it on Celestial Blues under Fischer's keyboards and Clarke's strings.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="blues" /> }} is the root under the soul and the jazz-rock here, named in the title and heard, as Jazz Journal puts it, in the track's blues flavours. A 1970s studio band can dress a blues in strings and synthesizer without losing it.

Celestial Blues also exists as an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }}. Gary Bartz's NTU Troop recorded the song on its 1971 album Uhuru, at seven and a half minutes; Bey's own recording three years later runs under three and a half.

In 2015 the French producer The Avener made it a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }}, a rework on his album The Wanderings of the Avener, the same album as {{ <track_link source="the-avener-ane-brun-to-let-myself-go.md" /> }}. Play the two side by side.

## Artistic Context

Andy Bey, born in Newark in 1939, sang from the age of 17 with his sisters Salome and Geraldine as Andy and the Bey Sisters, and worked with Horace Silver. Experience and Judgment, recorded in New York in 1973 and influenced by Indian music, was his solo album for Atlantic. His career revived in the 1990s; he won the Jazz Journalists Association's vocalist of the year award in 2003 and died in April 2025, aged 85.

## Impact and Reception

AllMusic gave the album four stars out of five, and reissues by Be With Records in 2016 and Speakers Corner later brought it back to the shops. In 2015 The Avener's rework put Bey's voice on a French electronic album. Start with this original, and listen to where everything came from.
