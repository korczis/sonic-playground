## Genre and Mood

**Fujiya & Miyagi** open their third album with **Knickerbocker**, indie electronic music built on a Krautrock pulse. It is hypnotic because the beat barely changes, playful because the words are chosen for their taste and sound, and quirky because of what those words are: ice-cream sundaes, a Danish storyteller and a Scottish child star.

## Song Summary

Knickerbocker was the lead single from Lightbulbs, released in September 2008, on Full Time Hobby in the UK. Its first seconds are David Best whispering a list of ice-cream words, vanilla, strawberry, knickerbocker glory, a knickerbocker glory being an ice-cream sundae. Paste noted how the band pairs that sundae with Lena Zavaroni, a child star whose life ended in illness, and Hot Press gave bonus points for namechecking her and Hans Christian Andersen. Matthew Perpetua of Fluxblog heard the whole thing as an inscrutable dream: ice cream, rows of light bulbs and the apparition of a former child star, images that make sense only inside the song.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

The whisper comes first. The Skinny's reviewer heard Best's hushed tone recalling Massive Attack's Daddy G and argued that the words are there purely for rhythm.

### Main Section

Underneath runs what Pitchfork called motorik pulses and retro-futurist electronics, the instruments making like metronomes. Best half-speaks rather than sings, and Heave Media heard the synths carrying the melody, weird and wavering where the lyric turns to Zavaroni's ghost.

### Outro

No source describes how the song ends. The video on this page, by Wade Shotter, runs a little shorter than the album version.

## Concepts in Focus

The beat is a {{ <concept_link name="motorik" /> }} pulse, the steady forward drive of 1970s German bands such as Can and Neu!. Pitchfork compared it to an old krautrock highway where you never run out of road, and Paste called the band's sound Krautrock-influenced minimal pop.

The vocal is close to {{ <concept_link name="spoken word" /> }}: The Line of Best Fit heard a semi-rap, Hot Press barely-there vocals. The voice works as another rhythm instrument.

The lyric behaves like a {{ <concept_link name="list song" /> }}, piling up flavours and names until a mood forms, the same trick as {{ <track_link source="easter-alien-babies.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Fujiya & Miyagi formed in Brighton in 2000 around David Best and Steve Lewis, named after a Japanese maker of tape recorders and the mentor from The Karate Kid. Lightbulbs followed Transparent Things (2006) and reached number 187 in the UK.

## Impact and Reception

Hot Press named it the album's key track, and Heave Media reported it as the band's most downloaded song on iTunes that September. Put it on and let the list carry you.
