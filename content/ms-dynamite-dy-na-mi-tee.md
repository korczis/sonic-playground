## Genre and Mood

**Ms. Dynamite** introduces herself on **Dy-Na-Mi-Tee**, literally. It is hip-hop and R&B with what The A.V. Club heard as a reggae inflection in her voice, upbeat and catchy, and its confidence is not a pose: it is a young Londoner telling you who she is and where she comes from.

## Song Summary

The title breaks her stage name into syllables. In the verses, as Vice put it, she raps about her upbringing and about music as escapism. Salaam Remi produced the track and wrote it with her; the credits also name Leonard Hibbert and Clement Dodd. Nathan Rabin of The A.V. Club heard the song consciously evoking the singalong infectiousness of the Fugees' Fu-Gee-La, which Remi had co-written.

## Musical Breakdown

No source we found walks through Dy-Na-Mi-Tee part by part, so this breakdown stays with what the critics describe. Vice heard her rapping about where she grew up, and her delivery and lyrics, which preach responsibility and self-love, invited comparisons with Lauryn Hill. The A.V. Club heard a singalong in the manner of Fu-Gee-La and, in her voice, something like a more reggae-inflected Nelly Furtado. The official video puts her in a distressed denim two-piece, an image Vice still recalled fifteen years later. Listen for the rapped verses, the name in the hook and the colour of her voice.

## Concepts in Focus

The title of Dy-Na-Mi-Tee splits a single word into syllables, which gives it a rhythm before any melody is added, and The A.V. Club heard the result as a singalong. That is how a {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} can work: listen for where the name comes back and how easily a crowd could chant it.

The word is a {{ <concept_link name="stage persona" /> }}. Niomi McLean-Daley performs as Ms. Dynamite, and the song is that persona's manifesto. Vice described her lyrics as preaching responsibility and self-love, so the persona is less a mask than a statement of values.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} fixed that image: Ms. Dynamite in denim, on her own terms. Compare how another young woman of those years handled image and a guest rapper on {{ <track_link source="kelis-terrar-good-stuff.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Born in 1981 and raised in Kentish Town, London, Ms. Dynamite first became known for her vocals on Sticky's UK garage track Booo!, a pirate radio staple. Her debut album A Little Deeper came out on Polydor on 10 June 2002, with ten tracks produced by Salaam Remi, a producer who had worked with the Fugees and would later work with Amy Winehouse. Dy-Na-Mi-Tee followed as its second single on 26 August 2002.

## Impact and Reception

The single reached number five in the UK, her highest position until Lights On with Katy B in 2010, and number two on the UK hip-hop and R&B chart. A Little Deeper won the 2002 Mercury Prize; Vice notes she was its first black solo female winner, at 21, and she gave the £20,000 to the NSPCC. In 2003 she won two Brit Awards. Play it and try not to spell along.
