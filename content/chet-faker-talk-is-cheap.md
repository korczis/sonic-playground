## Genre and Mood

**Chet Faker** found his signature sound on **Talk Is Cheap**. It is electronic music with a soul singer's heart and a downtempo pulse, smooth, sparse and smoky, and it feels introspective, soulful and deeply relaxed, the soundtrack to the moment a night turns quiet and honest.

## Song Summary

The song wrestles with the gap between words and feelings, the sense that saying something is never quite enough. Faker's husky, intimate voice carries that doubt over a minimal arrangement: a lazy, warm beat, soft keys and, above all, a languid saxophone line that has become one of the most recognisable hooks in modern Australian music. The production leaves plenty of air around every element, so each sound feels close enough to touch.

## Musical Breakdown

The track is compact and unhurried, its power coming from texture and restraint.

### Intro

The saxophone hook appears almost at once, mellow and slightly melancholy, over a soft, lolling beat.

### Verse 1

Faker's voice enters low and close, half-crooned, his phrasing loose and conversational, as if he is thinking aloud.

### Chorus

The title line arrives with a little more lift, warm keys and backing harmonies filling out the sound without breaking the late-night calm.

### Bridge

The arrangement thins to the beat and a few chords, letting the voice hang in space, the most vulnerable moment in the song.

### Outro

The saxophone returns to close the song, trailing off as gently as it arrived.

## Artistic Context

Chet Faker is the Melbourne-born singer-producer Nick Murphy, who first broke through with a slowed-down, soulful cover of Blackstreet's "No Diggity". "Talk Is Cheap" was released in February 2014 through Future Classic as the lead single from his debut album, Built on Glass, a record that took his blend of electronic production and soul to a much wider audience. He later returned to recording under his own name before reviving the Chet Faker moniker.

## Impact and Reception

The song was voted number one in triple j's Hottest 100 of 2014, climbed to number six on the ARIA singles chart and went platinum in Australia and New Zealand. It was also nominated for Best Video at the 2014 ARIA Music Awards and shortlisted for Song of the Year at the APRA Awards. More than a decade on, that saxophone line still sounds like the very definition of late-night electronic soul. Turn the lights down, pour something slow and press play.
