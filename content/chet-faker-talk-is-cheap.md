## Genre and Mood

**Chet Faker** found his signature sound on **Talk Is Cheap**. It is electronic music with a soul singer's heart and a downtempo pulse, smooth, sparse and smoky, and it feels introspective, soulful and deeply relaxed, the soundtrack to the moment a night turns quiet and honest.

## Song Summary

The song wrestles with the gap between words and feelings, the sense that saying something is never quite enough. Faker's husky, intimate voice carries that doubt over a minimal arrangement: a lazy, warm beat and, above all, a saxophone line that Clash heard as wistful and treated, and that has become one of the most recognisable hooks in modern Australian music. The production leaves plenty of air around every element, so each sound feels close enough to touch.

## Musical Breakdown

The track is compact and unhurried at about three and a half minutes, its power coming from texture and restraint.

### Intro

The saxophone hook appears almost at once, mellow and slightly melancholy, before a chilled-out beat drops in beneath it.

### Verse 1

Faker's voice enters low and close, half-crooned, his phrasing loose and conversational, as if he is thinking aloud.

### Chorus

The title line arrives with a little more lift, without breaking the late-night calm.

### Bridge

The arrangement stays sparse around the voice, the most exposed and vulnerable part of a song that never raises its volume.

### Outro

The saxophone returns to close the song, trailing off as gently as it arrived.

## Concepts in Focus

The song's {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is not a sung line but the saxophone melody that opens it. An instrumental hook works the same way as a vocal one: it is short, it comes early, and it returns. Because it is played rather than sung, the song's most memorable idea carries no words at all, which suits a track whose title doubts the value of talking.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Toby & Pete and released on YouTube the day the single came out, became part of the song's reputation: it was nominated for Best Video at the 2014 ARIA Music Awards, losing to Sia's Chandelier, and for Australian Video of the Year at the J Awards. Compare how Faker's voice moves from this sparse setting to the long disco groove of {{ <track_link source="marcus-marr-nick-murphy-birthday-card.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Chet Faker is the Melbourne singer-producer Nick Murphy, who first broke through with a slowed-down, soulful cover of Blackstreet's "No Diggity". He wrote and produced "Talk Is Cheap" himself; it was released on 11 February 2014 through Future Classic as the lead single from his debut album, Built on Glass, a record that took his blend of electronic production and soul to a much wider audience. He later recorded under his own name before reviving the Chet Faker moniker.

## Impact and Reception

The song was voted number one in triple j's Hottest 100 of 2014, and after that win it climbed to number six on the ARIA singles chart; it went platinum in Australia and New Zealand. It was shortlisted for Song of the Year at the 2015 APRA Awards, and in 2025 it placed 61st in triple j's Hottest 100 of Australian Songs. Reviewers were not unanimous: some found the vocal melody too level, but the saxophone line won almost everyone over. More than a decade on, it still sounds like the very definition of late-night electronic soul. Turn the lights down, pour something slow and press play.
