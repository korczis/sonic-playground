## Genre and Mood

**Muse** built **"Uprising"** as alternative rock in glam-rock boots: defiant, rebellious and energetic, a protest song with a dance-floor stomp. It is the most direct song on an album that ends in a three-part symphony, and the one that carried it.

## Song Summary

"Uprising" opens The Resistance, Muse's fifth album, and came out first, as its lead single, on 4 August 2009. Matt Bellamy wrote it and the band produced it at Studio Bellini on Lake Como, with Spike Stent mixing. The lyric is a call to resist control by the powerful and the media; Bellamy said it "sums up how a lot of people feel and it's that we need change." Musically it was inspired by Goldfrapp's electronic pop and by the glam rock of the 1970s and 1980s; Uncut heard in it a schaffel beat, the riff from Blondie's Call Me and leftist sloganeering. The album version runs just over five minutes; the official video, embedded here, is shorter.

## Musical Breakdown

The sources describe the song by its layers rather than its sections, so this breakdown follows them.

### The Beat

The schaffel beat is a shuffling, swung stomp, the rhythm of glam rock. Wikipedia gives the song in D minor at 132 beats per minute.

### The Bass

The band wanted the rhythm section to sound real. Chris Wolstenholme said they were after a bass sound that "almost sounded like a synth, but wasn't".

### The Synths

Bellamy plays guitars and synthesizers as well as singing. Several critics compared the song to the theme of Doctor Who, a television signature known for its electronic sound.

### The Refrain

The chorus turns the lyric into a chant of refusal, with handclaps and backing vocals from Tom Kirk, Adrian Bushby and Paul Reeve.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }} is a short figure repeated until it becomes the song's identity. Uncut heard Blondie's Call Me in this one, and the riff is what the stomp hangs on.

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} matters twice here: in Bellamy's own synth parts, and as the sound the bass imitates without being one. Compare {{ <track_link source="the-white-stripes-seven-nation-army.md" /> }}, where a guitar passes for a bass.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, by Humble TV, sets an uprising of teddy bears loose in a miniature city. It won Best Special Effects at the 2010 MTV Video Music Awards.

## Artistic Context

**Muse** is Matt Bellamy, Chris Wolstenholme and Dominic Howard. The Resistance brought them their first Grammy for Best Rock Album. Bellamy later said the conspiracy-theory subculture the song draws on had been "hijacked by the right", and the band refused American politicians who asked to use it at rallies.

## Impact and Reception

"Uprising" reached 9 in the UK and 37 on the Billboard Hot 100, Muse's first entry there, and topped the US Alternative Songs chart for 17 weeks. It is the band's best-selling single, double platinum in the US. PopMatters, which gave the album 4 of 10, still called it "easily the best track on the album": a Glitter stomp hacking away at the Doctor Who theme. Turn it up and stamp along.
