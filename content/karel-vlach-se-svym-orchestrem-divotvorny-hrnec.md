## Genre and Mood

**Karel Vlach se svým orchestrem** plays **Divotvorný hrnec** as a big band, the swing orchestra Vlach founded in 1939, on an LP Discogs files under jazz, stage and screen, and easy listening. No review of this track has been found, so playful and joyful are this page's reading of the two songs it joins, a comic number and a celebration of an engagement, not a description of how the orchestra plays them.

## Song Summary

Divotvorný hrnec is the Czech name of Finian's Rainbow, the 1947 Broadway musical by Burton Lane, E. Y. Harburg and Fred Saidy about an Irishman who brings a leprechaun's crock of gold to the American South. Discogs lists two songs in this medley: Množení (The Begat) first, Ten, kdo nemá rád (If This Isn't Love) second, both credited to Lane. It is the sixth of twelve tracks on an LP that Supraphon released in 1980, catalogue number 1113 2567, with arrangements by Jaroslav Dřevikovský and production by Jiří Baur; the digital edition, Ze světa muzikálů, lists it at 2:33.

### Main Section

The Begat comes first. On Broadway it belongs to the second act, where the bigoted Senator Rawkins, magically changed, joins a gospel quartet; in Prague Jan Werich sang it. If This Isn't Love follows, the first-act number in which the sharecroppers celebrate Sharon and Woody's engagement, sung in Prague by Rudolf Cortés and Soňa Červená. ## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="medley" /> }} joins several songs into one performance, and the whole album is built this way: each track is a musical heard through one or more of its songs, from Cabaret and My Fair Lady to Czech shows such as Golem. Finian's Rainbow gets two numbers, one from each act.

The {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }} decides the order, the joins and the colours. Dřevikovský set the whole LP, and the same pattern runs through it: on the track before this one, {{ <track_link source="karel-vlach-se-svym-orchestrem-annie-get-your-gun.md" /> }}, two Irving Berlin songs share three minutes in the same way.

## Artistic Context

Karel Vlach (1911–1986), born in Žižkov in Prague, founded his orchestra in 1939, and Czech Wikipedia follows it through Prague theatres from Divadlo V+W in 1947 onwards. Divotvorný hrnec is part of that story: Czech Wikipedia records that Jiří Voskovec and Jan Werich staged it on 6 March 1948, their last play together and probably the first American musical staged in Czechoslovakia, with Werich as the water sprite Čochtan, music adapted by Zdeněk Petr and played by Vlach's orchestra.

## Impact and Reception

No review of this album or track has been found. Divotvorný hrnec itself has lasted: it returned in Josef Dvořák's Čochtan vypravuje in 1993 and Jiří Suchý's Čochtanův divotvorný hrnec at Semafor in 2015. Supraphon put this recording on YouTube in 2019. Hear it as a souvenir of a 1948 premiere, played by the orchestra that played it then.
