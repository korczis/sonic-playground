## Genre and Mood

**Hindi Zahra** sings **Music (Remastered Version)** like a quiet thank-you to the thing she loves most. It is world music in the truest sense, a meeting of folk, soul and the North African traditions she grew up with, and it feels soulful, heartfelt and gently uplifting.

## Song Summary

"Music" is a love song whose object is music itself: the comfort, freedom and sense of home that singing can give. Zahra's voice, warm and smoky with a slight husk, moves with a jazz singer's ease over an intimate, organic arrangement: Fritz Sundermann and Thomas Naïm on guitars, Sjang Coenen on bass, Thierry Moutoussamy on percussion, and Zahra herself adding guitar, piano and percussion. Nothing is polished to a shine; the song sounds like it was played in a room by people who enjoy each other's company, which suits an album called Handmade.

## Musical Breakdown

The arrangement stays small and acoustic, its movement coming from the rhythm and the voice rather than from big changes.

### Intro

A supple guitar figure and light percussion establish a lilting, swaying groove, warm from the very first notes.

### Verse 1

Zahra enters softly, her phrasing relaxed and conversational, leaning back on the rhythm like a singer at ease in a small club.

### Chorus

The refrain lifts with a little more breath and brightness, a simple, singable melody that captures the joy at the song's centre.

### Bridge

The texture opens up for a moment of vocal and rhythmic play, a nod to the blues and jazz woven through her influences.

### Outro

The groove winds down gently, guitar and voice trading a last few phrases before falling quiet.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} of "Music" is made by hand. There is no drum kit on the track: the pulse comes from Moutoussamy's percussion and Zahra's own, from Coenen's bass and from two guitars that lock into each other, so the rhythm sways rather than drives. Listen to how much the guitars contribute to the beat; in a small acoustic band the rhythm section is everybody.

It is also a clear example of {{ <concept_link name="verse-chorus form" /> }}. The verses are relaxed and conversational, the chorus a singable refrain with a little more air in the voice, and because the arrangement barely changes between them, the lift you hear in the chorus comes almost entirely from the melody and the singer. Compare how the same musicians open the album on {{ <track_link source="hindi-zahra-beautiful-tango-remastered-version.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Hindi Zahra was born in Khouribga, Morocco, in 1979, of Amazigh (Berber) heritage, and moved to Paris at fifteen to live with her father; it is there that she built her career. Her debut album, Handmade, released in 2010 on Blue Note and EMI under licence from her own Oursoul Records, blended folk, jazz, soul and Berber influences, sung mostly in English with songs in Tashelhit; she produced, arranged and mixed it herself and even painted its artwork. "Music" is track nine. This remastered version comes from a later reissue of the album, which has also circulated with bonus tracks.

## Impact and Reception

Handmade was a breakthrough. It won Zahra the Prix Constantin in 2010 and the Victoire de la Musique for world music album of the year in 2011, and it was certified gold in France. Songs like "Music" showed why: warmth, sincerity and a voice that sounds lived in. It belongs on this playground as a small, glowing tribute to why any of us listen at all. Play it softly on a slow morning.
