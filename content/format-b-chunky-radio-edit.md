## Genre and Mood

**Format:B** make **Chunky** a cheeky slab of tech house, house and dance music. It is playful, groovy and relentlessly energetic, a track that seems to grin at you from the speakers.

## Song Summary

"Chunky" is built on exactly what the title promises: a thick, bouncing bassline with a wobble that sits right in your chest. Around it the Berlin duo arrange chopped vocal fragments, crisp percussion and quick stabs, keeping the mix punchy and uncluttered. The radio edit cuts the long club version down to a tight strut, keeping all the swagger while losing the extended DJ intro. Nothing here is subtle, and that is the charm: the low end is so physical that the track works as well on a car stereo as on a club system, and every element exists to serve the bounce. It is dance music as a good joke, told with perfect timing.

## Musical Breakdown

Short and punchy, the edit gets to the groove almost immediately and rides it hard.

### Intro

Drums and a teasing hint of the bass kick things off, filtered just enough to make you lean in.

### Main Section

The full bassline drops in, fat and rubbery, with vocal snippets bouncing around it like a call-and-response with the crowd.

### Bridge

A short breakdown strips the groove back to percussion and vocal chops, a breather that makes the return hit harder.

### Climax

The bass slams back in with every element firing, the most physical moment of the track.

### Outro

The groove rolls on before cutting away cleanly, leaving you wanting another round.

## Artistic Context

Format:B are a Berlin production duo known for their bass-heavy, playful approach to tech house. "Chunky" first circulated on the dance-floor before Ministry of Sound released it as a single in November 2015. Its official video, directed by The Surrender Monkeys, stars Benny, a drumming chicken puppet built by Puppets Magic, a wonderfully silly visual that matched the track's sense of humour perfectly.

## Impact and Reception

The single crossed over from the clubs to the charts, entering the UK Singles Chart at number 29 and the UK Dance Chart at number five. For a tech-house tune built on a bassline, that was a remarkable run, and it gave Format:B a wider audience than most underground producers ever reach. It earns its spot here for its irresistible bounce and its refusal to take itself too seriously. Turn it up and try not to nod along.
