## Genre and Mood

**Michelle Gurevich** sings **First Six Months of Love** like a toast raised at the end of a long night: a slow singer-songwriter ballad with the low lights and glamour of dark pop. It is romantic, but knowingly so, and the bittersweet, melancholic undertow is what makes it stay with you.

## Song Summary

The song celebrates the dizzy opening stretch of a love affair, the months when everything is new and nothing has gone wrong yet, while its very title admits that this stage ends. Gurevich delivers it in her trademark deep, unhurried voice, deadpan and tender at once, over a sparse, retro arrangement of soft synths and a slow, swaying beat. The wit is in the restraint: she never oversells the heartbreak, which makes it hurt more.

## Musical Breakdown

The song moves at a slow-dance pace, and its structure is simple enough to let the lyric do the work.

### Intro

A few warm keyboard chords and a gentle rhythm open the room, smoky and slightly faded, like a lounge just before closing time.

### Verse 1

Gurevich's voice enters low and close, describing the small rituals of new love with fond, slightly amused detail.

### Chorus

The title line arrives as a gentle refrain rather than a big hook. Sung almost with a smile, it carries both the glow of the beginning and the shadow of what comes after.

### Bridge

The arrangement thins and the mood darkens a shade, a moment of reflection before the last return to the refrain.

### Outro

The refrain lingers and the instruments fade slowly, like the last couple left swaying on an empty dance floor.

## Artistic Context

Michelle Gurevich is a Toronto-born, Berlin-based songwriter who first became known under the name Chinawoman, building a devoted following across Europe and especially in Eastern Europe with her melancholic, cabaret-tinged songs. "First Six Months of Love" appears on New Decadence, her fourth album, released in 2016. As one Berlin interviewer put it at the time, its songs reflect the fleeting relationships between people of a certain age in a big city, and this one is the sweetest and saddest of them.

## Impact and Reception

Without a major label or radio push, Gurevich's songs travel by word of mouth, and this one has become a fan favourite, still part of her concert sets across Europe and shared online in fan-made subtitled versions. It belongs on this playground as a reminder that a quiet song can be devastating. Listen to it late, with someone or about someone.
