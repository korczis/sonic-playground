## Genre and Mood

**Franz Ferdinand**'s **Take Me Out** made the Glasgow band famous, and it still sounds like two songs joined at the hip. It has been described as indie rock, post-punk revival, dance-rock, garage rock and art rock: energetic and wiry at first, then catchy and defiant once it slows into its famous riff.

## Song Summary

Alex Kapranos got the idea from a snipers' duel in the film Enemy at the Gates, which he said felt like a very good metaphor for romantic situations. So the lyric is a love song seen through a rifle sight: the singer is a cross-hair, a shot away from the other person, waiting for someone to move. The structure is the memorable part. The verses sounded better a little faster and the chorus a little slower, so Kapranos put all the verses at the beginning and then slowed down for all the choruses, which he called the wrong way to do it.

## Musical Breakdown

### Opening Section

The song starts fast and guitar-driven, with the notes of a chord played one after another, an idea influenced by Giorgio Moroder.

### The Tempo Change

Then the band shifts into a slower gear. The change was recorded live in the studio; producer Tore Johansson said the only editing was choosing the best take.

### Main Section

The second half is built on the riff and the chorus, while the drums move to disco hi-hat patterns and snare. A cymbal is caught as soon as it is struck, a touch borrowed from rock such as Queen.

### Outro

No source describes the last bars; listen for how long the slow groove holds.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="tempo change" /> }} is the whole design: the song spends its speed first and its weight afterwards, so the second half arrives like a different song.

The {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }} carries that second half, and it works through {{ <concept_link name="call and response" /> }}: Kapranos said the call-and-answer between the guitar riff and the vocal melody was inspired by the blues musician Howlin' Wolf.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Jonas Odell, puts the band inside a Dadaist animation of vintage figures and machinery; Kapranos named Dada, Busby Berkeley's films and Soviet propaganda as its influences.

## Artistic Context

Franz Ferdinand formed in Glasgow in 2002, with Kapranos and Nick McCarthy on guitars and vocals, Bob Hardy on bass and Paul Thomson on drums. Kapranos and McCarthy wrote the song in 2003; it was recorded at Gula Studios in Malmö with Johansson, who used several echo effects for what he called a marching, machinery, industrial feel. Domino released it in the UK on 12 January 2004 as the second single from the debut album Franz Ferdinand, which won that year's Mercury Prize.

## Impact and Reception

Take Me Out reached number three in the UK Singles Chart and number three on Billboard's Modern Rock Tracks, topped Triple J's Hottest 100 of 2004 and was voted the year's best single in The Village Voice's Pazz & Jop poll. Rolling Stone placed it at number 327 on its 2010 list of the 500 Greatest Songs of All Time; Daft Punk and Morgan Geist remixed it. Play it loud and wait for the brakes.
