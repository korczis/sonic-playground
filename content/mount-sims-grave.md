## Genre and Mood

**Mount Sims** steers **Grave** into the cold end of electronic music, where darkwave and electroclash meet. The mood is dark and haunting, but it is also hypnotic: the track moves slowly and deliberately, pulling you along rather than pushing you away.

## Song Summary

"Grave" lives up to its title. It is a five-minute descent built from patient rhythms, brooding synth lines and a low, deadpan vocal that sounds more like a confession at a graveside than a performance. The production favours space and repetition over spectacle, so every new layer feels like a step deeper into the dark. The album credits list André Lang on drums, Randy Twigg on additional vocals and Jackson & His Computer Band as additional producer, and the recording has the weight of a band rather than a lone laptop.

## Musical Breakdown

The track unfolds like a slow procession, each section adding a little more shadow.

### Intro

A sparse pulse and a murky synth tone set the scene, cold and unhurried, like fog settling over stone.

### Verse 1

Sims' voice arrives low in the mix, calm and unsettling, delivering the lyric with a detached, almost ritual flatness.

### Build-up

Drums and bass lock together more tightly, and the synths begin to thicken, raising the tension without ever breaking the tempo.

### Main Section

The full groove takes hold: a hypnotic loop that feels both danceable and funereal, the voices circling above it.

### Outro

The layers recede one by one, leaving a lingering hum that fades out like footsteps leaving a cemetery.

## Concepts in Focus

Mount Sims came out of {{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }}: his debut UltraSex appeared in 2002 on DJ Hell's International DeeJay Gigolo Records, the Munich label at the heart of that movement. By Happily Ever After he had moved on, and Wikipedia describes his later work as shaped by post-punk, new wave and darkwave. Grave shows what he kept, the deadpan voice and the machine pulse, and what he changed: a slower tempo, live drums and a horror-film darkness where the irony used to be. Compare his earlier club single {{ <track_link source="mount-sims-how-we-do.md" /> }}.

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} sets the temperature. Cold, murky tones that change slowly make the track feel nocturnal; there is no bright lead line to lift it. Against them, André Lang's played drums add weight and a human push that a drum machine would not.

## Artistic Context

Matthew Sims, who records as Mount Sims or Mt. Sims, was born in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, and is based in Berlin. Happily Ever After, released by Hungry Eye Records in October 2008, was produced and mixed by Thomas Stern of Crime & the City Solution and explores obsession, isolation, horror and madness; Grave is its second track, written, performed and produced by Sims, and it also appeared on A Grave E.P. and on the 2010 reissue Happily Ever After... Again on the Italian label Punch Records. Sims has also worked with The Knife and Planningtorock on the opera Tomorrow, In a Year.

## Impact and Reception

"Grave" is not a hit in any chart sense; it is a cult track from a cult artist, the kind of record passed between friends who love the darker corners of electronic music. Its appearance on three releases in two years shows how central Sims considered it. It belongs here for its atmosphere and restraint, proof that a slow tempo and a cold palette can grip as hard as any peak-time banger. Turn the lights down and listen to it all the way through.
