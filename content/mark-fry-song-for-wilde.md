## Genre and Mood

**Mark Fry**'s **Song for Wilde** is folk from a singer-songwriter's album that critics later hailed as psychedelic folk, and the feelings chosen here, warm and intimate, follow what it is about. No review we found describes the recording itself, so this page says what is documented and leaves the sound to your ears.

## Song Summary

Song for Wilde lasts two and a half minutes. A 2025 concert review calls it a sweet ode to Fry's then-baby brother as he learned to walk: a family song. Fry is its only credited writer. On the album it sits between two fragments of the title song, Dreaming with Alice, which runs through the record in short pieces.

A short song with one subject, a small child taking his first steps, and one voice, Fry's own.

### The Sequence

Track four of side one: the second fragment of Dreaming with Alice comes before it and the third after it, so the song is framed by the title song on both sides.

### On Stage

At his London concert in May 2025 Fry played it with a sitar-like tone on the guitar and bongos behind him. That is the live version; how the 1971 recording is arranged, no source says.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="remastering" /> }} is how most listeners hear this album now. Now-Again Records reissued it in 2020 from the original master tapes, recently found in Italy: Dan Johnson transferred them in Los Angeles and Bernie Grundman cut the lacquer straight from tape. The label calls it the first pressing from those tapes since 1972.

A {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }} carried the song further. It is on Shifting Sands, a collection of underground folk, and in 2011 MGMT chose it for their edition of Late Night Tales, beside {{ <track_link source="cheval-sombre-troubled-mind.md" /> }} and Dave Bixby's Drug Song. A compilation like that turns a lost song into a recommendation.

## Artistic Context

Fry, born in Epping, Essex, in 1952, went to Florence in 1970 to study painting at the Accademia di belle arti. The producer Vincenzo Micocci signed him to IT, an RCA subsidiary, and he recorded Dreaming with Alice in Rome with session musicians over three days in the summer of 1971. He left Italy that autumn, before it came out in 1972, and received a box of copies. Now-Again describes the album as equal parts pastoral folk and contemplative psychedelia. Fry became a painter; he returned to music decades later, from Shooting the Moon in 2008 to Not on the Radar in 2025.

## Impact and Reception

Fry learned by chance in the early 2000s that the album had become a cult record. It was reissued on CD in 2006, its admirers include Kieran Hebden of Four Tet and Colleen, and the highest auction price for an original copy, in 2013, was $4,061. Song for Wilde is one of its shorter songs; play it twice.
