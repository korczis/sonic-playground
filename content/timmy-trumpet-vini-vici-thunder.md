## Genre and Mood

**Timmy Trumpet** and **Vini Vici** made **Thunder** for festival stages. It sits where psytrance, the duo's home ground, meets the mainstage dance music Timmy Trumpet plays, and the feelings it aims at are the ones those stages run on: energy, euphoria and intensity, delivered fast.

## Song Summary

When WeRaveYou announced the release in October 2020, it described Thunder as a monotonic track centred on kick and bass and infused with trumpet sounds. That is the clearest published account of how it is built: the kick and the bass as the engine, the trumpet colour as Timmy Trumpet's signature. The single came out on Dharma, the label run by KSHMR under Spinnin' Records, and its official audio runs under three minutes.

## Musical Breakdown

No published analysis walks through Thunder section by section, so this review does not either. What the sources do give is enough to listen with: a low end of kick and bass that carries the track, trumpet-flavoured sounds on top, and a running time short enough that whatever happens has to happen quickly. An extended mix of about three and a half minutes appears on Timmy Trumpet's album Mad World, still compact by club standards.

## Concepts in Focus

Festival dance music is built on the {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }}: the music withholds something, often the low end, so that tension rises before a peak. At festival scale that tension is what makes thousands of people move at once.

The release is the {{ <concept_link name="drop" /> }}, the instant the full beat and bass return. In a track under three minutes there is little room for long preparation, so listen for how quickly Thunder moves from one to the other.

Psytrance and mainstage dance music share the {{ <concept_link name="four-on-the-floor" /> }} kick, a beat on every count of the bar; in a track WeRaveYou heard as centred on kick and bass, that pulse is the thread everything else hangs on. Compare how {{ <track_link source="anna-lunoe-radioactive.md" /> }}, by another Australian DJ, builds its own festival drops.

## Artistic Context

Timmy Trumpet, born Timothy Jude Smith in Sydney in 1982, started playing trumpet at four, taught by his father, and won a scholarship to the Conservatorium of Music before becoming a DJ who plays live trumpet over his sets. His breakthrough was Freaks with the New Zealand rapper Savage in 2014. Vini Vici are Matan Kadosh and Aviram Saharai from Afula in Israel, who worked together as Sesto Sento from 2001 and as Vini Vici from 2013; The Tribe and Great Spirit, with Armin van Buuren and Hilight Tribe, made them one of the best-known names in psytrance. Thunder was their second collaboration with Timmy Trumpet after 100 in 2018.

## Impact and Reception

WeRaveYou counted Thunder as Timmy Trumpet's sixteenth release of 2020, a year in which he was also playing streamed concerts, one of them from the Sydney Cricket Ground. We found no chart record for it; it is a festival and DJ-set track, made to be heard loud.
