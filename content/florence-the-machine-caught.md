## Genre and Mood

**Florence + The Machine**'s **Caught** is alternative indie rock at low flame. On the band's previous album, Ceremonials, the voices hit like a battering ram; this song is melancholic and introspective, and critics noticed above all how much it holds back.

## Song Summary

The song looks back at a relationship that the narrator knows she has to give up. Plugged In heard an allusion to Lot's wife, who was turned to salt for looking back, and an ominous reference to Persephone, bound to the underworld; The Line of Best Fit called the song a soliloquy of the desire for self-preservation. Gigwise summed it up as uncharacteristically understated, with a weary sort of melancholy.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

No review we found describes how the track opens. What they agree on is its pace: Bearded Gentlemen Music heard it moving like a slower version of the album's first side.

### Main Section

Two things carry it. The first is the lead vocal, which Gigwise heard as notably English, without Welch's usual American lilt, and therefore strangely bare and pleadingly sincere. The second is the choir of backing voices, which Drowned in Sound described as a steady simmer instead of the battering ram it had been on the previous album, Ceremonials.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="vocal harmony" /> }} is one of Florence + The Machine's trademarks, the stacked voices that make a chorus sound like a crowd. On Ceremonials, Drowned in Sound wrote, those harmonies were used like a battering ram; on Caught they keep a steady simmer. The same tool, used more quietly, turns from force into atmosphere. Compare {{ <track_link source="alt-j-breezeblocks.md" /> }}, where close harmony makes a gentle song feel crowded and tense.

A {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} shapes more than the sound. Markus Dravs produced Caught and most of the album; Welch told Hot Press he became a father figure who set her hours, from the time to arrive to lunch, and that the quiet, meditative process of making the record was good for her. Whether that discipline can be heard in a song this restrained is worth asking as you listen.

## Artistic Context

Caught is track eight of How Big, How Blue, How Beautiful, the band's third album, released by Island Records on 29 May 2015 and written by Welch with James Ford. Welch made it after a year-long break in which, she said, she had a nervous breakdown; the recording took a full year, mostly in London, and its songs turn to personal conflict and to learning to live and love in the world rather than escape from it. Asked by Hot Press about the song's lovelorn theme, Welch answered: "Oh gosh! Well... I didn't break it!"

## Impact and Reception

Reviewers kept stopping at Caught on an album full of bigger songs. The album went to number one in the UK and, for the first time for the band, in the US, and it was shortlisted for the 2015 Mercury Prize. Listen to it after the album's louder songs and notice how much the restraint says.
