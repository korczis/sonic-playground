## Genre and Mood

**Maxxi Soundsystem** and **Regrets We Have No Use For** sit squarely in deep house, in its moody form: a slow-burning groove, a voice that refuses to emote and a bass so heavy it becomes the main event. The mood is melancholic and sultry at once, the kind of record that, as its label put it, works its way under your skin while it makes you dance.

## Song Summary

The song is about regret and the helplessness of it, sung by Name One in a delivery Resident Advisor called sullen, almost disinterested. That distance is part of the design. Around the voice, Sam Watts, the Brighton producer behind the Maxxi Soundsystem name, builds what the same review called a tried-and-tested moody house formula with 808 claps, then lets the bass take over. The label, Hypercolour, described a monstrous, grumbling bass line under a wavy, frayed synth that swells and recedes in the mix, with soft shakers adding movement.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

Black Plastic heard the track open with falsetto notes, metallic bass lines and high-pitched melodies, a bright surface before the weight arrives.

### Build-up

As the vocal approaches, a deep, hollow bass and a clatter of drums mark the change, and the groove locks into place.

### Main Section

Name One's breathy voice floats above the beat while the synth line drifts louder and quieter. Black Plastic pointed to the details: bass lines that fade in and out, delicate vocal refrains, anguished synths.

### Bridge

Maxxi Soundsystem cuts the vocal into a percussive bridge, so the words stop being a lyric for a moment and become part of the rhythm.

### Outro

No review describes how the full seven-and-a-half-minute version ends, so listen for that yourself.

## Concepts in Focus

The track is a study in the {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} as the lead voice. Resident Advisor heard bass keys that seem to rise from deep underground and named the original's bass attack as its defining feature.

The claps that Resident Advisor heard are those of the {{ <concept_link name="roland tr-808" /> }}, a familiar texture that grounds the strangeness of the bass.

Name One is credited as a {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}, yet the producer treats the vocal as raw material, chopping it into rhythm in the bridge. The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} premiered by XLR8R uses found footage from the Italian horror film Suspiria and runs about three minutes, a much shorter cut than the 7:39 original.

## Artistic Context

Sam Watts had been rising fast with records for the labels Needwant and Futureboogie when Hypercolour issued this single in November 2012, on 12-inch and digital, as a double A side. The other side was a remix by Matthew Herbert, the found-sound producer whose work runs through {{ <track_link source="louie-austen-hoping-herbert-s-high-dub.md" /> }}; Resident Advisor found that remix eccentric but short of the original's impact.

## Impact and Reception

Resident Advisor called the original an aural juggernaut, and Black Plastic called the single a belter, while judging the vocal functional rather than game-changing but right for the track's emotional sunset mood. Find the full original mix, turn the bass up and let it take its time.
