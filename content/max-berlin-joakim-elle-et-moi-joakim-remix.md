## Genre and Mood

**Max Berlin**'s **Elle et moi (Joakim Remix)** is disco slowed to a walk, electronic music with the lights turned low. The DJ and producer Tiga, who has played it at his own parties, calls it cinematic and laid-back, "more of a mood record" than an energy record, and the mood is dreamy and relaxed with something haunting underneath.

## Song Summary

Elle et moi ("she and me") was a Max Berlin record from 1978, a dance hit in Belgium. Joakim, the French producer behind the Paris label Tigersushi, made an edit of it that Tiga first heard on vinyl around 2003. Groove Armada put this eight-and-a-half-minute version on their 2008 Late Night Tales mix. Tiga's description is the best guide there is: finger-snaps, beautiful strings, big chords and a strange, haunting voice, at a slow 116 beats per minute.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

By Tiga's account the track starts with finger-snaps. No source times the opening, but he stresses how much room the record leaves, "a lot of space in it, a lot of depth".

### Main Section

The strings and the big chords carry the middle, with the vocal floating over them. Tiga calls the whole thing "very delicate"; for him it is a record of atmosphere, not of power.

### Outro

No source describes how the remix ends. What Tiga does say is where it belongs: right at the end of a party, in the early morning, when the quiet starts to fill the room.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }}, or an edit in the DJ's sense, reshapes an existing recording for a new use, often longer and easier to mix. Joakim's version is a remix as rescue. Tiga calls Elle et moi "almost the definition of a lost classic", and the edit is how it came back into DJs' record bags.

The Late Night Tales series is a {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }} with a twist: each volume is chosen by an artist, here Tom Findlay and Andy Cato of Groove Armada. They blended all eighteen tracks into one continuous {{ <concept_link name="dj mix" /> }} with what the label calls a hint of Balearic flavour, so Elle et moi arrives straight after Depeche Mode's Enjoy the Silence and hands over to The African Dream. Compare how another compiler placed {{ <track_link source="king-kooba-california-suite-vagabond-mix.md" /> }} in his own Late Night Tales sequence.

## Artistic Context

Joakim Bouaziz studied piano at the Versailles conservatoire from the age of six to twenty-two, founded Tigersushi in 2000 and has remixed Charlotte Gainsbourg, Air, Tiga, Goldfrapp and Cut Copy. He also produced Paris bands such as Poni Hoax, heard in {{ <track_link source="poni-hoax-olga-kouklaki-budapest.md" /> }}. Groove Armada, the duo behind {{ <track_link source="groove-armada-superstylin.md" /> }}, compiled the twentieth Late Night Tales, released in March 2008.

## Impact and Reception

The song has had a long second life. A shortened version was used in Tom Barman's 2003 Belgian film Any Way the Wind Blows, and in 2021 Jarvis Cocker covered it on Chansons d'Ennui Tip-Top, his companion album to Wes Anderson's The French Dispatch. Put it on early in the morning, with the volume low, and wait for the snaps.
