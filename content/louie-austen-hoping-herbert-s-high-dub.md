## Genre and Mood

**Louie Austen**'s **Hoping**, in **Herbert's High Dub**, is a crooner's song taken into deep house by Matthew Herbert, a producer whose remixes one critic called clean without being sanitary and sparse without being empty. No review describes this dub on its own, so its two feelings rest on how critics heard Herbert's remixes of those years: warm and melodic at times, and in one reviewer's words sweet organic house. Read them as context, not as a listener's account.

## Song Summary

Hoping is a song from Only Tonight, the 2001 album Austen made with the producers Patrick Pulsinger and Mario Neugebauer. In 2002 it became a CD single of seven versions on Kitty-Yo and Cheap, with remixes by Naughty, Herbert, the Märtini Brös., Jimi Tenor and Sugar B. A reviewer heard the original's music as close to Olivia Newton-John's Physical, and found that none of the mixes were dissections of the song: each kept its winning elements and gave it a spin true to the remixer's strengths.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} rebuilds a recording from its parts, and a single like this one shows the practice at scale: one song handed to five remixers at once.

The word "dub" in the title points to {{ <concept_link name="dub" /> }}, the Jamaican habit of stripping a record down and treating what is left with echo and space. In house music a dub mix often means a version led by the groove rather than the song; how far Herbert takes that here no source says, so listen for it.

The remix also lives on a {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }}. Secondhand Sounds, a 2002 double CD on Peacefrog, gathers Herbert's remixes from 1996 to 2001, with this dub as the fourth track of the second disc beside his versions of Moloko and Serge Gainsbourg.

## Artistic Context

Austen, born Alois Luef in Vienna in 1946, was a jazz and bar crooner when Neugebauer discovered him in a hotel bar in 1998 and set his voice to techno beats; he called the result electrocrooning. Herbert, born in 1972, is a British producer of microhouse who often builds tracks from the sounds of everyday objects. Compare {{ <track_link source="louie-austen-one-night-in-rio.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Secondhand Sounds drew strong reviews, including 9.0 from Pitchfork. The dub stayed in DJ sets: MusicBrainz lists it on the 2004 mix Definition of House and in Solomun's Boiler Room set in Tulum in 2015. In 2021 Herbert's label Accidental Jnr paired it with his remix of Moloko's Sing It Back on a 12-inch. Play it and listen for what a remixer keeps of a crooner.
