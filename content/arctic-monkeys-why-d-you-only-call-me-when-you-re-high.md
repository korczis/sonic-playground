## Genre and Mood

**Arctic Monkeys** wrote **Why'd You Only Call Me When You're High?** as a rock band, but the sources hear it as R&B, funk rock and blues rock. The mood is sultry and groovy, with longing underneath: wanting someone at the worst possible hour.

## Song Summary

Paste's Robert Ham called it Alex Turner's "desperation for a 3 a.m. booty call", with the title as the rebuttal: the question is what the other person would say back. Turner wrote the words, the band the music; James Ford produced it with Ross Orton and played keyboards. It runs two minutes forty-one and never hurries. Critics fixed on its rhythm: "sultry, broken beats" for MusicOMH, "stuttering hip hop beats" for Pretty Much Amazing, "molasses-slow rhythms" for Slant.

## Musical Breakdown

### Main Section

The sources describe the song by its feel rather than by its sections. The beat is the centre: slow, broken, stuttering, closer to the hip-hop records that shaped AM than to a guitar band's rush. The Independent's Andy Gill heard the band "grind out a predatory, lurching funk-rock", and AllMusic's Stephen Thomas Erlewine said it "simmers" with a "distinctly danceable rhythm". No review maps it verse by verse, so this page does not either.

### Chorus

The title line is the hook and the turn of the story: the song is the call, the title the answer he does not want to hear.

## Concepts in Focus

The song is a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} built from restraint. Drowned in Sound heard Dr. Dre and Timbaland in it; compare Dre's own G-funk in {{ <track_link source="dr-dre-let-s-get-high.md" /> }}. What makes a groove here is not speed but the space between the hits.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Nabil Elderkin, turns the lyric into a night out: Turner drinks with the band in an East London pub, texts a woman named Stephanie, hallucinates her with other men and knocks on the wrong door. She sees seventeen missed messages and ignores them. It was nominated for Best Rock Video at the 2015 MTV Video Music Awards.

In January 2014 Miley Cyrus sang a {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} on MTV Unplugged, to critics' praise. Matt Helders said he wished the band had done some of what she did, because "she gets higher than we can in the voice."

## Artistic Context

The song is the third single from AM, the band's fifth album, recorded in Los Angeles and Joshua Tree between 2012 and 2013. Turner said the record sounded like a Dr. Dre beat given "an Ike Turner bowl-cut" and sent across the desert on a Stratocaster. It followed {{ <track_link source="arctic-monkeys-do-i-wanna-know.md" /> }} as a single on 11 August 2013, after a leak in late July.

## Impact and Reception

It entered the UK chart at number eight, topped the UK independent chart and reached number five on US Alternative Airplay; it has since been certified five times platinum in the US and four times in the UK. The Quietus heard "enough swagger to make a young LL Cool J jealous." Put it on late and listen to how little it does to sound this heavy.
