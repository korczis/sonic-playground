## Genre and Mood

**Vance Joy** wrote **Riptide** as a small song, and it became a record-breaking hit. It is indie folk and indie pop, also called folk-pop or twee pop, led by a ukulele: carefree and upbeat, romantic without being solemn. Red magazine's Hannah Gilchrist heard a California beach, a beer and friends in the waves.

## Song Summary

The song has been described as a coming-of-age love story, and it is known for its metaphors and pop-culture references. Joy has said the words came out like a stream of consciousness, a mash-up of images and things he had overheard people say, and that the point is the sound of the words rather than their sense. The title comes from a motel called Riptide where his family stayed on childhood holidays.

### Main Section

The verse chords and the first two lines date from 2008, when Joy wrote them on guitar at his home in Glen Iris, Melbourne, and set them aside. In 2012 a newer song reminded him of that draft and he combined them. The sheet music puts the song in D-flat major at 100 beats per minute.

## Concepts in Focus

Riptide's {{ <concept_link name="bridge" /> }} is where its story began. In 2012 Joy was playing a ukulele melody while a friend was at work; it became the instrumental bridge, and the chorus melody followed from it. The contrasting section is the seed, not an afterthought.

The song also shows what a {{ <concept_link name="demo" /> }} can do. Joy posted a rough version, just the first verse and chorus, on Facebook, and the encouraging response from friends and family persuaded him to persist. He recorded it in one afternoon with drummer Edwin White at Red Door Studios in Brunswick; John Castle added production.

Its {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Dimitri Basil and co-directed by Laura Gorun, depicts the lyric word for word. It was nominated for Best Video at the 2013 ARIA Music Awards.

## Artistic Context

Vance Joy is James Keogh, born in 1987, who played Australian rules football in the Victorian Football League before turning to open-mic nights in Melbourne; his stage name comes from Peter Carey's novel Bliss. Riptide was the second single from his debut EP God Loves You When You're Dancing, released by Liberation Music on 22 March 2013, and it reappeared on his first album, Dream Your Life Away, in 2014.

## Impact and Reception

Critics compared Joy to Paul Kelly and Jeff Buckley. Riptide topped Triple J's Hottest 100 of 2013, peaked at number six in Australia, reached number ten in the UK and number one on Billboard's Alternative Songs chart, and became the longest-charting song in ARIA history. Like {{ <track_link source="angus-julia-stone-big-jet-plane.md" /> }}, it shows how far a quiet Australian folk song can travel. Listen for the bridge: that is where it started.
