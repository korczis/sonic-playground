## Genre and Mood

**Apocalyptica** play **Seek and Destroy** with four cellos and nothing else: no guitars, no drum kit, no singer. It sits where neoclassical chamber music meets symphonic metal, and its mood is intense and energetic: the playing that Louder's reviewer heard as turbo-metal flurries keeps it moving from start to finish.

## Song Summary

Seek & Destroy is one of Metallica's oldest songs, written by James Hetfield and Lars Ulrich and released on their 1983 debut Kill 'Em All. Its lyric is about the urge to kill without acting on it, and it has been played at nearly every Metallica concert since 1982, usually with Hetfield leading the crowd in call and response. Apocalyptica's version is a 4:24 instrumental, one of three never-before-released recordings that Blabbermouth reported in May 2016 as bonus tracks for the 20th-anniversary reissue of their debut, Plays Metallica by Four Cellos, released on 22 July 2016.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} can keep every note of a song and change every sound. Seek & Destroy began as early thrash metal for electric guitars; here it becomes chamber music for bowed strings, and the shift is the whole point of the album.

It is also an {{ <concept_link name="instrumental" /> }}. A song that Metallica's audiences answer in call and response at nearly every concert loses its words entirely, so the melody that carried them has to speak alone.

What survives the change is the {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }}, a short repeated figure that anchors the song. Compare how it sounds under the bow with the riffs of {{ <track_link source="apocalyptica-battery.md" /> }}, recorded for the same 2016 edition.

## Artistic Context

Apocalyptica began as four classically trained cellists from Helsinki's Sibelius Academy, and Plays Metallica by Four Cellos, first released by Mercury in 1996, is where they started. For the 2016 reissue they added three new recordings, Battery, Nothing Else Matters and Seek & Destroy, to the remastered original eight. Seek & Destroy was already part of their concerts: that June they played it at the Sonisphere festival in Lucerne, and Blabbermouth published pro-shot footage two days before the reissue came out.

## Impact and Reception

Stephen Dalton called the three bonus tracks worthy inclusions. Reviewing the reissue in 2019, Reflections of Darkness called Seek and Destroy a powerful song that works perfectly at Apocalyptica's live shows. Play Metallica's original first, then this, and listen for where the crowd's answer used to be.
