## Genre and Mood

**Kalabrese**'s Independent Dancer has club tracks of ten minutes, and **Stone on Your Back** is something else on it: what his label calls a "wonderful airy ballad", set over a light bossa nova rhythm. The electronic production frames a song rather than a groove. Introspective and dreamy is this page's reading of a track that BlackPlastic heard as troubled and the label heard as airy.

## Song Summary

Stone on Your Back is the eighth of thirteen tracks on Independent Dancer, released by Sacha Winkler, alias Kalabrese, on his Zurich label Rumpelmusig on 26 April 2013, and it runs six minutes and forty-eight seconds. The credits name Winkler as producer and arranger, and Winkler, Can Oral and the cellist Dominik Löhrer as its writers. BlackPlastic read the song as "a troubled acknowledgment of the stresses of ambition", and of the resentment ambition can stir in those left waiting on the sidelines, without being sure which side Kalabrese takes.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the track part by part, so this breakdown names what the sources hear and points at where to listen.

### Intro

Listen for the rhythm first: the label calls it a light bossa nova, a pulse that sways rather than pounds.

### Main Section

Two instruments pull against each other here. The label points to the cello parts played by Dominik Löhrer; BlackPlastic heard a piano melody that "shifts uneasily", music that creates "a swirling fear, like storm clouds brewing".

### Outro

Listen for how the ballad lets go after almost seven minutes, and whether the unease is resolved.

## Concepts in Focus

Bossa nova is built on {{ <concept_link name="syncopation" /> }}: its guitar and percussion patterns place accents between the beats, so the rhythm seems to lean forward and float at once. Under an electronic ballad, a light bossa rhythm keeps the song moving without turning it into a club track.

In July 2014 Rumpelmusig released a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} EP of the album, and Frank Wiedemann reworked this song for it. Resident Advisor heard him twist the original into "shuffling analogue house" with "a stoned, reggae-paced groove", close in atmosphere to Acid Pauli's remix of Wanzka on the same record. The same EP rebuilt {{ <track_link source="kalabrese-khan-desperate-man-feat-khan.md" /> }} twice.

## Artistic Context

Kalabrese's Rumpelmusig calls itself a research station for unconventional dance music. Independent Dancer spreads that research over eighty minutes, from ten-minute club tracks to songs with guests; Can Oral, credited among this song's writers, is the Berlin singer Khan, heard on Desperate Man.

## Impact and Reception

BlackPlastic found the album brilliant and challenging but too long, its thirteen tracks petering out rather than ending; the song's afterlife is Frank Wiedemann's remix. Play the original quietly, listen to the cello and the piano disagree, then try the remix.
