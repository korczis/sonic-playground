## Genre and Mood

**Gabriella Cilmi** was sixteen when **Sweet About Me** made her name: pop that borrows its walk from swing jazz and its warmth from soul. Playful, catchy and a little sultry, it arrived in 2008 amid the soul singers who followed Amy Winehouse, and held its own because, the BBC's chart blog said, it swings a bit.

## Song Summary

Cilmi wrote it with Xenomania, Brian Higgins's team behind Girls Aloud and Sugababes, after raiding a Parisian record shop for inspiration; its fun came from her mother's love of Sweet and T. Rex. Wikipedia describes pop and swing jazz over a subtle electro beat derived from rocksteady grooves, with layered pop production. Cilmi said the lyric means we will all make mistakes, so we might as well have fun making them; Nick Levine of Digital Spy heard something more bitter under the melodic cuteness.

## Musical Breakdown

The sources describe the song's sound more than its sections, so this walk-through keeps to the parts they name.

### Intro

Paul Cashmere of Undercover FM wrote of the song's sultry opening notes, sung in a voice Levine heard as sweet and coarse at once, like honey drizzled on toast.

Here the song's two versions part. On the album version, this page's recording, the opening line comes back and a further line follows before the pre-chorus; the radio edit cuts the verse in half.

### Chorus

Jake Taylor of Sputnikmusic praised the song for not leaning on its chorus to hold your attention, trading it for brilliant singing and layered production throughout.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="swing" /> }} plays pairs of notes unevenly, so a rhythm lilts instead of marching. Here it lives inside a modern pop record, which Wikipedia puts in common time at 132 beats per minute, and the BBC's Fraser McAlpine credited the swing with lifting it above the other soul revivalists of 2008. Compare {{ <track_link source="paloma-faith-upside-down.md" /> }}.

A {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is what stays with you; Cilmi thought the song was like a nursery rhyme, the way it got stuck in your head.

A {{ <concept_link name="radio edit" /> }} is a shorter version for airplay. The CD singles opened with one, dropping two lines before the pre-chorus that the album keeps.

## Artistic Context

Cilmi began recording Lessons to Be Learned in Melbourne at thirteen and moved to London in 2007 to finish it. She loved Led Zeppelin and Kings of Leon, yet said she had to make a pop-sounding record to reach as many people as possible; she and Higgins met in the middle. Island released the album on 31 March 2008, its title taken from a line of this song.

## Impact and Reception

The song topped the Australian chart for five non-consecutive weeks, making Cilmi the youngest Australian artist to reach number one, and rose to number six in the UK, where PRS for Music counted it the most-played track of 2009. It won Single of the Year at the 2008 ARIA Awards, and Time Out named it the best song of 2008. McAlpine admitted it does not stop you in your tracks the way {{ <track_link source="amy-winehouse-rehab.md" /> }} did; put it on and let it swing instead.
