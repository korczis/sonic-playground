## Genre and Mood

**Miike Snow**'s **"Genghis Khan"** is electropop with funk and R&B in its bones: catchy enough to stick after one play, playful in its jokes, and built around a confession that is not playful at all.

## Song Summary

Andrew Wyatt wrote the lyric about a long-distance relationship he was not committing to, while not wanting the other person to see anyone else. He has said it made him feel like a tyrant, hence the emperor, and that he was naming a negative property in himself, not endorsing the man. The music came first: Christian Karlsson made the melody and beats in a Los Angeles studio, and by Karlsson's account the song came together in about thirty minutes.

## Musical Breakdown

### The Arrangement

Wikipedia lists drums, piano and synthesizer, layered vocals, chant-style parts and an Arabian riff. No source we found describes how the track opens or ends, so this guide does not either.

### Verses: The Tyrant Speaks

Wyatt sings in falsetto throughout, a high, light voice for words about possessiveness.

### Hook: Whoo-oo-oo

The hook has no words: Wyatt added the whoo-oo-oo after hearing a child sing along to a Gwen Stefani song, and it sits among the chanted, stacked voices.

### Bridge

Spin's Rachel Brodsky compared the structure of the bridge to Missy Elliott's "One Minute Man".

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="falsetto" /> }} usually suggests tenderness or euphoria. Here Wyatt stays up there for the whole song while singing about jealousy, and the contrast between the light voice and the heavy feeling is much of the song's character.

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} shows how little a pop song needs to be remembered: a syllable repeated in a melody, borrowed from a child's sing-along. Miike Snow had used the same trick of a bright surface over a gloomier lyric on {{ <track_link source="miike-snow-paddling-out.md" /> }}.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Ninian Doff, premiered on BuzzFeed on 12 January 2016. A villain cannot bring himself to kill his captive, a Bond-like spy; the spy comes back of his own accord, the two dance, and the villain leaves his unhappy marriage. Pontus Winnberg told QX the team picked the gay romance between villain and spy from twenty concepts. The official video runs longer than the 3:32 album track.

## Artistic Context

Miike Snow formed in Stockholm in 2007: the Swedish producers Christian Karlsson and Pontus Winnberg, known as Bloodshy & Avant, and the American singer Andrew Wyatt. "Genghis Khan" was the second single from their third album, iii: it came out on 3 December 2015 on Jackalope and Atlantic, three months before the album. Henrik Jonback co-wrote it and co-produced it with the band.

## Impact and Reception

Reviews were mixed. Clash's Luke Winstanley called it "outrageously catchy" and Consequence of Sound said it turns the ruler's legacy into comedy; the Boston Globe and Spin were less kind. It reached number 6 on Billboard's Alternative Airplay chart, became the band's first single to chart in Sweden, and went Platinum in the US in 2020. The video won Best Pop Video (International) and Best Cinematography at the 2016 UK Music Video Awards. Listen to how sweetly a tyrant can sing.
