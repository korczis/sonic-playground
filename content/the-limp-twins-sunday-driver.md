## Genre and Mood

**The Limp Twins** made **Sunday Driver** on an album that Discogs files under acid jazz, downtempo, future jazz and jazz-dance, and that Last.fm simply calls funky. The title sets the mood before a note is played: a Sunday driver is the person who takes the road at their own pace, and the song borrows that unhurried, relaxed and playful spirit.

## Song Summary

Sunday Driver is the tenth of eleven tracks on Tales From Beyond The Groove, three minutes and twenty-nine seconds long. Like the rest of the album, it was made by two friends and nobody else: the credits give the writing and arranging to both, the production, bass, guitar and tenor saxophone to Will Holland, and the lyrics, vocals and organ to Russ Porter. XLR8R heard the album as guitar-driven and funny, a record about the pair's childhood and friendship rather than a showcase of their other projects. That homemade, good-humoured scale is the detail that makes the track memorable.

## Musical Breakdown

### Sound

The album's styles, acid jazz, downtempo and jazz-dance, point to a groove-based track rather than a song that builds to a climax: a small band sound, made by two players, at an easy pace.

### Setting

According to the album cover, the duo recorded it in their bedrooms. That is the room you are listening to, and it suits a song named after a slow drive.

## Concepts in Focus

Sunday Driver is an honest piece of {{ <concept_link name="lo-fi" /> }}. Lo-fi is music made with modest means, often at home, and the Limp Twins made their album exactly that way, in their bedrooms, by their own account. Here lo-fi is not an effect chosen for its charm but the circumstances of the recording, and it fits the humour XLR8R praised in a scene that took itself very seriously.

The album's styles all depend on the {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }}: the felt quality of a rhythm, how drums, bass and the other parts lock together and push against each other in time. Funk and acid jazz are built on it, and downtempo slows it down. A track called Sunday Driver is a good place to hear a groove that relaxes rather than drives. Compare it with the patient Tru Thoughts soul of {{ <track_link source="alice-russell-high-up-on-the-hook.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

The Limp Twins were Will Holland and Russ Porter. Holland, born in 1980 in Bewdley, Worcestershire, is better known as Quantic, and until 2020 all his albums came out on the Brighton label Tru Thoughts; Porter also played in the Quantic Soul Orchestra. Their album Tales From Beyond The Groove was released by Tru Thoughts on 17 November 2003, and the label still carries Sunday Driver on its own YouTube channel.

## Impact and Reception

Reviewing the album for XLR8R in 2004, Jon Freer called it guitar-driven and praised its sense of humour in a downbeat scene that took itself very seriously. Sunday Driver found its widest audience through compilations: in 2004 Stéphane Pompougnac put it on Hôtel Costes, Volume 7, and it later appeared on Tru Thoughts' tenth-anniversary collection. Play it on a slow day, ideally with the window down.
