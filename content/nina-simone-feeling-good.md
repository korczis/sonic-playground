## Genre and Mood

**Nina Simone**'s **"Feeling Good"** is a show tune turned jazz and blues: bold, empowering and liberating, a song of emancipation that opens on a nearly bare voice and then lets a big band break in.

## Song Summary

Anthony Newley and Leslie Bricusse wrote it for their 1964 musical The Roar of the Greasepaint – The Smell of the Crowd, where it is sung by a character called "the Negro" after he wins a game rigged against him, a stand-in, uDiscover Music notes, for the racial and class inequality of 1960s Britain. Stanley Green called it a "booming song of emancipation". Simone recorded it in New York in January 1965 with Hal Mooney, who arranged and produced it, and it came out that June on I Put a Spell on You.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro: The Voice Alone

Far Out hears her voice "almost naked in the intro section, backed up by a minimalistic arrangement", which slowly swells, building tension.

### Main Section: The Brass Breaks In

Then the brass of the big band comes in "with a bombastic effect", the moment Far Out calls the gut punch of emotion. uDiscover credits Simone and Mooney with the jazzy horns now associated with the song.

### Outro: Scat and Ad-lib

Near the end, uDiscover writes, Simone shows her vocal improvisation by scatting and ad-libbing. No source we found gives the timing of any part, so this guide gives none.

## Concepts in Focus

Simone's recording is a {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} that replaced its original in the public mind. Cy Grant sang it first, on the show's opening night in Nottingham on 3 August 1964, and Gilbert Price sang it on Broadway, yet the song most people know is hers, and later versions such as {{ <track_link source="avicii-feeling-good.md" /> }} start from it.

The {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }} is what made the difference. uDiscover describes the stage number as mildly operatic; Mooney, Far Out writes, arranged the song strategically to expose Simone's strong vocals, holding the band back so that the voice carries the opening alone.

The scatting at the end is {{ <concept_link name="improvisation" /> }}: wordless syllables and free phrases from a singer trained in classical piano, jazz and blues, inside an arrangement that is otherwise written out.

## Artistic Context

I Put a Spell on You, issued by Philips, is what AllMusic called one of her most pop-oriented albums, and NPR's Audie Cornish called it the closest Simone came to pop. uDiscover places it after Nina Simone in Concert of 1964, the album with "Mississippi Goddam", amid the race riots of 1963 and 1964; its focus was on Black happiness and optimism.

## Impact and Reception

"Feeling Good" was not a single in 1965. A British Volkswagen advert used it in 1994 and it reached number 40 in the UK that July. Since then it has been sampled on Jay-Z and Kanye West's "New Day", heard in Cruella and Perfect Days, and certified double Platinum in the US. The official video, made with Dove and showing four generations of Black women, arrived in June 2021, 56 years after the recording. Let the opening bars draw you in before the brass arrives.
