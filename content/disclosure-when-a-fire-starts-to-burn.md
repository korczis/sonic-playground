## Genre and Mood

**Disclosure** built **When A Fire Starts To Burn** as house in its oldest sense: electronic dance music made of a drum machine, a bassline and a voice. The Fader called it a thick, classic house tune animated by an evangelical vocal sample, a floor filler. The mood is energetic and, through the voice of a motivational speaker, empowering.

## Song Summary

The voice belongs to Eric Thomas, a self-styled hip-hop preacher. Disclosure had wanted a rapper on their debut album, someone like Kendrick Lamar or A$AP Rocky, and schedules did not allow it. So Howard Lawrence bought a recording of Thomas on iTunes and cut it up rhythmically until it sounded as if he were rapping; Guy Lawrence added the beat and mixed it. Thomas is sampled again on the album's Intro.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

On the album the track follows the Intro, which also samples Thomas. The official video, longer than the album version, starts with a preacher testifying before his congregation before the beat takes over.

### Main Section

Thomas's cut-up phrases run over 909 drum sounds and a simple bassline. Guy Lawrence described the aim as a jacking Chicago house track and said that, with the sample right in your face, little else was needed underneath. Resident Advisor heard the drums hit crisply and the song run on its own tireless energy.

### Outro

No source describes how the track ends; listen for whether the voice or the drums have the last word.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} here means more than borrowing a phrase: Howard Lawrence rearranged a speech into new rhythmic lines, turning a talker into a performer on the beat.

The voice is {{ <concept_link name="spoken word" /> }}, not singing, and house has a long history of it. Compare {{ <track_link source="chuck-roberts-in-the-beginning.md" /> }}, the voice of the 1987 sermon that house music has sampled more than any other speech.

The {{ <concept_link name="drum machine" /> }} is the other half. Guy Lawrence chose retro 909 sounds for a deliberately jacking Chicago feel: a duo born in the 1990s reaching back to the house music they had learned from.

## Artistic Context

Disclosure are the brothers Guy and Howard Lawrence from Reigate, Surrey, who came to Chicago house, Detroit techno and UK garage by way of hip-hop and dubstep. The track was the fourth single from Settle, which PMR and Island released on 3 June 2013 and the duo produced themselves.

## Impact and Reception

Settle went to number one in the UK and was nominated for the Mercury Prize and a Grammy. The track's video was nominated for Best Director at the 2014 Berlin Music Video Awards, and the song was used in The 100. Turn it up and let Thomas preach.
