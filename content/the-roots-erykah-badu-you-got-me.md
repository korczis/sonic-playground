## Genre and Mood

**The Roots**' **You Got Me**, with **Erykah Badu** on the chorus, is hip-hop played by a band and dressed in soul and R&B: violas and violins, guitars and Scott Storch's keyboards around Questlove's drums. Philadelphia magazine calls it slinky. It is romantic and soulful, a love song that is mostly about reassurance.

## Song Summary

Okayplayer describes the song as a recollection of easing a lover's fears of being led astray. Black Thought raps, Eve raps the second verse, and Badu sings the hook. That hook was co-written by Jill Scott, who first recorded the chorus and bridge; MCA wanted a higher-profile collaboration for the lead single and had Badu re-record Scott's part, since Scott was then little known outside Philadelphia.

## Musical Breakdown

No source times the song; this follows what the credits and the drummer's own account describe.

### Verse 1

Questlove keeps the beat tight. Philadelphia magazine notes that on record he kept his snare and hi-hats pinched to mimic clipped drum-machine tones. The credits add violas and violins, two guitarists and Scott Storch's additional keyboards.

### Chorus

Badu's sung hook answers the rapped verses, the soul half of the song.

### Verse 2

Eve, then billed as Eve of Destruction, raps the second verse: her recording debut, on the album version only.

### Outro

On the album version the song stretches into an extended outro where Questlove plays a skittering solo. He told Modern Drummer it was a drum and bass homage: the band moved to London in 1994, when drum and bass was all the rage, and wanted to send its friends there a shout-out.

## Concepts in Focus

The song is a lesson in the {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}. The label picked the guest by fame, so the writer of the hook does not sing it here; Scott's original came out in 2005 on the compilation Home Grown!, and she sings it on {{ <track_link source="the-roots-jill-scott-you-got-me-featuring-jill-scott-live-1999.md" /> }}.

{{ <concept_link name="rapping" /> }} and singing split the work: the verses carry the argument, the chorus carries the feeling.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Charles Stone III, has cameos from Common and from Tracy Morgan as a rider on a bus, and Eve does not appear in it. The embedded video is that official clip, not the 4:19 album recording, so its edit may differ from what this page describes.

## Artistic Context

Things Fall Apart, the Roots' fourth album, came out on MCA in February 1999. It was recorded at Electric Lady in 1997 and 1998 alongside other Soulquarians projects, and it is the band's breakthrough; You Got Me, released as a single that January, caught on quickly on radio and MTV.

## Impact and Reception

The single reached number 39 on the Billboard Hot 100 and won the Roots and Badu the Grammy for Best Rap Performance by a Duo or Group in 2000. In 2026 the Recording Academy and the Black Music Collective awarded Eve a Grammy for her contribution at the Recording Academy Honors. Play the album version, and wait for the outro.
