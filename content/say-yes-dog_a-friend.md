> **"A Friend"** is the song that introduced **Say Yes Dog**: the title track of their first EP in 2013, and a song they kept for their debut album two years later. It is warm, melodic synth-pop with a dance pulse, the sound of a band that learned its craft in a conservatory and chose to make pop.

## Band Background

**Say Yes Dog** is a German-Luxembourgish electro-pop trio: Aaron Ahrends sings, Paul Rundel plays bass and Pascal Karier plays drums. Ahrends and Rundel grew up in Berlin and Karier comes from Luxembourg, and the three met studying sound engineering and jazz at the conservatory in The Hague.

They have released three albums, Plastic Love (2015), Voyage (2019) and DRÄI (2024), toured with the American band Capital Cities and played festivals across Europe. Their sound combines a live rhythm section with soft synthesizers and melodic hooks, closer to warm indie pop than to cold electro.

## About the Track: "A Friend"

**"A Friend"** first came out in 2013 as the title track of the band's self-released debut EP, produced and mixed by Shai Solan, alongside "Get It", "Around My Neck" and "Love You Back". In 2015 it reappeared on the album Plastic Love, recorded in a studio in Haarlem and released by Diskodogs Records.

### Symbolism in "A Friend"

The title puts friendship, rather than romance, at the centre, and the song treats it with the same tenderness pop usually saves for love. Its warmth comes from the contrast between a bright, danceable surface and a voice that sounds more reflective than celebratory.

### Mood and Atmosphere

The mood is **uplifting** and **heartfelt**, but never loud. The synths are bright and the tempo moves, yet the song keeps an easy, unhurried pace that suits listening as much as dancing.

## Song Summary

"A Friend" combines soft, pulsing synth-pop with a melodic vocal and a sing-along chorus. The rhythm section keeps a steady pulse, the synths provide colour and lift, and the song builds its feeling through repetition and a chorus that is simple enough to sing back after one listen.

## Musical Composition

### Intro: A Spark of Energy

The song opens with bright, pulsing synths that set an upbeat but unhurried tone before the voice arrives.

### Verse 1: The Beginning of Friendship

The first verse brings in Ahrends' gentle, melodic vocal over a steady rhythm. Bass and drums keep the groove simple so the melody can carry the song.

### Chorus: Celebrating Connection

The chorus is the heart of the track, a catchy, sing-along hook where the synths come forward and the rhythm lifts.

### Verse 2: Deepening Bonds

The second verse returns to the calmer texture of the first, keeping the pulse going while the voice stays intimate.

### Bridge: A Moment of Reflection

The bridge offers a brief pause in the song's energy: the instrumentation softens and the synths take on a more airy quality before the final chorus.

### Final Chorus: A Joyful Reunion

The last chorus returns with renewed energy, the synths and rhythm at their fullest, and brings the song to an uplifting close.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} is what makes Say Yes Dog a synth-pop band rather than a rock trio. With only voice, bass and drums in the line-up, the keyboards supply the harmony and the colour: bright, pulsing patterns in the intro, fuller chords under the chorus, softer, airier sounds in the bridge. Listen to how the synth parts change from section to section while the rhythm section stays steady; the arrangement moves through sound rather than through tempo.

The chorus is a {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} in the classic pop sense, short, repeated and built around the title, which is why the song worked as the calling card of an unknown band's first EP. Its simplicity is the point: a hook has to work the first time a stranger hears it.

## Overall Vibe

**"A Friend"** is feel-good synth-pop with a reflective heart. It is catchy without being pushy, and its warmth comes from the players as much as from the machines.

## Conclusion

In **"A Friend"**, Say Yes Dog found the formula they would build on: live bass and drums, soft synthesizers and a chorus you can sing. Listen to the 2013 EP version and the Plastic Love version side by side and hear how a young band's first song carried them into their first album.
