## Genre and Mood

**Ben Khan**'s **the green** is the album's exhale. It sits where alternative pop, hip-hop and electronic production meet, and its mood is groovy and relaxed. The Guardian heard loping, J Dilla-esque hip-hop, one of the strong slower moments that arrive after the clouds break.

## Song Summary

The green is a track from Khan's self-titled debut album, released on 10 August 2018 on Dirty Hit, and the writing credit is his alone, as Benedict Khan. Where much of the album is dense and humid, this is one of the songs that slows down. Two reviewers pointed to what holds it together: The Guardian to its loping hip-hop pulse, and XS Noize to its guitar, calling it guitar-driven. No review describes its lyric or its structure, so this page follows the sound and leaves the words to you.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} of a slow track depends less on speed than on placement: where each drum hit lands and how long the space after it lasts. Loping is the critic's word for that, a long, easy stride rather than a march.

Calling it J Dilla-esque points to {{ <concept_link name="quantization" /> }}. The Detroit producer famously switched off the quantize feature of his MPC sampler, letting drums fall slightly off the grid for a drunk, laid-back feel. No source measures the green's beat, so treat the comparison as a question to test by ear.

A {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} shapes how an artist sounds. For his debut album Khan worked with Flood, known for U2, The Killers and PJ Harvey, who according to The Guardian brought little big-budget gloss and kept a raw, bedroom-recorded quality.

## Artistic Context

Ben Khan is a London producer and singer, as The Guardian introduced him; an earlier track such as {{ <track_link source="ben-khan-savage.md" /> }} shows where his warped funk started. The Skinny described the record as drenched in the fluorescent synths and bubbling electronics he had become known for, and The Guardian heard sinister John Carpenter synth sounds put to the service of extremely funky pop, as on {{ <track_link source="ben-khan-monsoon-daydream.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

The album was well received: The Line of Best Fit heard cosmic soul and prismatic funk, XS Noize gave it 8/10 and named the guitar-driven the green among its highlights, and The Guardian picked it out among the slower songs. Play it after Monsoon Daydream and feel the temperature drop.
