## Genre and Mood

**Brock Berrigan** makes instrumental beats, and **The Celebration Song** is one of the best known of them: Chillhop lists it among his popular tracks. Deezer files the album it sits on under rap and hip-hop; Chillhop describes his style as a blend of hip-hop, jazz, funk and soul with a laid-back, groovy feel. Expect a relaxed, playful track of under three minutes rather than a song.

## Song Summary

The Celebration Song has no vocal, and The Find Magazine pointed out how rarely an instrumental gets a full video. MusicBrainz lists it first on April's Neck Brace, released in August 2011, and then on Night Owl, the album this page names; the dates given for Night Owl differ, with MusicBrainz saying 2013 and Deezer 2016. The video here is the artist's own audio upload, which runs a few seconds longer than the 2:53 album track.

## Musical Breakdown

No review describes this track part by part, so this breakdown describes how its maker works and what to listen for, not a sourced map of the song.

### The Beat

Chillhop says Berrigan samples jazz and soul records, chops them up and rearranges them, often adding live guitar, bass and drums. Guitar is his main instrument, he told Stereofox.

### The Arrangement

He credits a decade of songwriting before he turned to beats for his sense of structure and layering, which he said makes a track more interesting than a four-bar loop.

### The Ending

The album track lasts 2:53, closer to a sketch than a suite; no source describes how it ends, so listen for that yourself.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} is the core of Berrigan's craft: he told Stereofox he spends a few hours most evenings listening to records and looking for samples. A sampled beat lives on choices, which fragment, how long, what goes next to it. Hear the same craft in {{ <track_link source="kruder-dorfmeister-johnson.md" /> }}.

The {{ <concept_link name="loop" /> }} is the unit such beats are built from, and also the trap Berrigan says he tries to avoid by arranging sections.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} gave the track a plot. Written, directed and edited by Dakota Laden and produced by Aaron Goodwin as a Cinemanic production, it follows a detective, SherBrock Holmes, hunting vultures who turn violent in Minneapolis, a story The Find Magazine compared to Fargo.

## Artistic Context

Chillhop places Brock Berrigan in New Jersey. He performs in a chicken mask, a prop from a Halloween party that became his image, and told Stereofox in 2017 that he records late at night on a laptop, a beer at hand that he blames for the glitches in his beats. Chillhop's compilations have featured his work.

## Impact and Reception

The video first appeared on Chillhop's YouTube channel for a few years; after a copyright issue Berrigan posted it on his own channel in 2020. Put the audio on, then watch the vultures.
