## Genre and Mood

**Queen** were a stadium rock band, and **Another One Bites the Dust** is the moment they made a funk and disco record instead. It is groovy, confident and by now iconic, a rock band's biggest hit built almost entirely on a bass guitar.

## Song Summary

The song is John Deacon's, written by the bassist and built from the bottom up. Its bassline was inspired by Chic's Good Times, and Chic's Bernard Edwards later said that Queen's bass player had spent time hanging out at their studio. Deacon played almost everything himself: bass, piano, electric guitar and handclaps. Roger Taylor added a drum loop, Brian May guitar and an Eventide Harmonizer, and Freddie Mercury sang. There are no synthesisers on it, unusual for The Game, the first Queen album to use one. The song was recorded in 1980 at Musicland Studios in Munich, with Reinhold Mack producing alongside the band.

## Musical Breakdown

The sources describe the song's ingredients more than its sections, so this walk-through names only the moments they place.

### Intro

Just before the first lyric comes a swirling sound, where the effect of the harmonizer can be heard clearly. Like every effect on the record, it was made from piano, guitars, drums and tape played backwards at various speeds.

### Verse 1

Mercury sings over Deacon's bass and Taylor's drum loop; on the studio recording he sings every part.

### Chorus

The title line arrives over the same groove; in early live performances, Taylor sang lead on the chorus.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} usually supports a song; here it is the song. Wikipedia puts its tempo close to 110 beats per minute, near the rate recommended for chest compressions, which is why it has been used in a CPR training study. Compare how {{ <track_link source="queen-the-miami-project-another-one-bites-the-dust-a-skillz-remix.md" /> }} rebuilds a whole track around it.

Taylor's drum part is a {{ <concept_link name="loop" /> }}, a pattern repeated rather than played freely, which gives the song its machine-like steadiness.

This page is the 2011 {{ <concept_link name="remastering" /> }}, prepared when Universal reissued The Game. A remaster goes back to the original tapes and adjusts level and tone for new formats; the performance, the arrangement and the mix decisions of 1980 stay as they were.

## Artistic Context

The Game, Queen's eighth studio album, was released on 30 June 1980 on EMI in the UK and Elektra in the US. Michael Jackson, backstage after a Queen concert in Los Angeles, urged Mercury to release Another One Bites the Dust as a single, and it followed in August 1980. Its promotional video was filmed at Reunion Arena in Dallas on 9 August 1980 and directed by Daniella Green.

## Impact and Reception

The single spent three weeks at number one on the Billboard Hot 100 in October 1980, Queen's second and last American number one, and reached number two on the soul and disco charts and number seven in the UK. With more than seven million copies sold, it is Queen's best-selling single. Put it on and let the bass do the talking.
