## Genre and Mood

**Florence + The Machine** made their name with songs that crash and swell, and **St Jude** is the exception. Wikipedia files it as an electropop ballad with a minimalist, organ-led arrangement, and Stereogum noticed what it leaves out: no booming choir comes storming in on the chorus. The mood is melancholic and inward, and Atwood Magazine heard it as serene and resigned, like a prayer waiting to be answered.

## Song Summary

Florence Welch wrote the song during a break from music, after the St Jude storm struck north-western Europe in October 2013. Introducing it at its first concert, at the Dome in Tufnell Park in March 2015, she said storms had followed her around, real ones and emotional ones. The lyric joins the two: a relationship ending while the storm whips around the couple, addressed to Jude the Apostle, the patron saint of lost causes. Welch wrote it with James Ford, and Markus Dravs produced it, pushing her, Wikipedia says, towards more exposed material.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

The Vinyl District heard the song open on bucolic sounds before a cool synthesizer arrives and Welch follows in a hushed voice.

### Main Section

PopMatters describes the base as just an organ vamp and lightly brushed drums, with a choir of Florences behind her; backing singers repeat the saint's name, and Atwood Magazine heard haunting whispers in the chorus.

### Outro

No review describes the ending. Listen for the oboe that PopMatters heard gliding down beside Welch's voice.

## Concepts in Focus

St Jude is a {{ <concept_link name="ballad" /> }} in the modern sense: slow, about loss, with the voice in front and the accompaniment kept spare. Here one of pop's loudest singers holds back.

The {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }} does that work. Wikipedia credits woodwind arrangements to Dravs and Sally Herbert, with Rebecca Wood on oboe and cor anglais and Pete Harrison on bassoon: a few reed instruments colour the organ.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Vincent Haycock with choreography by Ryan Heffington, is filmed as one continuous shot: Welch walks through rain and sunset and ends on her knees in a field as birds wheel overhead. Haycock called it the band's version of the Divine Comedy, its first layer of hell, and it later became Chapter 3 of The Odyssey, the band's 2016 film built from the album's videos.

## Artistic Context

St Jude is the tenth and penultimate track of How Big, How Blue, How Beautiful, the band's third album, released by Island on 29 May 2015, just before the closer {{ <track_link source="florence-the-machine-mother.md" /> }}. The Line of Best Fit heard it as more pensive than the lead single What Kind of Man; {{ <track_link source="florence-the-machine-long-lost.md" /> }} is another of its quiet songs.

## Impact and Reception

Released as a download on 25 March 2015, St Jude reached number 89 on the UK downloads chart and number 36 on Billboard's Hot Rock and Alternative Songs. PopMatters ranked it among Welch's finest songs and The Independent called it the album's most touching performance; Sputnikmusic found it painfully trite, and in 2022 The Guardian placed it fourth among the band's songs. Play it after the album's louder tracks.
