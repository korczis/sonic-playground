## Genre and Mood

**Michal Horák**'s **Hej teto!** is a Czech songwriter's folk song about a death in the family, if the family includes a hamster. It is humorous and irreverent but played straight: an acoustic band, a minor key and a narrator who apologises in earnest. That deadpan is the joke.

## Song Summary

The narrator was supposed to look after his aunt's hamster. He let it run around the room, lay down on the sofa and felt something pressing into his back. It was only the remote control, but the hamster had gone quiet, and a strange smell was coming from the radiator. The refrain is his confession to the aunt; the last verse reveals the cause of death: the hamster had swallowed a magnet. Horák heard the story from a friend as a teenager, on holiday in the Alps, and found it "morbidly silly"; later he realised it was a story many people already knew.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

No source describes the opening bars. The credits name the band: violin, banjo, double bass and a second voice around Horák's own voice and guitar.

### Main Section

The verses move through G minor, B flat, F and C, and the refrain shifts to E flat, B flat, D and G minor, as the chord sheet under the official video gives them. After the first refrain the sheet marks a few bars on the refrain's chords without words.

### Outro

The last verse begins with the refrain's own words and delivers the punchline before the refrain returns for a final time, with some of its words echoed in brackets on the sheet.

## Concepts in Focus

Hej teto! is a classic {{ <concept_link name="novelty song" /> }}, built on an absurd story, but the tune holds up by itself: Horák says it came to him on the way back from a concert in Jáchymov, and only then did the hamster story fit it.

The song sits in a {{ <concept_link name="minor key" /> }}, G minor, the colour of a lament. In the refrain the D major chord pulls hard back to G minor, so the confession sounds tragic while the words describe a hamster welded to the heating.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed and written by Martin Hammerschlag, made Horák known in May 2018. Pavla Tomicová plays the aunt and the songwriter Pokáč a policeman; it was shot in a laboratory at Horák's old primary school, at a sewage plant and in his grandmother's flat, for her old-style radiator.

## Artistic Context

Horák, born in Hradec Králové in 1998, plays guitar, piano, harmonica and the heligonka. Hej teto! came out in May 2018 and opens his debut album Michalovo cédéčko, released by Supraphon in October 2019. He recorded it partly in a studio in Mníšek and partly at home. In 2021 he was named Discovery of the Year in the Český slavík poll.

## Impact and Reception

Horák did not expect it to become his most successful song. It has since turned up in camp songbooks, which he calls the greatest tribute a songwriter can get, and in 2021 iReport wrote that his concerts were unthinkable without it. Listen to the last verse, then play it to someone who has never heard it.
