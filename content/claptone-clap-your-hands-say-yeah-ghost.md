## Genre and Mood

**Claptone** brings **Clap Your Hands Say Yeah** into deep house on **Ghost**, a meeting of club music and indie rock vulnerability. The mood is haunting, melancholic and hypnotic: a groove that keeps your feet moving while the voice above it sounds like it is singing to someone who is no longer there.

## Song Summary

Ghost works because its two halves should not fit and do. Claptone's production is patient and warm, a steady house pulse with soft textures, while Alec Ounsworth, the singer and songwriter behind Clap Your Hands Say Yeah, brings his unmistakable quavering, fragile delivery. The contrast turns a club track into something closer to a confession, and the title captures the feeling exactly: presence and absence in the same breath.

## Musical Breakdown

The original mix runs close to seven minutes, building its spell slowly and letting the vocal arrive into a groove that is already fully formed.

### Intro

A deep, rounded kick and soft percussion open the track, with muted chords flickering in the background like candlelight.

### Build-up

The bassline enters and the groove settles into a relaxed, rolling sway, adding layers of gentle texture.

### Verse 1

Ounsworth's voice appears, high and wavering, its rawness standing out against the smooth production and making every line sound intimate and exposed.

### Chorus

The hook rises with a little more lift in the chords, the vocal stretching out into a melancholy refrain that lingers long after it ends.

### Bridge

The beat steps back and the voice and atmosphere carry the moment alone, the most ghostly passage of the track.

### Outro

The groove returns and gradually thins out, leaving the faint trace of the melody behind like a presence in an empty room.

## Concepts in Focus

Ghost is a lesson in choosing a {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} from outside your own world. Clap Your Hands Say Yeah became famous in the mid-2000s through MP3 blogs, a defining band of the blog rock era, and Ounsworth's voice belongs to that scene, not to the club. Claptone made a habit of it: his album Charmer later brought in Peter Bjorn and John, Jay-Jay Johanson and others from indie and pop.

Underneath, the {{ <concept_link name="house groove" /> }} never wavers: a four-on-the-floor kick, off-beat percussion and a rolling bassline at a relaxed tempo. That steadiness is what lets the vocal sound so fragile; listen to how the beat keeps dancing even in the saddest lines.

The track exists in two lengths. The full mix lasts almost seven minutes, made for DJs who need long intros and outros, while the official video uses a version of about three and a half minutes, close to the {{ <concept_link name="radio edit" /> }} on the release. The edit gets to the voice quickly; the long version lets the groove do its slow work first.

## Artistic Context

Claptone is the masked, Berlin-based house act whose golden, beaked plague-doctor mask conceals his identity. Ghost was released by the Berlin label Exploited on 10 February 2014, with a B-side called United, after his breakthrough No Eyes, and it later appeared on Claptone's 2015 debut album Charmer. Swedish director Mats Udd shot the official video at Ekebyhov Castle on Ekerö near Stockholm.

## Impact and Reception

Exploited reported that both No Eyes and Ghost topped the Beatport charts, and the record helped establish Claptone's reputation for pairing deep house with distinctive guest vocalists, a formula Mixmag summed up in 2015 by calling him one of the most visible characters in the global deep house scene. It belongs on this playground as one of the most emotional records in that run, a dance track with a broken heart. Listen to the full version and let it haunt you.
