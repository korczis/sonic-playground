## Genre and Mood

**Claptone** brings **Clap Your Hands Say Yeah** into deep house on **Ghost**, a meeting of club music and indie rock vulnerability. The mood is haunting, melancholic and hypnotic: a groove that keeps your feet moving while the voice above it sounds like it is singing to someone who is no longer there.

## Song Summary

Ghost works because its two halves should not fit and do. Claptone's production is patient and warm, a steady house pulse with soft, acoustic-flecked textures, while Alec Ounsworth, the singer of Clap Your Hands Say Yeah, brings his unmistakable quavering, fragile delivery. The contrast turns a club track into something closer to a confession, and the title captures the feeling exactly: presence and absence in the same breath.

## Musical Breakdown

The original mix runs close to seven minutes, building its spell slowly and letting the vocal arrive into a groove that is already fully formed.

### Intro

A deep, rounded kick and soft percussion open the track, with muted chords flickering in the background like candlelight.

### Build-up

The bassline enters and the groove settles into a relaxed, rolling sway, adding layers of gentle, organic texture.

### Verse 1

Ounsworth's voice appears, high and wavering, its rawness standing out against the smooth production and making every line sound intimate and exposed.

### Chorus

The hook rises with a little more lift in the chords, the vocal stretching out into a melancholy refrain that lingers long after it ends.

### Bridge

The beat steps back and the voice and atmosphere carry the moment alone, the most ghostly passage of the track.

### Outro

The groove returns and gradually thins out, leaving the faint trace of the melody behind like a presence in an empty room.

## Artistic Context

Claptone is the masked Berlin-based house producer whose golden, beaked mask has become part of the act. Ghost was released in 2014 on the Exploited label, featuring Alec Ounsworth of the American indie band Clap Your Hands Say Yeah, and it later appeared on Claptone's 2015 debut album Charmer. Swedish director Mats Udd made the official video, a shorter edit of the track.

## Impact and Reception

Ghost followed No Eyes to the top of the Beatport charts and helped establish Claptone's reputation for pairing deep house with distinctive guest vocalists from outside dance music, a formula he continued on Charmer. It belongs on this playground as one of the most emotional records in that run, a dance track with a broken heart. Listen to the full version and let it haunt you.
