## Genre and Mood

**Ilona Csáková**'s **Amsterdam** is mid-1990s Czech pop with a pop rock frame, the genre Czech Wikipedia gives its album. Its feelings, carefree and liberating, follow the theme that the same article names for the whole record: the freedom of Amsterdam.

## Song Summary

Amsterdam opens Csáková's second solo album, also called Amsterdam, released by EMI in 1995. Roman Štefl wrote both its music and its words, as the singer's official website credited it, and produced the album with Tomáš Dokoupil. The album was recorded in 1994 in Cologne, Germany, like her debut Kosmopolis, and a single of the same name came first, with two songs, Elegán and Tamtydá, available nowhere else. This page is the 1998 remastered version, which the label lists with a 1998 copyright for EMI Czech Republic.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the song, so this section describes only what the credits and the label state.

### The Recording

The album's players were Štefl on acoustic guitar and keyboards, Dokoupil on keyboards, Christoph Lindner on electric guitar and Jim Foster and Tomáš Waschinger on drums; Czech Wikipedia hears many of its songs as inspired by northern European music.

### The Voices

Behind Csáková the album credits a cast of backing singers, among them Elke Klein, Silke Hamann, Clelia Sarto and Tom Lehel.

### The Remaster

The 1998 version keeps the 1995 performance. No source says what the remastering changed; the label's upload runs 4:15 against the original album's 4:13.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="remastering" /> }} is the quiet kind of new version: the mastering stage is done again, usually for a reissue, while the arrangement and the performance stay as they were. That is why this is the same song as the 1995 hit, not a new take.

Štefl is a textbook {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} of 1990s Czech pop: writer, player and producer at once. Reviewing Csáková's next album Pink in 1997, Luna Kafé described Štefl alternating his own originals with hits from the sixties; Amsterdam itself pairs the title song with Malý vůz, Csáková's version of I Will Follow Him, also released as a single from the album.

Amsterdam also lives as many an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }}: a remix on the original album, an unplugged recording issued in 1998, a 2013 recording, and television performances from 1996 to 2016 that the singer keeps on her official channel.

## Artistic Context

Born in Cheb in 1970, Csáková sang with Laura a její tygři from 1987 to 1992 before going solo; for 1993, the year of her debut Kosmopolis, she won the Gramy award for newcomer of the year. With Amsterdam she moved to EMI, the label of her following albums.

## Impact and Reception

Czech Wikipedia names Amsterdam as the album that brought her first big hit, the title song. In the following years she came second among female singers in the Český slavík poll three times, and in 1998 she was the best-selling singer in the Czech Republic.
