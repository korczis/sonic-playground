## Genre and Mood

**Gipsy.cz** made their name with **Romano Hip Hop**, and the title says what the Czech band set out to do: join hip-hop to Romani music, a mix Radio Proglas filed under both world music and hip-hop. It is playful, by the reviewers' account full of humour and self-irony, and energetic, one of the fast, danceable songs that drive its album.

## Song Summary

The song is track four of the band's first album, also called Romano Hip Hop, released by Indies Scope on 20 November 2006; it runs 2:37. Czech Wikipedia says this single made the band known, and it reached MTV's World Chart Express. On the album, sung mostly in Romani with some Czech and English, Radoslav Banga, known as Gipsy, raps, sings, plays guitar and programmed the electronics, beside Vojta Lavička on violin, Petr Surmaj on accordion and guitar and Jan Surmaj on double bass.

## Musical Breakdown

### Main Section

No source walks through the song, so this is a guide, not a description. Karel Souček heard it as danceable, one of the fast songs that give the album its energy. Radek Antl's Musicserver review, without naming the track, heard a playful czardas calling out the title phrase, his example of the band's self-irony. Listen for the band of violin, accordion and double bass against the beats, and for where the rap gives way to singing. ## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="rapping" /> }} is half of the title. Gipsy came to the band from hip-hop, with a solo album in 2004, and Milan Tesař of Radio Proglas noted that rap is his home ground. Tesař still found the Czech rap the album's weakest part and the sung Romani its highlight. Listen for which of the two this song leans on.

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is the title itself: two words that join the Romani romano to hip hop and announce the whole project each time they come round. Compare how Czech hip-hop sounded a few years earlier in {{ <track_link source="defuckto-tak-je-to.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Banga formed Gipsy.cz with Lavička and the brothers Jan and Petr Surmaj after his 2004 solo album Ya favourite Cd Rom. The band won the 2006 Anděl award as the year's discovery, and the album reached eleventh place on the World Music Charts Europe. In 2007 the band became the first Czech group to play Glastonbury; in 2009, with a changed line-up, they represented the Czech Republic at Eurovision with Aven Romale and finished last in their semi-final without a point.

## Impact and Reception

Czech critics were divided on the album; those who singled out this song liked it. Of ten Musicserver reviewers, Jakub König wrote that the title track works fantastically, and David Věžník counted it among the album's stronger songs. Tesař called the album musically outstanding, more romano than hip hop. Hear which half wins for you.
