## Genre and Mood

**Moloko**'s **Familiar Feeling** is electronic dance-pop that takes its time. Its album version runs six and a half minutes, much of it spent getting ready: the mood is upbeat and romantic, the voice confident, as if the singer knows the chorus is coming.

## Song Summary

Wikipedia's article on Statues calls the song an upbeat remembrance: one lover trying to convince the other that the relationship was always meant to be, and that it should not be put at risk by jealousy and mistrust. It is arranged with hints of Latin and Irish folk music. The page plays the official video, a cut of under four minutes like the single's radio edit; the album version described below is almost twice as long.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

Lucy Davies of BBC Music called it one of the best introductions heard for a long time: Hammond organ noodling, Latin guitar and beats, then flourishes of fiddles that build the tension to an impossible level.

### Main Section

Then the track appears to halt and start afresh. Róisín Murphy enters with what Davies called a laid-back philosophy, the line "What's for you, won't pass you by", and the chorus that follows is, in Davies's words, big enough to justify all that building up and knocking down. In the bridge, listen for Mark Brydon's bass guitar trailing the vocal.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }} raises tension before a release; Familiar Feeling builds its first from organ, guitar and fiddles rather than filters or drum rolls, until the song stops and begins again.

That long opening is the first thing a {{ <concept_link name="radio edit" /> }} has to give up. The album version runs 6:30 and the single's radio edit 3:44; the shorter one led the single, and the video, too, runs under four minutes.

The song is also a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }}. After working with live musicians on Things to Make and Do in 2000, Moloko went back to electronic territory for Statues, yet Wikipedia names this song as one where the earlier record's organic influences remain. Compare the brass and bass drum of {{ <track_link source="moloko-forever-more.md" /> }}, four tracks later on the same album.

## Artistic Context

Moloko were Róisín Murphy and Mark Brydon, a duo formed in Sheffield. Familiar Feeling, written by both and produced by Brydon, was released by Echo on 17 February 2003 as the lead single of Statues, which followed on 3 March. The couple's relationship had ended in 2002, just before work on the album began, and Statues traces a relationship coming apart; it was their last studio album, and after its tour the duo disbanded.

## Impact and Reception

Familiar Feeling reached number 10 in the UK, number 2 on the UK indie chart and number 9 in Portugal, and Wikipedia's article on Moloko counts it, with The Time Is Now and the Boris Dlugosch remix of Sing It Back, among the songs they are best known for. Critics split: Adam Sweeting in The Guardian dismissed it as gibbering schmaltz, Davies heard an opening floor filler. Put on the album version, and give the introduction its full length.
