## Genre and Mood

**Fischerspooner** open their second album with **Just Let Go**, and it announces a change. It is still electro-pop and synth-pop with an electroclash pulse, but live drums and electric guitars push it towards rock, and Exclaim! found it awfully dance-floor-friendly. The mood is energetic, catchy and a little defiant, a song that sounds as if it is fighting something.

## Song Summary

Casey Spooner described the song as being about battles, between the mental and the physical. According to the Jackson Free Press, its vocals, unlike most of the record's, were written and recorded on the spot in a single day, in keeping with the song's idea. Warren Fischer and Spooner wrote it, and it was produced by the duo with Tony Hoffer and Nicolas Vernhes. It became Odyssey's lead single in 2005.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the track part by part; this breakdown keeps to what the reviews describe.

### Intro

As the first track on Odyssey, it sets the album's terms at once: Scene Point Blank heard a bouncy, infectious song that loops guitar, muted synths and a catchy bassline.

### Main Section

PopMatters heard it pulsing with electronics and with the flat vocalisations that characterise much electroclash, then taking on a new, rockier persona through crackling, combative electric guitars. The Jackson Free Press adds a detail to listen for: a vibraslap, the rattling percussion instrument made famous by Jimi Hendrix's All Along the Watchtower.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }} was the scene Fischerspooner came from: 1980s electro and synth-pop rebuilt with punk attitude, deadpan vocals and a taste for performance. Just Let Go keeps the deadpan voice and the machine pulse, but Wikipedia notes that its live drums and guitars gave it a markedly different sound from the debut album.

The {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} was part of the release plan. PopMatters counted half a dozen remixes before the album reached the shops, and Capitol released a remix 12-inch with versions by Thin White Duke (Jacques Lu Cont) and Tommie Sunshine after strong support from radio DJs and clubs.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, which the Jackson Free Press called retro-futuristic, was directed by Jon Kane. For a group that began as a stage show, the image was always part of the song. Compare where they started, with {{ <track_link source="fischerspooner-emerge.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Fischerspooner formed in New York in 1998, Warren Fischer a classically trained musician and Casey Spooner a video artist and experimental theatre performer. Their debut, #1, made them one of the more successful acts of electroclash. Odyssey, released by Capitol in April 2005, turned towards more structured electropop, with guests including David Byrne, Linda Perry and Susan Sontag. Spooner said the album was about being on Capitol, an icon of classic American music, and trying to embrace that cliché and take it apart at the same time.

## Impact and Reception

Just Let Go reached number 20 on the UK Singles Chart and number 7 on Billboard's Hot Dance Club Songs chart, and it was used in the second season of Nip/Tuck. PopMatters called it probably the most familiar thing on the album. Play it loud and listen for the guitars crackling against the synths.
