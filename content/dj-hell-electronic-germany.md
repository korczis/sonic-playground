## Genre and Mood

**DJ Hell** called his 2009 double album Teufelswerk, German for "devil's work", and **Electronic Germany** is one of its plainest statements. It is electro and techno with a title that sounds like a manifesto, and the sources hear it two ways at once: Amsterdam Dance Event wrote that it zooms and thrusts with menacing intent, while Cokemachineglow heard a playfully self-aware vibe of German pretension. Dark and ironic, then, and at eight minutes, built to hypnotise a club rather than to make a point and leave.

## Song Summary

The track was written by the German electro producer Anthony Rother, and the Cokemachineglow review names his contributions as the most noticeable on the album's club disc, singling out "the self-evident Electronic Germany". Ink 19 heard Kraftwerk-inspired house, with arpeggios and vocoders, and an homage to the contemporary Berlin sound. A German track that names its country and its music in two words, made with machine voices that recall Kraftwerk, is both tribute and wink.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="vocoder" /> }} turns a human voice into a synthesizer's, and in German electronic music it points straight at Kraftwerk. Using it on a track called Electronic Germany makes the reference unmissable.

An {{ <concept_link name="arpeggio" /> }} plays the notes of a chord one after another instead of together; repeated by a sequencer, it becomes the moving pattern that Ink 19 heard under this track, another sound of Kraftwerk's legacy.

Teufelswerk is a {{ <concept_link name="concept album" /> }}: one disc for Night, aimed at clubs, one for Day, which Cokemachineglow heard drawing on German Kosmische music. Electronic Germany sits second on Night.

## Artistic Context

DJ Hell, born Helmut Josef Geier in Bavaria in 1962, founded International DeeJay Gigolo Records in Munich in 1996; its revival of 1980s sounds made him one of the founders of electroclash, and the label first released {{ <track_link source="miss-kittin-frank-sinatra.md" /> }} and Fischerspooner. Teufelswerk brought in Bryan Ferry, Peter Kruder, Christian Prommer and P. Diddy. Rother, whose own {{ <track_link source="anthony-rother-brainshaker.md" /> }} works with the same machine palette, had already brought the same self-aware play on German pretension to his 2008 album My Name Is Beuys Von Telekraft.

## Impact and Reception

Teufelswerk reached the top 50 in Germany, and Amsterdam Dance Event called it Hell's masterpiece. Electronic Germany placed 26th for best song in the 2010 readers' poll of the German magazine Spex, and in 2011 it returned in a DJ Glen remix on Teufelswerk (House Remixes).
