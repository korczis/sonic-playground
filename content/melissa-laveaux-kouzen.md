## Genre and Mood

**Mélissa Laveaux**'s "Kouzen" is a traditional Haitian song played as folk with a surf-rock guitar: energetic, groovy and over almost before it has begun.

## Song Summary

The song is credited as traditional, and Laveaux's label traces it to its earliest recordings in the 1920s. She recorded it for Radyo Siwèl, an album of Haitian songs she arranged with the production trio A.L.B.E.R.T. The label calls her reading surf rock, Exclaim! hears a "guitar-driven ride", and Drowned in Sound singles out the track's "rhythmical guitar". It lasts two minutes and twenty-one seconds.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes Kouzen section by section, so this breakdown follows the players the album credits and what the reviews say about them.

### The Guitar

Laveaux plays electric guitar throughout the album, and here it is the engine: the reviewers hear a rhythmical, driving part at the centre of the track.

### The Cuatro and the Band

Drew Gonsalves, leader of the Trinidadian band Kobo Town, adds the cuatro, a small four-string guitar; Vincent Taurelle plays keyboards, Ludovic Bruni bass and Vincent Taeger drums.

### The Voices

Laveaux sings lead with background vocals from herself and Gonsalves. Drowned in Sound writes that next to the guitar of Kouzen, her voice proves as pliable as her playing.

## Concepts in Focus

Kouzen is a {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} of a song older than any one performer. Traditional songs have no original to be faithful to, only earlier recordings, so each new version is free to change the setting; a surf-rock guitar is a long way from a 1920s record. Compare {{ <track_link source="melissa-laveaux-needle-in-the-hay.md" /> }}, where she reworks a songwriter's song the same way.

The {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }} is the backbone of surf rock, a short guitar figure that repeats and carries a song. The reviews describe Kouzen's guitar as rhythmical and driving rather than notating it, so listen for the figure yourself.

## Artistic Context

Laveaux, born in Montreal in 1985 to Haitian parents, went back to Haiti for the first time since she was twelve and built Radyo Siwèl from traditional and vodou songs. The album looks at the American occupation of Haiti from 1915 to 1934; its title nods to the troubadour orchestras whose songs kept a record of the people's history and resistance. It was recorded in Pantin in April 2017 and released on No Format! in 2018.

## Impact and Reception

Radyo Siwèl was long-listed for the 2018 Polaris Music Prize, and in 2024, for No Format!'s twentieth anniversary, Melanie Charles remixed Kouzen into a jazzy jungle version. Turn it up; it will not take long.
