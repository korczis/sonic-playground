## Genre and Mood

**Azealia Banks** and **Lazy Jay** made **212**, a hip-hop record built on house: Wikipedia files it under hip house, electro house and dance rap. It is provocative and bold, and playful above all; Edward Okulicz of The Singles Jukebox called her playful, sassy, with a dirty mind to match her mouth.

## Song Summary

The title is the area code of Manhattan, where Banks grew up, and the lyric moves between Harlem references and explicit sexual boasts. The music is Lazy Jay's instrumental house track Float My Boat, made by the Belgian brothers Jef and Toon Martens and used as the song's base. Banks's voice does the rest: Erick Bieritz counted four distinct voices in a little over three minutes.

## Musical Breakdown

The song has no conventional verse-and-chorus shape; it moves through three sections.

### Verse 1

The first section pairs rapping with electro beats. Hazel Robinson heard her sound amazing on the rapid-fire rapping at the start.

### Breakdown

Halfway through, the beat recedes and Banks sings, in what Robinson called a taunting, cocky voice.

### Climax

The final section gradually merges into altered synths and goes into a build of shouting. Brad Shoup heard the pings of the beat morph into klaxon roars; Anthony Easton called it air siren aggressiveness.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" text="Sampling" /> }} usually means borrowing a fragment. Here the borrowed material is almost the whole musical base: 212 takes Float My Boat and turns an instrumental into a song by putting a voice on it. Shoup thought that made it sonically little more than a mixtape take on the original, and still understood why it was chosen.

The {{ <concept_link name="breakdown" /> }} at the halfway point is a dance-music device used for a rap song. When the beat thins out, the attention moves from the flow to the singing voice; when the energy returns, as Shoup put it, needling turns into pure menace.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Vincent Tsang and Paul Labonté in Montreal, is black and white: Banks in a Mickey Mouse sweater, dancing and rapping to the camera in front of a brick wall, with the producers Jacques Greene and Lunice in supporting roles. Jonathan Bogart noted how much of it focuses on her mouth.

## Artistic Context

Banks released 212 as a free download on her website in May 2011 and self-released it officially on 6 December 2011. It was the lead single of her 1991 EP of May 2012 and later appeared on her debut album Broke with Expensive Taste in 2014.

## Impact and Reception

Pitchfork named it Best New Track and later placed it sixth among the best songs of the 2010s; The Guardian ranked it second of 2011, and Rolling Stone put it among the 500 greatest songs of all time in 2021. It reached number 12 in the UK and number 7 in Ireland. Play it loud and count the voices.
