## Genre and Mood

**Labrinth** wrote **Mount Everest** as a boast, and it sounds like one: a short song where hip-hop, soul and the blues meet, confident, defiant and bold, though its maker has said something more fragile hides behind the bravado.

## Song Summary

The song opens on its claim: the world's highest mountain and Dubai's tallest tower have nothing on him, because he is on top of the world. Shouted boasts follow, about burning down his house and building it again for fun, and about more money than he knows what to do with. Labrinth has said it is about realising that what he chased was available from the start, a realisation that ends his old self: behind the cracks of the mask, he said, is someone who wishes to be free.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes the arrangement section by section, so this breakdown follows the lyric printed with the official upload.

### Intro

The song starts on the hook itself: no slow build, the boast comes first.

### Main Section

Between the hooks come wordless whoops, then the verse of boasts, each followed by a shouted tell 'em, like a crowd egging the speaker on.

### Outro

The lyric ends where it began, on the claim of standing on top of the world.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is the idea a song is built to stick with you, and here it is a single comparison: a mountain and a skyscraper measured against one man. The rest of the lyric varies that boast. Variety heard the music around it as a dynamic mix of Yeezus-esque hip-hop, Southern blues and classic soul.

It is also a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="sync licensing" /> }}: Variety reported that it played in the second episode of HBO's Euphoria, aired on 23 June 2019, two days after the single came out, and it later joined the soundtrack of the video game UFC 4.

In April 2022, with his score for Euphoria's second season, came a dream-like {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} directed by Tanu Muino, nominated for Best R&B/Soul Video at the UK Music Video Awards. Compare how {{ <track_link source="aloe-blacc-i-need-a-dollar.md" /> }} reached listeners through another HBO series.

## Artistic Context

Labrinth is Timothy Lee McKenzie, born in Hackney, London, in 1989, into a family of musicians whose home was filled with gospel. He produced Tinie Tempah's Pass Out before his debut album Electronic Earth (2012), and he composed the score of Euphoria. Mount Everest came out on 21 June 2019 as a single from his second album, Imagination & the Misfit Kid, released by Syco and RCA that November. He told Variety the song was him doing a Nina Simone slash Screamin' Jay Hawkins track, and that he loves merging different worlds and eras.

## Impact and Reception

Songfacts notes that it entered the UK singles chart 33 months after release, after trending on TikTok; the Official Charts Company lists a peak of number 58 in February 2022 and number 37 in Ireland, and the song carried its album onto charts in Canada, Norway and the US. Like {{ <track_link source="jain-makeba.md" /> }}, it found its second audience years later. Play it loud and hear how little it needs.
