## Genre and Mood

**Class Actress** made **Journal of Ardency** sound like the early 1980s on purpose. It is synth-pop and electro-pop with a new wave chill, the kind of record that critics compared to New Order, the Human League and Depeche Mode. The mood is melancholic and sultry at once, and nostalgic by design: Pop Thruster noted that it was made in 2010 but sounds like a 1980s synth-pop album.

## Song Summary

The title track of the trio's first EP is built on two things, a pulse of synthesizers and a voice. Pop Thruster described the pulsing synth sounds rolling perfectly into Elizabeth Harper's beguiling vocals, and that meeting is the song. Out heard the EP as melancholic yet bouncy, steeped in the gloom of synth-pop's glory days; the title, a journal of ardency, promises a diary of passion. No source describes the song's structure in detail, so the guide below follows what critics heard rather than a map of verses and choruses.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

The synthesizers set the pulse first. Listen for how steady it is, an electronic motion that the voice can lean on.

### Main Section

Harper's voice enters over the pulse. Critics compared her to Debbie Harry of Blondie and to Sarah Cracknell of Saint Etienne.

### Outro

The track runs 3:45. Listen for whether the synths or the voice have the last word.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} is the backbone here, not decoration. Early-1980s electro-pop, the sound The New York Times praised Class Actress for reviving, put synthesizers and programmed rhythm where guitars and drummers had been. On this track the synth pulse carries the rhythm and the mood together, and the voice floats above it.

A {{ <concept_link name="stage persona" /> }} is part of the story too. Harper told Out that the image came before the band, and that the name Class Actress let her be theatrical and fashion-forward. The name was a joke about her first ambition: she had moved from acting to music in Brooklyn. Compare another 1980s-minded record from the same years, {{ <track_link source="la-roux-bulletproof.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Class Actress began as a trio: Harper, a former singer-songwriter, with the producers Mark Richardson, whom she met after he remixed a song from her 2004 solo debut, and Scott Rosenthal. Journal of Ardency came out on 9 February 2010 on Terrible Records, the label run by Chris Taylor of Grizzly Bear, with artwork by Caroline Polachek. Harper named early Madonna, Depeche Mode, house, techno, the Smiths and Morrissey as influences. The debut album Rapprocher followed on Carpark in 2011.

## Impact and Reception

The EP drew praise from The New York Times for its early-1980s revival, and in 2022 Pop Thruster ranked it number 699 among its best 1,000 albums ever, calling it mesmerizing, soothing and gorgeous. Start with the title track, and let the pulse do the work.
