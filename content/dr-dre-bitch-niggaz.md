## Genre and Mood

**Dr. Dre** gives the thirteenth track of his 1999 album **2001** to four voices: his own, Snoop Dogg's, Hittman's and Six-Two's. It is West Coast hip-hop from the album's second half, which Okayplayer placed in the gangsta rap of its moment, with Dre at its centre. Dark, gritty and defiant are the feelings: Peter A. Berry heard vaguely sinister guitar under it and menacing verses from everyone involved.

## Song Summary

Credit on the album's track listing: 13. "Bitch Niggaz" (featuring Snoop Dogg, Hittman and Six-Two), 4:13.

Its writers are Andre Young, Brian Bailey, Melvin Bradford, Calvin Broadus and Craig Longmiles; Dre and Mel-Man produced it, as they produced every track on 2001 but one, and Dre mixed it with Richard "Segal" Huredia. Wikipedia credits a sample of Top Billin', the 1987 single by the American duo Audio Two. The subject, as Edge Induced Cohesion read it, is the hater who acts friendly to your face and hostile behind your back.

## Musical Breakdown

No source times the track or walks through it verse by verse, so this follows what the reviews and the credits say.

### The Beat

Okayplayer heard guitar with a vaguely sinister edge. Somewhere in the production sits the Audio Two sample; no source says where, so listen for it under the guitar and drums.

### The Verses

Four rappers take turns. No source gives their order or describes each verse; Okayplayer heard every one of them as menacing.

### Outro

The track does not end so much as hand over: on the album it runs straight into The Car Bomb, the skit that follows it.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="posse cut" /> }} gives one beat to several rappers, each with a verse of their own. Here the famous sit beside the barely known: Snoop Dogg next to Six-Two, whose only other 2001 appearance is Xxplosive.

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} lets a producer build on an older record. Top Billin' has been sampled or interpolated in more than 300 songs, according to Wikipedia; here a 1987 single feeds a beat made twelve years later.

The {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} credit runs through all of 2001. Hittman is credited on nine of its tracks, as a performer and a writer; compare {{ <track_link source="dr-dre-big-ego-s.md" /> }}, where he and Dre share the beat alone.

## Artistic Context

2001, Dr. Dre's second solo album, came out on 16 November 1999 on Aftermath and Interscope. It debuted at number two on the Billboard 200 with 516,000 copies in its first week, topped the R&B/Hip-Hop albums chart and has been certified six times platinum. Snoop Dogg also appears on its singles Still D.R.E. and The Next Episode; Hittman is among the writers to whom some of the album's lyrics have been attributed.

## Impact and Reception

The track was never a single. Okayplayer ranked it 13th of the album's songs in its 2024 anniversary list; in Soul In Stereo's 1999 Rewind, Edd Bowser put it eighth of 17 and Ronald Grant eleventh. Play it in the album's order, and let it run on into the skit that follows.
