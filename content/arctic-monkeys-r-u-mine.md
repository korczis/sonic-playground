## Genre and Mood

**Arctic Monkeys** reinvented themselves with **R U Mine?**, a slab of alternative rock that swaps the frantic indie rock of their early records for a heavier, slower swagger. It is unmistakably rock, energetic and bold, with a gritty, fuzzed-out tone that owes as much to desert rock and classic riffs as it does to the Sheffield band's own past.

## Song Summary

The song is a jealous, insomniac question fired at someone who will not commit: are you mine or not? Alex Turner delivers it in a torrent of rapid-fire, rhyme-stuffed lines, while the band locks into a muscular riff that stops and starts like a car revving at a red light. Matt Helders' drums hit hard and dry, and the falsetto backing vocals add a strange, almost soulful sheen to the menace. The memorable detail is the riff itself: thick, bending and instantly recognisable within two notes.

## Musical Breakdown

R U Mine? is built on tension and release, slamming between full-band stomp and sudden gaps.

### Intro

A distorted guitar lick bursts in almost before you are ready, quickly joined by the drums in a heavy, lurching groove.

### Verse 1

Turner's vocal comes in fast and conversational, packing images and internal rhymes into every bar while the riff grinds underneath.

### Chorus

The title question arrives with falsetto harmonies floating over the crunch, an odd and effective mix of swagger and vulnerability.

### Bridge

The band drops into a half-time section where the riff stretches out and the lyric turns pleading, a breather before the final charge.

### Outro

Everything slams back together for a last burst of riffing before cutting off abruptly, leaving the question hanging.

## Artistic Context

Arctic Monkeys first released R U Mine? in February 2012 as a standalone single between Suck It and See and the next record. Its weight and groove pointed the way forward, and the band later said it was a springboard for the whole of AM, the 2013 album on Domino that eventually included it. The black-and-white video, filmed largely in the back of a car with the band air-drumming and lip-syncing, won the NME Award for Best Video in 2013.

## Impact and Reception

The single became a fan favourite and a fixture of their live sets, and as part of AM it helped turn Arctic Monkeys into one of the biggest rock bands of the decade. Its video has drawn hundreds of millions of views on YouTube. It is on this playground because it is simply one of the great riffs of the 2010s. Play it loud, preferably in a car, and try not to drum along.
