## Genre and Mood

**The Prodigy**'s **Destroy** is a track the critics could not file in a single drawer. NME called it techno, DIY heard a retro hardcore sound, Metal Hammer a psycho-rave smackdown, and Mixmag humongous breakbeats and ravey synths. They agreed on the feeling: aggressive, energetic and intense, electronic music built for impact.

## Song Summary

Destroy is the fifth of fourteen tracks on The Day Is My Enemy, and it was never released as a single. Liam Howlett wrote and produced it with Zak H Laycock, credited on this track alone. The personnel list names no vocalist, although Sonicabuse heard robotic vocals; no source says whose voice that is or what it says. The embed is the album recording, uploaded by the band.

## Musical Breakdown

No source gives times, but two reviews together give an order: a strange opening, a heavy body, a noisy exit.

### Intro

Sonicabuse heard the track open with a bizarre eight-bit melody, the colour of an old games console.

### Main Section

From there, in Sonicabuse's words, it becomes the spiritual successor to "Breathe", with a gut-punch beat, robotic vocals and heavily distorted synth lines. Consequence of Sound heard its electro morph into dubstep.

### Outro

Consequence of Sound then heard it fade away into some piercing noise, and called that the Prodigy listeners want to be consumed by.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="distortion" /> }} is the deliberate overdriving of a sound until it clips and turns harsh. Sonicabuse placed the distortion of Destroy in its synth lines, not in guitars: listen for synthesizers pushed until they snarl.

A {{ <concept_link name="breakbeat" /> }} is a syncopated drum pattern in the manner of a funk break, the engine of the band's rave records. Mixmag, ranking Destroy 28th of the band's 31 best tracks in 2020, heard humongous breakbeats and proof that the band were as ferocious as ever.

A {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} decides how a track sounds; Howlett usually produces The Prodigy alone. Here Laycock shares the writing and production, as KillSonik do on {{ <track_link source="the-prodigy-wild-frontier.md" /> }}, the next track, whose melody The List also heard as eight-bit computer music. No source says which parts of Destroy are Laycock's.

## Artistic Context

The Day Is My Enemy, the band's sixth album, was recorded over several years and released on 30 March 2015 by Take Me to the Hospital and Cooking Vinyl in the UK. Howlett said that "violent" was the word that kept coming up when he described it. On the CD it follows {{ <track_link source="the-prodigy-ibiza.md" /> }}; on the 2LP edition that Sonicabuse reviewed, it opens side C, ahead of Rhythm Bomb and Beyond the Deathray.

## Impact and Reception

The album debuted at number one in the UK. Destroy drew warm notices: Consequence of Sound named it one of three essential tracks, The List heard it recall the band who shocked and rocked British electronic music into the charts, and Talkhouse, hearing the vibe turn more Jock Jams than action movie, called it a fine song for professional athletics. Classic Rock dissented, filing Destroy and {{ <track_link source="the-prodigy-rok-weiler.md" /> }} among the album's mindless macho stomps with adolescent titles. Play it loud and take a side.
