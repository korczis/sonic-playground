## Genre and Mood

**MBNN** and **Rowald Steyn** made **ilomilo** a deep house cover of a Billie Eilish song, and it sits between the club and the bedroom. The dance pulse is steady, yet the lyric is about loss, so the mood is bittersweet and full of longing rather than euphoric: a sad song you can move to.

## Song Summary

The original "ilomilo" is the eleventh track of Eilish's debut album When We All Fall Asleep, Where Do We Go? (2019), written with her brother Finneas O'Connell, who produced it. Its title comes from the puzzle game Ilomilo, whose aim is to bring two characters, Ilo and Milo, back together, and Eilish described the song as being about losing somebody, or being afraid of losing somebody. MBNN, a Russian producer, and Rowald Steyn, a Dutch one, kept the song and changed its setting: Spinnin' Records says they added guitar chords and a more profound beat and turned it into a tender deep house record.

## Concepts in Focus

ilomilo is a {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} in the strict sense: a new recording of someone else's song. It is also close to a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }}, and Wikipedia's chart listing calls it the Unofficial MBNN Remix. The difference matters: the version first appeared on YouTube at the end of 2019 as a bootleg, and it only became an official release once the vocal was cleared, which Steyn arranged along with its recording.

The {{ <concept_link name="radio edit" /> }} of 3:01 and the extended mix of 4:52 show how one dance record serves two uses. The short version suits playlists and radio; the long one gives a DJ an intro and an outro to mix. Compare how the original's companion track, {{ <track_link source="billie-eilish-bury-a-friend.md" /> }}, uses darkness openly where this cover smooths it.

## Artistic Context

Spinnin' Records released ilomilo on 21 January 2022. The label introduces MBNN as a producer whose music has reached millions of listeners and who plays DJ sets around the world, and Rowald Steyn as the producer of a worldwide charting remix of Wamdue Project's King of My Castle, whose earlier Spinnin' debut was a version of Fairground Attraction's Perfect.

## Impact and Reception

By the time of the official release, the label counted more than 81 million YouTube streams of the bootleg, with peaks on SoundCloud and Shazam. On radio, the version reached number 8 on the TopHit airplay charts in both Russia and Ukraine and appeared on their year-end lists.
