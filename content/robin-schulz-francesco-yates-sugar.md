## Genre and Mood

**Robin Schulz** and **Francesco Yates** turn **Sugar** into a summer-weight blend of deep house, dance and pop. It is upbeat without being frantic, playful in its phrasing and unapologetically feel-good, the kind of track that sounds best with the windows down and the sun low.

## Song Summary

The song is a flirtation set to a steady house pulse. Schulz keeps the production clean and springy: a rounded bassline, crisp claps and bright synth stabs that leave plenty of air around the voice. Yates carries it with a slick, soul-leaning delivery, and the hook interpolates Baby Bash's 2003 hit "Suga Suga", which gives the chorus an instant sense of familiarity even on a first listen. The single version runs three minutes and thirty-nine seconds; the extended mix on the same CD single stretches it to five.

## Musical Breakdown

The arrangement is compact and radio-shaped, built to get to the hook quickly and keep it bouncing.

### Intro

A filtered chord pattern and a light kick ease you in, with a hint of the vocal melody teasing what is coming.

### Verse 1

Yates enters close to the microphone, half-sung and half-crooned, while the bass starts to roll beneath him and the hi-hats tick in.

### Build-up

The energy rises in a short, controlled lift rather than a big festival-style swell.

### Chorus

The sugary hook lands with stacked vocals and a full groove, sweet, simple and hard to forget once it has looped a few times.

### Bridge

The beat thins out for a moment, letting the voice and chords breathe before the groove snaps back into place.

### Outro

The final chorus repeats and then peels away layer by layer, leaving the bass and claps to carry you out.

## Concepts in Focus

Sugar is built on an {{ <concept_link name="interpolation" /> }}. Instead of sampling the recording of Baby Bash's "Suga Suga", the hook re-sings part of it, and so the single's writing credits go beyond Schulz, Yates and the production trio JUNKX to include Nathan Perez, Baby Bash's own name, with Ron Bryant and Francisco Bautista. Listen to the chorus and notice how a borrowed melody can feel like your own memory rather than a quotation.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, released on YouTube on 24 July 2015, plays the song for laughs. The comedian Nathan Barnatt is Officer Finkleman, a rogue policeman who gyrates to the song while driving, with Yates in the car and Schulz on a concert billboard. At the end the officer dresses in Baby Bash's outfit from the "Suga Suga" video, a green T-shirt over a white one, baggy jeans, a chain and a white beanie, so the clip makes the same reference the music does.

Underneath it all is a {{ <concept_link name="four-on-the-floor" /> }} kick, the steady house pulse that lets everything else, bass, claps and voice, move loosely around it. That pulse is what turned Schulz's remixes into radio hits: dance music's engine with pop's lightness on top.

## Artistic Context

Robin Schulz is a German DJ and producer whose 2014 remixes of Mr. Probz's "Waves" and of {{ <track_link source="lilly-wood-and-the-prick-robin-schulz-prayer-in-c-robin-schulz-radio-edit.md" /> }} made his sunny, melodic deep house a European radio staple. Sugar was released in Germany on 17 July 2015 on Tonspiel under licence to Warner, as the second single from his second album of the same name, which followed in September. Francesco Yates, a young Canadian singer with a strong pull toward funk and soul, gave the track its vocal personality.

## Impact and Reception

Sugar became the album's biggest hit. It topped the singles charts in seven countries, including Germany, Austria and Switzerland, where it was Schulz's third number one after "Waves" and "Prayer in C", and reached number one in the Czech Republic, Hungary, Poland and Slovenia. It reached number 21 in the UK, outperforming the lead single "Headlights", and number 44 on the Billboard Hot 100, and it went platinum in the US and five-times platinum in Italy. It earns its place here for the lightness of touch: nothing is overcooked, and the groove never stops smiling.
