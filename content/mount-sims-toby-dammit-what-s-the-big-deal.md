## Genre and Mood

**Mount Sims** and **Toby Dammit** join forces on **What's the Big Deal**, and the result moves away from the glossy club sound of electroclash toward something scuffed and restless. Electronic textures, electro rhythm and a post-punk shrug meet in a mood that is edgy, ironic and raw, the song of someone who has seen the party wind down and is not impressed.

## Song Summary

The title is a question asked with a raised eyebrow, and the track carries that attitude throughout. Mount Sims brings his cool, deadpan delivery and synthetic textures, and the presence of Toby Dammit, a seasoned rock drummer, on the credit hints at the looser, more physical feel. The memorable detail is that tension between machine and hands, a groove that feels programmed and played at once.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

A tight beat and a gritty synth line start the track with no ceremony, a little rough around the edges on purpose.

### Verse 1

The vocal arrives flat and sardonic, more spoken attitude than melody, while the rhythm section keeps nudging forward underneath.

### Chorus

The title line becomes the hook, repeated with a sneer. The arrangement thickens just enough to give it weight without losing the dry humour.

### Bridge

A short break shifts the focus to the drums and the textures, the groove stripped down to its skeleton before it rebuilds.

### Outro

The track ends without a grand gesture, more of a shrug than a finale, which is exactly the point of the question it asks.

## Artistic Context

Mount Sims is the American producer Matt Sims, whose debut *UltraSex* came out on International DeeJay Gigolo Records in 2002. Toby Dammit is the stage name of the drummer Larry Mullins, known for his work with Iggy Pop and Nick Cave and the Bad Seeds. "What's the Big Deal" comes from *Happily Ever After*, released in 2008 on Hungry Eye Records, a compact twelve-track album of under half an hour credited to the pair.

## Impact and Reception

*Happily Ever After* was a low-key release, noticed mostly by followers of Mount Sims' earlier records; one review summed it up with the line "The party's over." That makes this track a fascinating footnote: the sound of an electroclash artist growing out of his scene. It belongs on this playground next to "How We Do" and "Good Service". Listen to all three in a row and hear the change.
