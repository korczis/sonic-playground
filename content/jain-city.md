## Genre and Mood

**Jain** offers an early glimpse of her sound on **City**, a track that blends pop, world music and electro-pop. Its mood is playful, vibrant and quietly introspective, mixing bright energy with a more reflective undercurrent than her better-known hits. It is a short, light song, but it carries more feeling than its size suggests.

## Song Summary

"City" captures Jain's talent for making something homemade sound fresh and full of colour. Her voice, clear and slightly mischievous, sits over a beat that mixes pop simplicity with rhythmic touches from the many places she lived as a child. The production is uncluttered and personal, and the song feels like a snapshot of a young artist finding her own way to mix influences that rarely meet. The arrangement stays light on its feet, with handmade-sounding percussion and simple keyboard parts, and the charm is in the details: a vocal harmony that appears for a single line, a beat that drops out just long enough to make you miss it.

## Musical Breakdown

The song is compact and catchy, its layers adding colour rather than weight.

### Intro

A simple, crisp beat and light percussion open the track, setting a relaxed but bouncy tempo.

### Verse 1

### Chorus

The hook brightens, layered vocals and a fuller beat giving the song its singalong lift. It is the moment the song's bright side shines through most clearly.

### Bridge

A quieter passage gives the song its introspective moment, before the rhythm returns.

### Outro

The beat and voice gently wind down, closing the song as unfussily as it began.

## Artistic Context

Jain is the stage name of French singer-songwriter Jeanne Galice, who spent much of her childhood in Dubai, Congo and Abu Dhabi, experiences that shape the mix of pop and African and Middle Eastern rhythms in her music. "City" first appeared on her 2015 *Hope* EP, alongside the single "Come", and was later added to the deluxe edition of her debut album *Zanaka*. Her early songs were built largely on her own programming and loops, and that do-it-yourself approach gives "City" its intimate, bedroom-studio feel, even as her voice already sounds made for bigger stages.

## Impact and Reception

While "Come" and "Makeba" became her breakthrough hits, "City" is a reminder of how complete her style already was on her first release. It is on this playground as a quieter companion to those hits, showing another side of her songwriting. Listen closely and enjoy the details.
