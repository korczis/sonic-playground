## Genre and Mood

**Urban Species** made **Destructive** out of British hip-hop and the jazz that ran through 1990s London. It sits between hip-hop, acid jazz and trip-hop, soulful and groovy, and the one review that describes it hears it grow from something small into something very big.

## Song Summary

Destructive is the second track of Blanket, the group's second album. The writing is credited to Peter Akinrinlola, known as Mintos, who leads the group, and the production to Raw Deal and Urban Species. Its most memorable detail, in the words of a 2009 review on the blog Useless Randomness, is its shape: the track "starts of small but swells to a wall-of-sound" by the chorus, which a female and male pair sing together. No source we found describes the lyric, so this page leaves it to your ears.

## Artistic Context

Urban Species came from Tottenham in North London. Mintos and DJ Renegade started writing together in 1988, and Talkin' Loud, a Phonogram label, signed them in 1992. Their debut Listen reached number 43 in the UK in 1994, and the group was often linked with acid jazz, mixing hip-hop with reggae, funk, dub and jazz. Blanket came out in continental Europe in late 1998 and in the UK a year later, with guests including Terry Callier, Imogen Heap, Blak Twang and MC Mell'O'. Its copyright line names Mercury Records.

The 2009 review gave Blanket 2.5 out of 5 and found its vocals at times drab, yet called Destructive "amazing" and said it "works really well all the way through". No other review of the song was found. Put it on loud and wait for the chorus.
