## Genre and Mood

**Eurythmics**, that is **Annie Lennox** and **Dave Stewart**, made **Sweet Dreams (Are Made of This)** one of the defining records of early-1980s synth-pop and new wave. It is dark and hypnotic, a cold machine groove under a voice that sounds in control, and it has long since become iconic.

## Song Summary

Lennox wrote the lyric in about half an hour after hearing the track, during an unhappy time after the end of her previous band, the Tourists; she later called it an expression of feeling vulnerable, hopeless and nihilistic, and said it is not about sex. Stewart found it too bleak and added a more uplifting line. The music began as an experiment: Stewart's beat and riff on one new synthesizer, Lennox answering on another, the two parts playing against each other. The yearbook's copy is the album version of Sweet Dreams, 2005 remaster: the same performance, recorded in 1982 and prepared again for the reissue.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

The track opens on a very low drum, a thud rather than a boom in Stewart's words, and that first downbeat was a happy accident.

### Verse 1

The bass sequence and Lennox's string part lock in. Sound On Sound notes that they play completely different things, which is why keyboard players find the song confusing.

### Main Section

Lennox's vocal, recorded on the one microphone the studio owned, carries the hook over the machine groove.

### Bridge

In the middle come the tuned bottles, filled with water until they matched the track's pitch and tapped with wood or metal.

### Outro

No source we found describes the ending, so listen for yourself: do the machines stop, or fade away?

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="drum machine" /> }} here was a rare one: a Movement Systems drum computer, bought for £2,000, one of about thirty made. It supplied the drums and drove the bass line too, triggering a {{ <concept_link name="sequencer" /> }} pattern on a Roland SH-101. Compare how {{ <track_link source="ibiza-deep-summer-sweet-dreams.md" /> }} keeps that sequence intact under a house beat.

{{ <concept_link name="remastering" /> }} prepares an existing recording again for release; it does not change the performance. In 2005 the album was re-released remastered, with six bonus tracks, together with the rest of the Eurythmics studio catalogue.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Chris Ashbrook in January 1983, shows Lennox with cropped orange hair in a man's suit, Stewart playing cello in a field and a boat, and a cow in the boardroom; it became a classic of early MTV, and it is the clip embedded on this page.

## Artistic Context

The album was recorded in 1982, mostly on a Tascam eight-track in the attic of an old warehouse in Chalk Farm, north London, with equipment bought on a £5,000 bank loan. RCA released it in January 1983, and the single, the album's fourth, on 24 January.

## Impact and Reception

The single reached number two in Britain and number one in the United States and Canada. Record Mirror heard a hybrid of electronic blues and soul. It entered the Grammy Hall of Fame in 2020 and the National Recording Registry in 2023. Play it loud and listen for the bottles.
