## Genre and Mood

**Synapson** put **London Ride** fifth on Convergence, the album that critics heard as their own kind of deep house. Discogs files the record as electro and dance-pop, Wikipedia calls the duo electro, nu-disco and deep house, and the reviews add an adjective: organic. The feelings we hear in that description are groovy and relaxed, a dance record that is in no hurry.

## Song Summary

London Ride is one of the few tracks on Convergence without a featured guest: where most of the album pairs the duo with a singer such as Victor Démé, Anna Kova or Bernhoft, this one is credited to Synapson alone. Alexandre Chière and Paul Cucuron composed it, and Qobuz's credits add a lyricist, Edward Vizard, so the track carries words. No source we found says who voices them, whether they are sung or sampled, or what they are about, and no review we found discusses London Ride on its own. What the sources do describe is the album around it, and that is where this review starts.

### The Pace

Qobuz's review hears the duo determined to slow the tempo down in order to magnify melody and groove, a deep house that, in its words, Synapson do not conjugate the way others do.

### The Instruments

La Parisienne Life noted that the album gives a large place to real instruments; Chière plays keyboards and saxophone and Cucuron plays drums and scratches.

### The Length

At 3:41 on the album, London Ride is a song-length track rather than a long club version.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} is the felt quality of a rhythm, how the drums, bass and other parts lock together in time. The reviews of Convergence put it at the centre: by slowing the tempo, Synapson give the groove and the melody room, the opposite of a peak-time track built on speed. Listen for what fills the space between the beats.

{{ <concept_link name="mixing" /> }} is the stage where the recorded parts are balanced into one finished picture: levels, placement, effects. The album credits say Synapson composed, produced and mixed every track themselves, and Florent Sabaton mastered it. Whatever you hear in the balance of London Ride is the duo's own decision.

## Artistic Context

Synapson are Alexandre Chière and Paul Cucuron, childhood friends who formed the duo in Paris in 2009. Convergence, their second album, came out in October 2015 on Parlophone, the Warner Music France label, with thirteen tracks; a deluxe edition in 2016 added five more. Its singles included {{ <track_link source="synapson-anna-kova-all-in-you.md" /> }}, the track just before this one, which reached the French top ten.

## Impact and Reception

Convergence peaked at number 26 in France. Qobuz's reviewer placed Synapson in a new French Touch beside Feder and The Avener. We found no single release and no review of London Ride itself. It belongs here as Synapson without a guest, a track to hear inside the record rather than on its own; play it after All in You.
