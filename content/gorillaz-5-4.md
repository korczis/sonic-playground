## Genre and Mood

**Gorillaz**' **5/4** is the cartoon band at its scruffiest. Q, reviewing the debut in 2001, heard snotty garage rock; Trouser Press grouped it with the album's catchier pop numbers. Both fit: a distorted guitar, a heavy backbeat and Damon Albarn's languid voice, gritty and energetic, with an irreverent shrug.

## Song Summary

"5/4" is the second track of Gorillaz, released by Parlophone on 26 March 2001, and runs 2:42. Albarn wrote it and plays the guitars, synthesizers and bass; Miho Hatori adds vocals, Kid Koala plays turntables and Jason Cox plays and programs the drums. Dan the Automator and Gorillaz produced it. The title, according to Wikipedia, refers to the time signature of the guitar riff that runs through the whole song, and that riff is the detail worth your attention.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

According to Flypaper's analysis, the song opens with a rickety, grizzled electric guitar hammering out hard double stops in a bluesy, flattened key, grouped in fives of eighth notes. With no drums yet, the title describes exactly what you hear.

### Main Section

Then the drums come in, kick on the first and third beats and snare on the second and fourth: a plain bar of four. They are much louder than the guitar, and from here the five-note riff keeps cutting across the bar line instead of marking it. No source maps the verses and choruses; follow the tug between the two layers instead.

### Outro

No source describes the ending, so listen for whether the riff and the beat ever settle on the same downbeat.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="time signature" /> }} says how music is counted. Flypaper's Martin Connor argues that "5/4" is really in 4/4: the drums, louder and steadier, set the meter, while the guitar repeats a grouping of five eighth notes twice per cycle on top. Written in 5/4, he shows, the drummer's part would shift its accents every bar, a much harder thing to play.

The guitar figure is a textbook {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }}, a short repeated pattern that anchors the song; its odd length against an even beat is the lurch you feel without counting.

The song also has a {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} that never got made. Jamie Hewlett storyboarded one before the single was switched; the animatic later appeared on the DVD Phase One: Celebrity Take Down and is on Gorillaz's YouTube channel.

## Artistic Context

Gorillaz began as Albarn and Hewlett's virtual band, and the debut was recorded between 1998 and 2000 at Albarn's Studio 13 in London and in Jamaica. Stereogum later noted that "5/4", like "Re-Hash", is not far from the Britpop of Albarn's band Blur, only rougher.

## Impact and Reception

According to the Gorillaz biography Rise of the Ogre, "5/4" was meant to follow "Clint Eastwood" as a single, until it was swapped at the last minute for {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-19-2000.md" /> }}. It stayed in the band's live set on their first tour and returned on the Humanz Tour with Jehnny Beth singing lead. Put it on, count along, and decide whether you are counting in five or in four.
