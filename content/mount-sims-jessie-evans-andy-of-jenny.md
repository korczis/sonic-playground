## Genre and Mood

**Mount Sims** and **Jessie Evans** team up on **Andy of Jenny**, a compact piece of electroclash that borrows the wiry tension of post-punk and the hooks of synth-pop. It is edgy, playful and a little provocative, a song that winks at you while it struts.

## Song Summary

In just over three minutes the track sketches a tangled little story around its two names, delivered with the deadpan, half-spoken cool that defined the electroclash era. A tight drum machine and buzzing analogue synths keep everything taut, while the two voices trade lines like a pair of conspirators. The memorable detail is that interplay: two personalities sharing one microphone and refusing to settle down.

## Musical Breakdown

Short and to the point, the song wastes no time on preamble.

### Intro

A dry drum-machine pattern and a nagging synth figure kick off immediately, cold and catchy at once.

### Verse 1

The first vocal arrives cool and clipped, half-sung and half-spoken, with the bass pulsing in stiff, robotic eighths.

### Chorus

The second voice joins and the hook snaps into focus, a chantable line that sounds made for a sweaty basement club.

### Bridge

The arrangement briefly drops to its bare skeleton, beat and bass, letting the voices play off each other in the gaps.

### Climax

Synths return at full buzz and the duet turns into a call and response, the most unhinged and fun the song gets.

### Outro

It stops almost as abruptly as it started, leaving a ringing synth note and the feeling you should play it again.

## Artistic Context

Mount Sims is the American electronic artist Matt Sims, a regular presence in the electro and electroclash scene of the 2000s and a collaborator of The Hacker, with whom he made the track Traces. Andy of Jenny appears on his 2008 album Happily Ever After, where it runs a lean 3:20; some listings print the title as Andy or Jenny. Jessie Evans brings her own underground punk and electro sensibility to the duet.

## Impact and Reception

Released when the electroclash wave had already crested, the song never became a club staple, but it captures everything that made the scene fun: attitude, humour and synths that bite. It is here as a cult favourite and a perfect short burst of energy, the kind of song that sounds best squeezed between two longer techno tracks to jolt a room awake. Turn it up and enjoy the swagger.
