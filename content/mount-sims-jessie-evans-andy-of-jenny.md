## Genre and Mood

**Mount Sims** and **Jessie Evans** team up on **Andy of Jenny**, a compact piece of electroclash that borrows the wiry tension of post-punk and the hooks of synth-pop. It is edgy, playful and a little provocative, a song that winks at you while it struts.

## Song Summary

In 3:20 the track sketches a tangled little story around its two names, delivered with the deadpan, half-spoken cool that defined the electroclash era. The album credits add two human touches to the synths: André Lang plays the drums, and Jessie Evans plays saxophone. The memorable detail is that friction between machine-like electronics and live players who refuse to sit still.

## Musical Breakdown

Short and to the point, the song wastes no time on preamble.

### Intro

A dry beat and a nagging synth figure kick off immediately, cold and catchy at once.

### Verse 1

The first vocal arrives cool and clipped, half-sung and half-spoken, with the bass pulsing in stiff, robotic eighths.

### Chorus

The hook snaps into focus, a chantable line that sounds made for a sweaty basement club.

### Bridge

The arrangement briefly drops to its bare skeleton, beat and bass, leaving gaps for the voice to play in.

### Climax

Synths return at full buzz and the saxophone cuts across them, the most unhinged and fun the song gets.

### Outro

It stops almost as abruptly as it started, leaving the feeling you should play it again.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }} was the turn-of-the-millennium scene that fused punk and new wave attitude with cheap synthesizers, drum machines and a deadpan, fashion-conscious delivery. By 2008, when this album came out, the wave had crested, but Mount Sims kept its essentials: cold synths, a sneer, and songs that are over before they outstay their welcome. Hear the same scene from the club side in {{ <track_link source="the-hacker-mount-sims-traces-oxia-remix.md" /> }}, made with the French electro producer The Hacker.

Jessie Evans appears here as a {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}, credited as co-artist on the release. Discogs lists her contribution on this track as saxophone, and she came from the same underground as Mount Sims, as a member of the punk and electro bands Subtonix, The Vanishing and Autonervous. A feature does not have to be a guest verse: here it is an instrument and an attitude.

## Artistic Context

Mount Sims is the American musician Matthew Sims, a composer, singer and producer and a regular presence in the electro and electroclash scene of the 2000s; he wrote, performed and produced Happily Ever After, with Thomas Stern also credited as producer and mixer. The album was released on 28 October 2008 on Hungry Eye Records, and Discogs files it under electro and darkwave. Andy of Jenny is track eight; the album sleeve prints the title as "Andy Or Jenny". Guest drummers recur across the record: André Lang here and on most tracks, Toby Dammit on What's the Big Deal?.

## Impact and Reception

Released when the electroclash wave had already crested, the song never became a club staple, but it captures everything that made the scene fun: attitude, humour and synths that bite. It is here as a cult favourite and a short burst of energy, the kind of song that sounds best squeezed between two longer techno tracks to jolt a room awake. Turn it up and enjoy the swagger.
