## Genre and Mood

**Seelenluft**'s **Horse with No Name** is a cover that takes a dusty folk-rock song to the beach. It is electronic, Balearic and lightly disco, dreamy and relaxed, and its nostalgia is built in: you know the tune before the first verse arrives.

## Song Summary

The song belongs to Dewey Bunnell of America, whose 1971 single topped the Billboard Hot 100 in 1972. Bunnell drew on childhood travels through the deserts of Arizona and New Mexico and called the song a metaphor for a vehicle to get away from life's confusion. Seelenluft keep the ride and change the vehicle. PopMatters heard a polished disco-pop cover, light and airy, a simple but effective Balearic take; Audiodrums heard the classic kept intact with just the right amount of electronics added.

## Musical Breakdown

No source we found times or analyses this recording part by part, so this guide stays with what reviewers describe.

### Intro

Listen for the setting before the song: the airy, Balearic frame PopMatters describes, built for a sunset rather than a desert.

### Verse 1

Florian Horwath, credited as featured artist, sings Bunnell's words. PopMatters calls the album's singer charming, less robust than the British voices he recalls, and that lightness suits the drifting lyric.

### Main Section

Across the album PopMatters heard understated electro and funk basslines shuffling with krautrock-like efficiency. Listen for how that steady motion carries a melody written for acoustic guitars.

### Outro

The cover runs past four minutes. Listen for whether it ends on the voice or lets the groove carry on alone.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} shows what in a song is essential. America's original was compared to Neil Young so often that Bunnell remembered the backlash; Seelenluft move it away from that comparison entirely, into electronic pop, and the melody survives the trip. Compare it with {{ <track_link source="jan-blomqvist-big-jet-plane.md" /> }}, another folk song slowed into a dance track.

The {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} credit matters on a producer's record. Seelenluft is the project of Beat Solèr; the voice belongs to a guest, Florian Horwath, credited on the track itself. The credit tells you who built the music and who sings it.

## Artistic Context

Beat Solèr, born in 1971 in Wil, Switzerland, records as Seelenluft. His best-known track before this one was Manila, from the 2002 album Out of the Woods, sung by twelve-year-old Michael Smith from Compton, which reached the British and Australian charts and topped club charts in France, Germany and Austria. Horse with No Name came out on International DeeJay Gigolo, the Munich label founded by DJ Hell, home of {{ <track_link source="fischerspooner-emerge.md" /> }}: first as a 2007 single, also on that summer's Global Underground Afterhours compilation, then on Birds and Plants and Rocks and Things, which PopMatters called Solèr's fifth full-length.

## Impact and Reception

PopMatters gave the album seven out of ten and picked this cover as the track most likely to get people talking, while wishing Seelenluft another hit. Two years later, Audiodrums filed it under its best of 2010. It is here because it shows how little a great song needs to travel. Play America first, then this.
