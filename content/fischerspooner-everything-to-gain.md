## Genre and Mood

**Fischerspooner** made **Everything To Gain** for Odyssey, the 2005 album which Wikipedia describes as the duo's turn from electroclash towards synth-pop. Its mood is ironic and theatrical: a sales pitch, sung by a duo that is also a performance troupe.

## Song Summary

According to the album's track-by-track press notes, Everything To Gain was the first song written for Odyssey, and a turning point for Warren Fischer: with it he found the touchstone for the album's production, a marriage of the orchestral Mellotron rock of the Moody Blues with Stooges-like proto-punk. The lyric came from a spam email advertising a diet pill, a message the notes call especially well crafted and remarkable in its sheer crassness. Casey Spooner explained the attraction: spam, he said, is a link to the new collective unconscious, a constant, low-grade input of subliminal messages.

## Musical Breakdown

No review describes Everything To Gain on its own, and no source walks through it or gives times; this guide keeps to what the notes and the credits say.

### Intro

No source describes the opening. The notes give the two things to listen for throughout: the orchestral weight of Mellotron-era rock and the blunt drive of proto-punk.

### Main Section

The words are the spam pitch, turned into a song's promises; the title itself sounds like advertising copy. Drowned in Sound heard the whole album as glossy, with that familiar semi-whisper over synthetic beats.

## Concepts in Focus

Everything To Gain is a {{ <concept_link name="deep cut" /> }}: never a single, placed fifth on the album, yet by the notes the song from which the record's sound grew. Album tracks like this are often where an album's ideas show most plainly.

It also shows what a {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} can be beyond the person at the desk. Nicolas Vernhes, described in the notes as Fischerspooner's longtime engineer and collaborator, worked with Fischer in Brooklyn, co-produced the track with the duo and shares the writing credit, as he does on {{ <track_link source="fischerspooner-all-we-are.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Warren Fischer and Casey Spooner spent over two years on Odyssey. The notes describe session musicians in Brooklyn studios, sessions in Los Angeles with Tony Hoffer, and a deliberate search for a rich, warm sound, full of analogue gear and live instruments, against the crisp digital surfaces of their debut #1; Fischer wanted that warm seventies FM sound coming off the radio. Guests included David Byrne, Linda Perry and Susan Sontag, and Mirwais helped with the final touches. Capitol and EMI released the album in April 2005. PopMatters heard the duo leaving the cold digital sound of the debut for the pop tradition of Erasure and the Pet Shop Boys.

## Impact and Reception

Odyssey earned generally favourable reviews and a Metacritic score of 70, but none of the reviews consulted for this page singles out this track. Listen for the diet-pill promises and decide whether they sound sincere.
