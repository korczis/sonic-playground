## Genre and Mood

**Apocalyptica** play **Harvester of Sorrow** as four cellists, with no guitars, no drum kit and no singer. It sits where neoclassical chamber music meets symphonic metal, and it keeps the original's mood: dark, heavy and intense, a slow grind rather than a sprint.

## Song Summary

Harvester of Sorrow was the first single from Metallica's 1988 album ...And Justice for All, written by James Hetfield and Lars Ulrich, with Hetfield's lyric. It follows a man abused as a child who becomes an alcoholic and an addict, turns his anger on his family and, the song implies, finally kills them. Apocalyptica recorded it as the third of eight covers on their 1996 debut, where it runs 6:15 against the 5:45 of Metallica's single. The version on this page is that 1996 recording, remastered for the album's 20th-anniversary edition of 22 July 2016.

### Intro

The song opens on its riff. One reviewer at Metal Music Archives felt that this introductory riff did not quite work on cellos.

### Main Section

As the song goes on, the same reviewer heard each cello take a different guitar line while one followed the vocal melody. Green Man Review described the album's plan in the same terms: four players covering melody, lead guitar, bass line and rhythm, so full that at first it is hard to believe only four cellos are playing.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} can keep every note and change every sound. Here a metal song becomes chamber music, and Teen Ink, which named it a favourite, heard in it how close the two bands' sounds really are.

The song is built on a {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }}, a short figure repeated until it becomes the song's identity. Here it moves from electric guitar to bow and string.

As an {{ <concept_link name="instrumental" /> }}, the cover drops the lyric entirely: a listener who does not know Metallica's words hears the darkness, not the plot.

Finally, {{ <concept_link name="remastering" /> }}: the 2016 edition gave the 1996 recordings a new master. Reflections of Darkness heard the remastered album as cleaner, with its details brought forward. {{ <track_link source="apocalyptica-battery.md" /> }}, added to the same edition, was a new recording instead.

## Artistic Context

Apocalyptica began as four classically trained cellists in Helsinki: Max Lilja, Antero Manninen, Paavo Lötjönen and Eicca Toppinen. After a 1995 show a label employee persuaded them to record their Metallica covers, and Mercury released Plays Metallica by Four Cellos on 10 May 1996, produced by Pekka Ritualuoto. It went platinum in Finland and gold in Germany and Poland.

## Impact and Reception

Opinions on this track split. Green Man Review counted it among the songs that are not quite as moving on cello, while adding that even the weakest song on the album is still fantastic; Teen Ink singled it out as a favourite. Louder, reviewing the 2016 reissue, heard an album of mostly sturdy and imaginative reworkings. Play Metallica's single first, then this, and decide where the sorrow went.
