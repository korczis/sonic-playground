## Genre and Mood

**Ivy Levan** brings **Cadre** along for **Like A Glove**, a sleek blend of pop, R&B and electro-pop with a dark, theatrical streak. The mood is confident and alluring, often provocative, the sound of someone who knows exactly how good the fit is and wants you to notice.

## Song Summary

The title says it all: this is a song about chemistry so tight it feels tailor-made. Levan's smoky, slightly vintage voice slinks over a bass-heavy, minimal beat, and the production leaves plenty of negative space so every purr and syllable lands. The memorable detail is her delivery, playful and dangerous in the same breath, part torch singer and part pop provocateur; Cadre then adds a rap verse that pushes the provocation further.

## Musical Breakdown

At just over three minutes, the track is built for maximum swagger with minimum clutter.

### Intro

A low, throbbing synth bass and sparse percussion set a slinky, nocturnal mood straight away.

### Verse 1

Levan comes in close to the microphone, half-whispered and teasing, the beat leaving her voice almost bare.

### Build-up

The rhythm tightens and the tension rises, a slow strut towards the hook.

### Chorus

The hook opens with her full, brassy voice over a heavier beat, the title line delivered with a knowing grin.

### Bridge

The feature section shifts the texture: Cadre's rap verse adds contrast before Levan takes the spotlight back.

### Climax

The final chorus brings her voice back to the front, the song at its most commanding.

### Outro

The track ends crisply rather than fading, a confident exit.

## Concepts in Focus

The credit "featuring Cadre" makes Like A Glove a lesson in the {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}. A guest verse brings a second voice and often a second style into a song, here rap into a soul-pop track. It can widen a song's reach or change its character; mxdwn's reviewer felt this one tipped the song into crudeness, which shows that a feature is a risk as well as an asset.

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is the title, a single image of a perfect fit. The minimal production is designed around it: with so little else going on, the line and the growl in Levan's voice are what you remember.

## Artistic Context

Ivy Levan was born in Tulsa, Oklahoma, and raised in Bentonville, Arkansas. Her sound, raw soul vocals over synthesizers, has been called "swamp-hop", and mxdwn described No Good as Southern-infused dance music, a growling voice against a sugary pop backdrop. The album, her debut, came out on 7 August 2015 through Cherrytree/Interscope Records and reached number 15 on Billboard's Heatseekers Albums chart; its official singles were Biscuit and Killing You, the latter featuring Sting. Like A Glove sits fifth on the eleven-track record, written by Levan with her producers Patrick Nissley and Lucas Banker. The video linked here is a performance version filmed at the DTS Magic Speaker.

## Impact and Reception

Like A Glove was not a single, and it was not a critics' favourite: mxdwn's review found it less refreshing than the rest of the album and singled out the rap verse as uncomfortable. Heard on its own terms, though, Levan's half of the song is a stylish, slow-burning showcase of her voice and attitude. That is why it earns a place here. Dim the lights and listen to how much she does with so little.
