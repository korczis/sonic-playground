## Genre and Mood

**Lamomali**, the Malian project of **-M-**, brings **Toumani Diabaté** and **Amadou & Mariam** together on **L'Âme Au Mali**, the song that gives the project its name. It is world music rooted in Malian folk tradition, warm and acoustic, and the mood is spiritual, celebratory and uplifting, a love letter to a country and its music.

## Song Summary

The title is wordplay: "Lamomali" sounds like "l'âme au Mali", the soul in Mali, and the song sets out to capture exactly that. Toumani Diabaté's kora provides the shimmering foundation, its cascading, harp-like runs weaving through a gentle, rolling rhythm. Matthieu Chedid's guitar and voice meet the unmistakable voices of Amadou Bagayoko and Mariam Doumbia, and the result feels less like a guest appearance than a gathering of old friends. The memorable detail is the kora, which seems to ripple endlessly under everything else. Nothing is hurried; the song moves with the relaxed confidence of musicians who have nothing to prove to each other, and that ease is a large part of its charm.

## Artistic Context

Lamomali is the 2017 album by Matthieu Chedid, the French rock star known as -M-, born from his long love affair with Malian music. It gathers Toumani Diabaté, one of the world's most celebrated kora players, his son Sidiki Diabaté, the singer Fatoumata Diawara and other Malian musicians. Amadou & Mariam, the blind couple from Bamako famous for "Dimanche à Bamako", join on this title track, linking the project to one of the best-known Malian acts of the century.

## Impact and Reception

The project found a large audience in France, playing major festivals such as Les Vieilles Charrues, and introduced many listeners to the richness of Malian music.
