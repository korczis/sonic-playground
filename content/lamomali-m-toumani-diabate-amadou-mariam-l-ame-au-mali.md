## Genre and Mood

**Lamomali**, the Franco-Malian project of **-M-**, brings **Toumani Diabaté** and **Amadou & Mariam** together on **L'Âme Au Mali**, the song that gives the project its name. It is world music rooted in Malian tradition and French pop, warm and acoustic, and the mood is spiritual, celebratory and uplifting, a love letter to a country and its music.

## Song Summary

The title is wordplay: "Lamomali" is a phonetic contraction of "l'âme au Mali", the soul in Mali, and Matthieu Chedid has described the project as a real utopia, an imaginary country made real, and a celebration of the "anomaly" in our lives. Toumani Diabaté's kora provides the shimmering foundation, its harp-like runs weaving through a gentle, rolling rhythm. Chedid's guitar and voice meet the voices of Amadou Bagayoko and Mariam Doumbia and of the French singer Jain, credited on the release, and the result feels less like a series of guest appearances than a gathering of friends. Chedid is credited as composer. The memorable detail is the kora, which seems to ripple endlessly under everything else.

## Musical Breakdown

The song unfolds like a conversation between voices and strings.

### Intro

The kora opens the track with bright, flowing patterns, immediately evoking the West African tradition it comes from.

### Verse 1

The voices enter warmly over the rolling rhythm, singing with an easy, affectionate lilt.

### Chorus

The title phrase becomes a shared refrain, joyful and simple, carried by several voices at once.

### Bridge

The instruments step forward, kora and guitar trading phrases in a short passage of interplay.

### Outro

The song settles back into its gentle groove and fades with the kora still singing.

## Concepts in Focus

The kora, a 21-string West African harp-lute, is built for the {{ <concept_link name="ostinato" /> }}. Skilled players play two things at once: a repeated accompaniment pattern called kumbengo and improvised solo runs called birimintingo. That is why one kora can sound like a whole band. Listen under the voices for the figure that keeps returning, and above it for the quicker flourishes that never quite repeat; the steady pattern is what lets everything else move freely.

The refrain is a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="vocal harmony" /> }} as community. Several very different voices, Chedid's, Amadou's and Mariam's, and Jain's, sing the title together, and the point is less a polished chord than a crowd of recognisable people joining in. The song's meaning, a shared soul, is acted out by the way it is sung.

## Artistic Context

Lamomali grew out of Chedid's trip to Mali in 2006, where he met the griot and kora master Toumani Diabaté and his son Sidiki Diabaté; the singer Fatoumata Diawara is at the heart of the collective. His bond with Mali began with Amadou & Mariam, the Bamako duo famous for "Dimanche à Bamako", who brought him to the Paris-Bamako festival in 2006 and introduced him to other Malian musicians. The album Lamomali was released on 7 April 2017 by Labo -M- and 3ème Bureau through Wagram, mixed by Philippe Zdar of Cassius; L'Âme Au Mali sits on side B of the vinyl with Amadou & Mariam and Jain as featured artists.

## Impact and Reception

The album won the Victoire de la musique for world music album in 2018. The tour with the Afro Pop Orchestra, from the Festival de Fourvière in Lyon to the Salle Pleyel in Paris, produced the live album Lamomali Airlines, which includes a longer version of this song. Toumani Diabaté died in 2024 during the recording of the second album, Totem, which is dedicated to him, and Amadou Bagayoko died in 2025. "L'Âme Au Mali" captures the project's spirit best: generous, joyful and respectful of the tradition it celebrates.
