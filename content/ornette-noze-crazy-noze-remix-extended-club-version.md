## Genre and Mood

**Ornette** sang **Crazy** as chanson-pop, and **Nôze** remade it for the club. Discogs files the 12-inch under deep house and lounge, Resident Advisor under house. Hypnotic and groovy are the feelings named here, drawn from RA's description of a filtered voice over a throbbing bass rather than quoted from it.

## Song Summary

Crazy (Nôze Remix Extended Club Version) is the long form of the remix. [PIAS] France put it on YouTube in December 2011 with the credit "remix and additional production by Nicolas Sfintescu and Ezechiel Pailhès for NOZE", and Get Physical Music pressed it as the A side of a 12-inch in July 2012, ahead of remixes by Phonique and Lopazz & Casio Casino. It runs six minutes; a 3:50 edit went onto Nova Tunes and Saint-Germain-des-Prés Café compilations. The version on this page is the one Anthony Pappa mixed into the first disc of Darkbeat's 10th Anniversary Collection in 2013, where it runs 6:54 between its neighbours.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

RA heard the remix open with a softly filtered a cappella, Ornette's voice on its own.

### Main Section

Then comes what RA called a robotic throb of a bassline, and the vocals develop well over it: effective, well made, not especially complicated.

### Outro

No source describes how the remix ends. In Pappa's mix it hands over to the Âme remix of Ry Cuming and Frank Wiedemann's Howling.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} can take a pop song somewhere its singer never planned: here, from a French album to a German label's 12-inch and an Australian DJ mix. An {{ <concept_link name="extended mix" /> }} goes further for DJs, giving them the long opening and close they need; the six-minute club version is the one RA reviewed and Pappa chose.

In a {{ <concept_link name="dj mix" /> }} the remix becomes one chapter of a set. Darkbeat's Daniel Banko described Pappa's disc as groovier and slower than his usual sound, music close to his heart, and the track sits there between remixes by Audiofly and Âme. The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} is what makes it work in that company: a robotic throb that a DJ can blend.

## Artistic Context

Ornette is Bettina Kee, a French-Italian singer-songwriter and multi-instrumentalist who began at eighteen playing keys for international artists, made three instrumental albums, acted for Jacques Rivette and took her stage name from Ornette Coleman. Crazy was the title track of her first album as a singer, released by Discograph in 2011; a deluxe edition added the Nôze remix. Nôze, the Paris duo of Ezechiel Pailhès and Nicolas Sfintescu, released their album Dring on Get Physical in 2011, heard in {{ <track_link source="noze-dop-in-the-back-of-my-ship.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

RA called it the most instantly arresting interpretation on the package. Darkbeat, a brand begun by a Melbourne DJ and promoter, put it on its tenth-anniversary set, and an NTS show was still playing it in 2018. Start with the filtered voice and let the bass take over.
