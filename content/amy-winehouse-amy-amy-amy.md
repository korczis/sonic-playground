## Genre and Mood

**Amy Winehouse** recorded **Amy Amy Amy** for Frank, a debut album that mixes jazz, soul, R&B and hip-hop. This song leans furthest toward the jazz club: sultry in its arrangement, and full of longing in what she sings.

## Song Summary

Amy Amy Amy is a song about wanting someone. The Loyola Phoenix hears Winehouse's infatuation with men continuing here, and Far Out Magazine describes it as particularly rich and sexually charged, Winehouse singing of intense longing to the sound of smooth trumpets, like a vintage jazz bar. The title is her own name, three times. uDiscover Music calls the song a cry from the heart, an early sign of the unstinting self-focus that would surface on "Rehab". The streaming credits name Winehouse, Matt Rowe, Felix Howard and Stefan Skarbek as its writers, with Rowe as producer and programmer and Howard on background vocals.

## Musical Breakdown

No review maps Amy Amy Amy section by section, so this page describes only what the sources hear in it and claims no tempo, key or form.

### The Arrangement

The backing is set around trumpets, smooth rather than brassy, which is what makes Far Out think of a vintage jazz bar.

### The Voice

The weight of the song is Winehouse's voice, the rich, smoky instrument critics single out on Frank, carrying the longing of the lyric.

### Outro

On the UK and US CDs the song never quite ends: it is the first part of the album's last track, which runs on into "Outro" and the hidden tracks "Brother" and "Mr Magic (Through the Smoke)".

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="hidden track" /> }} is a recording put on an album without its own listing. Frank's closing CD track lasts 13:14 in the UK and the US: Amy Amy Amy, then "Outro", then two songs the track list does not name, with other hidden songs on several other editions. Some digital editions split that medley into four separate tracks, which is why the song you can stream stands alone at about four minutes.

Matt Rowe's credits show what a {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} can be in R&B and pop: on this song he is producer, programming engineer and co-writer at once, shaping the instrumental as well as the recording. Frank had several producers, Salaam Remi, Commissioner Gordon and Jimmy Hogarth among them, and the UK and US editions credit the closing medley to Rowe and Remi.

## Artistic Context

Island released Frank in the UK on 20 October 2003, recorded between 2002 and 2003 in studios in Miami, New Jersey, New York and London. Winehouse was 19. She was not entirely happy with the result; in 2004 she told The Observer that she had never heard the album from start to finish. Amy Amy Amy was never a single.

## Impact and Reception

Frank was not a sensation: it took four months to reach number 13 in the UK, and only reached number 3 after Winehouse's death in 2011. It was shortlisted for the Mercury Prize in 2004 and has since sold over a million copies in the UK. No critic writes about this song at length. Play it to the end, and let the CD keep going.
