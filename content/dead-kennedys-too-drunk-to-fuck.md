## Genre and Mood

**Dead Kennedys** deliver a punchy and rebellious sound in **"Too Drunk to Fuck,"** a track that blends punk rock speed with biting satire. It is a quintessential example of the San Francisco band's habit of attacking a subject by impersonating it, with humour and sarcasm rather than a sermon.

## Song Summary

Jello Biafra's lyric depicts an outrageous, moronic party from the inside, sung by a character who is having the worst possible night and boasting about it. The music is East Bay Ray's heavy surf and garage riff, which the comedian Bill Bailey, who later covered the song, called "a very difficult riff to play… very, very fast". The joke is the gap between the bravado and the state of the singer.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro: Instant Impact

The track kicks off with the fast, surf-flavoured guitar riff, setting the tempo and the tone right away.

### Build-up: Rising Energy

The tempo stays high as Klaus Flouride's bass and Ted's drums lock in behind the riff. Biafra enters with his quivering, exaggerated delivery, already playing a character.

### Main Section: Catchy Chorus

The chorus is the title, shouted as a refrain. It is a sing-along by design, which is part of the satire: the crowd chanting it becomes the party being mocked.

### Climax: Full Throttle

The band push through the last choruses at full speed, the riff never letting up.

### Outro: Abrupt End

The song ends with the sound of a man vomiting, an abrupt and very punk way to close the party.

## Concepts in Focus

The song is driven by a {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }}, a short guitar figure repeated as the backbone of the track. East Bay Ray's riff comes from surf rock, the twangy, fast-picked guitar music of early-1960s California, played with punk speed and weight. That borrowing is typical of Dead Kennedys and part of the joke: the sunny sound of beach parties under a lyric about a party gone wrong.

Its afterlife makes it a good case study in the {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }}. In 2004 the French project Nouvelle Vague recorded it as gentle bossa nova, sung by Camille, and the comedian Bill Bailey covered it with his band Beergut 100. Listen to {{ <track_link source="nouvelle-vague-too-drunk-to-fuck.md" /> }} right after this one: the words are the same, yet without the riff's aggression the satire sounds colder and stranger.

## Artistic Context

**Dead Kennedys** formed in San Francisco in 1978 and became one of the defining American hardcore punk bands, known for Biafra's provocative, satirical lyrics. Too Drunk to Fuck was their fourth single, released in May 1981 on Cherry Red Records with "The Prey" on the B-side and produced by Geza X, with Biafra on vocals, East Bay Ray on guitar, Klaus Flouride on bass and backing vocals and Ted on drums. Both sides later appeared on the 1987 rarities album Give Me Convenience or Give Me Death.

## Impact and Reception

The BBC banned the single from Radio 1, chart listings shortened it to "Too Drunk To", and some shops refused to stock it, yet it reached number 36 on the UK Singles Chart and topped the UK Independent chart, the first UK top-40 single with the word in its title. Years later it caused a different fight: after a court awarded the band's catalogue to the other members, they licensed the song for the 2007 film Planet Terror, where Nouvelle Vague's cover plays in a rape scene, and Biafra criticised them for it. It remains a classic punk anthem, as funny and as uncomfortable as it was in 1981.
