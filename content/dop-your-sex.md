## Genre and Mood

**dOP** made **Your Sex** as deep house with a tech house edge, and the shop notes that came with the record call it deep and visceral, a dark, broody and characteristically cheeky affair. That pairing is the mood: dark and sultry in sound, playful in attitude.

## Song Summary

Your Sex is the title track of a three-track twelve-inch that the Paris trio released in June 2011 on Guy Gerber's label Supplement Facts (SFR029). The record carries the original, a second track called Half Naked and a remix of the title track by Paul Ritch; the digital edition adds a fourth, Art Department's Shades of Gotica Remix. The original runs 6:49, and the label's description is the fullest account of how it is built.

## Musical Breakdown

No review maps Your Sex bar by bar, so this walk-through keeps to what the release notes describe.

### Intro

The track opens with very little: a stripped-back beat and a vocal phrase on a loop. Listen for how long those two carry it alone.

### Build-up

The bass does the work: the notes describe the low end building the track up to a crescendo, so the change happens underneath you.

### Climax

At the top of the build come what the label calls deep acid frequencies, which it hears as a nod to the techno elite given a new depth and sensuality.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="loop" /> }} is the track's first idea. A short vocal phrase repeated over a bare beat becomes a rhythm in itself; the words matter less than the way they return.

The {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }} here comes from the bottom. Many dance tracks build with rising synths and drum rolls toward a drop; Your Sex lets the bass swell toward a crescendo, a slower, more physical tension.

Every version on the record is also a lesson in the {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }}. Paul Ritch, the label says, lifts the throbbing bass of the original into a fluttering, high-energy tool for the dance floor.

## Artistic Context

dOP are three childhood friends, Damien Vandesande, Clément Aichelbaum and Jonathan Illel; the release notes trace them back to a rock and roll cover band formed in 1994 for France's Fête de la Musique and a move into electronic music in 2005, and Wikipedia dates dOP itself to 2006. Their debut album, Greatest Hits, came out on Circus Company in 2010, and Resident Advisor named them among its top live acts in 2010, 2011 and 2012. XLR8R, previewing Half Naked from the same record, described the trio's sound as deeply subtle and playfully coy techno. Hear where they went next on {{ <track_link source="dop-close-up.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Your Sex reached the clubs it was made for quickly: Lee Curtiss included it in his Watergate 08 mix, released by Watergate Records on 4 July 2011. Play the original from the looped voice to the acid peak, then the Paul Ritch remix, and listen to how much the same bass can change.
