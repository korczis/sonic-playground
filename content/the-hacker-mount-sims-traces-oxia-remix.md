## Genre and Mood

**The Hacker** and **Mount Sims** wrote **Traces** as a piece of cold, wave-tinged electro; the **Oxia Remix** pulls it into techno and minimal territory. It is hypnotic and melancholic, a dark record that never rushes, and it carries the gloom of the original into a long, rolling groove built for the dancefloor rather than the bedroom.

## Song Summary

The original pairs The Hacker's synths with Mount Sims' deadpan vocal, with lyrics by Sims and backing vocals by Echo Danon, and its title says it all: it is about what lingers after something is gone. Oxia keeps that mood but rebuilds the track around a steady kick and a slowly evolving synth line, letting the vocal appear in shadows. The memorable detail is the patience of the build: the remix keeps tightening the screw for minutes before it finally opens up.

## Musical Breakdown

This is a DJ's version, structured in long, gradual phases.

### Intro

A dry kick and ticking hats arrive first, clean and functional, with a low synth hum hinting at the darkness to come.

### Build-up

A brooding bassline joins and the percussion multiplies, each phrase adding one more small detail to the machine.

### Main Section

The central synth riff emerges, cold and slightly mournful, looping hypnotically while filters slowly open around it.

### Bridge

The drums pull back and fragments of Mount Sims' voice drift through the space, a moment of eerie stillness in the middle of the drive.

### Climax

The kick slams back in with the riff at full brightness, the most euphoric the remix allows itself to be while staying shadowy.

### Outro

Layers are removed in reverse order until only the rhythm remains, ready to be mixed into the next record.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} can change what a record is for. The original Traces is an electro song built around Mount Sims' voice; Oxia keeps fragments of that voice and turns the track into six and a half minutes of techno, organised around a steady kick and a slowly evolving riff so that a DJ can play it in a set. The same twelve-inch carries Dexter's sparser version of Traces, a useful comparison: two remixers, one source, two very different tracks.

The remix is built on a long {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }}. Instead of announcing its hook, it adds one element at a time, kick, hats, bass, percussion, riff, and keeps tightening for minutes before it opens. The Skinny, reviewing the package in 2006, singled out exactly that: a well-structured build-up that suits the earlier part of a night, when a DJ is raising the energy rather than spending it.

## Artistic Context

The Hacker is the Grenoble producer Michel Amato, a key figure of French electro best known for his records with Miss Kittin, among them "Frank Sinatra"; in 1998 he founded the label Goodlife Records with his friends Oxia and Alex Reynaud. Traces, with lyrics and lead vocals by Mount Sims, appeared on his second solo album Rêves Mécaniques (2004), his first to enter the French albums chart. The Oxia remix followed on a 2005 twelve-inch on Different, alongside a Dexter remix of the same track and a Black Strobe remix of Flesh & Bone, and in 2006 Oxia included it on his mix CD Picture of Now. Oxia, also from Grenoble, later became widely known for his own anthem Domino.

## Impact and Reception

When the remix package came out, The Skinny was mostly unimpressed, finding the remixes short of the album, but singled out the Oxia version as the strongest of the set, praising its build-up and its usefulness earlier in a night. It has aged into a quiet classic of mid-2000s electro-techno crossover. Play it loud, late, and let the riff work on you.
