## Genre and Mood

**The Hacker** and **Mount Sims** wrote **Traces** as a piece of cold, wave-tinged electro; the **Oxia Remix** pulls it into techno and minimal territory. It is hypnotic and melancholic, a dark record that never rushes, and it carries the gloom of the original into a long, rolling groove built for the dancefloor rather than the bedroom.

## Song Summary

The original pairs The Hacker's analogue synths with Mount Sims' deadpan vocal, and its title says it all: it is about what lingers after something is gone. Oxia keeps that mood but rebuilds the track around a steady kick and a slowly evolving synth line, letting the vocal appear in shadows. The memorable detail is the patience of the build: the remix keeps tightening the screw for minutes before it finally opens up.

## Musical Breakdown

This is a DJ's version, structured in long, gradual phases.

### Intro

A dry kick and ticking hats arrive first, clean and functional, with a low synth hum hinting at the darkness to come.

### Build-up

A brooding bassline joins and the percussion multiplies, each eight bars adding one more small detail to the machine.

### Main Section

The central synth riff emerges, cold and slightly mournful, looping hypnotically while filters slowly open around it.

### Bridge

The drums pull back and fragments of Mount Sims' voice drift through the space, a moment of eerie stillness in the middle of the drive.

### Climax

The kick slams back in with the riff at full brightness, the most euphoric the remix allows itself to be while staying shadowy.

### Outro

Layers are removed in reverse order until only the rhythm remains, ready to be mixed into the next record.

## Artistic Context

The Hacker is the Grenoble producer Michel Amato, a key figure of French electro best known for his records with Miss Kittin. Traces, written with Mount Sims, appeared on his solo album Rêves Mécaniques (2004) on Different. The Oxia remix followed on a 2005 twelve-inch alongside a Dexter remix of the same track and a Blackstrobe remix of Flesh & Bone. Oxia, also from Grenoble, later became widely known for his own anthem Domino.

## Impact and Reception

When the remix package came out, The Skinny singled out the Oxia version as the most interesting of the set, praising its nicely structured build-up and its usefulness earlier in a night. It has aged into a quiet classic of mid-2000s electro-techno crossover. Play it loud, late, and let the riff work on you.
