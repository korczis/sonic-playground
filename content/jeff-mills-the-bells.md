## Genre and Mood

**Jeff Mills** made **The Bells** with almost nothing: a drum machine, a dissonant two-note figure, a bell melody and a bassline. It is techno in the Detroit tradition Mills helped found, hypnotic in its repetition and euphoric when the bells arrive. Mixmag described a distorting kick that punches you in the gut and a spiralling riff that producers have been trying to emulate ever since.

## Song Summary

The version here is the original, first released on Purpose Maker's Kat Moda EP; Discogs lists the EP as the source of this track on Sight Sound and Space. Accounts of its making differ: Resident Advisor says it came together in about an hour just after Mills moved to Chicago in the summer of 1993, XLR8R dates it to 1994. Mills played it out for two years before releasing it, at first from a custom 13-inch record.

## Musical Breakdown

Attack Magazine's breakdown counts the track in bars rather than minutes, at about 137.5 beats per minute.

### Intro

A distorted TR-909 kick opens in four-to-the-floor under a back-and-forth figure in A minor. After eight bars an open hi-hat enters on the offbeat, then rides, then claps set back behind a delay.

### The Bells

Eight bars after the section strips back to the notes, the bell melody arrives in two synth layers, the lower one brought in and out. Then the lower bell drops out and the high one rolls on alone.

### Bassline and Outro

Almost two minutes in, a sixteenth-note bassline fades in. The bell motif plays its last eight bars and is never heard again; the bassline runs alone, then the kick and percussion return and the track fades.

## Concepts in Focus

The four notes of the bells are a {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} that Resident Advisor called the most identifiable melody in techno, and the track rations them: they play for part of the record and then disappear for good.

The Bells belongs to {{ <concept_link name="club culture" /> }} more than to any album. Mills told XLR8R it was something he could use to say hello to the people, and in 2005 he performed it with the Montpellier Philharmonic Orchestra.

Sight Sound and Space is a {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }} curated by the artist: three discs, released by Axis on 4 October 2019, with a booklet in which Mills comments on each track. The Bells is the second track of the second disc, Sound. Compare its bassline with {{ <track_link source="donna-summer-i-feel-love.md" /> }}, which Attack Magazine hears behind it.

## Artistic Context

Mills, born in Detroit in 1963, was a founding member of Underground Resistance, and in 1992 founded Axis Records, later adding the sub-label Purpose Maker. Purpose Maker issued the Kat Moda EP in 1997, though XLR8R places the first release in spring 1996.

## Impact and Reception

Purpose Maker reissued The Bells for its tenth anniversary in 2006, its centre label calling it probably the most played techno track in history. Resident Advisor called Sight Sound and Space a functional manual for a sometimes bewildering archive and noted that The Bells is naturally there. Wait for the bells.
