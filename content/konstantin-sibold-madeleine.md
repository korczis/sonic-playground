## Genre and Mood

**Konstantin Sibold** built **Madeleine** for the long middle of a night: nine minutes of house at 121 beats per minute, in F major, with deep house patience. Its mood is hypnotic and a little dreamy, the feeling of a groove that keeps going while the room forgets the time.

## Song Summary

Madeleine is a club track first and a record second. It opened side A of Innervisions' Secret Weapons Part 5, a twelve-inch of four tracks by four producers, released in February 2013. The series name says what such tracks are for: records DJs carry because they work. Telekom Electronic Beats later called it his most acclaimed contribution to the Berlin label, so obviously crafted for the imprint that it is hard to imagine it anywhere else.

## Musical Breakdown

At nine minutes and one second the track is long enough to be heard as a slow arc rather than a song.

### Intro

The groove is set out at a tempo slightly below peak time, with all nine minutes still ahead and nothing in a hurry.

### Build-up

Listen for how the track adds and shifts its elements over the steady beat; in a track this long, small changes do the work that a chorus does in a song.

### Main Section

Listen for the melodic material over the groove; that is what a radio edit has to reach much sooner.

### Outro

Listen to how the original ends, and compare it with the radio edit, which has to stop more than four minutes sooner.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="house groove" /> }} at 121 beats per minute is steady enough to dance to for hours and slow enough to feel unhurried. Much of the hypnotic effect comes from that steadiness, held for nine minutes.

In 2022 Sibold made a {{ <concept_link name="radio edit" /> }}, four minutes and eleven seconds against the original nine, and Innervisions released both together. The label called it a new suit for the classic. Comparing the two shows what a DJ track does with its extra minutes.

The {{ <concept_link name="dj mix" /> }} came first. Madeleine appears in the tracklist of Dixon and Âme's Boiler Room set in Amsterdam on 25 October 2012, months before the record came out. That is how a secret weapon works: heard in a mix long before it can be bought.

## Artistic Context

Konstantin Sibold is a DJ and producer based in Stuttgart, a former resident of the club Rocker 33, where he started the Common Sense People party series with Leif Mueller. Groove named him, with Jon Charnis, as one of two little-known producers on an EP compiled by Âme and Dixon that also carried Culoe De Song and Mario Basanov. His later records appeared on Cocoon, Running Back and Afterlife. Its long patience recalls {{ <track_link source="marek-hemmann-gemini.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Groove was clear: the winner of this EP is Konstantin Sibold with Madeleine. Innervisions returned to it almost ten years later, calling it a classic, and in 2025 the original appeared on Armada's compilation Timeless. Like {{ <track_link source="sante-richard-judge-awake.md" /> }}, it outlived its first release. Give it all nine minutes.
