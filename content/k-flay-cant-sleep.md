## Genre and Mood

**K.Flay**'s **Can't Sleep** comes from a debut album that Wikipedia files under hip-hop and indie rock and that Entertainment Weekly heard as spaced-out rap beats paired with chiming indie rock. The feelings here, raw, hypnotic and intense, follow the few words written about the song itself: Vice heard raw lyrics and a mesmeric vocal flow.

## Song Summary

Can't Sleep is the third track of Life as a Dog, the album K.Flay made independently after her years at RCA, a period she has called an ill-advised marriage. Like everything on the record, it was written by Kristine Flaherty; she produced it with Lincoln Jesser and David Singer-Vine. It runs a little over four minutes. ## Concepts in Focus

Life as a Dog is a {{ <concept_link name="self-release" /> }} in its modern, crowdfunded form. K.Flay sold pre-orders through PledgeMusic, reaching 196 percent of her goal, sent the album to those backers on 10 June 2014 and released it digitally on 24 June on the label Bummer Picnic. It still reached number 2 on Billboard's Heatseekers Albums chart.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} came with the single release on 16 May 2015. Directed by Emily Ibarra and starring Jason Paul, it was premiered by Billboard as K.Flay taking on Bangkok.

{{ <concept_link name="sync licensing" /> }} gave the song a second life. In James Gunn's The Suicide Squad (2021), it plays as the team walks into a nightclub to capture The Thinker, and it is the seventh track of the film's soundtrack album.

## Artistic Context

K.Flay is Kristine Flaherty, an American artist who mixes rap with indie rock and electronic production. Life as a Dog, her first studio album after the self-released EP of {{ <track_link source="k-flay-acetaminophen.md" /> }}, preceded her signing to Night Street, where {{ <track_link source="k-flay-high-enough.md" /> }} became her best-known song.

## Impact and Reception

Can't Sleep was the album's fourth single. Screen Rant, ranking the film's songs, called it a nice addition to the soundtrack that adds little to the scene itself. Listen to it late, when the title is true.
