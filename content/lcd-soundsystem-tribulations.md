## Genre and Mood

**LCD Soundsystem**'s **Tribulations** is electro-pop with a dance-punk band's drive and a synth-pop memory: energetic on the surface, bittersweet underneath. Critics heard both layers.

## Song Summary

James Murphy wrote it, and The DFA produced it. Tom Breihan of Stereogum writes that some of the album's songs started as jokes and that Murphy likes to tell how this one was written to show a friend how easy it was to write a pop song; Breihan's answer is that if a merciless synth-stomp rager like this were that easy, more people would make one. Matt Cibula of PopMatters heard naked emotion and heartbreak beneath its lovely, shiny disco thump.

## Concepts in Focus

The video on this page is itself an edit. The single's CD and 7-inch led with a {{ <concept_link name="radio edit" /> }} of 3:52, and the official clip runs about four minutes, where the album version lasts 4:59 and the 12-inch's album version 5:01.

The single then gave the song to other producers. Each {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} moves it somewhere else: Tiga's Out of the Trance Closet Mix (6:02), the Lindstrom Mix (7:56) and a Shallow Version (4:45) on the 7-inch.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, first aired in August 2005, was directed by Dougal Wilson and produced by Colonel Blimp. Murphy walks through a series of screens, sometimes stepping out of one into the real setting only to walk into the next.

## Artistic Context

Tribulations is the third track of LCD Soundsystem's debut album, recorded between 2002 and 2004 and released on 24 January 2005 by DFA and Capitol in the United States and EMI elsewhere. The DFA, the production team of Murphy and Tim Goldsworthy, produced the album. The single followed on 27 September 2005, after Movement, Daft Punk Is Playing at My House and Disco Infiltrator.

## Impact and Reception

The album scored 86 on Metacritic and was nominated for the Grammy for Best Electronic/Dance Album; Vice later called the song an electro-pop classic. Stereogum's Ryan Leas counted it among the record's standouts and remembered it as an entirely different animal live by 2011. It became the opening theme of the radio show Sound Opinions and was used in The Office episode Night Out and the games Project Gotham Racing 3 and Driver: Parallel Lines.
