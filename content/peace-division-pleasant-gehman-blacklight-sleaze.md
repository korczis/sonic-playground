## Genre and Mood

**Peace Division** made **Blacklight Sleaze** with **Pleasant Gehman** as a house record in minimal clothing, built around a voice that talks rather than sings. Resident Advisor heard dark, minimal production and called the track a gnarly ode to the stripping underworld. The feelings follow from that: dark in the sound, gritty and provocative in a subject that dance music rarely puts into words.

## Song Summary

Gehman, an American writer, delivers a spoken monologue about what Resident Advisor called the leery, everyday truths of life as an exotic dancer. Underneath, the production stays spare: moody synths, hard kicks and odd electronic blips. The review praised above all the fit between the two: rarely in dance music has the union of music and voice been so symbiotic. No source publishes the text, so this page does not paraphrase it further.

### Main Section

What the sources describe runs through the whole track: the voice in front, telling its story, and around it a dark, minimal bed of synths, blips and kicks. Listen for how little the music does while the voice speaks, and for what changes when it stops.

## Concepts in Focus

Blacklight Sleaze is a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="spoken word" /> }} over a beat. A sung hook asks you to join in; a spoken monologue asks you to listen, and here it tells you exactly where the night takes place. Compare {{ <track_link source="chuck-roberts-in-the-beginning.md" /> }}, where the voice of house music's best-known sermon does the same work.

The {{ <concept_link name="kick drum" /> }} is what keeps a talking record on the dance floor. Resident Advisor names hard kicks as one of the few elements of the production, and in a track this sparse the weight of each one counts.

Then the {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }}. A month after the single, Radio Slave's vocal and dub mixes came out; in 2012 the label Dogmatik released new remixes by Dyed Soundorom and Gerd. Resident Advisor judged them good but not better than the original, which by then it called a well-worn classic.

## Artistic Context

Peace Division are a British house act whose releases go back to the 1990s; MusicBrainz lists Clive Henry, Justin Drake and Marvin Beaver among its members. The single came out on 20 February 2006 on NRK Sound Division, with Hear I Am beside it. Gehman sang in the punk band Screamin' Sirens in the 1980s, has written books including The Belly Dance Handbook, and is also known as Princess Farhana.

## Impact and Reception

No chart placing is recorded, but the track kept being reworked: by Radio Slave in 2006 and by two more remixers six years later, when Resident Advisor already called it a classic. Play it late, and listen to the voice as closely as to the beat.
