## Genre and Mood

**Rebeka Warrior** and **Marlène Saldana**'s **La valise** comes out of a theatre: Rebeka Warrior's music for Showgirl, a stage show its authors call a techno oratorio in song and dance, so techno, soundtrack and electronic music are its frame. The feelings named here, theatrical and provocative, are the show's: a retelling of Paul Verhoeven's notorious Showgirls (1995) that plays with camp, good and bad taste, and laughter.

## Song Summary

La valise is the second of six tracks on Showgirl, the play (Original Soundtrack Recording), released on 22 October 2021 on Warriorecords; like the opener, Le message, it features Saldana. Rebeka Warrior composed and produced it; Krikor mixed it, Tony Chauvin mastered it. The distributor names Julia Lanoë, Rebeka Warrior's own name, as composer and lyricist; the show's dossier says its lyrics were written by Saldana, Jonathan Drillet and Lanoë, some songs by her, others by the two authors. No source prints or describes the lyric of La valise, so this page does not paraphrase it. In Verhoeven's film the dancer Nomi is robbed by the man who drives her to Las Vegas and later demands her suitcase back; whether the song points to that, no source says.

## Musical Breakdown

No review describes how La valise is built, so the breakdown keeps to the credits and the show's makers.

### Main Section

The voice is Saldana's, the show's co-author, who plays many of its characters on stage; the music around her is Rebeka Warrior's. The dossier describes the whole score as working like a kind of bolero in the manner of Giorgio Moroder, the best clue the sources give: notice whether this song, too, grows by insistence.

### Music Video

The official video, published in January 2022, was directed by Saldana and Drillet with the show's own team: Sophie Perez's scenography, Jean Biche's costumes, hair and make-up. It runs 4:20, four seconds longer than the album track.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="soundtrack" /> }} usually means film music; this one serves a stage show about a film. The dossier says Rebeka Warrior takes over from David Stewart of Eurythmics, who scored Showgirls in 1995; hear where Stewart came from in {{ <track_link source="eurythmics-annie-lennox-dave-stewart-sweet-dreams-are-made-of-this-2005-remaster.md" /> }}.

The {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} credit, feat. Marlène Saldana, is here a stage role carried onto a record: the guest is the show's co-author and the voice of many of its characters.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} closes the circle, turning a theatre song about a film back into a screen image.

## Artistic Context

Rebeka Warrior is Julia Lanoë, singer of Sexy Sushi, Kompromat and Mansfield.TYA; the dossier quotes JD Beauvallet calling her music electroclash or techno-punk, with raw texts close to anarchism and dadaism. Her label Warriorecords calls itself queer, transfeminist and anti-racist. Saldana, born in Lyon in 1978, won the French theatre critics' prize in 2019 for playing Jacques Demy in Christophe Honoré's Les Idoles. Showgirl premiered in 2021 at the Théâtre Saint-Gervais in Geneva, during the festival La Bâtie.

## Impact and Reception

No review of the song itself turned up, but the show travelled: through France, Belgium and Switzerland, to Chaillot in Paris in 2023, to NYU Skirball in New York in 2024, and to the Carreau du Temple in Paris in 2026, whose listing called Rebeka Warrior an icon of the queer scene.
