## Genre and Mood

**Daft Punk** opened their run of collaborator portraits with the elder statesman of *Random Access Memories*, and **Episode 1, Giorgio Moroder** is eight minutes of a disco and electronic pioneer thinking aloud. It is spoken word, with no song in it, and the mood is inspirational, nostalgic and reflective at once: a producer looking back while impatient for what comes next.

## Song Summary

The Creators Project, the arts platform Intel ran with Vice, published the film on 3 April 2013, weeks before the album, as the first of The Collaborators, a series directed by the cinematographer Ed Lachman. Moroder talks about the Moog, about dance tempo and the body, about a packed Ibiza dance floor and about his work on I Feel Love. Then he turns to the two Frenchmen and calls them perfectionists, and means it as praise. The best anecdote comes from the studio: three microphones set up for his voice, one from the sixties, one from the seventies and one from the present, and his objection that nobody would ever hear the difference.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

The film starts on an argument rather than a biography. Moroder says the dance world needs something new, and the rest of the film asks what that could be.

### Main Section

The middle is his history in miniature, chapter by chapter: the first synthesizer, then rhythm, then the DJ, each remembered as a practical problem and its solution.

### Bridge

At 3:47 the subject becomes Daft Punk. He sets their weeks of searching for one sound against his own sessions, which took a few hours, and admits he is no details person, though the duo's way was needed.

### Outro

From 5:09 he weighs old methods against new and closes on the future of dance, more curious than wistful.

## Concepts in Focus

This is {{ <concept_link name="spoken word" /> }} at its plainest, a story with no melody, best heard next to {{ <track_link source="daft-punk-giorgio-by-moroder.md" /> }}. There the duo cut his memories into a track that follows the narration, so that when he says he knew he needed a click, a click track starts. Here the same voice runs free of any beat.

The {{ <concept_link name="analog synthesizer" /> }} is the subject of the second chapter (0:45). Moroder remembers discovering the Moog and using it for the pulsing backgrounds of early disco; later (5:09) he grants that digital is easier while backing the duo's refusal just to push notes and chords.

Then comes {{ <concept_link name="club culture" /> }} (3:07): about five thousand people in Ibiza and a DJ leading them like a conductor. For Moroder, dance music had become the world's pop.

## Artistic Context

*Random Access Memories* came out on Columbia in May 2013, and that month the films also appeared as an audio release of eight episodes. Moroder, the Italo-disco pioneer known for his records with Donna Summer, recorded his monologue for the album over two days, and the engineers chose each microphone to stand for a different decade of his life.

## Impact and Reception

Stereogum and Dancing Astronaut covered the episode the day it went up, Dancing Astronaut quoting the line that sums it up: "It's time to have something new in the dance world." In June 2023 Daft Punk released the films again on their own channel for the album's tenth anniversary. Watch it, then play Giorgio by Moroder and hear the voice again.
