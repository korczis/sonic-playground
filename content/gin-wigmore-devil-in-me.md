## Genre and Mood

**Gin Wigmore** sings **Devil In Me** as rock with blues in its veins, and a Sputnikmusic reviewer heard a polished surf-rockabilly sheen on it. It is a song about being left for someone else that sounds as if it rode in from a western. The mood is heartbroken and dark, with a longing that never quite turns into begging.

## Song Summary

Devil In Me is the fifth of eleven tracks on Gravel & Wine, Wigmore's second album, written by Wigmore with Eric Rosse and produced, like the rest of the album, by Butch Walker. In Wigmore's words, quoted by Diffuser.fm, it is "about the lengths of misery, despair and desperation a person goes through when being left for another by a lover", and she was drawn to "the sorrow and desire of giving in to drinking yourself to the point where it doesn't hurt anymore." Beat's Kelly Theobald put it more simply: an angsty track about lost love.

## Musical Breakdown

No source gives times or maps the song section by section; what follows collects what reviewers heard.

### Intro

Theobald heard a wild-west-esque intro. Entertainment Focus, less charmed, found the old-school drum beat at the start a little too like Abba.

### The Guitar

Paul Cole of Still Got The Fever heard Gwen Stefani's No Doubt starring in a spaghetti western, complete with that guitar from The Good, the Bad and the Ugly. Listen for the line that earns the comparison.

### Chorus

For Entertainment Focus the chorus is where the song freshens up and sounds more contemporary than its opening.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="film score" /> }} behind Cole's comparison is Ennio Morricone's for The Good, the Bad and the Ugly, released with the film in 1966, with Pino Rucher on electric guitar. A sound like that works as shorthand for a whole genre of film; the comparison is about a setting, not a quotation.

{{ <concept_link name="verse-chorus form" /> }} gives the song its arc. The reviews describe an intro that sets a period scene and a chorus that pulls it into the present, so the form does more than repeat: it moves the song between two eras.

An {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }} shows what survives without the studio. In the Old Queens Head Session, premiered by Diffuser.fm in 2013, Wigmore strums an acoustic guitar backed by a tight five-piece band.

## Artistic Context

Wigmore, from Auckland, is known for a high, raspy voice; her debut Holy Smoke topped the New Zealand chart in 2009. Before Gravel & Wine she spent two months in Mississippi and Alabama, then recorded in Santa Monica in 2011 with Walker and his band, The Black Widows. The New Zealand Herald heard country, Americana and Mississippi swamp rock in the result. The album came out on 7 November 2011 and debuted at number one in New Zealand. Hear {{ <track_link source="gin-wigmore-kill-of-the-night.md" /> }} just before it on the album.

## Impact and Reception

Wikipedia lists Devil In Me among Wigmore's singles without a chart position, but it got its own video in 2012 and, in MusicBrainz, a four-track Devil In Me EP that July. Play it loud and listen for the guitar.
