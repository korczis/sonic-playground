## Genre and Mood

**Zwicker** captivates listeners with **Oddity,** featuring **Olivera Stanimirov.** The track blends electronic rhythms with a sultry vocal and a disco pulse, and PopMatters picked it out as one of the album's discofied highlights.

## Song Summary

The song pulls the listener in with propulsive beats, a funky bass line and xylophone, the elements Off The Radar heard in it, with processed, atmospheric guitar around them. Olivera Stanimirov is one of the album's guest singers, and her voice gives the track its allure while the production keeps it moving. It is a track for late-night listening that still wants you to dance.

The track opens with its atmosphere and beat, the electronic elements creating a sense of mystery.

### Verse 1: A Dance of Emotions

As the first verse unfolds, Olivera Stanimirov's sultry voice enters and intertwines with the rhythm.

The final chorus returns with renewed energy and brings the track to its finish.

## Concepts in Focus

Oddity shows the {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} on a producer's album. Zwicker made Songs of Lucid Dreamers with several guest voices, Olivera Stanimirov among them alongside Heidi Happy and Matt Didemus of Junior Boys, and PopMatters found that the guests lend character while the overall feel stays consistent across the different singers. The producer is the author; the singers are colours in his palette.

The engine of the track is its {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }}. A funky, moving bass is what makes an electronic track sound like disco, and it is the part a listener's body follows first. Compare how {{ <track_link source="marbert-rocel-hush-now.md" /> }}, another Compost release, uses a much softer, slower foundation under a similar voice.

## Artistic Context

Zwicker is the alias of Cyril Boehler, a producer based in Zurich, Switzerland. Songs of Lucid Dreamers was released by the Munich label Compost Records in 2009 (catalogue number COMP 319CD), an album PopMatters described as stark, minimalist and often moody, full of analogue hisses and screeches.

## Impact and Reception

PopMatters gave the album 6 out of 10 but named Oddity, with "Ping Pong Muses", as the tracks that make it more than just another European café soundtrack, and Off The Radar predicted it would turn some heads. It is here for that groove and its odd colours. Listen for the xylophone.
