## Genre and Mood

**Dr. Dre**'s **Forgot About Dre**, with **Eminem**, is West Coast hip-hop as a statement of standing: a rap song built to remind everyone who Dre is. It is defiant in its message, intense in its delivery and irreverent wherever Eminem takes over. The Guardian's Thomas Hobbs heard it as Dre the world-weary veteran against Eminem his deranged apprentice.

## Song Summary

The song was the second single from Dre's 1999 album 2001, released on 29 January 2000, and runs 3:42 on the album. Wikipedia credits its writing to Andre Young, Marshall Mathers and Melvin Bradford, better known as Mel-Man, and its production to Dr. Dre and Mel-Man. Okayplayer's Peter A. Berry sums it up: Dre, in a verse written by Eminem, puts down his doubters with reminders of his résumé, and Eminem answers with tightly wound couplets. The video embedded here is the official music video, which runs longer than the album track and is not the same recording.

## Musical Breakdown

No source gives times, so this follows what the critics describe.

### The Strings

Martin Connor of Rap Analysis called the strings foreboding, typical of Dre: strong and threatening rather than soft and melodramatic.

### Dre's Verse

Dre makes the case for himself. Connor writes that here, unlike on The Chronic and with N.W.A, Dre did not write his rap himself, and praises his delivery.

### Eminem's Verse

Eminem's verse runs more than sixteen bars, by Connor's count, and he raps straight through a small, distorted guitar solo. Soul In Stereo's Edward Bowser called it a masterclass in breath control, pacing and humour.

### The Hook

Bowser also singles out the tongue-twisting hook, which he says took him forever to learn to recite.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="ghostwriting" /> }} is the hidden story of the song. A rapper is expected to write their own words, yet the verse Dre performs here was written by Eminem, a credited writer of the song.

Two {{ <concept_link name="rapping" /> }} styles meet head-on: PopMatters' Chris Massey, quoted on Wikipedia, called Eminem's rap frantic, and Hobbs compared him to a cartoon rottweiler chomping through the beat.

Eminem is the {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}, and the {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} directed by Philip G. Atwell swaps some of his explicit lines for a skit with the reporter Jane Yamamoto, then ends with Hittman's song Last Dayz. It won the 2000 MTV Video Music Award for Best Rap Video.

## Artistic Context

2001, Dre's second studio album, came out on Aftermath and Interscope on 16 November 1999. On it, compare the haunting piano of {{ <track_link source="dr-dre-big-ego-s.md" /> }}; for Dre and Eminem together again, hear Dre's production for Eminem's group D12 on {{ <track_link source="d12-american-psycho-ii.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Forgot About Dre reached number 25 on the Billboard Hot 100 and number 7 in the UK, where it is now certified triple platinum. It won the Grammy for Best Rap Performance by a Duo or Group in February 2001. Rolling Stone and The Guardian each ranked it third among Eminem's greatest songs. Listen to it for the moment the guitar arrives and Eminem simply keeps going.
