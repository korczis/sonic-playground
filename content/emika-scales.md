## Genre and Mood

**Emika**'s **Scales** is electronic music at the edge of art pop, the second song of a short record its maker describes in two words: bass-heavy and modern. The feelings named here, introspective and atmospheric, follow the EP as Emika presents it rather than a review of this track; no critic has written about Scales on its own.

## Song Summary

Scales runs 3:42 and sits between the title track and Circles on Frames, the EP Emika Records released on 25 July 2025. Its credits are short because one person holds almost all of them: Emika wrote and composed the music, arranged, recorded and mixed it in her own studio, produced it, and sings it, credited under her own name, Ema Jolly. Only the mastering went elsewhere, to Hue Jah Fink of Binary Feedback. Emika has said that she made the EP for her fans and that it sounds like her first album.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} usually stands between the artist and the record, making the decisions about sound. On Frames that role and the artist's are the same person. Emika trained as a classical pianist and composer, studied Creative Music Technology at Bath Spa University and worked as a sound designer for Native Instruments before her debut, and the credits show the result: no outside producer, no outside mixer.

The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} is the other clue Emika gives. She ties the EP's bass weight to the sound of her 2011 debut album, the record that came out of her years among Berlin's club scene around Berghain and Panorama Bar. Listen to Scales for the bass first and the melody second.

## Artistic Context

Emika is the English producer and singer Ema Jolly, born in Milton Keynes in 1986 and of Czech descent. Her first album came out on Ninja Tune in 2011; since 2014 she has released her music on her own label, Emika Records, from {{ <track_link source="emika-miracles.md" /> }} on DREI to the piano album Klavírní. Frames came after Haze and Vega, both from 2024. Its title song, the lead single, was billed as avant-pop born from Bristol's trip-hop and dubstep, and Emika said it was inspired by her then therapist, who advised her to put everything they had discussed in a frame.

## Impact and Reception

Frames was an independent release distributed by SRD, and Scales has not been reviewed on its own. Its place is in the EP's arc, so play it from the title track onwards.
