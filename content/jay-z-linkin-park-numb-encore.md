## Genre and Mood

**JAY-Z** and **Linkin Park** met halfway on **Numb / Encore**, and the meeting point is rap rock: a hip-hop verse and an alternative rock chorus sharing one beat. It is intense and defiant, with Chester Bennington's release in the chorus giving it its cathartic edge.

## Song Summary

The track joins two songs from 2003: Encore, from Jay-Z's The Black Album, and Numb, the closing song of Linkin Park's Meteora. Mike Shinoda built it from the a cappellas of The Black Album, sketching the first mash-ups on a laptop in the band's tour bus in under two days. Instead of releasing a cut-and-paste, the two acts re-recorded their parts between 16 and 19 July 2004 over Shinoda's new music, so Jay-Z raps Encore over Numb, and Bennington sings the chorus he wrote with his band. Kanye West's background vocals from the original Encore stayed in.

## Musical Breakdown

No source gives a timed walk-through, so this follows the parts the sources name.

### Verses

Jay-Z's verses come from Encore, now carried by Linkin Park's music rather than the original beat.

### Chorus

Bennington's chorus from Numb answers them. Songfacts heard Jay-Z's swagger merging with his melodic screams, and that contrast is the hook of the whole idea.

### The Band

Brad Delson on guitar, Phoenix on bass, Rob Bourdon on drums and Joe Hahn on turntables are credited on the recording, which Shinoda produced and mixed.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="mashup" /> }} usually lays one existing recording on another. Collision Course grew out of the MTV series Ultimate Mash-Ups and out of Danger Mouse's The Grey Album, which had set Jay-Z's Black Album against the Beatles; but Shinoda reportedly said it was better to re-perform the rap vocals over a new beat, which turns a mash-up into a new studio recording.

The {{ <concept_link name="rapping" /> }} and the singing take turns, each in its own style. Jay-Z told Aftonbladet: "I'm not trying to be Linkin Park. I'm not trying to be Jay Z."

The {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} is the author here. Shinoda has said he had been making mash-ups for five or six years by then; hear him as a rapper in {{ <track_link source="fort-minor-remember-the-name.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Jay-Z chose Linkin Park when MTV offered him the show. The six-track Collision Course came out on 30 November 2004 through Roc-A-Fella, Def Jam, Warner Bros. and Linkin Park's Machine Shop, and the single followed on 13 December. Its video, directed by Kimo Proudfoot, mixes the July 2004 show at the Roxy Theatre with black-and-white footage from behind the scenes.

## Impact and Reception

The EP entered the Billboard 200 at number one; the single reached number 20 in the US, 14 in the UK, where it stayed 45 weeks in the top 100, and number one in Ireland. It won Best Rap/Sung Collaboration at the 2006 Grammys, where Paul McCartney joined the performance with Yesterday, and in 2024 it passed a billion Spotify streams. Critics were split; the song outlasted them. Start with the first chorus.
