## Genre and Mood

**Vitalic** delivers an exhilarating experience with **You Prefer Cocaine**, a driving track that merges **electro** and **techno**. Its pulsing rhythm and relentless synth lines create an intense, hypnotic atmosphere made for late-night dance floors.

## Song Summary

You Prefer Cocaine is a high-energy track from the record that launched Vitalic, the 2001 Poney EP. It shows his production style already fully formed: powerful machine beats and aggressive, catchy synth lines, nothing acoustic, everything built for the club.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro: Building Anticipation

The track opens with a pulsating synth riff that gradually layers in, building anticipation.

### Verse 1: Driving Energy

A driving beat arrives with energetic synth patterns. The production emphasises rhythm and texture, a compelling base for the track's groove.

### Chorus: The Catchy Hook

The hook is the synth riff at full intensity, memorable and rhythmic, built to make a room move.

### Bridge: Hypnotic Breakdown

Midway through, a hypnotic breakdown brings synth variations and rhythmic changes, a short pause before the energy ramps back up.

### Verse 2 and Outro: Unrelenting Momentum

The second half keeps the pace high, the beat and synth arrangement driving through to an ending that never lets the intensity drop.

## Concepts in Focus

You Prefer Cocaine is pure {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} music. There are no guitars, no live drums and no samples of other records at its core: oscillators and filters make every sound, shaped to snarl and buzz. Vitalic later said that all the instruments on his first album, OK Cowboy, were synthesized, and this early track shows where that conviction began.

The hook is a {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }}, not a sung chorus. A short, aggressive synth figure repeats and mutates over the beat, doing the job a guitar riff does in rock: it is the part you remember and the part the crowd reacts to.

The track belongs to {{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }}, the scene of the early 2000s that International DeeJay Gigolo in Munich stood at the centre of. Vitalic's friend The Hacker, whom he met at the Paris Rex Club, suggested he send his tracks to the label's head, DJ Hell; Gigolo released the Poney EP in 2001, and it became one of the scene's defining records. For The Hacker's own dark electro, listen to {{ <track_link source="the-hacker-mount-sims-traces-oxia-remix.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Vitalic is the French producer Pascal Arbez-Nicolas, born in 1976. His first singles in 1996 and 1997 stayed in the underground; the Poney EP, released by International DeeJay Gigolo Records in October 2001 with Poney Part 1, Poney Part 2, La Rock 01 and You Prefer Cocaine, made his name. He wrote and produced the track himself. His debut album OK Cowboy followed in 2005, and the Poney tracks were reissued as Poney in 2006.

## Impact and Reception

The Poney EP was a huge success shortly after its release, and La Rock 01 became a club anthem in the summer of 2001, even turning up on rock compilations and on Miss Kittin's DJ mix album On the Road. You Prefer Cocaine, the EP's closing track, also travelled on Gigolo compilations such as American Gigolo. The EP helped establish Vitalic as a key figure of French electro, a reputation he built on through OK Cowboy, Flashmob and Rave Age.
