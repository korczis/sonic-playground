## Genre and Mood

**Niko Schwind** and **Heartbeat** made **Perfect Fit** out of very little: a voice, a guitar figure and a bass. It is deep house in the reduced, song-shaped way of Stil vor Talent, the Berlin label of Oliver Koletzki, and its mood is warm and groovy rather than euphoric. The label's word for it is restraint: minimalism with a pop impact.

## Song Summary

The label's notes describe the track in one sentence, and it is a good one: Heartbeat's warm voice and a simple guitar loop form a flawless musical symbiosis, accentuated by a synthetically creaking bassline. Heartbeat is credited with the lyrics and Schwind with the music. The original runs six and a half minutes; the single also carried a radio edit of just over four.

## Musical Breakdown

### Main Section

No review we found walks through the track bar by bar, so this page describes less than usual and keeps to the label's description. Listen for the three elements it names: the guitar loop, the voice laid over it and the synthetic bass underneath. In a track built this sparely, what is left out matters as much as what is there.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="loop" /> }} is a short phrase repeated without a seam, and house has always been built on them. Here the loop is not a drum break or a synth stab but a simple guitar line, and the track's warmth comes from letting it run while the voice does the changing.

The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} gives the groove its weight. The label calls it creaking and synthetic, which suggests why it is there: a little grit under a smooth vocal, so that the track moves a dance floor without losing its intimacy.

The single, released as SVT128 in May 2014, came with two versions besides the original, and each is a different kind of {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }}. Schwind's own is described as a reduced, tool-oriented club cut; the newcomer Proud's as a dreamy arrangement centred on atmospheric synths. Among the DJ feedback the label published, Kellerkind called it a perfect fit for his set and said he loved the original; compare his own deep house in {{ <track_link source="kellerkind-disco-on-the-dancefloor.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Schwind moved to Berlin in 2007, played clubs such as Bar 25, Club der Visionäre and Watergate, and signed to Stil vor Talent, where his second album Good Morning Midnight came out. Perfect Fit, the second track of his third album Grippin' World, released on 30 May 2014, came out three weeks ahead of it; Heartbeat also sings on the album's You Can't Stop Me. The label describes the album as elegantly reduced grooves, organic arrangements and accessible vocal melodies. Schwind later made the album Noordhoek with Koletzki, whose {{ <track_link source="oliver-koletzki-reality.md" /> }} came out the same month.

## Impact and Reception

We found no independent review of the single and no chart record; what survives is the label's description and the feedback it printed, from BBC Radio One's "ace" to DJ Tennis singling out the Proud remix.
