## Genre and Mood

**New Young Pony Club**'s **Ice Cream** is new wave played for the dance floor, the kind of record the mid-2000s filed under dance-punk and new rave: guitars and keyboards, a live drummer, a bass that does more than keep time, and a voice that never quite smiles. Its mood is sultry and playful at once, a come-on delivered with a straight face, provocative because it pretends to be about something sweet.

## Song Summary

On the surface the song is about ice cream. Nobody who reviewed it believed that for long: Gigwise called it a chocolate-flavoured love theme and joked that it gave a new meaning to Mr Whippy, and PopMatters heard an arch, disaffected exterior over the innuendo. What makes it stick is the distance between the words and the delivery: Tahita Bulmer sings the double meaning coolly, as an order rather than a plea, while the band keeps the groove tight.

## Musical Breakdown

### The Groove

Gigwise described the track as rough-edged, heavy on drums and bass and leaning towards disco, and the most seductive electro record since Air's Sexy Boy. No source walks through the song section by section, so this page does not map its parts: listen for how the rhythm section carries it from the first bar.

### The Voice

Bulmer's vocal is choppy and clipped, what the same review called clever dominatrix vocals; XLR8R heard her voice as what keeps the album bounding forward.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} is where New Young Pony Club's post-punk roots show. Reviewing Fantastic Playroom, PopMatters noted that the band put the bass guitar up front and let it carry much of the melody, and XLR8R heard grubby, syncopated basslines under synths and choppy guitars. On Ice Cream that weight in the low end is what turns a wry pop song into a dance record.

The song also spread through {{ <concept_link name="sync licensing" /> }}. It was used in a television advertisement for Intel, mentioned by both Gigwise and PopMatters, which put a song that began as a limited seven-inch in front of a mass audience.

Before that, the {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} did the work: in August 2006 Stereogum wrote that the video for Ice Cream was what was generating buzz for the band, whose campy synth-rock it compared to the Human League and Tom Tom Club.

## Artistic Context

New Young Pony Club were formed in London by Tahita Bulmer and Andy Spence, with Lou Hayter on keyboards, Sarah Jones on drums and Igor Volk on bass. Ice Cream was their first single, a limited seven-inch on Tirk Recordings in early 2005; it was reissued in September 2006 and again in July 2007 on Island, when it reached number 40 in the UK. It became the third track of Fantastic Playroom, the debut album Bulmer and Spence produced, released in July 2007 and shortlisted for that year's Mercury Prize.

## Impact and Reception

The reviews liked it with reservations. Gigwise gave the single four stars, XLR8R called it anthemic, and PopMatters, while rating the album highly, judged Ice Cream its weakest track and still a pretty good one. Put it on and listen for the moment the joke stops being a joke.
