## Genre and Mood

**Emika**'s **Without Expression** is electronic music leaning into R&B, and the reviews agree it is DREI's most unusual track. Glamglare heard it stray farthest from her musical comfort zone, close to some sort of twisted lounge jazz; Spectrum Culture heard a Banks-like R&B vibe that enhances its soul and intimacy. Hence the feelings here: intimate and soulful.

## Song Summary

The song is the sixth of eleven tracks on DREI and runs four minutes and twenty-four seconds. What sets it apart is simple: for once, as Glamglare put it, there is a real guitar in it. Ibiza Spotlight placed it in the album's second half, where the record nudges towards seductive R&B pop, and Spectrum Culture read in its motifs the creative strain Emika felt under record label protocol, before she produced, mixed and mastered DREI herself.

## Concepts in Focus

Because Emika did the {{ <concept_link name="mixing" /> }} of DREI herself, how loud the guitar sits against her voice and the electronics is her decision, and on this track it is the decision that matters most: one acoustic colour placed in an electronic mix.

An {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }} lets you check that. In 2017 she released DREI as instrumentals and a cappellas, and Without Expression is among the instrumentals. Played after the original, it shows what the guitar and the arrangement do when the voice is gone, and how much of the song's intimacy belonged to the vocal.

## Artistic Context

Emika is Ema Jolly, born in Milton Keynes to a Czech mother and based in Berlin. She left Ninja Tune, which had released her first two albums, and released DREI on her own Emika Records on 4 May 2015. Glamglare heard the album's prevailing mood as cold passion, in songs such as {{ <track_link source="emika-my-heart-bleeds-melody.md" /> }}; Without Expression follows {{ <track_link source="emika-what-s-the-cure.md" /> }} and moves the other way.

## Impact and Reception

Both Glamglare and Spectrum Culture singled the track out as the album's turn, towards guitar and lounge jazz for one and towards soulful R&B for the other. Listen for the guitar, then decide whether the title describes the song or what it is singing against.
