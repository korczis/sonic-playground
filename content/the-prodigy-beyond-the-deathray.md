## Genre and Mood

**The Prodigy** are pioneers of big beat and breakbeat-led rave music, and **Beyond the Deathray** is the moment on The Day Is My Enemy where they stop shouting. It is electronic and wordless, haunting and futuristic: Metal Hammer called it a well-placed space-age instrumental, and its intensity comes from tension rather than volume.

## Song Summary

Liam Howlett said that "violent" was the word that kept coming up when he described the album, and almost everything on it is built for attack. Beyond the Deathray, credited to Howlett and Neil McLellan, runs a little over three minutes with no vocal from Maxim or Keith Flint. Talkhouse heard a cinematic juggernaut of dreadful beauty; The List heard melody, grandeur and power. No source describes its sounds in detail, so this page describes only what the reviews do.

## Musical Breakdown

No source breaks the track into timed sections. The reviews agree on its shape: a long rise and an ending that refuses to pay it off.

### Build-up

Sonicabuse heard squelchy synths and echoing beats, a surprisingly ambient turn; Talkhouse heard three minutes of billowing, irresistible mounting tension.

### Outro

Rather than exploding, the track stops. Talkhouse described it harshly refusing to climax or continue, dropping the listener back into the rave tent where the party resumes.

## Concepts in Focus

An {{ <concept_link name="interlude" /> }} is usually a short piece between an album's main songs, and Drowned in Sound heard this one exactly so: a relatively restrained, sombre sci-fi interlude halfway through. It sits eighth of fourteen tracks, between Rok-Weiler and Rhythm Bomb, and its calm changes how the noise on either side of it sounds.

It is also a {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }} without a release. Dance music teaches the ear to expect a drop after rising tension; here the tension simply ends, and Rhythm Bomb picks the party back up.

With no voice to follow, the {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} parts carry everything. Critics disagreed about them: Sonicabuse heard a short aural MDMA trip, while The List found the synthesizer mayhem that was revolutionary two decades earlier too familiar to be startling.

## Artistic Context

The Prodigy formed in Braintree in 1990 around the producer and keyboardist Liam Howlett, with the dancers Keith Flint and Leeroy Thornhill, and were joined by the MC Maxim. The Day Is My Enemy, their sixth album, was recorded over years, largely on tour, in hotel rooms and on flights, and became the first on which Flint and Maxim worked on the songwriting with Howlett. It was released on 30 March 2015 by Take Me to the Hospital and Cooking Vinyl in the UK. Flint died in 2019.

## Impact and Reception

The album debuted at number one in the UK, the band's sixth consecutive chart-topping record. Reviewers who disagreed about the rest of it often agreed on this track: Consequence of Sound named it essential and probably the most beautiful track in The Prodigy's discography, and Talkhouse called it the crazy high point of the album. Hear it between its neighbours, in album order.
