## Genre and Mood

**Elephanz** make electro-pop with an indie-pop heart, and **Maryland** became one of the hits that carried their second album. We hear it as upbeat on the surface, with longing and something bittersweet underneath; Wikipedia notes that the album's singles often deal with love and with feeling lost. No critical review we could verify describes the song closely, so this page leans on what the band and the credits say.

## Song Summary

Maryland was written and composed by the brothers Jonathan and Maxime Verleysen and produced by Elephanz with Pierrick Devin and Lionnel Buzac. In a 2026 video, Max and Jon rebuild its arrangement live, layer by layer, naming four building blocks: beat, bass, riff and chords. This page follows the album version, 3:19 on the band's own upload, released on Elephanz in October 2017.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }} and a {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} are both repeated figures, but they do different jobs. The bassline anchors the harmony and the groove from below; the riff is a melodic hook that the ear follows. In their reconstruction of Maryland, Elephanz list the bass before the riff, a reminder that a pop song is often built from the bottom up.

In April 2018 the band released an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }}: Maryland featuring the singer Eugénie, who shares the writing credit on the lyrics of that version, with a video directed by Romain Daudet-Jahan. A duet can change a song's point of view while keeping its music.

## Artistic Context

Elephanz formed in Nantes in January 2008, Jonathan on vocals and guitar, Maxime on vocals and keyboards. Their debut Time for a Change appeared in 2013 and earned a 2014 Victoires de la Musique nomination for electronic album of the year. The self-titled second album, released on 13 October 2017, is more electronic than the first, with slicker production; Maryland was released from it as a single in 2018.

## Impact and Reception

Maryland was certified a gold single in France, and by November 2020 it had passed 14 million plays on Spotify. It is here for how simply it is built. Listen for the four layers, one at a time.
