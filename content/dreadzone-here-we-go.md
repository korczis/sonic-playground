## Genre and Mood

**Dreadzone** made **Here We Go** for the London band's fifth album, Once Upon a Time: Discogs files the album under breakbeat, dub and breaks, Wikipedia calls the band dub, reggae and electronic. The feelings named here, gritty and groovy, come from what the sources say about the album and its MC; no review we found discusses this track on its own.

## Song Summary

Here We Go is one of the album's MC tracks. The only voice credited on it is Spee, the MC who joined Dreadzone as a second vocalist in 2000, and the writing is credited to him under his own name, Spencer Graham, together with the band's Ben Balafonic and Greg Roberts. At 4:15 it is the shortest full track on a record where every other one runs past five minutes. Its history holds a small mix-up: the album's artwork printed its title and that of The Warriors the wrong way round, and the French release on Discogs corrects the order, noting that The Warriors is mostly instrumental while Here We Go is the one you can recognise by its lyrics.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes the beat or the structure of Here We Go; the sources establish who is on the record, and this breakdown stays with that.

### Main Section

Spee carries the track. A reviewer of the album heard his delivery as rougher, and found it suited the way Dreadzone's sound had evolved after their years as DJs on the English club circuit. Behind him, Greg Roberts and Ben Balafonic are credited with production and electronics; unlike Iron Shirt or Elevate, this track credits no guitar or bass from Steve Roberts.

## Concepts in Focus

The first idea to listen for is {{ <concept_link name="rapping" /> }}, or more exactly the MC tradition it sits in: a voice delivering words rhythmically over a beat, closer to speech than to song. On a Dreadzone record the MC comes from sound-system culture as much as from hip-hop.

The ground under him is {{ <concept_link name="dub" /> }}, the Jamaican craft of remixing a record at the mixing desk, which Dreadzone have built on since 1993. Their sound system hosted the Dubwiser club night at the Notting Hill Arts Club between 1998 and 2001.

The third is {{ <concept_link name="breakbeat" /> }}: drum patterns built from broken, syncopated loops, the style Discogs gives the whole album. Compare how it carries a sampled refrain in {{ <track_link source="dreadzone-iron-shirt.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Dreadzone formed in London in 1993 around the former Big Audio Dynamite drummer Greg Roberts. Ben Balafonic joined in 2001, and Once Upon a Time was recorded by Greg Roberts, Balafonic, Spee, Earl Sixteen and Steve Roberts and released in 2005 on the independent Functional label run by Biff Mitchell. The French release credits Spee with vocals and writing on three of its tracks: Elevate, I Know and Here We Go.

## Impact and Reception

Here We Go is not among the album's singles that Wikipedia lists, and no review singles it out. The album around it won over Rebel Base, whose reviewer recommended it to every reggae fan. Play it after {{ <track_link source="dreadzone-elevate.md" /> }} and listen for how one MC holds a track on his own.
