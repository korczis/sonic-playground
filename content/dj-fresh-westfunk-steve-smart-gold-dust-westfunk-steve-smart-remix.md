## Genre and Mood

**DJ Fresh** made **Gold Dust** as drum and bass; **WestFunk** and **Steve Smart** remade it as **Gold Dust (WestFunk & Steve Smart Remix)** for the 2012 single. This page files it under remix and electronic music rather than a narrower style, because no review describes how this version sounds. The feelings, energetic and carefree, belong to the song's whole family of versions rather than to anything a critic heard in this one.

## Song Summary

Gold Dust has lived many lives. It began on 16 August 2008 as a 7:51 instrumental 12-inch on Breakbeat Kaos. In 2010 it returned with a vocal by Ce'cile and reached number 24 in the UK. In 2012 a version with Ms. Dynamite went onto DJ Fresh's album Nextlevelism, and Ministry of Sound released a six-track download led by Shy FX's re-edit. This remix is the third track, between TC's and Kutz's, and at 5:13 it is one of the longer versions. Ministry of Sound put it on its YouTube channel on 8 November 2012, weeks before the download.

## Musical Breakdown

### Main Section

No review, timed analysis or interview walks through this remix, and this page will not invent one. The label's upload calls it simply WestFunk & Steve Smart's remix of DJ Fresh's Gold Dust. What the release does tell you: it runs more than two minutes longer than the radio re-edit, Dan Stein is credited as producer, WestFunk and Steve Smart as remixers, and the performers include Niomi Arleen Daley, better known as Ms. Dynamite, who shares the lyric credit with Stein. Listen for the beat, the vocal and the structure yourself.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} hands a finished song to another producer. A remix package sends one song into several scenes at once: Dancing Astronaut heard Shy FX's lead version as drum and bass, jungle and even reggae, while this pair came from the world of pop remixes.

Gold Dust is also a lesson in the {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }}: instrumental, Ce'cile vocal, Ms. Dynamite vocal, re-edit, five remixes. Start with {{ <track_link source="dj-fresh-shy-fx-gold-dust-shy-fx-re-edit.md" /> }}, then come back here.

{{ <concept_link name="club culture" /> }} explains the choice of remixers. Steve Smart was a long-standing resident at Ministry of Sound in London and toured with the club; the same company released the single.

## Artistic Context

DJ Fresh is Dan Stein, a drum and bass producer whose album Nextlevelism had given him two UK number ones by the end of 2012. Steve Smart, by his KISS profile, is a radio presenter and remixer for Bruno Mars, Diplo, DJ Fresh and others, who in 2011 celebrated three official UK number ones, among them his remixes of Example and JLS. WestFunk's profile says he began as a club DJ who missed big remixes of huge pop songs, and that his first chance came with a remix for Bruno Mars.

## Impact and Reception

The single's Shy FX re-edit reached number 22 in the UK and 39 in Ireland, and Wikipedia reports the song's versions together certified triple platinum. Press play and hear where WestFunk and Steve Smart take it.
