## Genre and Mood

**Avicii**'s **Feeling Good** takes one of the most covered standards in popular music and dresses it in electronic pop clothing with a deep soul core. The mood is uplifting and empowering, a song about new beginnings delivered with the patience of a sunrise rather than the rush of a festival drop.

## Song Summary

Feeling Good was written by Anthony Newley and Leslie Bricusse for the 1960s stage musical The Roar of the Greasepaint – The Smell of the Crowd, and Nina Simone's 1965 recording made it immortal. Avicii's version keeps the song's slow, majestic declaration of renewal and lets Audra Mae's smoky, powerful voice carry it, while soft, pulsing synths and a gentle swirl of electronics take the place of the brass that defined Simone's arrangement. The result is restrained by Avicii's standards and all the more affecting for it.

## Musical Breakdown

The arrangement grows steadily from an intimate opening into a full, glowing finish, following the shape of the lyric's dawn-to-daylight imagery.

### Intro

Muted, shimmering synths open the song, quiet and spacious, setting a reflective tone before the voice enters.

### Verse 1

Audra Mae's vocal arrives close and full of grain, savouring each image of birds flying high and the sun in the sky, with the electronics barely moving beneath her.

### Build-up

Pulsing synth chords and a light beat begin to rise, adding momentum without crowding the voice.

### Chorus

The title line opens up the whole mix: the vocal soars, the harmony brightens and the track finally lets its emotion out.

### Climax

The final pass through the refrain is the fullest moment, the voice at its most commanding over a swelling wash of synths.

### Outro

The instruments recede and the song settles back into calm, ending on a feeling of quiet resolve.

## Concepts in Focus

Feeling Good is a {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} of a song that has had many: written for a musical, made definitive by Nina Simone, then recorded by singers from soul to rock. A cover is a choice about what to keep. Avicii keeps the melody, the slow tempo and the declaration in the lyric; he replaces the arrangement, swapping Simone's piano and brass for synthesizers, and keeps the singer's grain by choosing a voice as rough-edged as Audra Mae's.

The arrangement works as one long, gentle {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }}. It begins almost empty, adds a pulse and chords under the verse, and saves the fullest sound for the last refrain. Unlike a club build there is no drop: the reward is the voice opening up, not a bassline arriving. Compare how {{ <track_link source="nina-simone-felix-da-housecat-sinnerman.md" /> }} turns Simone's own recording into dance-floor momentum instead.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} was the reason the track exists. Volvo Cars proposed the song for its 2015 New Beginning campaign, and Avicii produced the new arrangement for a film shot in Stockholm and Österlen in southern Sweden, at places that mattered to him, with his family and friends appearing. It tells his story of renewal after years of near-constant touring, which gives the lyric about a new dawn an unusually literal meaning.

## Artistic Context

Avicii, the Swedish producer Tim Bergling, released Feeling Good on 8 May 2015, the evening Volvo's campaign went live. In Volvo's press release he described six years of almost constant touring, with around 325 shows in a single year, as something that "couldn't continue", and Sweden as the place where he recharged. Audra Mae, an American singer-songwriter, had already worked with him on his 2013 album True, singing on Addicted to You, and her bluesy voice suits the song's weight. The single was issued by his label PRMD and sits apart from the big-room sound of his biggest hits.

## Impact and Reception

Because of its campaign, the track reached a wide audience beyond dance music, and it is often remembered as one of Avicii's more personal moments, a song about renewal from an artist who was openly seeking it. It belongs here for that honesty and for how gracefully it honours a classic. Listen to it as the day begins.
