## Genre and Mood

**Moloko**'s **100%** is electronic pop with a dance pulse, and its pull is the one the feelings name: hypnotic and catchy. It is not a club track built to peak; it is an album song that turns around its chorus until the chorus is what you remember.

## Song Summary

100% is the seventh of the ten tracks on Statues, the duo's fourth and final album, released by Echo on 3 March 2003. Like every song on the record, it is credited to Mark Brydon and Róisín Murphy, who produced the album themselves. Statues followed Things to Make and Do (2000), which had used live musicians; it went back to electronic territory while keeping some of that organic sound. By its release the couple's romantic partnership had ended, and listeners have read the album as the story of a relationship falling apart.

## Concepts in Focus

100% is a {{ <concept_link name="deep cut" /> }}: the album's singles were Familiar Feeling, Forever More and Cannot Contain This, and it was none of them. Yet it was played on the tour that followed; a live recording from Brixton Academy in November 2003 appears on the 2004 release 11,000 Clicks.

Its strength, by Hot Press's account, is the {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }}, a chorus line that repeats until it hypnotises. And its {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }} is what both other reviewers named: Brazilian rhythm and acoustic colour inside an electronic production. Compare it with {{ <track_link source="moloko-forever-more.md" /> }}, the album's second single, where the same pair build a seven-minute house track.

## Artistic Context

Murphy and Brydon met at a party in Sheffield in 1994 and named their 1995 debut after her opening line to him, Do You Like My Tight Sweater? Murphy later called the production of Statues massive and said its cost almost killed the band. The album was recorded in 2001 and 2002 at Metropolis Studios in London; Moloko toured Europe to promote it and disbanded shortly afterwards.

## Impact and Reception

Reviews of Statues were divided. Hot Press found it cold, sometimes glacial, and cluttered, but admired its originality; Visions gave it ten of twelve points and heard the warm breath of funk and soul in it. The album reached number one in Flanders and number 18 in the UK. Put 100% on after the singles and listen for the chorus that one doubtful critic could not shake.
