## Genre and Mood

**Elektrochemie**, the project of **Thomas Schumacher** with the singer **Caitlin** Devlin, released **Pleasure Seeker** in 2005, and it is techno that sings. The electronic frame is functional, a seven-minute track for DJs, but the feeling is dark, sultry and hypnotic: Get Physical, the label that first put it out, calls it a sensual track that connects with the listener immediately.

## Song Summary

Elektrochemie was, in the label's words, a vocal club project, and Pleasure Seeker is built around that idea. A throbbing Moog bass opens the track, and Devlin's strong, sultry vocals, heavily treated with effects, weave like an instrument across growling bass textures. Those textures gather power as they are filtered and dropped around the lyric, so the drama comes from sound being taken away and given back rather than from new melodies. The version here comes from Road Less Traveled, a 2019 WIR Musik release credited to Caitlin and Thomas Schumacher; its ISRC was registered in 2005, the year of the original single.

### Intro

The Moog bass comes first, throbbing and insistent; the label says it hooks you straight in.

The voice arrives, so heavily processed that, as the label puts it, it weaves like an instrument. Underneath, the bass sounds growl.

## Concepts in Focus

Pleasure Seeker starts with its {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} and never really lets go of it. A bass part that opens a track has to hold attention alone, setting the mood and the tension before anything else enters.

The engine of its drama is the {{ <concept_link name="filter sweep" /> }}. Filtering removes part of a sound's frequency range, and opening the filter again brings it back; when the label speaks of bass textures filtered and dropped, this is the motion to hear, energy rising and falling while the tempo stays fixed.

The track also belongs to a specific {{ <concept_link name="club culture" /> }}. Louisahhh!!! & Maelstrom, choosing their favourite vocal techno 12-inches for The Vinyl Factory, recalled finding it while digging for records in Berlin and called its lush, dark vibe and effected vocal performance a big influence. Compare {{ <track_link source="m-a-n-d-y-booka-shade-body-language-original-remaster.md" /> }}, another 2005 Get Physical record from the same moment of the label.

## Artistic Context

Schumacher met Devlin in her native Australia in 2001; after she moved to Germany she sang with his Elektrochemie project and toured with him. The Bremen producer Stephan Bodzin, who calls himself a melody man, joined at the start of 2005. Resident Advisor noted that the trio's sound had been compared to James Holden's and praised its humane electronica.

## Impact and Reception

Pleasure Seeker was followed on Get Physical by Don't Go in 2006, and the label later picked it as a Classic of the Week, calling it a massive dancefloor smasher. Play it loud, and wait for the bass to come back.
