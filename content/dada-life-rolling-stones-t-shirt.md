## Genre and Mood

**Dada Life** made **Rolling Stones T-Shirt** the friendliest track on their second album. It is electro house built for a crowd: energetic, playful and catchy enough that AllMusic called it the record's singalong highlight, adding that it could have fallen off a David Guetta album.

## Song Summary

The track is 3:22 of synth and bass with a hook meant to be sung back, and its story is mostly one of restraint. Dada Life said it took them more than a year and a half to finish. Along the way they tried rock drums, electric guitars and strings, then went back to the original arrangement and kept only one new thing, a small electronic bleep. Hypebeast, hearing it on release day, described electro synths laid over a heavy bassline and thought the record sounded as good as the versions the duo had been playing live.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }} is the real lesson here. Choosing which instruments play, and when, can change a song more than rewriting it. Dada Life tried a rock band's drums and guitars and an orchestra's strings on their electronic sketch, then returned to the first version with one small bleep added. An arrangement is also a list of things left out.

The single was launched with a {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} that Magnetic described as a twisted strip tease. Hypebeast found it quirky and playful, a good match for the track's energy, though the second half surprised and somewhat disappointed its writer.

It also came with a deluxe package of {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} versions by Chuckie and Cazzette, a way to send one track into many different DJ sets. Compare {{ <track_link source="dada-life-happy-violence.md" /> }}, which reached clubs the same way.

## Artistic Context

Dada Life are Olle Corneér and Stefan Engblom, a Stockholm duo formed in 2006 and named in homage to the Dada art movement. Rolling Stones T-Shirt was written by the pair with Anthony Mills and produced by them. It had been a fixture of their live sets before So Much Dada, their own label, released it as a single in April 2012. In October it became track seven of The Rules of Dada, released with Universal.

## Impact and Reception

Magnetic called it and Happy Violence two very successful singles; Elektro Daily called them Dada classics, cleverly wedged between the album's much-hyped new tracks; Dancing Astronaut listed it among the record's established favourites. The album reached number four on the US Dance/Electronic Albums chart and went gold in Sweden. Play it loud and decide whether the strings would have helped.
