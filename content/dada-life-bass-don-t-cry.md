## Genre and Mood

**Dada Life** made their name with electro house that does not take itself too seriously, and **Bass Don't Cry** is electro house at its most direct: energetic, bold and built for a big room. The title tells you where to listen. This is a track in which the low end does the talking.

## Song Summary

Bass Don't Cry is the eighth of eleven tracks on The Rules of Dada, the Swedish duo's second album, released on 16 October 2012 by their own label So Much Dada together with Universal. It is credited to the duo alone, Olle Corneér and Stefan Engblom, and runs a little over three minutes. In the running order it follows Happy Violence and Rolling Stones T-Shirt, two singles fans already knew, and Elektro Daily heard it begin at full speed straight after them. Magnetic Magazine called it a bass-heavy banger, something not heard in the duo's earlier tracks.

## Musical Breakdown

Two reviews from the album's release week describe the track; neither gives times, so the parts below follow their order.

### Intro

The track opens on a consistent, knock-like pluck, which Magnetic Magazine's Michael Wakabayashi compared to Nari & Milani's Atom.

### Main Groove

Elektro Daily heard the track centred on a rollicking melody led by the kick drum, with a quick hit of chainsaw synth at the end of each measure. Magnetic singled out the arrangement of the bass as what set it apart from the album's other tracks.

### Build-up and Drop

Then comes the duo's familiar move, a build that speeds up and a big drop. Elektro Daily thought Dada Life built nothing new or particularly unexpected from these elements, but that the sped-up build and the drop sounded as good as ever.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="kick drum" /> }} usually marks the beat. Here, as Elektro Daily heard it, it leads the melody too: rhythm and tune come from the same punching low thud, which is why the track feels so physical.

The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} is the point of the title. Listen for how the bass is arranged, not just how loud it is; that arrangement is what Magnetic heard as new for Dada Life.

The {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }} and the {{ <concept_link name="drop" /> }} work as a pair. The build speeds up and holds back the full beat, and the drop releases it. The same album's {{ <track_link source="dada-life-arrive-beautiful-leave-ugly.md" /> }} uses a filtered lead and a breakdown to do a similar job.

## Artistic Context

Dada Life are a Swedish duo formed in 2006, named in homage to the absurdist Dada movement of the early twentieth century and known for the humour of their live shows. They also release audio plugins, the best known a saturator called Sausage Fattener. The Rules of Dada, produced by the two of them, peaked at number 116 on the Billboard 200; AllMusic's David Jeffries heard crowd-pleasing, big-room EDM built for prime time.

## Impact and Reception

Bass Don't Cry was not one of the album's singles, and no source records it on a chart. Magnetic's reviewer had it on repeat for a good while and hoped to hear that kind of bass arrangement in the duo's future productions; Elektro Daily heard a formula, done well. Play it loud enough to feel the kick.
