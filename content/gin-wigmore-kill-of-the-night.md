## Genre and Mood

**Gin Wigmore** sings **Kill Of The Night** like a woman on the prowl. It is rock with blues grit and a soul singer's punch, bold, defiant and gritty from the first beat, a song that walks into the room and dares you to look away.

## Song Summary

The song plays with the image of the hunt: the night as a chase, desire as something to be stalked and caught. Musically it is built on stomping drums, a heavy, swinging rhythm and a bluesy strut that leaves plenty of space for her voice. Wigmore's rasp, smoky and sharp at once, is the star; she bends notes, snarls and croons in turn. The memorable detail is that voice, instantly recognisable and impossible to confuse with anyone else. Around it, Butch Walker's production is big rather than rough: AllMusic described the album as all "big, stomping drums and kinetic high vocals", and this song is the clearest case. The swagger is the point, and it never lets up.

## Musical Breakdown

The arrangement is lean and rhythmic, designed to frame the singer.

### Intro

A hard, stomping beat kicks off the song, setting a predatory, swaggering pace.

### Verse 1

Wigmore's voice enters low and taunting, savouring every line as the rhythm section stalks beneath her.

### Chorus

The hook lands with full force, her rasp opening up into a defiant, chantable cry.

### Bridge

The band pulls back briefly, building tension before the final charge.

### Outro

The last chorus and the stomp drive the song to a sharp, satisfying close.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} here is a strut rather than a dance beat. The drums stomp on a heavy, swinging pattern and the rest of the band leaves space around it, so the rhythm feels like someone walking slowly towards you. Listen to how the verse holds back on top of that pattern, so that when the chorus arrives the same groove suddenly sounds aggressive.

The chorus is a {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} in the most practical sense: short, shouted with full force and easy to remember. Wigmore wrote the song with two experienced pop songwriters, Julian Hamilton of the Australian electronic duo the Presets and the Scottish songwriter Gary Clark, and the economy shows: the whole song runs 3:25 and gets to its hook quickly.

That economy also made it a natural {{ <concept_link name="soundtrack" /> }} song. Music supervisors placed it in the 2015 film Spy, in episodes of Teen Wolf, 666 Park Avenue and The Umbrella Academy, and it became the theme of AMC's FearFest Halloween season and of the Netflix series Crazyhead. Each placement attaches a new scene to the same hunt, and for many listeners the song is now inseparable from one of them.

## Artistic Context

Gin Wigmore is a singer-songwriter from Auckland, New Zealand, who made her name with the 2009 debut album Holy Smoke. "Kill Of The Night" comes from her second album, Gravel & Wine, released on 7 November 2011. Before recording she spent two months travelling in Mississippi and Alabama for Southern inspiration, then made the album in Santa Monica, California, with the producer Butch Walker and his band the Black Widows; it pushed her retro soul and blues influences into bigger, rockier territory. The album debuted at number one in New Zealand and went double platinum there. It sits alongside songs like "Man Like That" as an example of her taste for dramatic, rhythm-driven numbers with a vintage edge.

## Impact and Reception

The song became one of the defining tracks of her catalogue, less through the charts than through the screen: its instant, cinematic attitude made it one of the most licensed songs of her career. When Gravel & Wine reached the United States in April 2013, Wigmore promoted it by opening for Phillip Phillips and playing the whole Vans Warped Tour. It belongs here as proof that a great voice and a great stomp are sometimes all you need. Turn it up and let it strut.
