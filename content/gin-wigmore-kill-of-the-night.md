## Genre and Mood

**Gin Wigmore** sings **Kill Of The Night** like a woman on the prowl. It is rock with blues grit and a soul singer's punch, bold, defiant and gritty from the first beat, a song that walks into the room and dares you to look away.

## Song Summary

The song plays with the image of the hunt: the night as a chase, desire as something to be stalked and caught. Musically it is built on stomping drums, a heavy, swinging rhythm and a bluesy strut that leaves plenty of space for her voice. Wigmore's rasp, smoky and sharp at once, is the star; she bends notes, snarls and croons in turn. The memorable detail is that voice, instantly recognisable and impossible to confuse with anyone else. Around it, the band plays with a rough, roadhouse looseness that keeps the song from feeling polished, as if it were cut in one take late at night with the amplifiers still warm. The swagger is the point, and it never lets up.

## Musical Breakdown

The arrangement is lean and rhythmic, designed to frame the singer.

### Intro

A hard, stomping beat kicks off the song, setting a predatory, swaggering pace.

### Verse 1

Wigmore's voice enters low and taunting, savouring every line as the rhythm section stalks beneath her.

### Chorus

The hook lands with full force, her rasp opening up into a defiant, chantable cry.

### Bridge

The band pulls back briefly, building tension before the final charge.

### Outro

The last chorus and the stomp drive the song to a sharp, satisfying close.

## Artistic Context

Gin Wigmore is a singer-songwriter from Auckland, New Zealand, who made her name with the 2009 debut album Holy Smoke. "Kill Of The Night" comes from her second album, Gravel & Wine, released in 2011, a record that pushed her retro soul and blues influences into rougher, rockier territory. It sits alongside songs like "Man Like That" as an example of her taste for dramatic, rhythm-driven numbers with a vintage edge.

## Impact and Reception

The song became one of the defining tracks of her catalogue, its instant, cinematic attitude making it feel like the soundtrack to a scene that has not been filmed yet. Live, it remains a set highlight where her voice really gets to bite. It belongs here as proof that a great voice and a great stomp are sometimes all you need. Turn it up and let it strut.
