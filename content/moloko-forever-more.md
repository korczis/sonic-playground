## Genre and Mood

**Moloko**'s **Forever More** is house and dance music with a disco heart. On the album it runs past seven minutes, long enough for a hypnotic groove to turn into something closer to a party, and the feelings it is filed under follow that arc: hypnotic first, then energetic and uplifting.

## Song Summary

Forever More is the fifth track of Statues, written by Mark Brydon and Róisín Murphy and produced by Brydon. Wikipedia, drawing on Contact Music, says it makes extensive use of two sounds, bass drums and brass horns. The album version is 7:22 long and the single edit 3:47. The video on this page belongs to the single and runs just under four minutes, so the album version is not what you hear here.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

No source used here describes how the album version opens, beyond the hypnotic house that Contact Music heard at the start of the track's journey.

### Main Section

From there, in Contact Music's account, the song evolves into full-on disco commotion, driven by whirling brass and pulsing bass under Murphy's vocal. Adam Sweeting of The Guardian heard the same ingredients less kindly: synth-bass, twitching horns and plastic organ.

### Outro

How the seven minutes end, no source says; listen for whether the brass or the beat has the last word.

## Concepts in Focus

The gap between the versions is a clear case of the {{ <concept_link name="radio edit" /> }}. More than three and a half minutes separate the album track from the single edit, and a radio edit gets its length by cutting repeats and long passages. To hear the long passages, put on the album version after the video.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Paul Gore, shows Murphy dancing in a tunnel and gradually joined by dancers. According to the trade magazine Televisual, as Wikipedia reports it, Murphy improvised in front of a chroma-key screen and the dancers were filmed in the tunnel later, so they sometimes move out of time with her, an effect Gore wanted.

The single also carried a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} package aimed at clubs. The CD paired the edit with a dub mix by François Kevorkian and Eric Kupper, and the 12-inch had their vocal mix at 10:34; Contact Music adds remixes by Can 7.

## Artistic Context

Statues, released by Echo on 3 March 2003, was Moloko's fourth and last studio album, made after the end of Murphy and Brydon's relationship. Forever More followed Familiar Feeling as its second single on 23 June 2003, with the outtake Take My Hand as a B-side; Moloko disbanded after touring the album.

## Impact and Reception

The single reached number 17 in the UK and topped the UK dance chart. Contact Music called it an uplifting dance-floor filler; RTÉ's reviewer thought it could be the new Sing It Back, as slinky and tight if not quite as catchy. Hear the single in the video, then the album version, and decide what the extra minutes add.
