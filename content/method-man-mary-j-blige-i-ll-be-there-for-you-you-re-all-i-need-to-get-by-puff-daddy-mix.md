## Genre and Mood

**Method Man** and **Mary J. Blige**'s **I'll Be There For You/You're All I Need To Get By - Puff Daddy Mix** is the most radio-shaped form of their hip-hop love song: R&B warmth wrapped around a rapper's devotion, romantic and soulful without going soft.

## Song Summary

The song began as All I Need, a track on Method Man's 1994 debut album Tical, produced by RZA. For the 1995 single it was remade as a duet: Method Man raps three verses, and Blige sings the intro, the chorus and the backing vocals. Every version borrows from the 1968 Marvin Gaye and Tammi Terrell duet You're All I Need to Get By, hence the slash in the title. The Puff Daddy Mix, the radio version, swapped RZA's music for a new instrumental with a repeated vocal sample from the Notorious B.I.G.'s Me & My Bitch.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

Blige opens the record: the soul frame is set before any rap arrives.

### Verses

Method Man carries the three verses alone. The song is often cited as the first of the "thug-love" duets: a hard-edged rapper and a soul singer meeting on the subject of love.

### Chorus

Blige sings the chorus on the Gaye and Terrell melody, re-sung rather than lifted from their tape.

### The Beat

The sources describe the mix's new instrumental only by its Biggie vocal sample, so listen for that recurring voice.

## Concepts in Focus

The chorus is an {{ <concept_link name="interpolation" /> }}: Blige performs the melody and words of You're All I Need to Get By again, so Nickolas Ashford and Valerie Simpson join the writing credits, though not a second of the 1968 recording is used.

The Biggie line is the opposite case, {{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }}: a fragment of an existing recording, Me & My Bitch from Ready to Die, repeated inside the new one. One record holds both ways of borrowing.

The Puff Daddy Mix is also a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }}: Sean Combs kept the vocals and rebuilt the music around them, turning an album track produced by RZA into a radio single. Trackmasters later said in a 2012 interview that they produced the remix and were credited only for drum programming. Compare how a borrowed Marvin Gaye feel ended up in court with {{ <track_link source="robin-thicke-t-i-pharrell-williams-blurred-lines.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Method Man, born Clifford Smith, is a member of the Wu-Tang Clan, and Tical, released by Def Jam on 15 November 1994, was the first Wu-Tang solo album after the group's debut, with a dark, murky sound by RZA. The duet with Blige came out as a single on 25 April 1995, and the Puff Daddy Mix later joined the album's deluxe edition.

## Impact and Reception

The single reached number three on the Billboard Hot 100, topped the R&B chart for three weeks, reached number ten in the UK and went platinum. It won the 1996 Grammy for Best Rap Performance by a Duo or Group, and Complex later ranked it first among hip-hop love songs. NME found the Combs mix "slick as hell, but still booming".
