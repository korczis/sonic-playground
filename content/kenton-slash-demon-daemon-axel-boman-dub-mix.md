## Genre and Mood

**Kenton Slash Demon** made **Daemon** as a big, melancholic house track; Axel Boman's dub mix keeps the melancholy and takes away the bulk. The result is house music in a dub frame, dreamy and atmospheric, with a bassline so large that the label used it as the selling point.

## Song Summary

Daemon was the third and final EP of the duo's trilogy The Schwarzschild Solution, named after Karl Schwarzschild's theory of gravity and celestial bodies. Tartelet's release notes describe the original as a grand journey with relentless, seductive beats and a yearning, melancholic mood, sung by Jon Philpot of the Brooklyn band Bear in Heaven and a member of When Saints Go Machine. The EP paired that original with two remixes by Axel Boman: a long Epic remix, exclusive to vinyl, and this dub, which the label called dreamy, atmospheric and stripped-down, with an extremely massive bassline.

## Musical Breakdown

No review or timed analysis describes the dub mix passage by passage, so this breakdown stays with what the release notes say about the whole.

### Main Section

The dub is defined by subtraction. Where XLR8R heard the Epic remix stretch the original into a darker, fidgety slow-burner with pulsing rhythms and repetitive vocoder hooks, the dub goes the other way: fewer elements, more air, and the bass at the centre. No description says how much of the original vocal survives.

### The Bassline

The label's own description puts the bassline first, and the title of the mix tells you why: in a dub, the low end is what remains when everything else has been thinned out.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="dub" /> }} began as a Jamaican way of remixing a record down to drums and bass, with the rest treated by echo and drop-outs. In house music the word lives on for stripped-back versions, and that is what Boman's dub is beside his Epic {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }}: the same source, two opposite directions.

The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} is the hinge. With less around it, a bass part stops being support and becomes the melody a crowd follows.

A {{ <concept_link name="dj mix" /> }} carried the record further: MusicBrainz lists a Dixon edit of Boman's dub on Live at Robert Johnson, Volume 8, a 2011 mix album in the club's own series. Compare how {{ <track_link source="konstantin-sibold-madeleine.md" /> }} also spread through Dixon's sets.

## Artistic Context

Kenton Slash Demon are the Copenhagen producers Jonas Kenton and Silas Moldenhawer, who also play in When Saints Go Machine; that band's Wikipedia entry calls the duo's parallel project rhythmic electro-house, against the band's more soulful sound. Tartelet released Daemon on 28 March 2011. Axel Boman, a Stockholm DJ and producer, came to it as the maker of Purple Drank, one of the previous year's big hits.

## Impact and Reception

Tartelet hoped the EP would make people dance with tears in their eyes and imagined it still moving dancefloors decades later. The dub's life in a Dixon mix suggests DJs heard what the label heard in it. Listen to it after the original, and notice how much feeling survives with so little left on the track.
