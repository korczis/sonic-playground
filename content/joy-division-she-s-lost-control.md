## Genre and Mood

**Joy Division** recorded **She's Lost Control** twice, and the version on TOTAL is the second: the extended, more electronic 12-inch version of 1980. It is post-punk at its most austere, dark and haunting, with an intensity that never needs to raise its voice.

## Song Summary

Ian Curtis took the lyric mainly from his job as an Assistant Disablement Resettlement Officer at a rehabilitation centre in Macclesfield in 1978 and 1979. There he met a young woman with epilepsy who was desperate for work; her seizures disturbed Curtis, who had epilepsy himself. She stopped coming to her appointments, and he later learned that she had died during a seizure. The song also carries his awareness of the stigma faced by people with neurological conditions. Musically it centres on Peter Hook's bassline, played high on the neck, and on a mechanistic drum beat by Stephen Morris.

### Intro

Bass and drums are the core of the song: a melodic bass part above a beat closer to a machine than to a rock drummer.

### Verse and Refrain

Curtis sings the verses as short, observed lines, and the title comes back again and again as the refrain. Producer Martin Hannett recorded each drum separately on songs he considered potential singles, so that nothing bled across.

### The Extra Verse

The 12-inch version contains a verse that is not on the album version; finding it is the quickest way to tell the two recordings apart.

## Concepts in Focus

She's Lost Control is a textbook case of {{ <concept_link name="post-punk" /> }}: the tension of punk kept, its speed and noise taken out, the bass moved to the front and the voice held at a distance.

Its {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} is the lead instrument. Played high on the neck, Hook's part is a melody in its own right, so much so that 808 State sampled the riff for Contrique in 1993.

This page reviews an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }}. The first recording, made in April 1979 at Strawberry Studios in Stockport, runs just under four minutes on Unknown Pleasures. The second, recorded at the same studio in March 1980, is longer and more electronic and adds a verse. The {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }}, Martin Hannett, produced both; live, Joy Division played it faster and more aggressively.

## Artistic Context

Joy Division first played the song live in June 1978. Factory released the 12-inch version in 1980: in the UK as the B-side of Atmosphere, in the US as the A-side of the 12-inch FACUS 2, with typography by Peter Saville on the sleeve. It reappeared on the compilation Substance in 1988 and, in 2011, on Rhino's TOTAL: From Joy Division to New Order, as its fourth track.

## Impact and Reception

The song has been covered by Grace Jones, Girls Against Boys, Siobhan Fahey and Spoek Mathambo. Two films about Factory and the band dramatise its recording: 24 Hour Party People suggests the drums were recorded on the studio roof, and Control shows Morris spraying an aerosol can into a microphone as percussion. Play the 1979 and 1980 versions back to back and listen for what the second one adds.
