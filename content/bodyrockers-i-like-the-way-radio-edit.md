## Genre and Mood

**Bodyrockers** built **I Like The Way** from dance music, electro and rock. It is energetic, bold and playful, a track that wants you moving from the first bar and makes no apologies about it.

## Song Summary

The song is a direct, flirtatious celebration of someone's moves on the dance floor, carried by a crunching guitar riff, a strutting beat and a hook simple enough to shout along to after one listen. Bodyrockers fused the punch of a rock band with the loop-based drive of club music, landing on a sound that felt at home both in indie discos and on mainstream radio. The production is big and glossy, every element pushed to the front: the guitars buzz, the drums slap and the bass pumps in time with the kick. There is no hidden depth and no pretence of one, only a groove designed with total clarity.

## Musical Breakdown

The radio edit keeps the track lean, wasting no time between riff and hook.

### Intro

A heavy, distorted riff and a pounding beat arrive together, setting a confident, swaggering tone.

### Verse 1

The vocal enters with a cool, half-spoken delivery, riding the groove rather than fighting it.

### Chorus

The hook hits hard and catchy, the whole track built around that one irresistible line about the way you move.

### Bridge

The arrangement drops back for a moment, letting the riff and drums breathe before the final push.

### Outro

The last chorus repeats with full force before the track cuts out, leaving the riff ringing.

## Concepts in Focus

The track rests on a {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }}, the short repeated guitar figure that anchors it from the first second. In rock the riff is the band's signature; here it does the job a synth stab would do in a club track, repeating over a dance beat until it becomes the record's identity. An Australian review at the time singled out exactly that rocking guitar line as what lifted it above an ordinary dance single.

Above the riff sits the {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }}: a single line about the way someone moves, repeated rather than developed. The riff and the vocal hook are two different kinds of hook working together, one for the body and one for the voice.

This page reviews the {{ <concept_link name="radio edit" /> }}, the version cut to around three minutes for radio. It starts with the riff instead of a long mix-in intro and drops repeats a DJ would want, which is part of why the song worked so well in adverts and on daytime playlists.

## Artistic Context

Bodyrockers were a duo of the Australian house DJ Kaz James, once a protégé of Frankie Knuckles, and the British singer-guitarist Dylan Burns from Herne Bay. They formed in 2004 after Burns played guitar over one of James's DJ sets in Australia, toured Australia and Asia together, and recorded "I Like the Way" in a single day in the UK. They wrote and produced it themselves. Mercury released it in the UK on 18 April 2005, and it appeared on their self-titled album the same year. It arrived at a moment when dance-rock crossovers were everywhere, but few hit as directly.

## Impact and Reception

The single debuted and peaked at number three on the UK Singles Chart and finished 2005 as the 16th best-selling single of the year in the UK; it also reached number 12 in Australia, topped the Australian club chart and was nominated for Best Dance Release at the 2005 ARIA Music Awards. It went gold in the UK, Australia and New Zealand, and its afterlife was in advertising, with campaigns for BMW, Citroën, Ford, Diet Coke and others, and the 2005 Victoria's Secret fashion show. Two decades on, that riff still identifies the song within a second or two. Put it on and watch the room change.
