## Genre and Mood

**Gorillaz** made **Murdoc Is God** the loudest corner of their Demon Days era: two minutes and twenty-six seconds that PAPER called an ear-shattering piece of noise rock and Spectrum Culture a crunchy rock jam. Intense and dark are this page's words for what those critics heard; irreverent is its reading of a track that declares the band's own cartoon bassist divine.

## Song Summary

There is no story here. The lyric pages give its words as the title, repeated, and Sputnikmusic's reviewer heard the music as an unforgiving snippet of ultra-distorted bass pummeling. MusicBrainz credits the writing to Gorillaz and the production to Jason Cox, James Dring and the band, with Cox engineering and mixing and Dring on programming. It is short and blunt, a snippet in the reviewer's word, more a sketch of a character than a song about him.

## Musical Breakdown

No review describes the track section by section, so this breakdown keeps to the elements the sources name.

### Bass

The bass carries everything. Distorted until it pummels, in Sputnikmusic's word, it is the surface of the track, not its floor.

### Chant

Over it, the one line: Murdoc is god. Repetition turns it from a sentence into a chant.

### Outro

On D-Sides the next track is Spitting Out the Demons, and the same reviewer heard the two as a pair: this one precedes and complements that funky, considerably less distorted groove, the noise before the dance.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="distortion" /> }} overdrives a signal until it clips and gains harmonics. On a bass it adds grit and weight at once, and here it is pushed so far that the instrument becomes the whole texture.

Murdoc Niccals is the fictional bassist of Gorillaz, the virtual band Damon Albarn and Jamie Hewlett formed in 1998. A {{ <concept_link name="stage persona" /> }} is usually a performer's mask; in Gorillaz the masks are the band, and a track named after one of them extends the cartoon into the music itself. Hear the contrast with the bright single that first carried it, {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-de-la-soul-feel-good-inc.md" /> }}.

The band's channel posted an official HD {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} for the track in 2010; PAPER remembered the track coming with a visualizer of a quivering inverted crucifix.

## Artistic Context

The track first appeared in 2005 on the Japanese CD EP of Feel Good Inc. and on the UK DVD single of Dirty Harry, released on 21 November 2005. In November 2007 Parlophone and Virgin gathered it onto D-Sides, a compilation of B-sides, bonus tracks and previously unreleased songs from the Demon Days sessions, recorded at Studio 13 in London and in Hong Kong. Spectrum Culture heard it as harking back to the straightforward stylistic fusion of the band's first album.

## Impact and Reception

D-Sides reached number 63 in the UK, 71 on the Billboard 200 and number 2 on the US dance albums chart, with generally favourable reviews. Murdoc Is God stayed obscure, but in 2022 PAPER, writing about the Cracker Island era, called its title the motto behind the band's latest developments. Play it loud, then let Spitting Out the Demons answer.
