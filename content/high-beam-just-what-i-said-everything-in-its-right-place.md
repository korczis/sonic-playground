## Genre and Mood

**High Beam** built **Just What I Said (Everything In Its Right Place)** from two sources far apart in time. The project's own site says it marries the soulful voice of Johnny Adams with a reinterpretation of Radiohead's chord progression. Apple Music files it as electronic and a DJ database as deep house. Soulful and hypnotic are this page's reading of that pairing: a voice from New Orleans soul over a progression whose original Pitchfork called, in 2009, too hypnotic to apologise for.

## Song Summary

The single came out on 14 February 2025 as a self-release and runs 3:16. Apple Music credits Connor Wittig as composer, lyricist and producer. The bracket in the title points to Everything in Its Right Place, the song that opens Radiohead's 2000 album Kid A. Which Johnny Adams recording the voice comes from, no source we found names.

## Musical Breakdown

No review walks through the track, so this breakdown stays with the three elements the sources name.

### The Harmony

The chords come from Radiohead's song, reinterpreted. How closely they follow it, and on which instruments, the sources do not say.

### The Voice

Adams, who died in 1998, was called the Tan Canary for a voice of several octaves, swooping mannerisms and falsetto. Listen for how much of that range the track uses.

### The Beat

Under both sits a dance beat, filed under electronic and deep house; its pattern and tempo no review describes.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="chord progression" /> }} is the order in which a song's chords move. Radiohead's is unusual: Steve Reich, who rewrote the song for his 2012 piece Radio Rewrite, observed that it is in F minor but never lands on the F minor chord, so it never truly comes home. A progression that does not resolve can circle for as long as a dance track needs.

The single is a {{ <concept_link name="self-release" /> }}: Deezer lists High Beam as its label and Apple Music shows the copyright as High Beam's own. The artist's site calls it his viral release.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} arrived later. In October 2026 High Beam posted a video by Josiah Billock, writing that it came a year and a half after the track and thanking everyone who had made it part of their lives.

## Artistic Context

High Beam is the musical project of Connor Wittig, an electronic producer and DJ based in Vancouver. His site lists official remixes for Moby and Teddy Swims and an EP in progress. Johnny Adams, born in New Orleans in 1932, began in gospel, moved to secular music in 1959 and had his biggest hits in the late 1960s with Release Me and Reconsider Me.
