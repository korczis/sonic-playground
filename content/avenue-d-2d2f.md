## Genre and Mood

**Avenue D** made **2D2F** inside the New York electroclash scene of the early 2000s. Discogs files the album it comes from under electro and synth-pop, and the EP later named after it under electro, punk and synth-pop. The mood is provocative and irreverent: a crude joke turned into a chorus by a duo whose singles include Do I Look Like a Slut?

## Song Summary

2D2F is a song about drinking and sex. The AV Club's review of DJ-Kicks quoted its chorus, a warning not to get too drunk for sex, and the title is that phrase in shorthand. Discogs credits the writing to the two members, Debbie Attias and Daphne Gomez-Mena, with Mikey Lamar; Roger Greenawalt recorded and mixed it, and the digital release from !K7 credits him as producer, with a 2002 copyright on the recording. This page's video is the 2:58 version from Bootleg, the album the duo sold through their own website in 2004.

## Musical Breakdown

No published review describes the beat, the synths or the form of 2D2F, so this breakdown stays with what the sources document: the chorus, the voices and the song's life on stage.

### Chorus

The chorus carries the song. It is the one part a critic quoted, and the part the title abbreviates.

### Vocals

Debbie D. and Daphne D. sing their own words; how they split the lines no source says, and this review does not guess.

### On Stage

The band's own channel later posted a video for 2D2F cut from live performances, edited by Daphne D. herself.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }} fused 1980s electro and new wave with punk attitude, deadpan voices and a taste for performance. Avenue D came straight out of it: Wikipedia calls them protégés of the DJ Larry Tee, at whose parties they performed. Hear the scene's art-school side in {{ <track_link source="fischerspooner-emerge.md" /> }}.

A {{ <concept_link name="dj mix" /> }} carried 2D2F furthest. On 19 April 2004 Studio !K7 released DJ-Kicks: Erlend Øye, and the Norwegian singer placed 2D2F seventh, between Alan Braxe and Fred Falke's Rubicon and The Rapture's I Need Your Love.

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is the chorus itself. A hook is built to stick, and this one stuck even with a critic who disliked it: the AV Club quoted it rather than describe the music.

## Artistic Context

Avenue D were Debbie D. and Daphne D., who came from Miami, formed the duo in New York in 2001 and mixed hip hop, electro and new wave. Bootleg was the first album they released; in 2005 a 500-copy 12-inch, the 2D2F E.P., was pressed in Japan with the song as its opener. The duo announced their split in November 2007.

## Impact and Reception

The AV Club's Andy Battaglia called 2D2F one of Øye's dicey selections, electroclash at its cloying worst, while praising the smart line the mix drew through dance music. The song still named an EP and travelled on the DJ-Kicks series. Play it loud, with a sense of humour.
