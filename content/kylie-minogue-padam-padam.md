## Genre and Mood

**Kylie Minogue**'s **Padam Padam** is pop for the dance floor, a synth-pop and dance-pop single only two minutes and 46 seconds long. Its subject is desire, its title the sound of a heartbeat, and its mood moves between sultry and silly: Pitchfork's Harry Tafoya called it one of the album's camp highs, The Guardian's Owen Myers a shiny bubble of dance-pop joy.

## Song Summary

Ina Wroldsen and Peter Rycroft, who produces as Lostboy, wrote it in two days in February 2022 in a small London studio, for no artist in particular. The word padam came from Wroldsen's English mother-in-law, who described her heart going "ped-ou"; they bent it into something that sounded right. Minogue recorded her vocal herself, on a laptop in a London hotel room. Wikipedia places it on C phrygian dominant, a Mixolydian scale with its second and sixth lowered, which adds to its tense feel.

## Musical Breakdown

No source gives times; this follows Lostboy's account to Headliner.

### Intro

A single padam, pitched down an octave, saturated and soaked in reverb, retuned to each chord so it plays almost like an instrument.

### Verse and Chorus

A hard kick and a dark bass drive it, with industrial textures in the synths. Halfway through the first chorus a vocoder arrives and returns in the second; an eight-second reverb swells on beats one and three, so the vocal keeps opening and closing.

### Outro

Eight to sixteen bars of drums and bass fade out alone.

## Concepts in Focus

The pulse you feel is {{ <concept_link name="sidechain compression" /> }}: a compressor keyed to the kick pushes the synths and the voice down on every hit, so the whole mix breathes in time, a heartbeat built into the production.

The {{ <concept_link name="vocoder" /> }} adds a robotic edge against Minogue's sweet lead, the same contrast Daft Punk used on {{ <track_link source="daft-punk-instant-crush-feat-julian-casablancas.md" /> }}.

{{ <concept_link name="pitch shifting" text="Pitch shifting" /> }} turns the opening padam from a word into a sound, an octave below her voice.

The ending borrows from the {{ <concept_link name="extended mix" /> }}: a DJ can mix out of those bare bars, and Lostboy saw them as adding underground credibility to a shiny pop record.

## Artistic Context

Padam Padam was the lead single and opening track of Tension, Minogue's sixteenth studio album, released on 22 September 2023 by BMG and her own company, Darenote, and a number one in the UK and Australia. Wroldsen had earlier co-written and, uncredited, sung {{ <track_link source="calvin-harris-disciples-how-deep-is-your-love.md" /> }}. Sophie Muller shot the video at a motel in Sun Valley, Los Angeles, with Minogue in a red Mugler catsuit.

## Impact and Reception

BBC Radio 1 at first declined to playlist it, which drew accusations of ageism, then added it. It peaked at number eight in the UK, topped the US Dance Airplay chart, spread through TikTok and memes, and became a gay anthem played at pride parades. Stereogum's Tom Breihan called it a sleek, thumping, catchy-as-hell dance-pop jam, and in 2024 it won the first Grammy for Best Pop Dance Recording, her first Grammy since 2004.
