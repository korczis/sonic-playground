> The title *"Euro Trash Girl"* belongs to an American rock song, and Chicks On Speed took it as a found object: a cover recorded by three art students in Munich, played as cultural critique and playful rebellion.

### Part 1: The Anthem of Playful Rebellion

"Euro-Trash Girl" was written by the American band Cracker, first released on their 1992 EP Tucson and then hidden at the end of their 1993 album Kerosene Hat; the single version ran to eight minutes. Chicks On Speed turned it into something else entirely: a short, deadpan electronic track that treats the song less as a story than as material for an art piece.

Electroclash, the scene Chicks On Speed are best known for helping to start, set itself against the polish of mainstream pop and the seriousness of much electronic music. It borrowed punk's do-it-yourself attitude and the cold synthesizers of early-1980s synth-pop, and it valued irony, fashion and performance as much as songs.

---

### Part 2: Electroclash Energy

*"Euro Trash Girl"* falls squarely in **electroclash**, combining the raw attitude of **punk** with the synthetic textures of **experimental pop**. Its **rebellious**, **ironic** and **playful** tone comes from the distance between the song and the performers: an American alt-rock tale sung back by a European art collective.

## About the Band

**Chicks On Speed** describe themselves as a feminist art ensemble, formed in 1997 at the Academy of Fine Arts in Munich by the art students Alex Murray-Leslie and Melissa Logan, with Kiki Moorse, who came from fashion journalism, as the third member until 2006. They began by staging exhibitions and illegal parties at the Seppi Bar, an art project modelled on Zürich's Dada-era Cabaret Voltaire, and coined their name when they needed one to sell 25 collage paintings made in a single night. Their work has always spanned performance, fashion, textile and instrument design and art education as well as music.

## Song Summary

*"Euro Trash Girl"* opens with minimal beats that set a hypnotic rhythm, and the vocals arrive flat and half-spoken in the electroclash manner. Cracker's song is a wry, rambling tale in the first person; Chicks On Speed keep the words and change the frame, so a story about searching for a girl turns into a cool, ironic performance about the cliché itself. The 1998 single was produced by Tobi Neumann, and the Discogs notes for the 2000 album describe its version as a reworking of the cover the German band F.S.K. had recorded in 1996.

## Sound Breakdown

### Intro: Raw and Minimalist

The track begins with stripped-down beats and a raw, lo-fi feel. The deliberate simplicity amplifies its punk spirit.

### Midsection: Satirical Layers

As the song goes on, retro-futuristic electronic elements wind around the voice. The irony is in the delivery: the words are sung as if quoted.

### Climax: Unapologetic Rebellion

The final stretch gets noisier and looser rather than tidier, an ending that fits a group more interested in attitude than in polish.

## Concepts in Focus

Euro Trash Girl is a {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} that changes almost everything except the words. Cracker's original is an eight-minute American alt-rock song with guitars and a storyteller's voice; Chicks On Speed's version is a four-minute electronic track sung by three women in Munich. Covers were central to the group's early output: their first single was a cover of The Normal's "Warm Leatherette", and "Mind Your Own Business" by Delta 5 and "Kaltes Klares Wasser" by the German punk band Malaria! followed. Choosing which songs to cover was part of their statement.

The record also comes straight out of a {{ <concept_link name="club culture" /> }}. Alex Murray-Leslie worked the door and curated live-art events at Munich's techno club Ultraschall, and through that scene the group met Peter Wacha, known as Upstart, of the Disko B label, who joined their own label Go Records. Go was a "suicide label": its catalogue numbers counted down from ten to zero, and Euro Trash Girl, a split 7-inch with the band Mäuse, was GO 09. Listen to the track as an artefact of that world, where art openings, illegal bars and techno clubs blurred together.

That world became known as {{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }}, and Wikipedia counts Chicks On Speed among the catalysts of the genre. Euro Trash Girl has the scene's signatures in miniature: a deadpan voice, cheap-sounding electronics worn as a badge, and a cover chosen as much for what it says as for how it sounds.

## Overall Vibe

"Euro Trash Girl" is less a song than a gesture: an American road story claimed and reframed by a European art collective. It invites listeners to dance, laugh and think about how much of a song's meaning depends on who sings it.

## Conclusion

Chicks On Speed's Euro Trash Girl was one of five singles that preceded their 2000 debut album Chicks On Speed Will Save Us All!, and it also appeared on The Un-Releases and The Re-Releases of the Un-Releases that year. Those records helped define electroclash before the genre had a name, and a few years later the group's collaboration with Peaches, "We Don't Play Guitars", became a worldwide club hit.
