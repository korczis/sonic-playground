## Genre and Mood

**Felix Da Housecat**'s **Monkey Cage** is electronic music from a Chicago house veteran working in his electro mode: groovy, edgy and, for a track barely two minutes long, surprisingly intense. It does not try to be a song in the usual sense. It is a short, taut passage on an album designed to be heard from start to finish.

## Song Summary

Monkey Cage is the ninth of sixteen tracks on Virgo Blaktro and the Movie Disco and runs just over two minutes. Felix Stallings Jr. wrote and produced it himself. Sources say little about the track on its own, and this review describes only what they describe. Iain Moffat of No Ripcord counted it among the finer moments of Felix's catalogue and gave the clearest picture of its sound: a catwalk thwack underpinned by an encroaching alarm. The beat struts; something behind it keeps getting closer.

## Musical Breakdown

No source times the track or breaks it into sections, so the parts below are the three things the sources let a listener listen for.

### Intro

On the album the track follows It's Been a Long Time. Rachel Couch of The Cavalier Daily heard the whole record riding one beat and its songs melding into one another, so listen for where one track ends and the next begins.

### Main Section

The body of the track is the runway beat and the alarm that No Ripcord heard closing in underneath it: a groove you could walk to, with tension built into its sound rather than into a melody.

### Outro

After about two minutes the track hands over to I Seem 2B the 1, another piece of under two minutes, and the album moves on.

## Concepts in Focus

Virgo Blaktro and the Movie Disco is a {{ <concept_link name="concept album" /> }}. Wikipedia describes it as centred on a revival of 1970s and 1980s black music, and Felix spoke of a black, soulful groove and compared its sound to Sly & the Family Stone. A concept album explains why a track like Monkey Cage exists at all: at two minutes it reads as a scene in a larger film rather than a single built to stand alone.

It is also a portrait of the {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} as artist. Felix is credited as the only writer and producer, so every sound here, the beat and the alarm included, is his choice. For the same producer's earlier electroclash, hear {{ <track_link source="felix-da-house-cat-silverscreen.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Felix Stallings Jr., born in Chicago in 1971, belongs to the second wave of Chicago house; acid house pioneer DJ Pierre helped him release his first single in 1987. Kittenz and Thee Glitz brought him mainstream attention in 2001, and Devin Dazzle & the Neon Fever followed in 2004. Virgo Blaktro, recorded in Barcelona and Antwerp in 2006 and 2007, came out in late September 2007 on Different Recordings and on 2 October on Nettwerk, after leaking online that August.

## Impact and Reception

Reviews were split: AllMusic gave the album four stars, Pitchfork 5.4 out of 10, and No Ripcord, despite complaining of vocoder overload, called its maker one of the best techno artists of the day. Play Monkey Cage in its place on the album, between its neighbours, and hear the alarm arrive.
