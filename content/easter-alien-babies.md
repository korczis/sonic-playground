## Genre and Mood

**Easter** made **Alien Babies** as electropop, and Crack Magazine has called the duo a moody synth-pop band with a calm sense of melancholy. That is the frame for a song quirky in what it says and eerie in how little it explains: a voice listing strange things as if none of them were strange.

## Song Summary

Alien Babies is the second of ten tracks on The Softest Hard, the Berlin duo's second album, released on 20 December 2012 and, according to its credits, written and recorded by the band at Fluma Studio. Its lyric is an inventory. Almost every line begins with "I see", and what follows mixes food and its packaging with small creatures and the alien babies of the title. AQNB later described it as an eerie inventory of foodstuffs and their packaging. No review describes the arrangement of this song, so this page says little about its sound.

## Musical Breakdown

The lyric sheet, published by the band, gives the song its shape; the notes below follow it.

### Verse 1

The list begins with the title and runs on: soy protein, a finger reaching out, the world spinning, bottles of ginseng, horseshoe crabs on a shore.

### Chorus

The refrain is a single image, mushrooms being born out of shrimps, repeated. It is the most absurd line in the list and the one the song returns to.

### Bridge

Then the capitals of the lyric sheet give way to lower case, and the list stops. The voice is now watching someone's jaw at the disco, too far away to touch, so happy it could die: the only moment of direct feeling in the song.

### Verse 2

The inventory resumes with silverfish on a bathroom floor, pearl tapioca and soft-centred eggs on a hotel plate, before the refrain and the jaw passage return.

## Concepts in Focus

Alien Babies is a {{ <concept_link name="list song" /> }}. Instead of a story it adds item to item, and the mood comes from the accumulation: ordinary groceries, odd animals and impossible births are given the same weight.

The band is known for {{ <concept_link name="spoken word" /> }} delivery: Wikipedia and AQNB both describe Stix Omar's voice as monotone and half-spoken, and AQNB hears Max Boss's dark beats suspended in a kind of torpor. Omar told AQNB that very often Boss makes the beat first and she has to force words onto it.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, posted by the band in December 2012, credits a dancer, Britta Thie, crystals courtesy of the Berlin Creamcake collective, and the same Fluma Studio.

## Artistic Context

Easter are the Norwegian vocalist Stix Omar and the German Max Boss. Crack reports that they crossed paths in 2005 and began making music and short films together, under the umbrella name Easterjesus. Their debut album, Ur a Great Babe, came out in 2011; Wikipedia describes abstract lyrics about sexuality and hedonism. Compare the flat delivery with {{ <track_link source="miss-kittin-frank-sinatra.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

When AQNB interviewed the band in 2016, it treated Alien Babies as an older song and an early sign of the food imagery critics might read as a fixation, a reading Omar rejected. Read the list along with the voice, and decide what it adds up to.
