## Genre and Mood

**Jan Blomqvist** turns **Big Jet Plane** into deep house: electronic, melodic and stretched out over almost six minutes. It is a cover in the truest sense, dreamy, longing and melancholic, keeping the soul of the original while placing it somewhere new.

## Song Summary

Angus & Julia Stone's song is a gentle, romantic dream of flying away with someone on a big jet plane, simple and almost childlike in its imagery. Blomqvist slows it down and wraps it in warm, glowing synths, a soft kick and drifting atmospheric layers, then sings it himself in his weary, intimate voice. The result is less a lullaby than a late-night flight, the city lights shrinking below. The memorable detail is the patience: the track takes its time, letting the melody unfold over the beat rather than rushing toward a drop.

## Musical Breakdown

The rework follows a long, slow arc typical of melodic house sets.

### Intro

Soft pads and a subtle pulse rise out of the silence, setting a calm, nocturnal atmosphere.

### Verse 1

Blomqvist's voice enters close and understated, delivering the familiar lyrics in a hushed, introspective tone.

### Build-up

The groove gathers weight, with bass and percussion lifting the track gradually off the ground.

### Chorus

The famous hook arrives over the full groove, the image of the plane transformed into a gentle, floating release.

### Outro

The layers thin out slowly, leaving the pulse and a few melodic echoes fading into the distance.

## Artistic Context

The song was written by Angus Stone and first recorded under his alias Lady of the Sunshine on the 2009 album Smoking Gun, before Angus & Julia Stone re-recorded it for their 2010 album Down the Way. That version topped the Triple J Hottest 100 of 2010. Jan Blomqvist, a German producer, singer and live performer from the Berlin scene, released his electronic version in 2011, and it became one of the tracks that established his signature blend of songwriting and melodic house, later heard on records like "Time Again".

## Impact and Reception

Blomqvist's cover became a favourite of the melodic house world, frequently played live and remixed by others, and it introduced the song to dancefloors far from its folk origins. It stands as one of the best examples of how a song can travel between genres and still feel sincere. Put on headphones, dim the lights and let it lift you off the ground.
