## Genre and Mood

**Florence + The Machine** made their third album a turn toward plainer, more personal songs, and **Hiding** belongs to its edges: a bonus track of the deluxe edition. It sits in the band's alternative and indie rock, and its feelings here, introspective and emotional, come from the album it was made for rather than from any review of the song, because none describes it.

## Song Summary

Hiding runs 3:52 and is credited to Florence Welch and James Ford, who also produced it. That credit is what sets it apart. Markus Dravs produced almost all of How Big, How Blue, How Beautiful; Hiding is the only song of the standard or deluxe edition with Ford at the desk. Its title happens to echo how Welch described the album: songs dealing in reality, she said, which frightened her because she was not hiding behind anything.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="bonus track" /> }} is a song added to one edition of an album. The deluxe How Big, How Blue, How Beautiful added five: Hiding and Make Up Your Mind, then demos of Which Witch, Third Eye and the title track. Hiding is one of only two finished new songs among them.

The {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} shapes how a song sounds. Welch chose Dravs because he had produced Björk's Homogenic and could balance organic and electronic sound; she wanted something big with warmth and gentleness, closer to a band. Ford's history with her goes further back: he produced Dog Days Are Over and three other songs on the debut Lungs. He also co-wrote two songs of the standard album, Caught and St. Jude, both produced by Dravs. Hear {{ <track_link source="florence-the-machine-caught.md" /> }} next to Hiding: the same writing partnership, two producers.

## Artistic Context

How Big, How Blue, How Beautiful came out on Island on 29 May 2015 after a year's break in which, Welch said, she had a bit of a nervous breakdown. It is more refined and stripped-down than the earlier records, with folk, blues and gospel influences. James Ford is a member of Simian Mobile Disco and The Last Shadow Puppets and a producer for Arctic Monkeys, Klaxons and Gorillaz.

## Impact and Reception

Hiding was never a single, and no review found for this page discusses it. The album around it entered the UK chart at number one, became the band's first number one on the Billboard 200, was shortlisted for the Mercury Prize and earned five Grammy nominations.
