## Genre and Mood

**Shit Robot**'s **Losing My Patience**, sung by **Alexis Taylor** of Hot Chip, is electronic dance music with a synth-pop heart: a producer's track built to move a floor, carried by a voice that sounds anything but sure of itself. It is groovy, but the feeling it leaves is intimate, closer to a confession than a party.

## Song Summary

Losing My Patience is the third track of From the Cradle to the Rave, the 2010 debut album of Marcus Lambkin, the Dublin-born DJ and producer who records as Shit Robot. MusicBrainz credits Taylor with the lead vocal. The reviews describe the track mostly through that voice: PopMatters heard it quiver like Bryan Ferry's, and Resident Advisor wrote that the song came across as a beefier La Roux while carrying an understated vulnerability. No source describes the lyric in any detail, so this page does not interpret it.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} shapes this record more than usual. From the Cradle to the Rave is a producer's album with a different guest almost everywhere: Nancy Whang, the Juan Maclean, Planningtorock, James Murphy. On Losing My Patience the guest is the one who makes the song recognisable, so much that reviewers compared it to other singers rather than to other producers.

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} is the other half. Resident Advisor reached back to 1982-style synth-pop and to electro in the old, vintage sense to describe the album: an older electronic vocabulary, played with precision.

The song also had a second life as a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }}: the April 2011 DFA single paired the original with a seven-minute Hot Chip remix and remixes and a dub by the Unabombers.

## Artistic Context

Lambkin met James Murphy in New York in 2000 and the two started DJing together; he moved to rural Germany in 2004, began producing his own music and has released on Murphy's label DFA since 2006. The Line of Best Fit heard the album as an autobiography of his life in dance music, from Detroit house and techno to New York electro. Murphy co-wrote several of its tracks and mixed it.

## Impact and Reception

The album drew mixed to positive notices: Entertainment.ie and NME reviewed it favourably, while PopMatters found much of it derivative. Several reviews named Losing My Patience, and what they named was its singer. Listen to Taylor's voice against the machines.
