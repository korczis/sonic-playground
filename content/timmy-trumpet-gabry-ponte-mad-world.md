## Genre and Mood

**Timmy Trumpet** and **Gabry Ponte** turn **Mad World** into a dance record without letting go of its sadness. It is electronic dance music built for a festival crowd, but it is also a cover of one of the most melancholic songs in pop, and the track lives in that contradiction: melancholic in its words, euphoric in its drop, bittersweet in the space between them.

## Song Summary

Mad World began in 1982 as a new wave single by Tears for Fears, written by Roland Orzabal and sung by Curt Smith. In 2001 Michael Andrews and Gary Jules slowed it to voice and piano for the film Donnie Darko, and that version became a UK number one in 2003. Timmy Trumpet and Gabry Ponte start from the Andrews and Jules arrangement: a pitched-up vocal, sung here by Julianna Isabella Pollifrone, its piano melody turned into the drop, and the trumpet running through it all.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

The vocal is there from the start, pitched up and lighter than Gary Jules's hushed delivery, so the familiar words arrive before the beat has decided what kind of song this is.

### Build-up

Drums come in early and the tension rises, the standard grammar of a festival track, with the lyric still floating on top.

### Drop

The drop is the famous piano melody, played as the peak rather than as a lament. It is a clever choice: the most recognisable part of the 2001 version becomes the moment the crowd is waiting for.

### Outro

Trumpet runs accent the beat throughout, and they are what to follow to the end: Timmy Trumpet's signature on someone else's song.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} can stay faithful or change a song's meaning. This one is a cover of a cover: it keeps Orzabal's song and the Andrews and Jules arrangement, but moves both from the bedroom to the main stage. Timmy Trumpet said of the original that it is the song he wishes he had written.

The {{ <concept_link name="drop" /> }} is the point where a dance track releases the tension of its build-up. Most drops are built on synthesizers or bass; this one is built on a piano line that millions already know, which is why it works as both a release and a recognition.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Shaun David Barker and Matt Henry, was filmed in Philadelphia the day after the 2020 US election, amid protests and unrest, and follows a young woman through the end of a hard year. It ties the cover to its moment, a year of lockdowns.

## Artistic Context

Timmy Trumpet is Timothy Jude Smith from Sydney, who started playing the trumpet at four, won a scholarship to the Conservatorium of Music and built a DJ career around playing his instrument live; his breakthrough was Freaks with the New Zealand rapper Savage. Gabry Ponte is the Italian DJ and producer who co-founded Eiffel 65, whose Blue (Da Ba Dee) sold more than eight million copies. Mad World came out as a single through Smash The House on 13 November 2020 and became the title track of Timmy Trumpet's debut album, released on 4 December.

## Impact and Reception

Dance media greeted it as a reimagining rather than a remix, noting how the piano section and the trumpet carry the track. It takes an old song about feeling alone and hands it to a crowd. Play it after the Gary Jules version and listen to how the same piano line can mean two different things.
