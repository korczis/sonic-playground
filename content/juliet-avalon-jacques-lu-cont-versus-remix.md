## Genre and Mood

**Juliet**'s **Avalon (Jacques Lu Cont Versus Remix)** is house and electro-pop from the middle of the 2000s, an electronic club version of a song critics found haunting. The BBC heard the original as hauntingly simple and Juliet's voice as celestial; Metro Weekly heard a haunting but beautiful melody at a fast, feet-moving clip, the dreamy and energetic pull a club remix of it has to keep.

## Song Summary

Avalon was the debut single of the American singer Juliet Richardson and the advance single from her album Random Order. Metro Weekly read it as a song about the afterlife, written around opposites: rising and falling, losing and finding. The song was written by Juliet and Stuart Price, who also produced it; Jacques Lu Cont is one of Price's own aliases, so this is the producer remixing his own record. The label dates the recording to 2004, and its official upload runs six minutes and thirty-six seconds.

## Musical Breakdown

No review describes this remix section by section, so what follows names what to listen for rather than claiming what happens when.

### The Vocal

Juliet's voice is the thread; the BBC called its timbre celestial.

### The Synths and the Beat

Metro Weekly heard lush synthesized touches and a fast, danceable pace in Avalon; listen for how much of that the remix keeps in front of the beat.

### The Long Form

At more than six and a half minutes, this is a length made for club play rather than radio; listen for how long the beat runs without the voice.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} usually brings an outside ear to a song. Here the remixer is the {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} of the original: the BBC credited Price with the blueprint of Juliet's sound, and the official credits list him as writer, producer and mixer of this very version. Wikipedia describes his remixes in general as club-led beats with arpeggios and riffs, keeping the original vocal arrangement while recasting everything around it.

The same description mentions vocal lines faded and filtered for a climactic effect, which is a {{ <concept_link name="filter sweep" /> }}: a filter slowly opened or closed so that a sound moves from muffled to bright. Listen for whether the vocal or the synths open up this way as the track builds.

## Artistic Context

Juliet had fronted the pop-rock band 1 Plus 1 before she was introduced to Price in 2004. Random Order followed in August 2005 on Virgin, and the BBC heard in it electronic house with pop sensibilities. Price, born in Paris and raised in Reading, had recorded as Les Rythmes Digitales and with Zoot Woman. He first worked for Madonna by remixing tracks from her album Music through its producer Mirwais, heard on his own in {{ <track_link source="mirwais-naiive-song.md" /> }}, and he is best known for his work on her 2005 album Confessions on a Dance Floor.

## Impact and Reception

Avalon reached number one on Billboard's Hot Dance Club Play chart in March 2005, the UK top 30 in April and the top five of the European dance chart in May. Radio 1 made it an Essential New Tune. Play it loud and follow the voice through the beat.
