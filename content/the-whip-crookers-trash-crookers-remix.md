## Genre and Mood

**The Whip** wrote **Trash** as dance-rock, a band's song; **Crookers** turned it into **Trash (Crookers Remix)** for the electro-house clubs of 2008. Spin called the original a snotty, exhilarating dance-rock rave-up, and the feelings here, gritty, defiant and energetic, come from that and from a Miami New Times critic who heard the remix leave the listener quaking in the gutter.

## Song Summary

Trash is a song about knowing who you are and daring to be so, whatever side of the tracks you come from, in the Miami New Times' reading. Its build-up starts on I wanna and lands on be trash, what the same critic called the crushing admission. The band's version runs past six minutes, and The Skinny thought its chorus so cool that it justified the long build-ups and repetitions. The Crookers remix keeps that length, at about six minutes. No review says what the duo changed.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} hands a recording to another producer. Remixing was much of Crookers' work in those years: Wikipedia lists their remixes for Armand Van Helden, Chromeo, Kelis and Diplo around the same years, and Trash among those of 2008.

The song itself is built on the {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }}: The Skinny's prolonged build-ups are its design, tension raised and held before the release, the same tool a club track lives on.

Trash also travelled through {{ <concept_link name="sync licensing" /> }}: Wikipedia lists the song in ITV's FA Cup adverts, a national Coors Light campaign from 2009, the theme of Channel 4's Rude Tube, and the racing games MotorStorm: Pacific Rift, Midnight Club: Los Angeles and Forza Motorsport 3. No source says which version each one used.

## Artistic Context

The Whip, from Manchester, released their first single in 2006; Bruce Carter and Danny Saville wrote the album's songs. Trash came out first in 2006 on Lavolta Records, then again on Southern Fried in 2008, the label of their debut album X Marks Destination, released on 24 March 2008 and produced by Jim Abbiss and Liam Howe. Crookers were Phra and Bot from Milan, active as a duo from 2003 to 2012, whose Day 'n' Nite with Kid Cudi reached number two in the UK.

## Impact and Reception

MusicBrainz lists the remix first on the remix single of Trash in March 2008. Remix Marks Destination followed in August, with it as the second track, and in 2012 it returned on From Then Until Now, a Crookers retrospective. Reviewing the 2009 US edition, Spin advised the band to study these remixes. Play the band's Trash first, then this, and hear the anthem become a club track.
