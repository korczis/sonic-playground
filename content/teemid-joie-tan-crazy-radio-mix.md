## Genre and Mood

**TEEMID** and **Joie Tan** take **Crazy**, Gnarls Barkley's frantic 2006 classic, and turn it into deep house for the hours after midnight. It is a cover, but an electronic reinvention more than a copy, and its mood is melancholic, dreamy and hypnotic, the same lyric about losing your mind now sung from inside a slow, glowing haze.

## Song Summary

The original raced on nervous energy; this version slows the pulse and lets the words sink in. Joie Tan sings the familiar lines softly and close to the microphone, and TEEMID wraps her voice in warm chords, a gentle four-on-the-floor kick and echoing space. The radio mix trims the arrangement to its essentials. What makes it memorable is the reframing: madness becomes a quiet, almost tender confession.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

Soft chords and a muted kick open the track, spacious and calm, a long way from the urgency of the original.

### Verse 1

Joie Tan's voice enters fragile and clear, delivering the famous opening line about losing her mind almost as a whisper. The beat stays restrained so the lyric can breathe.

### Build-up

Percussion and bass gather slowly, the groove gaining a hypnotic sway while the vocal climbs gently toward the hook.

### Chorus

The famous refrain lands over the full deep house groove, melancholy and danceable at the same time. It feels less like a question than a resigned smile.

### Bridge

A quieter passage lets the voice float almost alone over the chords, a moment of stillness in the middle of the night.

### Outro

The beat returns briefly and then thins away, leaving the vocal echoing into silence.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} can change a song's meaning without changing a word. Gnarls Barkley's Crazy, by CeeLo Green and Danger Mouse, was inspired by spaghetti western scores and built on a sample of Last Men Standing by Gian Piero and Gian Franco Reverberi, which is why the Reverberis share the songwriting credit. That record was tense and theatrical. Joie Tan and TEEMID keep the melody and the lyric and take away the urgency, so the same words sound like an admission instead of a performance.

The version on this page is the {{ <concept_link name="radio edit" /> }}, here called the radio mix. Dance producers usually make a longer club version for DJs and a shorter one, around three and a half minutes, for radio, streaming and video; the release also had a club edit and a remix EP. The radio mix gets to the voice quickly, which suits a record whose whole appeal is the voice.

## Artistic Context

Joie Tan, a singer from Singapore, first recorded her own cover of Crazy; the producer TEEMID, described in the press as French and Brussels-based, picked it up and reworked it into this version. It was circulating on music blogs by December 2013 and was released officially in 2014 through Warner Music Germany, with a remix EP and an official video directed by François Devauchelle of Vorticoso.

## Impact and Reception

The track circulated on blogs about a year before its official release, and its success was enough for the label to follow it with a whole remix EP. It is on this playground because it shows how a song everyone knows can still surprise. Put it on late, turn the lights down, and hear the lyric as if for the first time.
