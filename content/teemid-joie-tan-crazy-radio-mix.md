## Genre and Mood

**TEEMID** and **Joie Tan** take **Crazy**, Gnarls Barkley's frantic 2006 classic, and turn it into deep house for the hours after midnight. It is a cover, but an electronic reinvention more than a copy, and its mood is melancholic, dreamy and hypnotic, the same lyric about losing your mind now sung from inside a slow, glowing haze.

## Song Summary

The original raced on nervous energy; this version slows the pulse and lets the words sink in. Joie Tan sings the familiar lines softly and close to the microphone, and TEEMID wraps her voice in warm chords, a gentle four-on-the-floor kick and echoing space. The radio mix trims the arrangement to its essentials. What makes it memorable is the reframing: madness becomes a quiet, almost tender confession.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

Soft chords and a muted kick open the track, spacious and calm, a long way from the urgency of the original.

### Verse 1

Joie Tan's voice enters fragile and clear, delivering "I remember when I lost my mind" almost as a whisper. The beat stays restrained so the lyric can breathe.

### Build-up

Percussion and bass gather slowly, the groove gaining a hypnotic sway while the vocal climbs gently toward the hook.

### Chorus

The famous refrain lands over the full deep house groove, melancholy and danceable at the same time. It feels less like a question than a resigned smile.

### Bridge

A breakdown lets the voice float almost alone over the chords, a moment of stillness in the middle of the night.

### Outro

The beat returns briefly and then thins away, leaving the vocal echoing into silence.

## Artistic Context

"Crazy" was the breakout hit of Gnarls Barkley, the duo of CeeLo Green and Danger Mouse, and it was built on a sample from the Italian composers Gian Piero and Gianfranco Reverberi. Joie Tan, a singer from Singapore, first posted her own cover of it online; the producer TEEMID picked it up and reworked it into this version. It was released in 2014 through the German label TONSPIEL, with an official video directed by François Devauchelle and a remix EP.

## Impact and Reception

The track circulated on music blogs about a year before its official release, and its success was enough for the label to follow it with a whole remix EP. It is on this playground because it shows how a song everyone knows can still surprise. Put it on late, turn the lights down, and hear the lyric as if for the first time.
