## Genre and Mood

**Tak abyste to věděla** is a film song, Czech pop of 1964 written for the screen, and it is playful and romantic: a small comedy of courtship that works without the pictures. **Hana Hegerová**, **Karel Gott** and **Waldemar Matuška** sing it together, three of the best-known voices of Czechoslovak popular music in the 1960s in one song.

## Song Summary

The song comes from Kdyby tisíc klarinetů, the film musical made in 1964 from Jiří Suchý's stage play, with songs by Jiří Šlitr (music) and Suchý (words). Its situation is simple. In ČT24's description, Matuška and Gott court Hegerová in song, and the words cast Matuška as the "sladký a milý" (sweet and dear) suitor and Gott as the "hezký" (handsome) one. The title, roughly "just so you know", addresses a woman with the formal vy: a declaration made with good manners.

## Musical Breakdown

No source we found describes the arrangement or the order of the verses, so this breakdown follows the roles.

### The Two Suitors

Matuška and Gott take the parts of the two men. ČT24, writing about the film's ballad Tereza, calls Matuška's voice a full baritone; here it is set against Gott's, so the rivalry is heard as well as told.

### The Woman They Court

Hegerová is the one being courted, and her voice completes the trio. ČT24's verdict is that the song is well written for three different voice types, and that is its design: three singers, each recognisable at once.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="voice type" /> }} is the family a voice belongs to, by its range and its colour: baritone or tenor, alto or soprano. Šlitr wrote this song for three of them, and the casting does the storytelling. Before a word is understood, you hear two different men and one woman.

The song is also a lesson in the {{ <concept_link name="soundtrack" /> }} as a hit factory. In 1964 Supraphon released the film's songs on seven singles, this one paired with V opeře on Supraphon 013529, and in 1965 on an LP, so the songs were on record around the time the film reached cinemas in January 1965. Novinky.cz lists Tak abyste to věděla among the film's songs that became lasting hits. Today's streaming credit, Hegerová {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" text="featuring" /> }} Gott and Matuška, is a modern label for a trio.

## Artistic Context

Šlitr and Suchý were the central figures of the Semafor theatre in Prague in its golden era, and the film drew much of its cast from Semafor and the Rokoko. Hegerová, born in Bratislava in 1931 and later called the queen of Czechoslovak chanson, appeared at Semafor from 1961 to 1966. Matuška was a Semafor singer and actor, and Gott, at the start of a career that brought him 42 Český slavík awards, sings in the film too. The recording on the compilation Paběrky is the 1964 film recording.

## Impact and Reception

The film had about four million viewers in 1965, though Suchý recalled that Starci na chmelu, released earlier and in colour, won the contest between the two musicals. ČT24 placed this song among the hits the film gave its singers. Listen for the three voices and pick your suitor.
