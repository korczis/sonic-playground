## Genre and Mood

**Basement Jaxx** have always made house music that refuses to behave, and **Galactical** is a short, colourful blast of it. House, dance and electronic pop collide in a mood that is playful, euphoric and cosmic, the soundtrack to a party that has accidentally drifted into orbit.

## Song Summary

The track is a celebration with a science-fiction costume on, a three-minute burst of bounce led by singer Vula Malinga, who co-wrote it with the duo. Felix Buxton and Simon Ratcliffe pile up bright synths, a deep, rounded bass and tribal-sounding beats in the restless, maximal way that marks their best work. The memorable detail is its sense of fun: nothing here is cool or restrained, and that is the appeal.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

The track launches quickly, with a burst of synth colour and a beat that is already bouncing before you have found your feet. There is no slow fade in; the party is already on.

### Verse 1

Vula's airy voice floats over the groove while the bass pushes underneath, and the production keeps throwing small, twinkling sounds at the edges of the mix.

### Chorus

The hook is built for singing along, big, simple and slightly absurd in the best Jaxx tradition, with the beat pushing hard underneath.

### Bridge

The arrangement thins for a moment, a little weightlessness before the rocket fires again.

### Outro

The last section piles everything back in and then cuts away almost abruptly, which suits a track this short and this hyperactive.

## Concepts in Focus

Galactical is a good place to hear what a {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} does in house music. Stereogum described its bass as deep and amorphous, and that is the right word: it is less a sequence of sharp notes than a soft, heavy pressure that moves the track, leaving the top of the mix free for the voice and the synth sparkle.

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is the title, repeated and sung rather than built into a long melody, which is exactly what a dance track needs: something a crowd can catch on the first hearing. Basement Jaxx have built hooks like this since their late-1990s breakthrough, in the same era as {{ <track_link source="daft-punk-around-the-world.md" /> }}.

Finally the {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, credited to director Hadaya Turner, sends neon-striped extraterrestrials dancing across a backdrop that could be deep space or the deep sea. For a bonus track it did a lot of work: the video is how most listeners met Galactical, and its imagery has become the song's meaning.

## Artistic Context

The Brixton duo Basement Jaxx, Felix Buxton and Simon Ratcliffe, have been among British dance music's great eccentrics since the nineties, with hits such as "Where's Your Head At" and "Red Alert". "Galactical" is a bonus track on the special edition of *Junto*, their seventh studio album and their first since *Zephyr* in 2009, released on 25 August 2014 on their own Atlantic Jaxx label with PIAS. The duo produced the album themselves; Ratcliffe said they wanted it to sound positive, bright and uplifting after the darker *Zephyr*. "Galactical" was released ahead of the album, on 13 August 2014, with its video.

## Impact and Reception

*Junto* reached number 30 in the UK and number 3 on the UK dance albums chart, and drew generally favourable reviews (a Metacritic score of 71), with The Guardian calling it a decent return. "Never Say Never" was its biggest single, topping the US dance club chart, while "Galactical", a bonus with a psychedelic video, became a favourite for fans who like the duo at their silliest. Turn it up, dim the lights and float.
