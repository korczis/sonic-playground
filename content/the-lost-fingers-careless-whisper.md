## Genre and Mood

**The Lost Fingers** play **Careless Whisper** as gypsy jazz: an acoustic cover, for two guitars and a double bass, of one of the biggest pop songs of the 1980s. The mood is nostalgic for anyone who knows the original and bittersweet by inheritance: Michael drew the lyric from his own experience of infidelity, imagining a partner who finds out.

## Song Summary

George Michael released Careless Whisper on 23 July 1984, credited to him alone in Britain and to Wham! featuring George Michael in the United States. He and Andrew Ridgeley wrote it: Ridgeley the chord sequence, Michael the lyric and the saxophone melody that Steve Gregory played on tenor. The Lost Fingers keep the words and give everything else to the line-up of a Django Reinhardt-style trio. Their version runs 4:53, track nine of their debut album.

## Musical Breakdown

No source we found describes this recording part by part, so this review stays with its line-up and leaves the arrangement to your ears.

### The Strings

Christian Roberge and Byron Mikaloff play the guitars and Alex Morissette the double bass. There are no drums and no saxophone: whatever the original did with its studio band, these three instruments have to do here.

### The Voices

Roberge sings lead and Morissette backing vocals, in English, as on every track of Lost in the 80's except the French Incognito.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} usually keeps a song's melody and words and changes its setting. This one has a harder problem than {{ <track_link source="the-lost-fingers-pump-up-the-jam.md" /> }}, the album's opener: the most famous part of Careless Whisper is not sung at all.

That part is a {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }}, the distinctive tenor saxophone line Michael composed for the record. He insisted on a tenor sax, and the recording was slowed by a semitone so Gregory could reach the top note. Listen for how a trio with no horn deals with a song whose identity rests on one.

The trio's idiom is {{ <concept_link name="swing" /> }}: gypsy jazz began in Paris around 1934 with Django Reinhardt and Stéphane Grappelli, its rhythm guitar strumming a percussive pompe that stands in for drums. Reinhardt lost the use of two fingers in a fire in 1928, and his story stands behind the band's name.

## Artistic Context

Roberge and Mikaloff met the jazz double bassist Morissette at the Quebec Conservatory in 2006, and the three set out to pay homage to the 1980s, with humour, in gypsy jazz. Lost in the 80's came out in Quebec on 6 May 2008 through Tandem and Distribution Select and across Canada in January 2009, a whole album of 1980s songs from Technotronic to Alannah Myles.

## Impact and Reception

The album sold more than 100,000 copies in Quebec, peaked at number two on the Canadian albums chart and was nominated for Album of the Year at the 2009 Junos. Careless Whisper was not everyone's favourite: Hans Werksman, at Here Comes the Flood, put it with Incognito among the album's ugly moments. Hear it, then go back to the saxophone.
