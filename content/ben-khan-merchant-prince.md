## Genre and Mood

**Ben Khan**'s **merchant prince** belongs to an album that 5 Finger Review heard as a blend of pop, funk and R&B in futuristic, electronics-soaked grooves, and that The Skinny found more concerned with atmosphere than with beats. Those words describe the record; no review this page found describes this song, so what follows says what is known and leaves the rest to your ears.

## Song Summary

"merchant prince" is the twelfth of fifteen tracks on Ben Khan, released by Dirty Hit on 10 August 2018, and runs 2:43. The writing is credited to Benedict Khan alone. Its title is not explained anywhere this page could find, and that suits the album's publicity, which, as God Is In The TV reported, carried the line "Explanation can ruin art."

### The Sound Around It

The Skinny heard fluorescent synths and bubbling electronics across the record, with more emphasis on atmosphere than on thumping beats, and what it called a Blade Runner aesthetic.

### The Shape

Writing about the opener, 5 Finger Review found no clear verse-chorus structure in Khan's songwriting, but melodies and grooves that still catch the ear, and vocal harmonies and doubling that make his lines sound big and full.

### Its Place in the Sequence

## Concepts in Focus

Ben Khan makes a good case study in who does what on a record. According to 5 Finger Review, Khan played every instrument himself, yet three specialists shaped the result.

Flood, known for his work with PJ Harvey, Depeche Mode and Nine Inch Nails, was the {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }}, guiding the recordings toward a finished album. Spike Stent, whose credits include Björk and Frank Ocean, did the {{ <concept_link name="mixing" /> }}, balancing every part into one stereo track. Dave Cooley, who has worked on J Dilla and M83 records, handled the {{ <concept_link name="mastering" /> }}, the final pass that sets loudness and tone so the fifteen tracks sit together.

## Artistic Context

Khan is a London singer, producer and multi-instrumentalist who emerged with two EPs in 2014 and 2015 and then, as The Skinny put it, pretty much disappeared before returning on his own terms. Hear the album's opener {{ <track_link source="ben-khan-2000-angels.md" /> }} and the nearby {{ <track_link source="ben-khan-love-faded.md" /> }} for the sound the reviews describe.

## Impact and Reception

The album divided critics: The Line of Best Fit gave it 8.5 out of 10, while God Is In The TV wrote that there was little point discussing it track by track. "merchant prince" drew no comment of its own. Play it in sequence and decide for yourself.
