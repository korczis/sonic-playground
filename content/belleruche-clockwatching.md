## Genre and Mood

**Belleruche** released **Clockwatching** as the first taste of their third album, and it carries what made the London trio distinctive: trip-hop and soul built by a singer, a guitarist and a turntablist, groovy and a little playful, with the tougher edge their label and reviewers heard on 270 Stories.

## Song Summary

Tru Thoughts put Clockwatching out on 23 August 2010 as a 7-inch single, with an unusual, dubby electronic cover of The Beat's Mirror in the Bathroom on the flip, seven weeks before the album. The band's own album notes credit every song to the three members, who wrote, performed, engineered and produced the record themselves, in a place they call the cave; J. Howes mixed and mastered it. What the lyric says, no source we found describes, and no review walks through the song itself, so this page keeps to what was written about the single and the album around it.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes Clockwatching part by part. This breakdown follows the elements that the band's notes and reviewers name on 270 Stories as a whole.

### Vocal

Kathrin deBoer sings. The Radio Ponsonby review of the album heard her looping her voice, tweaking it with guitar effects pedals and layering harmonies on top of one another.

### Beat and Turntables

The album notes speak of scratchy beats; the same review heard DJ Modest's beats as harder-edged than before, with a nod to darker contemporary influences.

### Guitar

Ricky Fabulous plays the bluesy guitar the reviewers mention, and on this album he added bass guitar to many tracks, which the review heard as a lively interplay between the two.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="vocal harmony" /> }} usually means several singers. On 270 Stories it can be one: deBoer stacks her own voice, which Tru Thoughts described as honeycomb vocal harmonies. Listen to how full the voice sounds for a trio with one singer.

{{ <concept_link name="turntablism" /> }} is the record player played as an instrument. Belleruche's first album was called Turntable Soul Music, and the In The Pocket reviewer praised this album for ignoring the supposed limitations of a vocals-guitar-turntable trio.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Jamie Roberts for the production company Archer's Mark and shot on 16mm film, was picked as Video of the Week on BBC 6Music's lunchtime show, a promo the label called highly creative.

## Artistic Context

Belleruche formed in North London in 2005 and released three 7-inch records on their own Hippoflex label before signing with the Brighton label Tru Thoughts, which released {{ <track_link source="belleruche-anything-you-want-not-that.md" /> }} and their first two albums. The Liberty EP previewed the new material in March 2010, and 270 Stories followed on 11 October 2010.

## Impact and Reception

The single gathered radio play from Huey Morgan on 6Music, John Kennedy on XFM and Ross Allen of Ministry of Sound, and the In The Pocket review advised newcomers to start the album with Clockwatching. The song returned on the band's Best Of in 2013, the year they split; they announced new music in 2025. Start where that reviewer said to.
