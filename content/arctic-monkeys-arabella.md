## Genre and Mood

**Arctic Monkeys**' **Arabella** is filed as hard rock, and it earns the label in its chorus, but it is built like something else. Far Out heard hip-hop drums, a short but effective riff and Black Sabbath-like chords, a fusion of late-1990s hip-hop and R&B with 1970s hard rock. The mood is bold and sultry, and Rolling Stone called it a Seventies-inspired dreamscape: a swagger that keeps drifting into a daydream.

## Song Summary

Alex Turner wrote the words; the music is credited to all four members. The song is a portrait of a woman Far Out describes as a fantasy figure, an escape from reality, and the magazine reads the title as a nod to Barbarella, the 1968 Jane Fonda film, which gives her a glamorous, cosmic, space-age air. Musically it moves between a verse of skittering guitar and a heavy chorus, and saves its biggest release for the bridge and the guitar solo that ends it.

## Musical Breakdown

No source times the song; the reviews describe its parts.

### Verse

MusicRadar hears delayed and muted clean guitar licks that skitter in and out of the edge of the mix, over the hip-hop drums Far Out describes. Not everyone was won over: WhatCulture called these verses the song's main problem.

### Chorus

By the chorus, in Far Out's words, the song truly rocks. MusicRadar hears a stomping riff alternated with tapped hi-hat, pure Jimmy Page, and chordal stabs that recall Led Zeppelin's Good Times Bad Times.

### Bridge

Rolling Stone says every glistening piece of the song explodes when it reaches its bridge, with frantic vocals and a driving solo.

### Outro

At its close the song melts back into heavy rock, MusicRadar writes, with a warped, mid-heavy fuzz solo that channels Black Sabbath's Tony Iommi.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }} is a short instrumental figure repeated until it becomes a song's signature. Rolling Stone and WhatCulture both hear Arabella's as channelling Black Sabbath's War Pigs, and the band plays along with the comparison: live, they often insert part of War Pigs into the song, which gives Turner time to change guitars for the solo.

That {{ <concept_link name="guitar solo" /> }} is where the song's 1970s side takes over completely, all fuzz and warped midrange. The {{ <concept_link name="bridge" /> }}, the contrasting section late in a song, is where Rolling Stone heard the whole track come alive. ## Artistic Context

Arabella is the fourth track of AM, released by Domino on 6 September 2013 and produced by James Ford with Ross Orton. Turner cited Outkast, Aaliyah and Black Sabbath among the album's influences and compared its sound to Dr. Dre-style beats with a 1950s rock twist.

## Impact and Reception

Sent to radio as the album's fifth single in early 2014, with a video by Jake Nava, Arabella reached number 70 in the UK and has since gone double platinum there and platinum in the US. Rolling Stone ranked it the band's tenth-best song in 2023, Paste its fourth. Listen for the moment the verse gives way to the riff.
