## Genre and Mood

**Apocalyptica** play **Master of Puppets** with four cellos and nothing else: no guitars, no drums, no voice. The cover sits between neoclassical music and symphonic metal, as intense as the original and darker. The Green Man Review heard it as wild, dark and rich.

## Song Summary

Master of Puppets is the title track of Metallica's 1986 album, written by James Hetfield, Lars Ulrich, Cliff Burton and Kirk Hammett. Hetfield said it deals with drugs, with how they end up controlling you instead of you controlling them. The original is known for its relentless downpicking and its long instrumental section. Apocalyptica recorded it for their 1996 debut, Plays Metallica by Four Cellos, where at 7:17 it is the longest of the eight tracks.

## Musical Breakdown

Few sources describe this recording in detail; what follows sets it against the original.

### Intro

Metallica's song opens on a riff that Dave Mustaine credited to Lars Ulrich. No source describes how the four cellists divide it; listen for which cello takes the low line and which the attack.

### Main Section

The Green Man Review described the method: four players covering melody, lead guitar, bass line and rhythm. Teen Ink heard this track as fast-paced and well put together, with solos and vocal pitches, so the cellos carry Hetfield's melody as well as Hammett's guitar parts.

### Middle Section

Reviewing a 2017 concert in New York, Super Conductor heard it as a bluesy harmony solo over a steady, rising plucked accompaniment, building to the rolling, locomotive roar of all four instruments. That was live, not this recording, but it tells you what to listen for.

### Outro

No source describes how the recorded version ends. Listen for whether it closes on the riff or on something new.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} chooses what to keep. Apocalyptica keep the notes of a thrash song and replace everything that made it sound like metal.

That composition rests on its {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }}, a short figure repeated until it becomes the song's engine. Played with bows, a riff written for fast downpicking has to find its bite somewhere else.

The hardest test is the {{ <concept_link name="guitar solo" /> }}. Hammett's main solo is famous for a harmonic he found by accident, pulling his high string off the fretboard and reaching for the whammy bar; Super Conductor noted that the cellists were skilled enough to pull his solos off.

Finally, {{ <concept_link name="remastering" /> }}: this is the 1996 performance prepared again for the 2016 edition, while {{ <track_link source="apocalyptica-battery.md" /> }}, the other song from Metallica's 1986 album on that edition, was recorded new.

## Artistic Context

Apocalyptica formed in 1993 at Helsinki's Sibelius Academy: Eicca Toppinen, Paavo Lötjönen, Max Lilja and Antero Manninen, classically trained cellists who began as a Metallica tribute band. A label employee invited them to record after a 1995 show; Mercury released the album on 10 May 1996, and it went platinum in Finland and gold in Germany and Poland. Wikipedia's list of members credits Lötjönen, the rhythm and bass cellist, with the lead cello on Master of Puppets from 1996 to 2019.

## Impact and Reception

Super Conductor remembered the debut blazing through this song with gusto and glee, and at the 2017 concert it drew the evening's first roars from the crowd. Loudwire, listing the best covers of the song, pointed out that Apocalyptica's came before Metallica themselves played with a symphony orchestra. Hear Metallica's version first, then this one, and listen to how the middle section breathes.
