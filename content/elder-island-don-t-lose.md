## Genre and Mood

**Elder Island** made **Don't Lose** a languid piece of indie electronic music with its roots in downtempo and trip-hop. A Listen With Monger reviewer heard a chilled lounge groove in the line of Morcheeba and Zero 7 and called it a louche, sultry party tune; Cheatbook wrote of the band's hypnotic sound. The feeling is relaxed, but never quite asleep.

## Song Summary

The song took the Bristol trio about three years to finish. It began during the sessions for their second EP, Seeds in Sand, with some vocals and rough ideas, and the band told GoldenPlec that what it was then is dramatically different from what it became. Working on their debut album in 2018, they gathered every recording of it they could find, and the one that excited them most was a dodgy phone recording, which they used as the base. Clash called the result nuanced, textured and beautifully balanced, sitting between the pointed pop of Little Dragon and Bonobo's electronics, and dominated by Katy Sargent's stirring vocal.

### Main Section

The groove sets the pace, languid and lounge-like in the Monger review. Over it Sargent sings in a voice that reviewer imagined as Beth Ditto at about 40 per cent ferocity, keeping it down so as not to wake the neighbours. For a brief moment, Written in Music heard a male voice slide in beside her, a smooth addition that the reviewer heard as a nod to a seventies dance floor.

## Concepts in Focus

Don't Lose is a quiet lesson in {{ <concept_link name="lo-fi" /> }} as a decision rather than a limitation. After three years of attempts, the band chose a phone recording as the foundation because it had something the cleaner versions lacked.

The {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} is the other half of its appeal: a slow, steady feel that the reviews keep describing as lounge, the kind that pulls you along without raising its voice. Compare how a single voice rides a patient beat in {{ <track_link source="bonobo-andreya-triana-the-keeper.md" /> }}.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, premiered by Clash in September 2018, is a nostalgia-soaked tribute to 1970s science education films: a series of experiments leads to a games console designed by the band, the EI DL-410.

## Artistic Context

Elder Island formed in Bristol in 2014, as an audiovisual project called Trouvaille, and grew from folk towards house. The trio are Katy Sargent on vocals and cello, Luke Thornton on bass and programming, and David Havard on guitar and keyboards. Don't Lose came out as a single in September 2018, ahead of their first full-length album, The Omnitone Collection, released on 8 February 2019.

## Impact and Reception

Reviewing the album, Written in Music said the band made the most of that brief smooth injection in Don't Lose, and Here Comes The Flood heard the record making inroads into jazz, folk and trip-hop. Put it on late and let the groove decide the pace.
