## Genre and Mood

**Massive Attack**'s **Splitting the Atom** is trip-hop turned faintly sinister: electronic music built on an organ figure, handclaps, synth bass and four voices taking turns. The mood is dark and eerie rather than heavy, and the critics' images for it, a spooky carnival, a song for Nick Cave, point the same way.

## Song Summary

The song announced the sessions that became Heligoland: it premiered on BBC Radio 1 in August 2009. Its credits explain its sound: Robert Del Naja (3D), Grant Marshall (Daddy G), the band's long-time collaborator Neil Davidge and Damon Albarn wrote it, Albarn played keys and synth bass, and the vocals are shared between 3D, Daddy G, Albarn and the Jamaican singer Horace Andy. Marshall is credited as its producer. No source used here explains the lyric, so this page leaves it to the listener.

### Intro

### Main Section

Critics disagreed about the same features. Drowned in Sound wrote that the track never rises above a two-chord organ refrain and found its handclaps hideously out of date; the same review described Horace Andy wrapping his spindly Caribbean lilt around the song. RTÉ's Harry Guerin heard it as the theme tune for a spooky carnival, with Marshall as the barker letting you in on something quietly. The Irish Times thought it sounded as if it were written for Nick Cave, if perhaps a bit too synth-heavy.

## Concepts in Focus

The title credits a {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}, but Horace Andy is hardly a guest. He has worked with the Bristol group since 1990 and contributed to all five of their albums; on Heligoland he also sings Girl I Love You. Here his voice is one of four credited on the track.

The song's second life is a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="sync licensing" /> }}: it was licensed to the FIFA 11 video game and became the opening theme of the 2011 HBO series Luck. A title sequence needs a mood more than a story, and this song is mostly mood.

Two promotional {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} versions were made in 2009, one directed by Baillie Walsh and one by Edouard Salier; the band's VEVO channel carries both.

## Artistic Context

Splitting the Atom opened a four-track EP released as a download on 4 October 2009, every song with a guest singer, then became track three of Heligoland, released by Virgin Records on 8 February 2010 after sessions from 2005 to 2009. The album reached number 6 in the UK. Compare {{ <track_link source="massive-attack-gui-boratto-paradise-circus-gui-boratto-remix.md" /> }}, a club reworking of another Heligoland song.

## Impact and Reception

The EP drew mixed reviews, with a Metacritic score of 57, and the title track divided critics: Drowned in Sound heard a song going nowhere, RTÉ a carnival of menace. Play it loud, and decide whether two chords are a limit or a trap.
