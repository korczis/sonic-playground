## Genre and Mood

**Kalabrese** and **Sarah Palin** sing **Fresh and Foolish** together, on an album that its label placed between indie and electronica and that Cool Hunting heard spanning electronica, funk, disco and indie. The feelings on this page, intimate, warm and playful, are our reading of a duet that BlackPlastic simply called beautiful.

## Song Summary

Fresh and Foolish is the fourth of thirteen tracks on Independent Dancer, which Sacha Winkler, alias Kalabrese, released on his Zurich label Rumpelmusig on 26 April 2013; it runs just under five minutes. The vinyl tracklist credits it "feat. Sarah Palin". The label introduced her as its newest discovery, a singer from Zurich, and she has nothing to do with the American politician of the same name, a joke BlackPlastic made too. She also sings with Kalabrese on the album's opener, Purple Rose, so Fresh and Foolish is the second of their two duets on the record.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} usually means a guest who adds a name and a voice to someone else's track. Here the host sings too, so the guest turns a solo song into a conversation. Kalabrese did this several times on the album: with Sarah Palin twice, with A.C. Kupper on Let The Good Times Roll and with Khan on {{ <track_link source="kalabrese-khan-desperate-man-feat-khan.md" /> }}.

The {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} is what holds a song like this together. BlackPlastic heard the whole album as warm electronic funk that applies the focus of minimal to a chaotic funk record: few elements, each placed with care, so the movement comes from how they lock together rather than from how many there are.

## Artistic Context

Independent Dancer came six years after Kalabrese's debut Rumpelzirkus of 2007. Rumpelmusig compared its sound to James Murphy and Nicolas Jaar, heard in {{ <track_link source="nicolas-jaar-time-for-us.md" /> }}, and listed its guests, among them the cellist Dominik Löhrer and the brass players Michael Flury and Benjamin Danech.

## Impact and Reception

Fresh and Foolish was never a single, and its recorded reception is a single sentence: BlackPlastic, which found the album brilliant, challenging and a little irritating, singled it out as a highlight and regretted that it came so early. That is reason enough to give it five minutes of your own.
