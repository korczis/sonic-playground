## Genre and Mood

**Waldeck** and **Patrizia Ferrara**'s **Never let you go** is electro-swing with its roots in jazz and lounge. Motion FM called it a catchy tune, a lightly presented concession to the friends of swing that also seems francophile and pensive, and the same review said the album makes you feel you remember something you never lived: hence nostalgic, reflective and catchy.

## Song Summary

Never Let You Go is the fifth track of Atlantic Ballroom, released by Dope Noir in October 2018. Dope Noir presented the album as the legitimate successor to Waldeck's signature record Ballroom Stories, ten years on. The song is sung by Patrizia Ferrara, whom Motion FM describes as half Sicilian and half Austrian, back in Austria after the last years of her singing career in Brooklyn. It was released with an official video, and in 2023 the same recording returned on a single called Never Let You Go (Beach Club Conviction).

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="swing" /> }} is a way of playing pairs of notes unevenly, the first longer than the second, so the music lilts rather than marches. Electro-swing producers borrow it from the jazz of the dance halls. Here, by Motion FM's account, the swing is a gesture rather than the whole point: light, and folded into a quieter, more French mood.

Ferrara is the album's {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}, and more than a guest. Motion FM wrote that Waldeck's short excursion into Spaghetti Western and Italian canzone, his 2016 album Gran Paradiso, led him to the key element of Atlantic Ballroom: Patrizia Ferrara, who became his main singer and leads the album's other guest voices. She sang on his later releases too, among them One Of These Days in 2020 and the EP It Might Be French in 2021.

## Artistic Context

Klaus Waldeck, born in Vienna in 1966, studied law before returning to music and founded his label Dope Noir in 2001. Ballroom Stories, from 2007, the album of {{ <track_link source="waldeck-memories.md" /> }}, reached number 49 in Austria. Atlantic Ballroom reached number 65. Motion FM traced its influences to Henry Mancini, John Barry and Wes Montgomery, and heard in it the American jazz musicians who worked in Paris in the early 1960s.

## Impact and Reception

Motion FM praised Waldeck for refusing to warm up old recipes and creating a new, refreshing ballroom. Never Let You Go is here for that light touch: it sways rather than swings hard. Put it on in the evening and let it take you to a Paris you never saw.
