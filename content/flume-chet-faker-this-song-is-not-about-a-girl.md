## Genre and Mood

**Flume** and **Chet Faker**'s **This Song Is Not About a Girl** is the fast end of their partnership: electronic dance music with a disco pulse, euphoric and groovy, but with an intensity that never quite lets it relax. Renowned for Sound called it a rainy-day dance anthem that builds to euphoria without ever losing the brooding quality the two share.

## Song Summary

It is the third and last track of Lockjaw, the three-track EP the two Australians released on Future Classic on 22 November 2013, and it runs 4:14. Both are credited as writers, Harley Streten (Flume) and Nick Murphy (Chet Faker). Where the EP's single, Drop the Game, is downtempo and accessible, EARMILK heard this one as the other end of the spectrum, the pair finding their inner disco and funk glitz. Indie Shuffle liked its nu-disco tempo.

## Musical Breakdown

No source times this recording; the parts below follow the order in which reviewers described them.

### Opening

Renowned for Sound found the start unexpected, with a fast tempo and a lounge ambiance.

### The Groove

EARMILK described Flume's backdrop as an upbeat bassline and guitar plucks joined to experimental drum clacks and effects, with blaring soundscapes glowing behind them.

### The Vocal

Faker's delivery here struck EARMILK as indie-leaning, with momentum and a gritty edge rather than the R&B style his fans know, his sultry side showing only in single lines. Renowned for Sound found the vocal largely covered up, making this Flume's moment to shine.

### Finale

The track ends in what EARMILK called a wholesome finale, dripping with Flume's touch, where Faker's sultrier side comes through most.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }} usually means a short passage before a drop. Here reviewers heard the whole track as one: Renowned for Sound described a lounge opening that slowly turns into an anxiety-filled adrenaline ride, and EARMILK heard the soundscapes foreshadowing the finale from early on.

The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} is what gives the track its disco character. EARMILK put an upbeat bassline at the centre of Flume's backdrop, with plucked guitar answering it, the classic pairing of disco and funk.

## Artistic Context

The two had met on Left Alone from Flume's 2012 debut album before making Lockjaw together. Compare the slow, sultry {{ <track_link source="flume-chet-faker-drop-the-game.md" /> }} that opens the same EP: the Sputnikmusic reviewer heard a melancholy styling running through all three tracks, whatever their tempo.

## Impact and Reception

The track reached number 52 on the ARIA Singles Chart and number 12 on the ARIA dance chart in December 2013. Renowned for Sound wished the vocal were less buried, and EARMILK predicted the track would grow on a listener with repeated plays. Give it more than one.
