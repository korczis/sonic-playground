> "The Wheel" by **IDLES** is a song about a cycle that will not stop turning: alcoholism handed down from one generation to the next. Joe Talbot sings about begging his mother to stop drinking when he was about ten years old, and the title's wheel is the repetition he could not break for her and had to break for himself.

**Official audio**

{{ <video id="2MKk-33XRGM" title="IDLES - The Wheel (Official Audio)" /> }}

The song is driven by a heavy, pounding rhythm section, a repeated one-note guitar figure and Talbot's urgent, hoarse vocal. Its force comes from saying something painful directly and loudly, with a room-shaking chorus rather than a confession whispered in private.

## Genre and Mood

**IDLES**' "The Wheel" sits between **punk**, **post-punk** and **alternative** rock. The track is **raw** and **introspective**, a family story told without softening, and yet **empowering** in the way it turns that story into something a crowd can shout back.

## About the Artist

**IDLES** are a punk band from Bristol, England: Joe Talbot on vocals, Mark Bowen and Lee Kiernan on guitars, Adam Devonshire on bass and Jon Beavis on drums. Their songs often address masculinity, mental health, class and grief, and their fourth album, Crawler, released on Partisan Records on 12 November 2021, turned to Talbot's struggles with addiction and trauma. Produced by Kenny Beats with Mark Bowen, it reached number 6 in the UK and was nominated for Best Rock Album at the 65th Grammy Awards.

## Song Summary

The track is the second song on Crawler and runs 3:25. NME summarised it in one line: it "sees him begging his mother to stop drinking when he was just 10-years-old". The verses tell the story in short, blunt images; the refrain returns to the turning wheel; and the last part of the song becomes a call for a hallelujah from the back of the room, as if the band were leading a service.

## Sound Breakdown

### Intro: A Compelling Start

The song opens heavy, with a fuzzy low end and slow, weighty chords that announce the subject's seriousness before a word is sung.

### First Verse: Confronting Reality

Talbot sets out the scene with his mother, plainly and close to shouting. His delivery brings urgency and sincerity rather than polish.

### Chorus: Anthem of Empowerment

The refrain about the turning wheel arrives with the full band, and the guitars hammer one repeated note through it, a riff of pure insistence.

### Bridge: Moment of Reflection

The hallelujah section shifts the song from story to ritual: Talbot addresses the audience directly, and the band holds the groove beneath him.

### Final Chorus: Strength Unleashed

The last stretch returns at full intensity, Talbot howling the call until he is hoarse and the song ends on the same turning motion it began with.

## Concepts in Focus

The guitar part shows how little a {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }} needs. Reviewing Crawler for Guitar.com, Gary Walker heard Bowen and Kiernan "in menacing form, a murderous one-note riff spearing through the chorus". A one-note riff has no melody to enjoy; its power is rhythm, tone and repetition, which is exactly what a song about a cycle needs.

The hallelujah section borrows the {{ <concept_link name="call and response" /> }} of a gospel church or a preacher's sermon: the leader calls out, the congregation answers. Talbot calls to the back of the room, and in a concert the crowd does answer. That turns a private family story into a shared moment, and the borrowed religious language sharpens the irony of a song about a different kind of dependency. Compare the satirical take on drink in {{ <track_link source="dead-kennedys-too-drunk-to-fuck.md" /> }}, forty years earlier.

The video at the top of this page is a {{ <concept_link name="live recording" /> }}: IDLES performing the song in the KEXP studio in Seattle on 9 November 2021, three days before the album's release, with Cheryl Waters hosting. A live studio session keeps the band's real-time interplay and roughness, with no overdubs to hide behind; play it against the official audio above and listen for what the room adds.

## Overall Vibe

“The Wheel” captures IDLES' ability to make raw pain into a collective release. Its **raw**, **introspective** and **empowering** charge comes from refusing to choose between confession and celebration.

## Conclusion

In "The Wheel," **IDLES** tell one of the hardest stories on Crawler as loudly as they can, with a riff that never lets go and a call for a hallelujah that makes the audience part of it. It is a must-listen for anyone who wants to hear how modern punk handles real damage.

## Full Lyrics

The lyric moves from a child kneeling to beg his mother, "with a bottle in one hand", through a missing dog, lost friends and a lost job, to a moment alone among seagulls, each verse returning to the wheel that turns "again and again".
