## Genre and Mood

**Elizeth Cardoso**'s **Eu Bebo Sim** is a samba from 1973 whose title means "yes, I drink", and Portuguese Wikipedia lists it among the popular songs that celebrate drinking. Carefree and festive in subject and a little defiant in its title, it later found a second home under samba rock, the dance-floor blend of samba with rock, soul and funk.

## Song Summary

João do Violão wrote the song with Luiz Antônio, and Portuguese Wikipedia calls it celebrated in Elizeth Cardoso's voice. Nova Brasil FM reports that hers was the first release, a single on Copacabana with Meu Carnaval on the other side. The recording on YouTube comes from her Disco De Ouro, credited to the producer Milton Miranda and dated 1973, with the album released at the start of 1974.

### Main Section

What can be said rests on the credits: Elizeth Cardoso sings, Milton Miranda produced, and the record runs about three and three-quarter minutes. Listen for the title line, the hook the whole song turns on, and for how a singer known as A Divina carries a lighthearted samba.

## Concepts in Focus

Eu Bebo Sim is a lesson in the {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }}. Portuguese Wikipedia counts eleven other acts who recorded it in 1973 alone, among them Os Bambas, Golden Boys, The Big Seven and the Banda do Canecão, and three more in 1974. The covers kept coming: the rock band Velhas Virgens in 1997, the sertanejo duo Felipe & Falcão in 1999, Elza Soares with Monobloco in 2002, Bebeto with Seu Jorge in 2006, a fado version by the Portuguese group Ús Sai de Gatas in 2011 and Simone Mazzer in 2017.

It also lives on through {{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }}. Planet Hemp's second album, Os Cães Ladram Mas a Caravana Não Pára, released in 1997, uses Elizeth Cardoso's Eu Bebo Sim in the track Nega do Cabelo Duro, carrying a 1973 samba into Brazilian rap.

## Artistic Context

Elizeth Cardoso, born in Rio de Janeiro in 1920, was discovered at her sixteenth birthday party by Jacob do Bandolim and sang on the radio from 1936. In 1958 she sang Canção do Amor Demais, the album of Vinicius de Moraes and Tom Jobim songs that launched bossa nova, but her base, as Wikipedia puts it, was always samba; she was called A Divina and released more than forty albums before her death in 1990. Samba rock grew from the dance parties of São Paulo's black communities, pioneered by acts such as Jorge Ben; hear {{ <track_link source="jorge-ben-jr_take-it-easy-my-brother-charles.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

The song's afterlife shows how well it lasted. Sounds of the Universe presents Elizeth Cardoso's recording as a model of samba rock on a reissue pairing it with Elza Soares's Deixa A Nega Gingar, and it reappeared on compilations such as Humbatuque Apresenta Samba Rock in 2003 and Samba Rock Vol. 1 in 2007.
