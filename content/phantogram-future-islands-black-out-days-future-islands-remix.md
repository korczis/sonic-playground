## Genre and Mood

**Phantogram**'s **Black Out Days (Future Islands Remix)** is the Baltimore band **Future Islands** taking a nervous, beat-driven electronic rock song towards synth-pop balladry. Noisey heard the band's ultra-romance meeting the duo's nervous tension, as pretty as it is predatory; Stereogum heard Phantogram's sound softened into something heartfelt and dramatic. Haunting, romantic and turned inward, it is a remix for the end of the night rather than its peak.

## Song Summary

The original Black Out Days, sung by Sarah Barthel, came out as a single in September 2013 and became the third single from Phantogram's second album, Voices, in June 2014. Future Islands' version premiered on 21 July 2014. The remix slows the song considerably, and in Exclaim!'s words the original's throbbing 1990s pulse and woozy synth oscillations are blacked out, replaced by subtler percussion and washes of sound. Indie Shuffle heard a heavy bassline, subtle synth and gentle reverb around Barthel's haunting vocal.

## Musical Breakdown

No source times the remix part by part; the reviewers describe what changed against the original, and that is what to listen for.

### Main Section: Beat and Tempo

The drive goes first: the remix moves slowly, and the percussion is reduced to softer tones. The song's tension remains, but it no longer pushes.

### Main Section: Keyboards and Bass

Altcitizen heard keyboards that would fit any song on Future Islands' own album Singles, released that spring, and said the result sounds almost piece for piece like a Future Islands song with Barthel's voice superimposed. Underneath, a heavy bassline carries the weight the beat has given up.

### Main Section: The Voice

Barthel's vocal stays at the centre, now surrounded by gentle reverb.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} is usually imagined as a way to make a song more danceable. This one goes the other way: Future Islands take out the drive and leave the voice in a larger, darker room. Exclaim! heard it as the aftermath of the original, a slit of light peeking through the clouds. Compare how Junior Boys rebuilt Caribou's song for the floor in {{ <track_link source="caribou-junior-boys-odessa-junior-boys-mix.md" /> }}.

The room is made with {{ <concept_link name="reverb" /> }}: the gentle reverb reviewers heard around Barthel places her voice in a space larger than the mix. The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} does the rest. With the pulse thinned out, a heavy bass becomes the main thing holding the song together.

## Artistic Context

Phantogram are Sarah Barthel and Josh Carter, a duo from Greenwich, New York, formed in 2007, who describe their music as electronic rock, dream pop and trip hop. Voices, released by Republic Records on 18 February 2014 and produced by the band with John Hill, debuted at number 11 on the Billboard 200. Future Islands, formed in Greenville, North Carolina, and based in Baltimore, had their own breakthrough that spring: their Letterman performance of Seasons (Waiting on You) in March went viral, and Singles followed on 4AD.

## Impact and Reception

The remix was welcomed on its premiere by Noisey, Stereogum, Exclaim! and smaller blogs alike. Republic released it as a single in September 2017, more than three years later. Listen to the original first, then let this version slow everything down.
