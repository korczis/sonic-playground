## Genre and Mood

**Avia**'s **Why Should I Cry** belongs to the French downtempo of the early 2000s. Discogs files the 2003 single under future jazz and downtempo, the style also called nu-jazz, and the track has spent most of its life on lounge compilations. Exclaim! heard its album as uplifting yet mellow, with a warm atmosphere made for lazy summer Sundays; relaxed and warm are the words for it.

## Song Summary

The track is the work of one producer. Pierre Aviat, who records as Avia, wrote, produced and mixed it, and the track's own credits add one guest: Andy Jenks on turntables. Beyond that, no review we found describes this track in particular, so this page says only what the credits and the album's reviews support.

### Main Section

Exclaim!'s review of the album, written by Emily Orr in 2003, describes downtempo beats mixed with jazzy instrumental melody, an uncluttered and tasteful set in which samples are used sparingly and cleverly disguised. It names other tracks, not this one.

### Turntables

Andy Jenks is credited on turntables on the album and with scratches on the single's original version. Where his part falls, no source says.

### The Remixes

The 2003 single on Nettwerk set the original version beside a remix by Sporto Kantes and another by Wicked Lester, with vocals by Coco Love Alcorn.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="turntablism" /> }} is the art of playing records as an instrument: scratching, cutting and manipulating a record by hand. In hip-hop it is a performance; in a studio production like this one it is a texture added by a specialist, which is why the credits name Andy Jenks separately from the producer.

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} rebuilds a track from its parts. The Nettwerk single shows how far that can go: one remixer reworked the track, another brought in a singer of his own. Hearing the versions one after another is the quickest way to hear what each remixer kept.

## Artistic Context

I See That Now first appeared in France on 30 April 2002 on Les Disques du Catalogue, distributed by Wagram Music, and in the UK that August. A 2003 edition on Nutone, licensed from Catalogue, reordered the tracks and notes they were recorded between June 2001 and February 2003. Exclaim! reported that Avia was already recognised in France and the UK, praised by Laurent Garnier and Richard Dorfmeister, and that his music was heard in the Quebec film Les Invasions Barbares.

## Impact and Reception

The track's afterlife is in compilations. MusicBrainz lists it on So Frenchy So Chic in 2002, It Sounds Different: Dub Addicts in 2003, City Lounge in 2005, Lounge Classics in 2006 and French Touch, Volume 01: Electronic Music Made in France in 2014, among others. Put it on and listen for the turntables.
