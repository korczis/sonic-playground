## Genre and Mood

**Drake**'s **One Dance**, with **Wizkid** and **Kyla**, is a pop song built from dancehall, afrobeats and UK funky. It is groovy rather than urgent and warm rather than flashy: Jon Caramanica of The New York Times called it a transnational dance-floor lullaby and one of Drake's breeziest songs.

## Song Summary

The song began with a sample. Drake had been a fan of Do You Mind, the Crazy Cousinz remix of a 2008 UK funky song by the British singer Kyla, and persuaded his producer Nineteen85 to use it as a bridge. The South African producer DJ Maphorisa slowed its tempo and added background vocals by the Nigerian star Wizkid. Production took about a week. Kyla says the track was already finished when Drake's team reached her; they first asked for a sample, then made her a featured artist because Drake wanted to keep her part.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

Pitchfork noted that Kyla's voice and the resonant piano are the first things heard, so the song opens on its borrowed material rather than on Drake.

### Main Section

The body of the song is what Billboard called a bouncing, piano-led dancehall groove. Published sheet music puts it in B-flat minor at 104 beats per minute, circling three chords, which keeps the mood unhurried. Drake's part spans a little over an octave in that sheet music, from A-flat 2 to B-flat 3, with Wizkid's voice in the background.

### Outro

No source describes the ending in detail. The album version lasts under three minutes, short for a song that became so ubiquitous.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} is the song's foundation: the piano stabs and hook of Do You Mind run through it, and Crazy Cousinz and Kyla share the writing credit.

A {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} credit usually means a guest who records new parts. Here Kyla's feature grew out of the sample: in her words, it was more than a sample.

The embedded video is a {{ <concept_link name="live recording" /> }}: Drake performed One Dance on Saturday Night Live on 14 May 2016 with Hype. The official upload is this performance rather than the studio version, so listen for what changes on stage.

## Artistic Context

One Dance was released on 5 April 2016 with {{ <track_link source="drake-the-throne-pop-style.md" /> }}, a more conventional rap track meant to cushion criticism of a departure. It was Drake's first dancehall single as lead artist and, after the remix of Wizkid's Ojuelegba, his second afrobeats song, and it led into his album Views on Young Money, Cash Money and Republic.

## Impact and Reception

One Dance was Drake's first number one as lead artist in the US, where it topped the Hot 100 for ten weeks in all, and Wizkid became the first Nigerian artist to chart there. It spent fifteen weeks at number one in the UK, was the best-performing single worldwide in 2016 according to the IFPI, and in October 2016 became the most-streamed song on Spotify until {{ <track_link source="ed-sheeran-shape-of-you.md" /> }} overtook it. Start with the piano, then listen to how little the song needs to move a room.
