## Genre and Mood

**Gabriella Cilmi** sings **Cry Me a River** as a soul song, though it was written as a twenty-first-century pop hit. This is her cover of Justin Timberlake's 2002 single, not the 1950s jazz standard of the same name, and she strips it of its studio gloss so that the heartbreak and the vindication sit right on the surface of her voice.

## Song Summary

Timberlake's original, written with Scott Storch and Timbaland and produced by Timbaland, is built from clavinet, guitars, beatboxing, synthesizers and Gregorian-style chants, a sleek, beat-driven reproach to an unfaithful lover. Cilmi keeps the melody and the story of betrayal and cold satisfaction and throws away almost everything else. In her hands the song becomes a vocal showcase: a husky, surprisingly mature voice that sounds less like the winner of the break-up than like someone still hurting.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro: Setting the Tone

The performance opens without any of the original's production signatures; the arrangement is sparse and the voice comes in quickly.

### Verse 1: Soft Vulnerability

Cilmi's first verse is subtle but charged, delivered with restraint, the grain of her voice carrying the sorrow of someone deeply hurt.

### Chorus: Emotional Climax

In the chorus her voice rises and the refrain opens up, a contrast between the held-back verses and the release of the hook.

### Bridge: Building Intensity

The intensity grows as the performance heads for the last choruses, her delivery becoming more forceful.

### Outro: A Quiet Farewell

The song ends with the voice lingering on the final line, closer to a sigh than a slammed door.

## Concepts in Focus

This is a clear case of a {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }}: a new recording of a song by an artist other than its original performer. A cover can be faithful or transformative, and this one transforms. Timberlake's record was defined by Timbaland's production as much as by its melody, so removing that production and leaving only a singer tests what the song really consists of. What survives, the tune and the story, turns out to be enough.

The versions of the song Cilmi released in 2008 are each a {{ <concept_link name="live recording" /> }}, listed in MusicBrainz as a live version and a live session take rather than a studio production, and you can hear why. A live take keeps the small roughnesses and the sense of a room that a studio would polish away, and for a song about raw feeling that is the point. ## Lyrical Themes

The lyrics of "Cry Me a River" explore:
- Betrayal by a lover, and the discovery of it
- Heartbreak that hardens into vindication
- The refusal to forgive once the other person comes back
- Poetic justice, as the narrator watches the former lover regret what they did

## Historical and Cultural Impact

Timberlake's "Cry Me a River" was the second single from his debut solo album *Justified*, reached number 3 in the US and number 2 in the UK, and won the Grammy for Best Male Pop Vocal Performance; it was widely read as a song about his break-up with Britney Spears, which he later confirmed. It shares its title, but nothing else, with Arthur Hamilton's 1953 standard made famous by Julie London. Cilmi recorded her cover in 2008, the year her debut album *Lessons to Be Learned* came out on Island and her single "Sweet About Me" reached number one in Australia and number six in the UK.

## Legacy

Cilmi was discovered at thirteen singing the Rolling Stones' "Jumpin' Jack Flash" at a street festival in Melbourne, and she grew up idolising Janis Joplin and Nina Simone; this cover shows that lineage clearly. In 2008 she won six ARIA Awards, including Best Female Artist. Her version of "Cry Me a River" stands as a reminder that a pop song with a great melody can be reclaimed as soul by a singer with the right voice.
