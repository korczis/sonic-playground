## Genre and Mood

**Marian Hill** made **One Time** out of very little: a voice, a loop and a saxophone. It sits where R&B meets electro-pop, and its feelings are sultry, playful and catchy. WXPN, reviewing the duo's first EP in 2014, picked it as the catchiest of the set, a song that hooks you on the first listen.

## Song Summary

The arrangement is lean. Samantha Gongol sings; Jeremy Lloyd builds the track around a loop that WXPN called infectious, with a saxophone sample of Steve Davit, who has played with the duo since the very beginning. Nylon described the formula as slow-burn R&B with the tempo raised just a bit and some saxophone added, and called the saxophone's arrival a blink-twice moment. Gongol later described the song as really light, really bold and sassy.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} here is personal rather than archival. Lloyd told Flaunt that Davit, the saxophonist of their live show, is sampled on One Time: the horn is a real player's recording, reused inside a programmed track.

The {{ <concept_link name="loop" /> }} is the song's frame, as WXPN heard it: Lloyd's infectious loop under Gongol's voice. In a song built this way, change comes from what is added above the repeating figure.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, posted in May 2015, grew from what the duo told Nylon was the crazy silent energy of empty streets in the middle of the night.

## Artistic Context

Marian Hill are the Philadelphia producer Jeremy Lloyd and singer Samantha Gongol, who met in middle school and played Marian Paroo and Harold Hill in a school production of The Music Man. One Time appeared on their debut EP Play in 2014 and again on Sway, released on 17 February 2015. Remixes by Aquilo and Louis Futon followed.

## Impact and Reception

One Time was the duo's first viral moment: it spread through a popular Vine clip, caught the attention of labels and became, in Lloyd's words, the song everyone sang along to at their first shows. Wikipedia's discography lists it at number 38 on Billboard's Alternative Songs chart and certified Gold in the United States. Listen for the moment the saxophone comes in.
