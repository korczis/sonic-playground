## Genre and Mood

**Say Yes Dog**'s **"Talk"** is synth-pop and electro-pop with a doubt at its centre. It is bittersweet and introspective: bright machines on the surface, a wary voice underneath.

## Song Summary

"Talk" opens Plastic Love, the trio's debut album, released on 4 September 2015 by Diskodogs Records. Vanguard's review heard in it a fear that too many words are damaging, and that quiet is the best shield against failed intimacy. The tension of the song, in that reading, lies between its sound and its singer: hopeful, expectant synth notes betrayed by the paranoia in the vocals.

## Musical Breakdown

No source we found describes "Talk" part by part, so this breakdown stays with what reviewers said about its sound and about the album it opens.

### The Synths

The synthesizers carry the colour and, in Vanguard's hearing, the hope. Festivalinfo heard synths as the main thread of every song on the album, over static computer beats.

### The Voice

Aaron Ahrends sings the doubt. Renowned for Sound heard the album's vocals as dry, monotone and digitally reworked, with a robotic feel; Festivalinfo called the delivery sober.

### The Bass

Paul Rundel's bass completes the trio's sound; Festivalinfo heard groovy basslines added to the synths across the record.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} in "Talk" does more than decorate: it argues with the lyric. A reviewer heard hope in the synth notes and fear in the voice, and that contrast is a classic synth-pop device, a bright electronic surface over an anxious song.

The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} is what keeps such a song moving. With a bassist rather than a guitarist as the trio's third member, the low end carries the groove the synths float over; compare how the same line-up builds the warmer {{ <track_link source="say-yes-dog_a-friend.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

**Say Yes Dog** is a German-Luxembourgish trio: Ahrends sings, Rundel plays bass and Pascal Karier plays drums. Ahrends and Rundel grew up in Berlin, Karier comes from Luxembourg, and the three met studying sound engineering and jazz at the conservatory in The Hague. Plastic Love was recorded in a studio in Haarlem; on "Talk" all three are credited as writers and Ahrends as producer. It followed their 2013 EP A Friend, and they later toured with Capital Cities.

## Impact and Reception

Black Plastic called Plastic Love introspective electronic pop for fans of Hot Chip and Metronomy, "a simple, touching mission statement from a band with huge promise", made for gloomy autumn evenings. Vanguard called it an impressive debut; Festivalinfo found its songs too alike but its details playful. Listen to "Talk" first, then the rest of the album it opens.
