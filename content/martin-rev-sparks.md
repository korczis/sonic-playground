## Genre and Mood

**Martin Rev**'s **Sparks** belongs to a strange corner of electronic music, the experimental pop that one half of the synth-punk duo Suicide made on his own. No published review describes Sparks itself. Its two feelings here, dreamy and nostalgic, come from how its label describes the album Strangeworld as a whole: melodic and dreamy, shaped by Rev's memories of doo-wop. Treat them as a frame for listening, not a description of these three and a half minutes.

## Song Summary

Sparks is the second of thirteen tracks on Strangeworld and runs 3:30. Rev wrote it alone, and he produced the whole album himself in a rented private studio in New York. The reissue's press text describes the record as melodic passages dissolved into a thicket of fragments and set pieces, a shimmer between rhythm loops and Rev's voice, with the voice used as one more instrument rather than as a conventional lead. Wikipedia calls the album's style light bubblegum pop; The Quietus called it croony and loopy, "perhaps best described as avant-garde if we're being generous". Whether Sparks has a voice at all, no source says.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }} can give an old track a new audience, and Sparks found one that way. In 2011 Andrew VanWyngarden and Ben Goldwasser of MGMT put it at number sixteen of twenty on Late Night Tales: MGMT, a mix of what they called "post-punk, cult indie and counter-culture figureheads". Suicide's Cheree appears earlier on the same mix, so Rev is there twice, once with his duo and once alone. Hear the company Sparks keeps in {{ <track_link source="jacobites-hearts-are-like-flowers.md" /> }}.

Strangeworld also shows the {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} and the artist as one person: Rev wrote Sparks and produced the whole album himself, without an outside producer to shape the sound.

## Artistic Context

Martin Rev, born Martin Reverby in Brooklyn in 1947, was one half of Suicide, the duo with the singer Alan Vega. His solo work ranges, in Wikipedia's words, from the harsh, abrasive no wave of his first album to the heavy synthesizer rock of To Live. Strangeworld, his fifth solo album, followed See Me Ridin' of 1996, a record of keyboard melody sketches and doo-wop-influenced songs. It came out in 2000 on Puu, a Finnish imprint linked to Sähkö Recordings.

## Impact and Reception

The album got a second life twenty years later, when Bureau B reissued it on vinyl and CD on 16 October 2020, with a press text that still found its world strange and enigmatic. Start with Sparks, then play the album through, and compare it with Suicide's Cheree on the same MGMT mix.
