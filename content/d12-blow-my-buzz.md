## Genre and Mood

**D12**'s **Blow My Buzz** is hip-hop from the Detroit group's 2001 debut, Devil's Night, and it is as provocative, gritty and irreverent as the rest of a record that NME called Eminem's most misogynistic, homophobic, violent and anally fixated trip to date. At five minutes and ten seconds, it gives the whole crew the microphone.

## Song Summary

Blow My Buzz is a posse cut. Shazam's credits list all six members as vocalists: Eminem, Proof, Bizarre, Kuniva, Swifty McVay and Kon Artis. Eminem produced it, with Jeff Bass as co-producer; the same credits give Bass the keyboards, the guitar and the bass guitar, and give Denaun Porter, Kon Artis under his own name, the programming. Seven people share the writing credit, Bass among them.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the track part by part; this follows what its credits and its reviewers name.

### The Beat

Wikipedia's track listing names no sample here. The credits split the instrumental between Bass's keyboards, guitar and bass and Porter's drum programming.

### The Verses

RapReviews' Steve "Flash" Juon heard Kon Artis and Kuniva as an excellent tag-team combo on the lead single Purple Pills and wrote that they repeat the feat successfully on this track.

### The Lyrics

The words drew the sharpest objection. Plugged In's Bob Waliszewski singled out a verse in which the narrator boasts of using Rohypnol to knock out a woman he wants to have sex with.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="posse cut" /> }} hands one beat to a whole crew. With six voices in five minutes, Blow My Buzz works like a relay, and the praise for two of its less famous members shows how the form lets a group's other MCs step out of a star's shadow. The same album's {{ <track_link source="d12-american-psycho.md" /> }} does it with three.

The {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} here is a member of the group. Eminem produced the track, and his co-producer Jeff Bass is, in Wikipedia's words, one of the most influential people in Eminem's career: one half of the Bass Brothers. He later co-produced Lose Yourself with Eminem, which won the Oscar for Best Original Song.

Blow My Buzz also had a life as a {{ <concept_link name="soundtrack" /> }} song. In November 2001 it reappeared as track three of The Wash, the soundtrack to DJ Pooh's comedy starring Dr. Dre and Snoop Dogg, in which Eminem plays an uncredited part. That album reached number 19 on the Billboard 200.

## Artistic Context

Proof formed D12 in Detroit in 1996, inviting local rappers including Bizarre, Bugz, Denaun Porter and Kuniva. After Bugz was shot and killed in May 1999, Eminem joined as his replacement, according to Wikipedia. Devil's Night came out on Shady and Interscope on 19 June 2001, a year after The Marshall Mathers LP.

## Impact and Reception

Devil's Night entered the Billboard 200 at number one with about 371,881 copies in its first week and number two in Britain, and went double platinum in the United States. Reviews were mixed; RapReviews called it an overall success. Blow My Buzz was never a single, but the soundtrack carried it further. Listen to how each of the six voices takes the beat.
