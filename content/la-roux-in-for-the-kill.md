## Genre and Mood

**La Roux** made **In For The Kill** the sound of 2009 looking back at the early 1980s: synth-pop and electro-pop with a pop chorus, cold machines and one voice that refuses to stay in the background. It is bold and energetic on the surface, and underneath it is a song of longing, about wanting someone who may not want you back.

## Song Summary

Elly Jackson told Xfm that the song is about telling someone how you feel regardless of what you get back. She wrote the verse and the chorus on guitar at home after travelling to Paris to confess to someone who did not feel the same; the title came from a headline about The Killers that her mother had left for her. The track itself was built with her partner Ben Langmaid, and its sound, by Wikipedia's account, owes a heavy debt to the title theme of the 1980s cartoon Pole Position.

## Musical Breakdown

No source maps the song section by section; this follows what critics heard.

### Main Section

Dave Moore of the Singles Jukebox heard a backing that puts the singer front and centre, whacking a solid melody around like a piñata. Exclaim!'s Cam Lindsay heard icy, high-pitched vocals over pulse-pounding beats; Hillary Brown found the whole thing thin but undeniably hooky.

### Breakdown

Toward the end, Keane Tzong noted, everything drops away for one moment and lets Jackson's voice reach new highs.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} here is used as a period instrument. Ian Mathers heard a Build Your Own Depeche Mode Starter Kit, Alex Macpherson heard cheap keyboards plinking like a pub tribute to the 1980s, and both were describing the same deliberate choice: sounds that announce their decade.

The song's second life came through a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }}. Skream's Let's Get Ravey remix appeared on the CD single, then in Entourage, game adverts and the Dredd trailer; The Observer's Gareth Grundy called it a proper remix that nudges the original into thrilling new territory.

The first {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, uploaded here in 2009, is Kinga Burza's 1980s-inspired film: Jackson drives a Toyota MR2 down a dark road, puts on sunglasses for the chorus, meets another version of herself, and ends with her eyes glowing white. A second video, filmed at New York's Hotel Chelsea, promoted a slightly altered US single in 2010.

## Artistic Context

La Roux were Elly Jackson and producer Ben Langmaid, who wrote and produced the song; Serban Ghenea mixed it. It was the second single from their debut album La Roux, released on Polydor in June 2009, which was shortlisted for the Mercury Prize and won the Grammy for Best Electronic/Dance Album.

## Impact and Reception

Released in March 2009, the single reached number two in the UK, finished sixth on the year-end chart and later went double platinum; in the US it topped the Hot Dance Club Songs chart in 2010. NME placed it 64th among the best tracks of fifteen years, while the Singles Jukebox critics scored it anywhere from 0 to 9. Listen for the moment it all drops away and decide which side you are on.
