## Genre and Mood

**Mary J. Blige**'s **Family Affair** is R&B, hip-hop and dance music at once, the record on which the singer often called the Queen of Hip-Hop Soul asked everyone to get up. It is celebratory and groovy, but not carefree: the beat has weight, and the voice has grit.

## Song Summary

The lyric celebrates partying and dancing in a vocabulary of its own; BET and Vibe both singled out its invented words hateration and dancery. The music came first. Dr. Dre made the instrumental in September 2000 for Rakim's cancelled album Oh, My God, with Mike Elizondo on bass and Camara Kambon on keyboards, then sent it to Blige, who sang lyrics by Bruce Miller, Kambon and Elizondo over it.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

The sources describe the beat rather than its first bars: Stereogum's Tom Breihan heard churning strings, booming drums and staccato piano, and Billboard's Chuck Taylor a muscular funk groove.

### Main Section

The published sheet music puts the song in G-sharp minor at 94 beats per minute, moving between two chords, C-sharp minor and G-sharp minor seventh. Over that loop Blige stacks sleek, deep-voiced harmonies and, in Taylor's hearing, a guttural growl.

### Bridge

In late May or early June 2001, at Dre's suggestion, Blige added a bridge and wrote its words herself.

### Outro

No source describes how the track ends. The embedded official video is a 3:45 edit; the album version runs 4:25 and the radio edit 4:04.

## Concepts in Focus

Family Affair shows what a {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} can be in hip-hop: the author of the music, not only the person at the desk. The beat existed before the singer or the lyric, and Dre also shaped the form by asking for the bridge.

It is also a good example of a {{ <concept_link name="minor key" /> }} in a party song. G-sharp minor and a two-chord loop could sound mournful; here they give the groove its seriousness, perhaps why Breihan found it vaguely stressful.

The {{ <concept_link name="bridge" /> }} is the one place the loop gives way to something new, and it belongs to Blige as a writer.

## Artistic Context

Blige, born in the Bronx, made her debut in 1992 with What's the 411? on Uptown/MCA. Family Affair, recorded at Record One in Sherman Oaks and Quad in Manhattan, was released on 24 July 2001 as the lead single of No More Drama, her fifth studio album. That album moved away from the blues-soaked R&B of its predecessor, Mary, back toward the hip-hop beats of her early-1990s work, and this song is its clearest example.

## Impact and Reception

It topped the Billboard Hot 100 for six weeks, Blige's first and only number one there, reached number one in France and the top ten in fourteen other countries, and was nominated for a Grammy. Rolling Stone ranked it 95th among the best songs of the 2000s, and The Guardian's Alexis Petridis praised the simplicity and force of Dre's production. Blige sang it at the Super Bowl LVI halftime show in 2022. Put it on and listen for the piano.
