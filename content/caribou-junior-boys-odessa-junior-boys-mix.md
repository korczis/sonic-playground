## Genre and Mood

**Caribou**'s **Odessa (Junior Boys Mix)** takes an indie-electronic pop song and points it at the club. Resident Advisor called it the most floor-ready of the single's remixes; the mood stays close to the original's, hypnotic and melancholic, but the groove underneath it is new.

## Song Summary

Odessa opened Caribou's 2010 album Swim: Dan Snaith's song about a relationship coming apart, sung over a bassline built from a sample. The Canadian duo Junior Boys kept his voice and rebuilt much of what surrounds it. Stereogum heard a funkier synth bass under the vocal in place of the original's organic lushness, and singled out how the remix amplifies the little monkey-like cry that punctuates each phrase. Resident Advisor heard shreds of sax and tom fills from the original, with percolating electro and glowing neon synths added. The remix first appeared on the Odessa single in spring 2010 and then opened Swim Remixes that autumn.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes the remix section by section or gives times, so these notes follow its elements rather than its order.

### The Voice

Snaith's vocal stays at the centre, the one part carried over whole.

### The Bass

The new synth bass is funkier than the original's, and it is what moves the song towards the dance floor.

### The Fragments

Pieces of the original's sax and its tom fills return in shreds, and the cry between phrases is pushed forward.

### The Synths

Around them sit what Resident Advisor calls percolating electro and glowing neon synths, Junior Boys' own colours.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} decides what to keep. Here the answer is the vocal and a few fragments, and the rest is new; Drowned in Sound thought the remix departed so far from the original that it left the song's premise gasping for air. Play it against {{ <track_link source="caribou-odessa.md" /> }} and judge where the song still lives.

The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} is the clearest change. The original's bass was built from a sampled sound; Junior Boys replace it with a funkier synth bass, and a new bassline is often all it takes to change what a song asks the body to do.

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} supplies the rest: the percolating electro patterns and glowing neon synths that Resident Advisor describes are the duo's signature, painted around a voice that is not theirs.

## Artistic Context

Junior Boys formed in 1999 in Hamilton, Ontario; Jeremy Greenspan and Matt Didemus make electropop rooted in 1980s synth-pop, UK garage, techno and R&B. Their link with Snaith is long: as Manitoba he remixed for their High Come Down EP in 2004, and in 2011 Caribou chose them for the ATP festival he co-curated. City Slang released Swim Remixes in the UK on 26 October 2010 and Merge in the US on 2 November, with fifteen remixes; an eleven-track version came bundled with the album.

## Impact and Reception

The critics disagreed. Resident Advisor heard the floor-ready peak of the single, Stereogum praised what it did with the cry, and Drowned in Sound found it fine but unspectacular. It still opens the remix album. Play the two versions back to back and listen for the cry.
