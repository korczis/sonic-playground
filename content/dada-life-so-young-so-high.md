## Genre and Mood

**Dada Life** built **So Young So High** for the big room: electro house with a heavy low end, made to be shouted back by a crowd. It is energetic and carefree, a party song whose lyric is as playful as the rest of the album, but what sets it apart is what the duo do to the voice.

## Song Summary

It is the fourth track on The Rules of Dada and, as Michael Wakabayashi of Magnetic Magazine pointed out, the first vocal track on the album that had not been released before. A pop-style vocal carries the hook, and the duo treat it as raw material: Lauren Lipsay of Elektro Daily heard the voice turned into an instrument by pitch manipulation and glitchy repeats. Olle Cornéer told Dancing Astronaut it was his favourite track on the album, and that he was proud of taking a high-pitched vocal style and making it much harder-hitting.

## Musical Breakdown

No timed analysis of the track has been published, so the parts below follow what reviewers describe rather than a clock.

### Intro

Magnetic heard the track open with siren-like sounds. On the album it comes straight after an instrumental, so the arrival of a voice is itself an event.

### Hook

The vocal hook is catchy; Wakabayashi heard an anthem in it, and both he and Elektro Daily predicted it would become a crowd favourite.

### Drop

Elektro Daily heard the chopped, pitched vocal sharpen the impact of the drop. Jacob Schulman of Dancing Astronaut compared the distorted vocals to Otto Knows' Million Voices.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="pitch shifting" /> }} means raising or lowering a recorded sound's pitch. Pushed far enough, a voice stops sounding like a singer and becomes a synth line with consonants. That is the trick here: a sung line is pitched up, cut into repeats and played like an instrument.

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is the part that sticks, and in this track the hook is the treated voice rather than a synth riff. It works as the setup for the {{ <concept_link name="drop" /> }}: the higher and more insistent the hook, the heavier the bass feels when it arrives. Compare {{ <track_link source="dada-life-feed-the-dada.md" /> }}, which sets a sugary vocal against a hard drop in a similar way.

## Artistic Context

Dada Life are the Stockholm duo Olle Cornéer and Stefan Engblom, formed in 2006 and named after the art movement. The Rules of Dada came out on 16 October 2012 on their own label So Much Dada with Universal, produced by the two of them; Dancing Astronaut noted how heavily it uses the Sausage Fattener plugin. So Young So High followed {{ <track_link source="dada-life-kick-out-the-epic-motherf-ker.md" /> }} and Feed the Dada as a single on 8 March 2013.

## Impact and Reception

The album reached number 4 on the US Dance/Electronic Albums chart and went gold in Sweden. The single grew through remixes: Dillon Francis chopped the vocal again and added trap-style shouts, released by So Much Dada on 16 January 2013, and the March single added remixes by Tiësto and Andybody and a Dada Philharmonic version. Play it loud and listen for the moment the voice stops being a voice.
