## Genre and Mood

**Emily Browning**'s **Sweet Dreams (Are Made Of This)** is a cover made for a soundtrack, and it turns a synth-pop hit into dark pop. The mood is haunting and dark from the first line: the song keeps its melody and its words, but the machine pulse that drove the original is gone, and what replaces it sounds like a dream about to turn into a nightmare.

## Song Summary

Browning recorded the song for Zack Snyder's 2011 film Sucker Punch, in which she plays Babydoll, and it opens the film. The co-producer Tyler Bates pointed out that her first line of dialogue comes 27 minutes in, so the song introduces her character before she speaks; Marius de Vries, who produced the covers with him, said that having her sing meant commenting in song on her character's situation. The lyric about everybody looking for something, using and being used, takes on the weight of the story it now frames.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

mxdwn described the opening as Browning's small but sweetly broken voice creeping along a light orchestration, where Dave Stewart's pulsing synth lines stood in 1983.

### Main Section

The arrangement does not stay light. Eventually, in mxdwn's words, metal guitars and a string section reminiscent of Led Zeppelin's Kashmir give the track the Danny Elfman treatment: the dream grows dark, theatrical and loud around the same fragile voice.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} can copy a song or remake it, and this one remakes it. Eurythmics built Sweet Dreams on a drum computer and a synthesizer bass sequence; here an orchestra and guitars take over, and a hard, cool hit becomes something closer to a lament. Play it next to {{ <track_link source="eurythmics-annie-lennox-dave-stewart-sweet-dreams-are-made-of-this-2005-remaster.md" /> }} and listen for what survives: the melody and the words, almost nothing else.

As {{ <concept_link name="soundtrack" /> }}, the song does a job dialogue usually does. It plays over the film's opening, while the heroine is still silent, and tells the audience how to feel about her before the story explains anything.

## Artistic Context

Browning is an Australian actress, born in Melbourne in 1988, known before Sucker Punch for Lemony Snicket's A Series of Unfortunate Events. Her audition for the film included singing, and when the crew liked the result she was offered more songs; Sweet Dreams was one of three she recorded. The soundtrack, released by WaterTower Music on 22 March 2011, sets its covers in orchestrations with a rock edge, produced by Tyler Bates and Marius de Vries.

## Impact and Reception

Reviewers singled out Browning's voice above all on this track. A review quoted on Wikipedia said she added a lush darkness and a vibrant vulnerability and compared her to Portishead's Beth Gibbons; mxdwn called the result as haunting as Helena Bonham Carter's hair. The album reached number 22 on the Billboard 200 and number one on the Top Soundtracks chart. Start it in the dark and let the guitars find you.
