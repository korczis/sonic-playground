## Genre and Mood

**Scissor Sisters** took **Comfortably Numb**, the progressive rock classic from Pink Floyd's The Wall, and turned it into disco. Their version is a cover in nu-disco and hi-NRG clothes: theatrical in its falsetto, hypnotic in its groove, and still melancholic, because the words about numbness are the same.

## Song Summary

The original, music by David Gilmour and lyrics by Roger Waters, appeared on The Wall in 1979 and as a single in 1980. Scissor Sisters kept the song and replaced almost everything around it: Wikipedia describes a radically re-arranged, disco-oriented cover. Luke McManus of RTÉ called it a hi-NRG take sung in the highest falsetto since the Bee Gees; Drowned in Sound said it turned a stoned Pink Floyd epic into a silver-trousered electro Bee Gees song.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} can be faithful or can move a song into another genre, and this one moves it a long way, from a rock concept album to the club. Side by side, the two show how much of a song lives in its arrangement rather than its melody and words.

The {{ <concept_link name="falsetto" /> }} is the hinge. Shears told Gay City News that he had always loved singing in falsetto, and the reviewers kept reaching for the Bee Gees. In disco, falsetto usually sounds weightless or euphoric; against a lyric about losing feeling, it can also sound eerie.

The version began as a {{ <concept_link name="b-side" /> }}. In 2002 the band signed a two-single deal with the small New York label A Touch of Class, and Comfortably Numb backed their first single, Electrobix. The flip side proved more popular, became a club hit and brought the band to Polydor.

## Artistic Context

Scissor Sisters were founded in New York in 2000 by Jake Shears and Babydaddy; Ana Matronic, Del Marquis and Paddy Boom completed the line-up. The band produced their self-titled debut themselves, recording it in 2002 and 2003, and Polydor released it in the UK on 2 February 2004 with Comfortably Numb as track three. According to the band's history, they sent Pink Floyd a copy and received positive remarks from Roger Waters and David Gilmour.

## Impact and Reception

Released as a single on 19 January 2004, it reached number ten in the UK and number one on the UK Dance chart and was nominated for the Grammy for Best Dance Recording.
