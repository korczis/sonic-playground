## Genre and Mood

**Il Divo** take **Quién Será**, a Mexican bolero-mambo from the 1950s, into classical crossover: four trained voices, an orchestra and a Latin rhythm section. The mood is romantic, as the whole album means to be, and underneath it runs the longing of the original Spanish lyric, melancholy words under a title that asks: who will it be?

## Song Summary

The song has two lives. Pablo Beltrán Ruiz first recorded it in 1953 as an instrumental cha-cha-chá with his orchestra; Pedro Infante sang it in 1954 and made it one of his signature songs. Luis Demetrio wrote the Spanish words and most of the song; he says Beltrán contributed only the first few chords, but Demetrio sold his rights, so Beltrán is often credited. Then Norman Gimbel dropped the melancholy Spanish words and wrote new English ones: as Sway, sung by Dean Martin in 1954, it became a pop standard. Il Divo go back to the Spanish.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} usually means a new performer; Quién Será shows it can also mean a new language. The tune travelled into English as Sway and was recorded by everyone from Dean Martin to Michael Bublé; Il Divo return it to the words it began with.

The group is built on {{ <concept_link name="voice type" /> }}. Simon Cowell wanted the sound of the Three Tenors in a young multinational quartet: two tenors, Urs Bühler and David Miller, the Spanish baritone Carlos Marín, and Sébastien Izambard, a pop singer. A melody shared among them changes colour with each voice; no source says how the lines are divided here.

The {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }} decides how much of the dance floor survives. The original was a big-band cha-cha-chá; this recording adds a symphony orchestra, and the two credited arrangers had to fit both. Compare {{ <track_link source="bjork-it-s-oh-so-quiet.md" /> }}, another old song recast for a big new sound.

## Artistic Context

Il Divo were formed in 2003. Amor & Pasión, their first album sung entirely in Spanish, was recorded in Miami between February and September 2015 and released by Syco Music, first in Japan on 28 October 2015; it mixes bolero, mambo and tango from Spain, Cuba, Argentina and Mexico, and was their last album for Sony. Marín died in December 2021.

## Impact and Reception

The album went to number one on Billboard's Latin Albums chart and reached number 13 in Britain. The Philippine Star found it very well worth the wait. Hear Dean Martin's Sway, then this, and listen to what the Spanish words change.
