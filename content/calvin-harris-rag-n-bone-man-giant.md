## Genre and Mood

**Calvin Harris** and **Rag'n'Bone Man** made **Giant** a house song with a soul singer at its centre. Wikipedia files it as gospel-inspired dance and house, and Bandwagon called it a "fist-pumping dance track". The feelings follow from that pairing: uplifting and energetic in the beat, soulful in the voice.

## Song Summary

The idea is contrast. Harris, who produced it, keeps the backing light, a steady house beat with piano and organ; Rag'n'Bone Man, who wrote it with Jamie Hartman and Troy Miller, sings over it in the deep voice that Bandwagon heard as bringing "gospel-like weight". Around them sits what Bandwagon called a "trumpet-heavy, pulsating-synth-laced backbeat". The horns are the detail people remember, for better or worse.

## Musical Breakdown

### Main Section

No timed analysis maps Giant section by section, and this page does not invent one. The Singles Jukebox reviewers, who disagreed about almost everything else, describe the same ingredients: the "organ peels and house piano" set against "the gospel bigness" of the voice (Alfred Soto), keyboards, a "steady beat" and a "jazzy drop" (Juana Giaimo).

### Breakdown

The instrumental breaks belong to the horns. Will Adams enjoyed "how present the horns are" and noted that Harris spreads them across the stereo field and keeps the backing nimble; Joshua Minsoo Kim found the horn-filled breaks ill-conceived. Listen and choose a side.

## Concepts in Focus

Giant is credited to Harris "with" Rag'n'Bone Man, a variation on the {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} credit: the producer owns the track, the guest owns the voice. Here the singer is also one of the song's writers, and the single is sold on both names.

The {{ <concept_link name="house groove" /> }} is what holds the soul elements together. Piano and organ bring the colours of soul and gospel; the steady beat underneath is house, the same marriage heard in {{ <track_link source="axwell-max-c-i-found-u-radio-edit.md" /> }}.

Two months after release, a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} EP gave the song to six other producers: Robin Schulz, Purple Disco Machine, Weiss, Michael Calfan, Audien and Laidback Luke. Compare one with the original and hear how much of the song lives in the voice.

## Artistic Context

Recorded in 2018 and released by Columbia on 11 January 2019, Giant came out as a standalone single and was later collected on Harris's first compilation, 96 Months, in 2024. The two performed it on The Graham Norton Show and at the 2019 Brit Awards. The video follows a man in a blue jacket and trousers running from the suburbs into a forest, where he dances with riders in red jackets.

## Impact and Reception

Critics were divided, but listeners were not. Giant spent five weeks at number two in the UK, topped the charts in Belgium and Scotland and the US Dance Club Songs chart, and PPL named it the most played song in the UK in 2019. It was nominated for Song of the Year at the 2020 Brits and won the Ivor Novello Award for Most Performed Work. Turn it up and listen for the horns.
