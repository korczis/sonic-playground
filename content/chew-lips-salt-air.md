## Genre and Mood

**Chew Lips** made **Salt Air** as synth-pop for the dance floor, the indie dance of 2009 with a disco pulse under it. It is catchy in the most literal way, short and quick to lodge in the head, and bittersweet: NME called it "an addictive slice of heart-wrenching disco-pop", and both halves of that phrase matter.

## Song Summary

Salt Air was the trio's second single on the French label Kitsuné, released on 6 July 2009, a few months after their debut, Solo. It is under three minutes long. The writing is credited to all three members, the singer Alicia Huertas, known as Tigs, and the multi-instrumentalists Will Sanderson and James Watkins. NME's live reviewer described it as the filthiest ode to a car crash she had heard, a hint that the bright surface hides something darker; the review does not explain the lyric further, and neither does this page.

## Musical Breakdown

### Main Section

No published review walks through the single part by part, so this breakdown stays with what was written about the band at the time. Seeing them at The Social in London in March 2009, NME's Lisa Wright counted an array of Korgs, drum machines, laptops and strings, with the two men simply pulsing while Tigs prowled the stage, and she called their set clever, streamlined synth-pop full of eight-bit soul-disco gems. Kitsuné's own biography speaks of glossy electronica and an almost cinematic sweep.

### Remixes

The single came out with remixes by Jupiter, Dekker & Johan and Two Door Cinema Club, plus a bonus remix by Tommy Sparks & The Fury; a remix by Alex Kapranos of Franz Ferdinand appeared on a Kitsuné Maison compilation.

## Concepts in Focus

A trio can sound like a full dance band because of the {{ <concept_link name="drum machine" /> }}: it holds the disco pulse steady while the players' hands stay free for keyboards. The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} does the rest (NME counted several Korgs on stage), and that is how Chew Lips kept their promise not to sound like just another guitar band.

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} package sends a single out with versions by other artists, so that DJs of different tastes can each find one to play. Comparing them with the original shows what belongs to the song and what belongs to its production.

## Artistic Context

Chew Lips formed in London in 2008. After Solo and Salt Air on Kitsuné, they made their debut album Unicorn with David Kosten, released in January 2010; Salt Air is not on it. The band were quiet by 2015, when Huertas said she was writing only for other artists.

## Impact and Reception

In 2009 NME placed Chew Lips among the year's bold synth-pop newcomers led by women, beside La Roux and Little Boots; hear {{ <track_link source="la-roux-bulletproof.md" /> }}. Salt Air never made it onto the band's album; it remains a short, bright single with a shadow in it. Play it twice: once to dance, once to listen.
