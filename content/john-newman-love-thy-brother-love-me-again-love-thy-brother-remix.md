## Genre and Mood

**John Newman** sang **Love Me Again**; **Love Thy Brother** remixed it. Their version moves the 2013 hit towards indie dance while keeping its soul at the centre: the longing of the original is still there in the voice, with a funkier groove under it.

## Song Summary

The song is Newman's plea to a lover for a second chance, written with Steve Booker. The remix keeps that voice and builds a new backing around it. The one review of this version, on the blog My Music Is Better Than Yours in June 2013, picked out two things: funky electric guitar and big-room piano. The blog had long admired the duo for bringing rock and funk influence into their remixes, and called this one its introduction to Newman's warm voice.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} rebuilds a recording from its parts. The vocal and the song stay Newman's; the setting is the remixers'. Here the reviewer singled out the instruments, funky electric guitar and big-room piano; critics of the original heard horns, strings and handclaps.

Love Me Again was released with four remixes, each an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }} for a different audience. The digital download put Kove's drum and bass first, then Gemini and Ejeca, and this one last, as track five. Compare the stretched piano house of {{ <track_link source="john-newman-ejeca-love-me-again-ejeca-remix.md" /> }}, just before it.

A {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} is the feel that makes a rhythm move, more than its tempo. A funky guitar part is one of the classic ways to shape it, and that is what the reviewer pointed to here.

## Artistic Context

Love Thy Brother are the Montreal producers and DJs Conor and Liam Clarke, brothers, as Vice's Noisey reported in 2015. Conor, the elder, co-founded the production company Saintwoods; Liam was producing in Toronto while still in high school. The original Love Me Again, produced by Booker and Mike Spencer, was Newman's debut solo single; the remix bundle was released on 30 June 2013.

## Impact and Reception

The original went to number one in the UK. The remix found a life of its own on compilations: MusicBrainz lists it on The Disco Boys, Volume 14 and Now Chillout in 2014 and on Now That's What I Call House in 2015. Play the original first, then this one, and listen to what the guitar changes.
