## Genre and Mood

**Joe Goddard** made **Taking Over** the quiet end of a club record. Electronic Rumors called it a soft and sweet electropop tune; Black Plastic heard it as more pop than dance, a spectral electronic ballad. The mood is dreamy and intimate, and the same review found it surprisingly personal, which is unusual from a producer best known for making people dance.

## Song Summary

The song is about being overwhelmed. Black Plastic read it as the love of music starting to consume you at the moment on a night out when everything comes together. The music is gentle: Resident Advisor heard Goddard laying sweet and sleepy vocals over soft synths and a plaintive guitar line, and Black Plastic heard gently picked guitar melodies under his softest possible delivery. The detail that sticks is the refrain, the title sung again and again until it does what it describes.

## Musical Breakdown

No review maps the track bar by bar; this breakdown follows the lyric Greco-Roman published with the video and what the reviews heard.

### Intro and Refrain

The song begins with its refrain rather than a verse, the line about feeling it taking over, repeated over soft synths.

### Middle Section

The words turn more serious: a sense of death standing close, a plea to forget oneself, a promise to grow old together, and the admission that the listener already knows the singer's heart.

### Outro

The refrain returns and the song closes as it opened, still caught in the feeling.

## Concepts in Focus

The refrain is the {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }}: in the published lyric the title line opens and closes the song, repeated many times, so the hook is the idea itself, a feeling that keeps coming back.

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} is used here for softness, not drive. Goddard builds club tracks with synths, as in {{ <track_link source="joe-goddard-mara-carlyle-she-burns.md" /> }} on the same EP, but in Taking Over they become a cushion for a voice and a guitar.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, released in November 2013, was directed by Mark James. A young woman takes a cab home after a night out with a snack and falls in love with it: Large Don, a cheeky, singing doner kebab inspired by the painter Giuseppe Arcimboldo. Electronic Rumors called it hazy, with just the right amount of weirdness.

## Artistic Context

Joe Goddard, born in London in 1979, co-founded the synth-pop band Hot Chip with Alexis Taylor, is a member of The 2 Bears and in 2007 co-founded the label Greco-Roman. The Taking Over EP came out on Greco-Roman on 17 June 2013 with four tracks: Step Together with Boris Dlugosch, Bassline '12, She Burns with Mara Carlyle, and this title track, which closes it.

## Impact and Reception

Resident Advisor called Taking Over a lovely closer while preferring the instrumental Bassline '12; Black Plastic called it beautiful. The EP version runs 5:36; the video runs 3:44. Start with the video, then hear the full version through.
