## Genre and Mood

**Daft Punk** built **Harder, Better, Faster, Stronger** out of a funk sample and a list of verbs, and it became a cornerstone of French house. Electronic precision meets a funk swagger in a mood that is energetic, futuristic and, by now, simply iconic.

## Song Summary

The lyric is a mantra of self-improvement recited by a voice run through a talk box and vocoder: work it, make it, do it, makes us harder, better, faster, stronger. The groove is chopped from Edwin Birdsong's 1979 track "Cola Bottle Baby", looped and filtered until it feels like a machine with soul. The genius is in the arrangement of the words: the same phrases are cut up, reordered and stacked until they become rhythm themselves.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

The sampled riff arrives first, a strutting, slightly dirty funk figure that sets the tempo and the attitude.

### Verse 1

The robot voice begins listing commands, each word landing on the beat like a step in an assembly line. It is simple and instantly memorable.

### Build-up

The vocal phrases start to overlap, and the filtered sample opens up, adding bite and brightness as the energy rises.

### Main Section

The words are chopped and recombined faster and faster, pairs of phrases interleaving until the line becomes a puzzle you can sing.

### Climax

Everything stacks into a dense wall of vocoder and groove, the mantra repeated with full force like a factory at peak production. It is euphoric in a strangely disciplined way, a rush that never loses its grid.

### Outro

The layers strip back to the core loop and the track winds down with the same mechanical confidence it started with.

## Artistic Context

The track appeared on *Discovery*, Daft Punk's second album, released in 2001 by Thomas Bangalter and Guy-Manuel de Homem-Christo. Its official video is a segment of *Interstella 5555*, the animated film made with the Japanese artist Leiji Matsumoto that set the whole album to pictures. The record pushed their sound toward pop, disco and robotic vocals, and this track is its most compact statement: a found sample, a handful of words and an obsessive sense of arrangement.

## Impact and Reception

The song grew bigger with every year. Kanye West built his 2007 hit "Stronger" around it, and Daft Punk joined him on stage at the 2008 Grammy Awards, introducing the robots to a new audience. It remains one of the most sampled, quoted and remixed electronic tracks ever made. It earns its place here as pure design. Work it, make it, do it: press play.
