## Genre and Mood

**Amy Winehouse** sings **You Know I'm No Good** as a smoky meeting of soul, R&B and jazz phrasing. It is raw and gritty in its honesty, and bittersweet in the way it makes a confession of betrayal sound almost seductive.

## Song Summary

The song is a first-person admission of cheating on a good man who loves her, sung without excuses. Winehouse narrates a messy relationship in vivid, unglamorous detail and presents herself as helpless before her own self-destructive impulses, owning her worst behaviour with a mix of guilt and defiance. Mark Ronson's production frames her with a slinky groove, swinging drums and stabbing horns, so the whole confession struts. The contrast between the frankness of the lyric and the polish of the retro arrangement is what makes it unforgettable. Her phrasing is the real instrument: she sings lazily behind the beat, clips words short, then lets a single syllable stretch out. It is the sound of someone who knows she is in the wrong and refuses to pretend otherwise.

## Musical Breakdown

The song rides a single uptempo groove, with the horns and the voice doing the dramatic work.

### Intro

The band opens the track with the groove already locked, and sharp, punchy horns quickly set the noir mood.

### Verse 1

Winehouse enters with a conversational, almost casual delivery, painting the scene in unglamorous detail.

### Chorus

The title line arrives as a shrug and a warning at once, her voice dropping into a darker, richer register.

### Bridge

The horns swell and recede as the story deepens, the groove never letting go of its swagger.

### Outro

The arrangement holds its tension to the end, the final lines lingering with unresolved guilt.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} of You Know I'm No Good comes from how it was made. Ronson recorded most of his Back to Black songs at Daptone in Brooklyn with members of the Dap-Kings, Sharon Jones's band: drums, piano, guitar and bass were played together in one room, the drums captured with a single microphone, with plenty of spill between the instruments. Three of the band's horn players, on baritone sax, tenor sax and trumpet, added the brass. Listen for how the parts lean on each other in time; that slight looseness is what a click track and separate overdubs would have ironed out.

The song also exists as a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} with a verse by Ghostface Killah of the Wu-Tang Clan. In the United States that version led the album's release, and a longer edit with more Ghostface verses appears on his album More Fish. Billboard thought the original "could be no better" but heard the rap verse as a "ferocious discordant sputter" in the middle. Comparing the two shows how a guest verse can turn a private confession into a conversation.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} was directed by Phil Griffin, who also made the clip for "Rehab", and shot on sets built at 3 Mills Studios in East London. It moves Winehouse through a bar, a bedroom and a bathtub, staging the lyric's affair as a small film.

## Artistic Context

You Know I'm No Good comes from Back to Black, released by Island in October 2006 and produced in two halves by Salaam Remi and Mark Ronson; this song is one of the six Ronson tracks, written by Winehouse alone. Tom Elmhirst, who mixed the single, later described Ronson's original mix as radical in its panning, with the drums all on one side. The album leaned on the sound of 1950s and 1960s girl groups, and the song was released as its second single in the UK on 8 January 2007, reaching number 18.

## Impact and Reception

Entertainment Weekly ranked it the second-best song of 2007, and People called it instantly memorable. Back to Black became one of the best-selling albums of the century and brought Winehouse five Grammys in 2008, and her performance of this song and "Rehab" at the 2008 ceremony, broadcast by satellite from London, pushed it back up the US charts. Arctic Monkeys covered it on their 2007 tour. Few pop songs are this candid about the singer's own failings; listen closely to the phrasing, every line is lived in.
