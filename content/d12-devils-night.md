## Genre and Mood

**D12**'s **Devils Night** is hip-hop and rap as a guided tour of a burning city. It is dark, gritty and provocative by design: the Sputnikmusic reviewer heard it trying to paint a picture of the hell that is Detroit and then cast the group as the devils who reign over it.

## Song Summary

The song shares its name with the album, and both take it from Devil's Night, the Detroit tradition of setting abandoned buildings on fire on the night before Halloween. RapReviews heard the album as the soundtrack to the crew's story, laced with macabre humour. Eminem produced the track, with Jeff Bass credited as co-producer; the label's credits add DJ Head as drum programmer and list seven writers, from Marshall Mathers and Denaun Porter to Rufus Johnson (Bizarre) and DeShaun Holton (Proof).

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the track part by part; this follows the Sputnikmusic review and the credits.

### The Beat

The reviewer heard a good beat with a Satanic feel. The album's personnel list gives Jeff Bass bass, keyboards and guitar on this track and DJ Head its drum programming, and Wikipedia's sample credits name no sample for it.

### The Verses

The members do not simply queue up. In the reviewer's words they are interweaved, with Eminem flowing smoothly into Swift, who in turn converses with Kon Artis.

### The Chorus

Eminem performs the hook, according to the same review.

### Bizarre's Turn

Bizarre, the reviewer noticed, starts to turn self-deprecating here, which sets the scene for Revelation, the song about growing up that closes the album's track list.

## Concepts in Focus

Devils Night is a {{ <concept_link name="posse cut" /> }} with a twist: as the reviewer describes it, the voices are interwoven rather than lined up in separate blocks, so the track plays like a conversation among the crew. Compare the more conventional relay of {{ <track_link source="d12-blow-my-buzz.md" /> }} on the same album.

Its {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} belongs to Eminem, the group's most famous voice: the chorus is what a listener carries away, and here it carries the album's title.

The {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} credits show how a group album gets made from inside the group. Eminem built the track, Jeff Bass co-produced and played its bass, keyboards and guitar, and DJ Head programmed the drums.

## Artistic Context

D12, the Dirty Dozen, formed in Detroit in 1996; after Bugz was killed in 1999, Eminem joined the core line-up, and the group recorded Devil's Night in his memory. Released on 19 June 2001 by Shady Records and Interscope Records, it was the first album released on Shady Records, produced mainly by Eminem and Dr. Dre. RapReviews heard it picking up where Amityville and Under the Influence left off on The Marshall Mathers LP, darker and moodier than its lighthearted lead single.

## Impact and Reception

Devil's Night entered the Billboard 200 at number one and went double platinum in the United States despite mixed reviews. The title track was never a single; the Sputnikmusic reviewer gave it four out of five, a strong song that is not thematically advanced. Play it as the album's centrepiece, then go straight on to Revelation.
