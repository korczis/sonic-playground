## Genre and Mood

**My Morning Jacket** put **Highly Suspicious** third on Evil Urges, and it is where the album stops behaving. The band is filed under alternative rock and rock, but this song is something else: The Line of Best Fit heard ridiculously high vocals, heavy guitars, maniacal laughter and a general theatrical feel. Theatrical and quirky are the feelings named here, and the reviews that follow explain why nobody agreed on more.

## Song Summary

The song is built on a contrast of voices. Jim James, who wrote it, sings very high, and a low, rumbling line answers him; the Line of Best Fit reviewer compared that reply to Nick Cave or Tom Waits. Around them are heavy guitars with the odd solo, and the AV Club heard the track open on a synth bass and end up swallowed by a chant-along chorus that sounded sung by a military robot and a giggly little girl. It runs a little over three minutes.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="falsetto" /> }} is the light register above a singer's normal voice, and it carries very different meanings: tenderness in soul, glamour in disco. Here it is pushed so high that it reads as a character rather than a confession. Compare the knowing soul falsetto of {{ <track_link source="electric-guest-this-head-i-hold.md" /> }}.

The verses work as {{ <concept_link name="call and response" /> }}: a high call, a low answer, as if two figures were arguing inside one song.

The song also shows {{ <concept_link name="sync licensing" /> }} at work. It was used in the season six premiere of One Tree Hill and in an episode of American Dad called My Morning Straitjacket.

## Artistic Context

Evil Urges, My Morning Jacket's fifth studio album, was recorded at Avatar Studios in New York in November 2007, produced by Joe Chiccarelli and Jim James, and released on 10 June 2008 by ATO Records in the US and Rough Trade in the UK. The band then was James, Tom Blankenship, Patrick Hallahan, Carl Broemel and Bo Koster. The album reached number nine on the Billboard 200 and was nominated for the Grammy for Best Alternative Music Album, which went to Radiohead's In Rainbows.

## Impact and Reception

Few songs have split critics so neatly. The Line of Best Fit called it inventive and exciting, so much so that it made the rest of the album seem duller. Stereogum admitted it still did not know what to make of it. Three years later, Jim DeRogatis of WBEZ called it a serious contender for one of the worst songs ever recorded. Listen once and decide which side you are on.
