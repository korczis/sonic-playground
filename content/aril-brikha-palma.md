## Genre and Mood

**Aril Brikha**'s **Palma** is techno in the Detroit line: its label's Bandcamp page tags it Detroit techno beside techno, house and ambient, and Discogs files the record under techno, tech house and minimal. The mood is groovy and atmospheric rather than hard. Bleep's description of the EP hears the title track moving in sweeps of chords and arpeggios over a deep, grooving bassline, which is a good summary of where its feeling comes from.

## Song Summary

Palma is the title track of a three-track EP, with Porto Colom and San Agustin, all written and produced by Brikha. All three titles are names of places on Mallorca, though no source I found says what they mean to him. Brikha first released the EP himself as a download on 26 September 2010; in December 2011 his label Art of Vengeance pressed it on 12-inch vinyl as AOV 004 and released it digitally on 5 December. On the record, Palma takes side A alone at 45 rpm, and at eight minutes and thirty-eight seconds it is the EP's middle length.

### Main Section

Listen for the harmony as the moving part and the bassline as the anchor.

## Concepts in Focus

An {{ <concept_link name="arpeggio" /> }} is a chord played one note at a time, and in Palma, as Bleep hears it, the arpeggios share the work with sweeping chords: the harmony itself becomes rhythm. That is a Detroit habit, where melody and harmony carry as much feeling as the drums.

Underneath, the {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} keeps the track grounded. A deep, grooving bass gives the chords something to move against, and in a track this long it is what keeps the dance floor with it.

Brikha's instrument is the {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }}. Wikipedia notes that he started on the keyboard at seven and was using a sequencer by seventeen. No source lists the gear behind Palma, so the page does not guess it.

## Artistic Context

Brikha was born in Iran in 1976 and grew up in Jönköping, Sweden; as a teenager he heard Depeche Mode, Kraftwerk and Jean-Michel Jarre, and friends called what he made Detroit techno. His Art of Vengeance EP came out on Fragile in 1998, and his debut album Deeparture in Time on Transmat. In 2011 Art of Vengeance became the name of his own label, which reissued that album in February and released {{ <track_link source="aril-brikha-forever-frost.md" /> }} in April, before Palma in December.

## Impact and Reception

I could find no press review of Palma. Clone greeted the 12-inch as Brikha back on his own label with post-Detroit techno tracks of the usual high quality, and a later EP there, Definition of D, was announced as a tribute to Detroit. Compare the melodic techno of {{ <track_link source="kolsch-grey.md" /> }}, then give Palma all eight and a half minutes.
