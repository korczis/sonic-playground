## Genre and Mood

**Gorillaz** are known for dub basslines, hip-hop guests and cartoon menace; **12D3** shows how wide the project was from the start. It is alternative pop on acoustic instruments, filed among the trip-hop of the first Gorillaz records but gentler than any of it, warm and soulful: PopMatters heard acoustic guitar, acoustic piano and warm, gospel-influenced vocal harmonies.

## Song Summary

12D3 came out of the sessions for the first Gorillaz album but not onto it. It appeared on the band's first record, the Tomorrow Comes Today EP, and a year later closed the songs of G-Sides, the B-sides collection of that period. Where most early Gorillaz is built on beats and samples, this one rests on a guitar, a piano and a group of voices.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="b-side" /> }} is the extra track of a single or EP, the one that was not the point of the release. 12D3 is the fourth and last song of the Tomorrow Comes Today EP of 27 November 2000, after the title track, Rock the House and Latin Simone. The first three reached the album; 12D3 did not.

That is why the {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }} matters. G-Sides gathered the B-sides of the first Gorillaz era, first in Japan on 12 December 2001, then in the US and Europe in 2002. It opens with the Soulchild remix of 19-2000, heard on {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-19-2000-soulchild-remix.md" /> }}, and closes its songs with 12D3: the loudest and the quietest Gorillaz of the period on one disc.

{{ <concept_link name="vocal harmony" /> }} is what PopMatters heard first. Voices singing different notes of one chord can sound like a choir; here the reviewer heard gospel warmth in them, rare for a band that usually let one cool voice sit over a beat.

## Artistic Context

Gorillaz are the virtual band Damon Albarn and the artist Jamie Hewlett formed in 1998, with four cartoon members; the singer 2-D is voiced by Albarn. Albarn recorded the debut from 1998 to 2000 in London and Jamaica, and the sessions produced the EP first. G-Sides credits every song to Gorillaz and its production to Gorillaz and Dan the Automator, and was released by Parlophone.

## Impact and Reception

G-Sides reached number 65 in the UK and 84 on the US Billboard 200, and Metacritic's average of its reviews is 65. Marshall Bowden of PopMatters found nothing on it to match Clint Eastwood or 19-2000, but said the disc ends on a strong note with 12D3, called it a beautiful song and hoped the band would try more experiments like it. Play it after the hits and hear the cartoon band drop its mask.
