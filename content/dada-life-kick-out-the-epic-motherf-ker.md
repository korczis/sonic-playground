## Genre and Mood

**Dada Life** open their second album with **Kick Out The Epic Motherf\*\*ker**, and it is big-room electro house at full volume: energetic, intense and still playful, because with this Stockholm duo the joke and the bass arrive together. This page is about the vocal version that opens The Rules of Dada, about 3:23 long.

## Song Summary

The track first came out on 5 December 2011 on the duo's own label So Much Dada as an original mix of almost six minutes. On 12 March 2012 Universal Music AB released it again with a vocal: a radio edit, a clean edit, an extended vocal mix and an instrumental. The vocal version credits two more writers, Adam Baptiste and Vincent Pontare. AllMusic's David Jeffries heard the result build from a literate motivational seminar to a lunkheaded rave riot.

## Musical Breakdown

No review we found walks through the album version or gives times; the parts below follow the cited reviews, partly of the original mix.

### Intro

Elektro Daily heard the album open perfectly with ominous synths and a steady kick drum pulse; Dancing Astronaut's album review described a familiar thumping bass and a familiar vocal that draws listeners in.

### Drop

In December 2011 Dancing Astronaut described the original mix's eerily looming, razor-sharp synths and heavy kicks leading to a large drop aimed at the crowd.

### Breakdown

The same review heard a breakdown that quiets down before the bass returns louder than before.

### Outro

The album version ends inside three and a half minutes; the extended vocal mix runs almost six.

## Concepts in Focus

This is a clear case of the {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }}: a club original, a vocal single, an instrumental and an album cut, each with its own job. The {{ <concept_link name="radio edit" /> }} trims the long mix to a length radio and playlists can use.

The {{ <concept_link name="drop" /> }} is the moment a build-up resolves into the full beat and bass. Dancing Astronaut's account of this track, a drop followed by a breakdown that pulls back so the bass can hit harder, shows big-room pacing built for a crowd.

The 2012 {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, which IMVDb credits to director Mickey Finnegan, stages the same contrast: the duo stand calmly in suburban scenes, then destroy things in slow motion when the intense sections arrive. An earlier official video, from December 2011, was cut from footage of their Freaknight Halloween show.

## Artistic Context

Dada Life are Olle Corneér and Stefan Engblom, a duo formed in 2006 and named after the absurdist Dada movement. The Rules of Dada came out on 16 October 2012 on So Much Dada with Universal; the two produced it themselves, and this track was its first single, followed by {{ <track_link source="dada-life-feed-the-dada.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

The single reached number 18 in Sweden and number 42 in Ireland, and Wikipedia's discography lists it as certified Platinum in Sweden. Elektro Daily found it still drew listeners in after months of festival play, and in 2018 We Rave You called it the duo's most iconic release.
