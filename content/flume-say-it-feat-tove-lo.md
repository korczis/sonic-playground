## Genre and Mood

**Flume** and **Tove Lo** made **Say It** a future-bass song that sounds like weather: sultry in its vocal, intense in its drums, bittersweet in what it is about. Billboard called it a slower jam with a highly sexual side, portraying a painful, repetitive breakup over a melodic chorus and soft beats.

## Song Summary

Flume went looking for Tove Lo after hearing her Habits (Stay High) in a Los Angeles bar following a Kanye West show, and contacted her the next day. She was in the city between tours; she built melodies over one of his instrumental ideas, and the two wrote the song in two days. Daniel Johns joined as co-writer, co-producer and additional vocalist.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

No source we found describes the opening bars on their own, so we start where the critics did, with the voice.

### Verse

Katherine St Asaph of The Singles Jukebox heard Flume's production staying out of the way of a crystalline voice; Crystal Leww heard the verses let the vocals tumble out.

### Chorus

The chorus is where opinions split. Will Rivitz heard a stuttering half-time beat and tom rolls under one of the year's most gyroscopic choruses; Crystal Leww heard it echo, clang and fill up the space. Katie Gill disliked the chorus beat, and Cassy Gress its long vocal silences.

### Main Section

William John heard handclaps build tension before synths heavy as concrete take over. DIY found the middle section purposeless, the song unsure whether to be a slow-burner or a party starter.

### Outro

How the song ends is not described in any source we found; Tim de Reuse felt it circled one point for four minutes.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="half-time" /> }} feel puts the snare's backbeat half as often, so the song seems to move at half the speed while the tempo stays the same. That is what Rivitz heard in the chorus, and it explains why a fast track can feel slow, heavy and stormy.

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} parts carry the feeling the voice holds back: St Asaph pointed to a wailing synth guitar that adds emotional weight. Compare the wobbling chords of {{ <track_link source="flume-holdin-on.md" /> }}, made four years earlier.

As a {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}, Tove Lo is credited as co-writer and co-producer, not only as the singer.

## Artistic Context

Say It is the third single from Skin, Flume's second album, released by Future Classic in Australia and Mom + Pop in the US; Skin won Best Dance/Electronic Album at the 2017 Grammys. Annie Mac premiered the song on BBC Radio 1 on 20 April 2016, and it was released two days later.

## Impact and Reception

It reached number five in Australia, topped the ARIA dance chart, reached four in New Zealand and 60 on the Billboard Hot 100, and went four times platinum in Australia. A Clean Bandit remix followed in October 2016. Turn it up for the chorus and decide which critic was right.
