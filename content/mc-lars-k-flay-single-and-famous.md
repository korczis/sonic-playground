## Genre and Mood

**MC Lars** and **K.Flay** close their 2009 EP with **Single and Famous**, and a critic heard it as the record's powerhouse. It is hip-hop and rap, but its engine is a guitar: the nerdcore blog Hipster, please! heard a driving, tinny guitar with just enough punk rock in it. The feelings named here, defiant and cathartic, come from that review, which called the song the ultimate break-up anthem.

## Song Summary

The song is about getting over someone by telling them off. In the review's words, it is about finding the strength to move on by delivering a surgically precise parting insult to an ex, and it is harshly emotional and almost uncomfortably autobiographical. It is the seventh and last track of Single and Famous, 3:50 long, and the critic called it the EP's musical powerhouse, its lyrical haymaker and its emotional centre. Where much of the EP sounds like K.Flay, he wrote, this one is textbook MC Lars, at least in the instrumental.

## Musical Breakdown

No source maps the song's sections or gives times, so the parts below follow the one review, not the recording's order.

### Sound Breakdown

The guitar anchors the track: driving, tinny and punkish. The critic called the instrumental textbook MC Lars and heard in his performance energy left over from This Gigantic Robot Kills, his solo album of the same year.

### Main Section

Both artists perform. The review heard MC Lars, laid back for most of the EP, finally pushing hard, and K.Flay sounding pained, angered and relatable among the aggression.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="rapping" /> }} is the craft both started from, and here it meets a rock arrangement. MC Lars describes his style as post-punk laptop rap; this song shows what the phrase can mean: rap over a punkish guitar rather than over a soul sample.

The critic's warmest line goes to K.Flay's {{ <concept_link name="double tracking" /> }}: her vocal doubling, he wrote, had never sounded better. Recording a line twice and playing both takes together thickens a voice.

The EP had no outside {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} in charge. The upload on MC Lars's channel says the two produced it themselves, with help from the Rondo Brothers and the Dust Collector.

## Artistic Context

MC Lars, born Andrew Nielsen in 1982, studied English at Stanford and Shakespeare at Oxford and is called a pioneer of lit-hop; he founded Horris Records in 2004, which later became self-distributed. K.Flay, born Kristine Flaherty in 1985, began making music at Stanford and released her laptop-made mixtape Suburban Rap Queen in 2005. Single and Famous, their first collaboration, came out on Horris in September 2009. She signed to RCA in 2012 and in 2016 became the first artist on Night Street Records at Interscope.

## Impact and Reception

The EP was a small independent release, and the Hipster, please! review is the only one found. Its verdict on the whole record was mixed, a little underwhelming at first, then tuneful, good-natured and worth the price, but it saved its praise for this closing track. MC Lars posted it on his YouTube channel in 2015. Play it after Somebody's Watching Me and hear how the EP ends.
