## Genre and Mood

**Egbert** released **Vrijheid**, Dutch for freedom, as the title track of a three-track EP in October 2011, and it is techno with the lights on. The producer King Unique, writing in the feedback Egbert posted with the track, liked its glistening chords and twinkling percussion and called it a lovely bit of sunshine techno; Dosem called the whole release a melodic techno EP. Warm, energetic and upbeat are the words Egbert's own biography uses for his sound, and they fit what the DJs heard here.

## Song Summary

Vrijheid is an instrumental club track of about seven and a half minutes, released on Gem Records beside Groovegg and Dieper. Its subject is in its title and its effect is described by the people who played it. Laurent Garnier named Vrijheid his pick from the EP, Agoria said it stood out, Ramon Tapia called it big and Martin Eyerer called it very driving stuff. Not every DJ chose it: Adam Beyer and Len Faki preferred Dieper, which Pig & Dan's Pig called a growling techno beast. That split says something about the record: Vrijheid is the bright one.

## Artistic Context

Egbert van der Gugten is a Dutch techno producer and live act. His own biography says he produced in a loft in Rotterdam until Secret Cinema (Jeroen Verheij), in the biography's words a Dutch techno legend, found him online and gave him, at twenty, a stage and a label, and that Sven Väth then asked him for a Cocoon release, Vreugdevuur, in 2009. Verheij founded Gem Records in 2009, and Gem released Egbert's debut album in 2013.

## Impact and Reception

Vrijheid was a DJ record rather than a chart one, and the roll call of DJs who said they would play it, Hernan Cattaneo, Martin Eyerer and Orde of Slam among them, is its reception. Play it in the daytime, and listen for the chords.
