## Genre and Mood

**K.Flay** blends alternative rock, electronic production and a hip-hop flow in **"High Enough"**, a sound that feels both raw and uplifting. Her label called it one of the most openly upbeat tracks on her album, and the mood is empowering and reflective at once.

## Song Summary

K.Flay has said the song began as a reply to the many songs about getting high: what if I'm already high enough? The lyric compares love to a drug and settles on wanting to feel exactly what she is feeling, without anything altering it. Musically it fuses, in the release's words, breezy rhythm and bubbly guitar lines, with K.Flay's smooth flow matched to a sing-song melody. The catchy chorus and steady beat drive the idea home.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro: Stripped-Down Vibe

The song begins stripped down, guitar and K.Flay's low-key vocal setting an intimate tone and leaving her voice at the centre.

### Build-up: Adding Depth

Bass and electronic beats come in, adding layers of texture and building the contrast between the calm opening and the fuller chorus.

### Main Section: Power in Simplicity

The chorus turns the title into the song's mantra. It is short and singable, its simplicity carrying the message.

### Bridge: Reflective Pause

A quieter passage offers a moment of introspection before the final chorus, her delivery at its most conversational.

### Climax: Anthemic Finish

The final chorus brings beats, guitar and vocals together in the song's fullest moment.

### Outro: Gradual Decline

The track closes by returning to a more subdued state as the beat fades.

## Concepts in Focus

High Enough is a clean example of {{ <concept_link name="verse-chorus form" /> }}. The verses carry the flow, the observations and the argument; the chorus returns with the same words each time, so the question in the title becomes the thing you remember. Mapping where each section starts is a good first exercise in hearing song structure.

That returning chorus carries the {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }}: the title phrase itself, set to a sing-song melody that contrasts with the rhythmic, half-rapped verses. Producer Mike Elizondo, who co-wrote the song, keeps the arrangement light enough that the hook never has to fight for space.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Lorraine Nicolson and premiered in May 2017, adds a story the lyric does not tell: an abusive couple in a rundown trailer park briefly stop fighting and dance when K.Flay, in the next trailer, plays music on a cassette deck. The video reframes a love song as a song about what music itself can do.

## Artistic Context

K.Flay is the American artist Kristine Flaherty, whose work mixes indie rock, electronic production and hip-hop. She was the first signing to Night Street Records, the Interscope imprint founded by her friend and collaborator Dan Reynolds of Imagine Dragons. "High Enough" was released on 10 March 2017 as the third single from her second album, *Every Where Is Some Where*, after "Blood in the Cut" and "Black Wave"; the album, produced by Mike Elizondo and Tommy English, followed in April 2017.

## Impact and Reception

"High Enough" reached number 4 on the US Billboard Alternative Airplay chart and became her best-known song, certified platinum in the US and gold in the UK and France. Its honesty about wanting to feel things fully is what has kept it in rotation. Play it once for the hook and once for the words.
