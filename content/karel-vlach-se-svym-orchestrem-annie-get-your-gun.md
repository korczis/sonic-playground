## Genre and Mood

**Karel Vlach se svým orchestrem** plays **Annie, Get Your Gun** as a big band: the orchestra Vlach founded in 1939 grew up on swing, and Discogs files the album under jazz, stage and screen, and easy listening. No review of this track has been found, so romantic and joyful are this page's reading of the two songs it joins, a love duet and an ensemble number named for the morning sun, not a description of how the orchestra plays them.

## Song Summary

The track is a medley of two Irving Berlin songs from his 1946 Broadway musical Annie Get Your Gun, the fictionalised story of the sharpshooter Annie Oakley and Frank Butler. Discogs lists They Say It's Wonderful first and I Got the Sun in the Morning second, under the Czech titles Úžasná láska and Slunečné ráno on the domestic sleeve. It is the fifth of twelve tracks on an LP that Supraphon released in 1980, catalogue number 1113 2567, as Karel Vlach se svým orchestrem uvádí... at home and Musicals On Parade for export; the digital edition is called Ze světa muzikálů and lists the track at 2:46.

I Got the Sun in the Morning, Annie's second-act number with the ensemble, follows it. Discogs credits the arrangements of the whole LP to Jaroslav Dřevikovský and its production to Jiří Baur.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="medley" /> }} joins several songs into one performance. The whole album is built this way: each track is a musical, many of them heard through two or three of their songs, from Cabaret and My Fair Lady to Czech shows. Annie Get Your Gun gets two numbers, one from each act.

The {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }} is where a medley lives or dies, because it decides the order, the soloists and the joins. Here one arranger, Dřevikovský, set the whole album, and Discogs credits a soloist on several tracks, Tymich's tenor saxophone on this one. The album also plays two songs from the Czech film musical Kdyby tisíc klarinetů; hear another song from that film, in its original version, on {{ <track_link source="hana-hegerova-karel-gott-waldemar-matuska-tak-abyste-to-vedela.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Karel Vlach (1911–1986), born in Žižkov in Prague, founded his orchestra in 1939. Czech Wikipedia describes its swing, modelled early on Benny Goodman's band and after the war on Glenn Miller's, and its work in Prague theatres: Divadlo V+W from 1947, Divadlo ABC from 1954 and the Hudební divadlo v Karlíně from 1962. In 1963 he was named Zasloužilý umělec.
