## Genre and Mood

**Gin Wigmore**'s **Happy Ever After** is a break-up song that refuses to slow down. It sits where Gravel & Wine sits, between pop, blues and soul: the New Zealand Herald called it bluesy, and Music Feeds heard a wistful arrangement that is nevertheless uptempo. Heartbroken, bittersweet and soulful are this page's three words for that mix.

## Song Summary

Happy Ever After is the eighth of eleven tracks on Wigmore's second album, released in New Zealand on 7 November 2011, and it runs 3:22. She wrote it with the producer Butch Walker and Jake Sinclair. The theme is in the title: as the Herald's Lydia Jenkin summed it up after talking with Wigmore, the song is about an ex who turned out not to be the happy ever after she thought he was. Music Feeds quoted the line that sums it up, her "happy ever after wasn't you", and still called the song more than a ballad.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

Music Feeds describes the start as wistful yet uptempo, carried by throaty choir vocals and a beat that will not quit. No source times the parts or names a tempo, so this page does not either.

### Main Section

The lead is Wigmore's own; the backing voices, according to MusicBrainz, belong to Walker, Sinclair and the guitarist Fran Capitanelli. Sinclair plays bass, organ and drums, Walker guitar and percussion. Femme Metal Webzine heard a catchy chorus and a mood that is, for all its subject, quite positive.

### Outro

How the song ends is not described in any review; listen for whether the beat or the heartbreak has the last word.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="blues" /> }} here is less a twelve-bar form than a colour. Wigmore spent two months in Mississippi and Alabama before recording, and the Herald heard the album pick up the deep, swampy alchemy of the Mississippi; on this song it heard her as soulful and broken.

The {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} is part of the band. Butch Walker produced the album in Santa Monica in 2011 with his own backing band, the Black Widows, and on this track he co-wrote, played and sang.

The backing voices make a {{ <concept_link name="vocal harmony" /> }} of a particular kind. Wigmore said that nearly all the backing vocals on the album are a group of men, a chain-gang sound of men singing, raw and strong.

## Artistic Context

Wigmore was 25 when Gravel & Wine followed her debut Holy Smoke. She described the album's persona as a feisty woman who is angry and has had her heart broken, and the Herald heard her playing a southern woman scorned throughout. Compare {{ <track_link source="gin-wigmore-black-sheep.md" /> }}, the album's defiant opener.

## Impact and Reception

Gravel & Wine debuted at number one in New Zealand and went double platinum; released in the United States in April 2013, it reached number 87 on the Billboard 200. The Herald gave it 4.5 stars, and its reviewer wrote of this song that she believed every word. Music Feeds found a new element on every listen; try a second one.
