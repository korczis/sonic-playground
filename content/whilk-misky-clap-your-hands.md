## Genre and Mood

**Whilk & Misky** made their name with **Clap Your Hands**, a folk song with a bossa nova heartbeat and an indie sensibility; Indie Shuffle called it an "infectious bossa nova-esque acoustic track" and compared it to José González and Kings of Convenience. It is playful and carefree, the sound of a summer afternoon that refuses to end, and quietly uplifting in the way only a song built on a few chords and a crowd of hands can be.

## Song Summary

At its core this is a simple invitation: join in. An acoustic guitar picks out a gently syncopated, Brazilian-leaning rhythm, the vocals stay warm and unforced, and the handclaps of the title become the percussion section. The memorable detail is how little it needs; the song feels like it could be played on a doorstep and still fill a street.

## Musical Breakdown

The arrangement grows by accumulation rather than by big production moves.

### Intro

A lone guitar sets the sway, its bossa nova lilt immediately putting your shoulders at ease.

### Verse 1

The voice arrives soft and close, almost conversational, with the guitar rocking back and forth beneath it like a hammock.

### Build-up

Extra voices and percussion start to gather, a few claps at first, then a fuller pulse that tells you the refrain is coming.

### Chorus

The title line opens up into a communal singalong, claps landing on the beat and the melody turning from wistful to sunny.

### Bridge

The arrangement thins for a moment, leaving guitar and voice exposed before the hands return.

### Climax

The final refrains stack voices and claps into a warm, cheerful crowd, the closest the song gets to a party.

### Outro

The rhythm slowly winds down, the last strums hanging in the air like the end of a good evening.

## Concepts in Focus

The guitar rhythm is built on {{ <concept_link name="syncopation" /> }}, the habit of bossa nova, born in Rio de Janeiro in the late 1950s, of placing accents slightly ahead of or between the main beats. Instead of strumming evenly, the guitar leans and sways, which is why the song feels relaxed and moving at once. Clap along on the beat and listen to how the guitar keeps slipping around your claps. Compare {{ <track_link source="nouvelle-vague-too-drunk-to-fuck.md" /> }}, which uses the same bossa nova sway for a very different song.

The song also had a second life as a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }}. The Bosnian-German DJ and producer Solomun made a version that took it from acoustic folk into deep house, with a separate radio edit. A remix like that keeps the voice and the hook and replaces the handclaps and guitar with a club groove, so the same song can work in a field at noon and in a club at 3 a.m.

## Artistic Context

Whilk & Misky are a London duo formed in 2012: the singer and guitarist Charlie Dickens and the electronic producer and instrumentalist Nima Khaste. Wonderland summed up their contrast as "Nima likes cold industrial techno whilst Charlie likes angst infused soul", and placed their sound somewhere between Chet Faker and Flume. Clap Your Hands broke them in 2014 after a Radio 1 "Next Hype" play on Zane Lowe's show, and it led their debut EP The First Sip, released that year while several labels competed to sign them.

## Impact and Reception

The track travelled far beyond indie blogs, helped by the Solomun remix that carried it onto deep house dance floors. It is here because it proves a tiny, human-scale song can be just as infectious as any club anthem. Press play and, yes, clap along.
