## Genre and Mood

**Angus & Julia Stone** made **Big Jet Plane** sound like it was recorded on a lazy afternoon with the windows open. It is indie folk at its gentlest, acoustic and unhurried, with a mood that is dreamy, romantic and softly nostalgic: a love song about leaving everything behind, sung by someone in no hurry to go.

## Song Summary

The lyric is a fantasy of escape, of taking someone away on a big jet plane to somewhere far from here. Angus sings lead in his drowsy, laconic voice, Julia's higher harmony curls around it, and the arrangement grows from guitar into a quiet swell of drums and a fuller band. The memorable detail is the sibling blend, two voices that sound like they share one breath.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

A soft, repeating guitar figure opens the song, warm and slightly hazy, as if heard through a summer screen door.

### Verse 1

Angus enters almost mumbling, his phrasing loose and sleepy. The words paint a simple picture, and the guitar keeps rocking gently underneath.

### Chorus

The title line arrives with Julia's harmony, and the song lifts just enough to feel like take-off, lighter and brighter without losing its calm.

### Build-up

Drums and fuller instrumentation gradually join, the arrangement swelling in slow waves rather than one big rise.

### Climax

The last choruses carry the most weight, voices and band together, the daydream at its most vivid and its most tender.

### Outro

The band pulls back and the guitar is left alone again, the plane disappearing into the clouds. It ends the way a good daydream does, gently and a little too soon.

## Concepts in Focus

The song is a gentle lesson in {{ <concept_link name="vocal harmony" /> }}. Julia does not decorate Angus's melody from a distance; she sings close above it, and the siblings' similar timbre makes the two lines fuse into one wider voice. Notice how much warmer the chorus feels the moment her part arrives, even though nothing gets louder.

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is the title line itself, a simple, childlike image repeated until it sticks. It is a useful counterexample to the idea that hooks must be loud: this one is sung almost sleepily, and its pull comes from the melody and the repetition alone. That melody is sturdy enough that dance producers keep rebuilding the song, from {{ <track_link source="jan-blomqvist-big-jet-plane.md" /> }} to {{ <track_link source="recable-big-jet-plane-recable-remix.md" /> }}.

Big Jet Plane also exists as an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }} by its own writer. Angus Stone first recorded it under his solo alias Lady of the Sunshine for the 2009 album Smoking Gun, produced by Govinda Doyle; the siblings' 2010 re-recording, co-produced again by Doyle, is the one that became a standard. Comparing the two shows how much a duo's second voice changes a song that was already written.

## Artistic Context

Angus and Julia Stone are a brother-and-sister duo from Australia. Angus wrote "Big Jet Plane" and first released it as Lady of the Sunshine in March 2009; the siblings then made it the third single from *Down the Way*, their second album, issued on 7 May 2010 by EMI. They produced most of the album themselves. *Down the Way* debuted at number 1 on the ARIA albums chart and won Album of the Year at the 2010 ARIA Music Awards, where the Stones also won Producer of the Year.

## Impact and Reception

The song won Single of the Year at the 2010 ARIA Music Awards and topped triple j's Hottest 100 of 2010, the Australian listener poll, later placing ninth in the station's Hottest 100 of the 2010s. It peaked at 21 in Australia and 23 in France, but its life has been longer than its chart run: it is certified 11 times platinum in Australia and platinum in the US, and it has been covered and remixed many times, from Jan Blomqvist's 2011 electronic cover to the 2017 version by Alok and Mathieu Koss. Put on headphones, close your eyes and let it carry you somewhere far away.
