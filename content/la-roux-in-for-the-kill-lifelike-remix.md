## Genre and Mood

**La Roux** handed **In for the Kill** to the Paris producer Lifelike, and the remix moves the song from synth-pop into his own field of electro house and nu-disco. The original's longing stays in the voice; around it the mood turns hypnotic and energetic, a club track built for length rather than for a radio chorus.

## Song Summary

The song is Elly Jackson's 2009 confession of feeling for someone whatever comes back, written and produced with Ben Langmaid and a number two in the UK. Lifelike's version, Wikipedia notes, runs over six minutes against the original's four, and Kitsuné released it in France as a twelve-inch single and a digital download. The label's own description is vivid: a breathtaking, heady track whose repetitive and relentless glass-breaking beat takes you further and further, like driving on a motorway at night.

## Musical Breakdown

No published analysis maps the remix part by part; this follows what the label and the reviewers heard.

### Main Section

The beat is the engine. Kitsuné heard it as repetitive and relentless, and the length lets it run: the remix gives Jackson's high, cold voice a long stretch of road instead of a four-minute pop frame.

### Hook

The song's hook survives the change of setting. Even BlackPlastic, which disliked the mix, granted that it is a catchy tune.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} can aim a song at a different audience, and In for the Kill had two famous ones. Skream, a dubstep musician in Wikipedia's words, made the best-known one, Let's Get Ravey, as the page for {{ <track_link source="la-roux-in-for-the-kill.md" /> }} tells; Lifelike's took the song towards the French dance scene he came from. Hear them side by side and the song's melody turns out to bend either way.

The remix also shows how a {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }} works for a label. Kitsuné put it on Kitsuné Maison Compilation 7 in June 2009, the seventh volume of the label's own series. DIY called La Roux another worthy addition; BlackPlastic called her return on this mix the worst moment of the set, while still calling Lifelike ace.

From there it travelled into the {{ <concept_link name="dj mix" /> }}: MusicBrainz lists it, cut down, inside Ministry of Sound's In the Club! and Mashed 5 mixes in the summer of 2009.

## Artistic Context

Lifelike is Laurent Ash, a Paris producer of electro house and nu-disco whose career, French Wikipedia says, began in 2002. Thomas Bangalter and DJ Falcon played his single Black Chess for months on their Together tour that year. His Discopolis with Kris Menace came out on Alan Braxe's label Vulture, and his remixes include Chromeo, The Presets and Moby. La Roux had a Kitsuné history too: the label had released their earlier single Quicksand.

## Impact and Reception

Skream's remix is the one that went on to television and film trailers; Lifelike's travelled through the label's compilation and DJ mixes. Put it on loud at night and let the beat drive.
