## Genre and Mood

**John Newman**'s **Love Me Again** is a soul song built for the dance floor. Wikipedia files it under northern soul, dance-pop and breakbeat; the critics of The Singles Jukebox heard horns and house piano in the same bars. The mood is longing pushed into energy: a man asking for a second chance, singing it loud enough to fill a club.

## Song Summary

The song is a plea for a second chance, and Newman sings it with the raw edge of a soul singer rather than the polish of a pop vocalist. Underneath, four chords loop: Gm, B-flat, Dm and C, in G Dorian. Around them the arrangement builds what one critic called a house-injected floorfiller: a choppy guitar, house piano, horns, cascading strings and handclaps. What stays is the contrast: a heartbroken voice over a beat that refuses to slow down.

## Musical Breakdown

No source maps the song bar by bar; these notes follow what the critics heard.

### Verse

Newman's voice sits over what Brad Shoup called a Morse-code guitar: short, clipped notes instead of strummed chords.

### Chorus

The chorus opens up, massive in the critics' words, with handclaps and piano driving it and strings cascading around the voice.

### Breakdown

Scott Mildenhall singled out the gloomy, wobbling breakdown, where the arrangement drops back before the song gathers itself again.

### Outro

The song ends on its plea, the same four chords still turning under the voice.

## Artistic Context

John Newman was born in 1990 in Settle, North Yorkshire; his mother introduced him to Motown and Stax, and northern soul shaped his sound and his dancing. He first reached the charts as the voice of Rudimental's Feel the Love, a UK number one in 2012. Love Me Again, written with Steve Booker and produced by Booker and Mike Spencer, was his debut single, released in June 2013 as the lead single of Tribute, which Island issued on 14 October 2013.

## Impact and Reception

Love Me Again went to number one in the UK and number 30 on the US Billboard Hot 100, went triple platinum in the UK and was nominated for British Single of the Year at the 2014 BRIT Awards; PopMatters ranked it the second-best song of 2013. It reached FIFA 14 and the closing credits of Edge of Tomorrow. Critics split on the production, never on the voice. Play it loud and listen for the moment the strings join the claps.
