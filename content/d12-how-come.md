## Genre and Mood

**D12**'s **"How Come"** is hip-hop turned inward. Most of D12 World is loud and irreverent; this one is rap about the group itself, bittersweet and introspective, which the Chicago Maroon heard as gloomy and Hot Press as "a three-way 'Et tu' meditation on broken honour codes sweetened with a nagging melody".

## Song Summary

"How Come" was the second single from D12 World, sent to American radio on 8 June 2004. Witt & Pep, the duo of Bryan Johnson and Dewitt Moore, produced it, and they share the writing credit with Marshall Mathers (Eminem), Denaun Porter (Kon Artis) and DeShaun Holton (Proof). The song is about the relationships between the members: Eminem refers to his relationship with Proof, Kon Artis talks about Eminem and Kim's relationship, and Proof talks about the rift between him and Eminem. The album version runs 4:09.

## Musical Breakdown

No source we found describes the beat, the opening or the ending, or gives times; this guide keeps to the voices and what the critics heard.

### Chorus

The chorus is Eminem's. Joe Hanson of the Chicago Maroon called it catchy, and it is the "nagging melody" that, for Hot Press, sweetens a bitter subject. It returns between the verses and gives three grievances one question.

### Verses

Three members take a verse each. Kon Artis has the second, about Eminem and Kim; Eminem and Proof each address what has happened between the two of them. Bizarre and Swifty McVay also recorded verses, heard only on the extended version of the UK and European CD singles.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} matters more than usual here. The verses pull in three directions; the chorus, the same melody after each, makes them one song.

"How Come" also exists as an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }}. The extended mix on the CD singles runs 4:41 and keeps the verses of Bizarre and Swifty McVay, which were cut from the album. Compare the two: with five voices it becomes a group discussion; with three it is closer to a private argument.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} stages that argument. It opens with a home video of Eminem rapping at an underground show with Proof and Bizarre, then shows the members fighting with Eminem in the Shady Records studio: he rose to stardom, they cannot get a deal. It ends on a clip of {{ <track_link source="d12-git-up.md" /> }}, and the whole film is known as How Come Plus Git Up.

## Artistic Context

D12 formed in Detroit in 1996 around Proof, and Eminem joined after Bugz was killed in 1999. D12 World, released by Shady and Interscope in April 2004, was the group's second album and its last with Proof, who was killed in 2006.

## Impact and Reception

The single reached number 4 in the UK, Ireland and Australia, number 6 in New Zealand and number 27 on the Billboard Hot 100, and was certified gold in the United States. The Chicago Maroon counted it among the album's two standout tracks, calling it "a bitter look at relationships between two people that grow apart". Embrace covered it for BBC Radio 1's Live Lounge, keeping only Eminem's lyrics. Listen for how a catchy chorus carries an uncomfortable conversation.
