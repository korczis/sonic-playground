## Genre and Mood

**Marian** open their album with **For You**, and it sits right on the seam between deep house and electronic pop. It has the reduced, patient sound of a club producer, but it is built as a song, with a singer, a melody and something to say. The mood is romantic and bittersweet, the longing of a love song carried by a groove that never quite lets itself go.

## Song Summary

For You is, in the duo's own words, a love song. The notes on their album page describe how it is made: a sub bass drones up from the depths, cello sounds caress it, and it finally dissolves into a bittersweet mid-tempo house dance. The track starts almost underground, gains warmth from an acoustic instrument, and only then becomes something you could move to. Fabian Reichelt's intimate vocal keeps it personal throughout.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

The sub bass arrives first, low and slow, a pressure in the room more than a melody.

### Verse 1

Reichelt's voice enters close to the microphone, and the cello sounds wrap around the electronic frame, softening its edges.

### Main Section

The beat settles into a moderate house tempo. Nothing is rushed: the groove sways rather than drives.

### Outro

The song does not explode; it dissolves, as the album notes put it, into the dance it has been approaching all along.

## Concepts in Focus

For You begins with its {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }}, and that is unusual. Most songs introduce the bass after the beat or the chords; here a sub bass, low enough to be felt as much as heard, is the first thing you meet.

The second idea is the {{ <concept_link name="house groove" /> }} used at song pace. Marek Hemmann's club records, like {{ <track_link source="marek-hemmann-gemini.md" /> }}, ride their groove for nine minutes. For You keeps the steady house pulse but holds it to a moderate tempo and about four minutes, so the groove serves the singer rather than the dance floor.

Finally, a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }}: in 2013 Freude am Tanzen released a remix edition of the album, on which the German trio Marbert Rocel reworked For You into a version of about seven and a half minutes. Comparing the two shows what a remixer keeps and what they rebuild.

## Artistic Context

Marian is the duo of Marek Hemmann, born in Gera in 1979 and based in Jena since 2005, and the singer Fabian Reichelt. Hemmann had spent years refining a housy, sometimes melancholic style, solo and with Mathias Kaden as Hemmann & Kaden; in 2010 he and Reichelt made the Left/Right EP, which brought pop-friendly elements into that sound. Only Our Hearts to Lose followed on 13 June 2011 on Freude am Tanzen, the Jena label that grew out of a local house party series in the mid-1990s. Resident Advisor noted the album's cellos, jazz organs, intimate vocals and electronic beats.

## Impact and Reception

Only Our Hearts to Lose was meant to blur the line between electronic production and classic songwriting, and For You, its first track, is the clearest statement of that aim. The album earned a remix edition two years later, with names from the label's circle such as Mathias Kaden and Douglas Greed, while Hemmann went on to his solo album Bittersweet in 2013. Start it in a quiet room and let the bass rise first.
