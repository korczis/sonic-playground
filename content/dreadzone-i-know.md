## Genre and Mood

**Dreadzone** placed **I Know** eighth on Once Upon a Time, their fifth album: Discogs files the record under breakbeat, dub and breaks, Wikipedia calls the band dub, reggae and electronic. The feelings named here, groovy and gritty, come from what the sources say about the album and its MC; no review we found describes this track on its own.

## Song Summary

I Know is one of the album's three songs for Spee, the MC who joined Dreadzone as a second vocalist in 2000. The French release credits him as the only voice and, under his own name, Spencer Graham, as a writer, alongside the band's Ben Balafonic and Greg Roberts. It runs a little over five minutes. Greg Roberts told Rebel Base before the release that the album was more vocal than its predecessors, with more harmonies and lyrics, only about three instrumentals and less reliance on samples; I Know is part of that shift.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes the beat, the structure or the lyric of I Know, so this breakdown stays with who is on the record and what was said about the album.

### The MC

Spee carries the track. Rebel Base's reviewer heard the album's sound as rougher than before, shaped by the band's years as DJs on the English club circuit, and found it suited the voice of the South London MC.

### The Production

Behind him, Greg Roberts and Ben Balafonic are credited with production, arrangement and electronics, and Balafonic with the mix. Steve Roberts plays guitar, bass and keyboards on four other tracks; this is not one of them.

## Concepts in Focus

The first idea is {{ <concept_link name="rapping" /> }}, or more precisely the MC tradition it belongs to: words delivered rhythmically over a beat, nearer to speech than to song. Spee told Rebel Base he took his influences from reggae singers and old-school hip-hop and made them his own, and on stage he sees his job as entertaining the crowd.

Under him lies {{ <concept_link name="dub" /> }}, the Jamaican craft of remaking a record at the mixing desk, which Dreadzone have built on since 1993; their sound system hosted the Dubwiser club night at the Notting Hill Arts Club from 1998 to 2001.

And there is {{ <concept_link name="breakbeat" /> }}, drums built from broken, syncopated patterns, the style Discogs gives the whole album. Hear Spee over the same ground in {{ <track_link source="dreadzone-here-we-go.md" /> }}, the next track.

## Artistic Context

Dreadzone formed in London in 1993 around the former Big Audio Dynamite drummer Greg Roberts. Ben Balafonic joined in 2001, and Once Upon a Time was recorded by Greg Roberts, Balafonic, Spee, Earl Sixteen and Steve Roberts and released in 2005 on the independent Functional label. Balafonic left in 2006, and Steve Roberts died that October. The track's YouTube upload comes from a later edition on the band's own Dubwiser label.

## Impact and Reception

I Know was not among the album's singles, and no review singles it out. Rebel Base, reviewing the album, recommended it to every reggae fan. Play it next to {{ <track_link source="dreadzone-elevate.md" /> }} and Here We Go, the album's other Spee songs, and hear how one MC holds each of them.
