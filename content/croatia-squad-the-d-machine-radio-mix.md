## Genre and Mood

**Croatia Squad** made **The D Machine** for the peak of a club night, and this **Radio Mix** packs it into three and a half minutes. The notes of its publisher, Sirup Music, call it "straight up nu-disco music"; the producer's own roots are in house, disco and funk. The mood is gritty, groovy and energetic: Dancing Astronaut heard "a more aggressive original" that "ushers a gritty tone to the forefront".

## Song Summary

This is a club track first. No source describes verses or a chorus; the publisher lists the ingredients as "pounding beat sections and knocking bass lines as well as some vocoded and spoken vocal chops", arranged around "teasing breakdowns and addictive drops". Orange County EDM heard "deep basslines and steady hi-hats" and expected listeners to be "dancing along to the beat". The radio mix keeps that machinery and trims the time it takes: the original mix runs close to five minutes, this one 3:36.

## Musical Breakdown

### Main Section

No source maps the radio mix bar by bar, and this page does not invent a timeline. What the sources agree on is the engine: a steady, pounding beat, hi-hats ticking above it and a deep, knocking bass line that carries the groove.

### Breakdown

The breakdowns hold the beat back and leave the vocal chops, vocoded and spoken, in the foreground. The publisher calls them teasing, which is the job: to make the dance floor wait.

### Drop

Each drop brings the full beat and bass back. How many of the original's drops the radio mix keeps, no source says.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="radio edit" /> }}, here called a radio mix, is the version of a club track cut for radio and streaming: a quicker start, fewer repeats. Compare it with the original mix, which gives DJs more bars to blend in and out.

The {{ <concept_link name="vocoder" /> }} turns a voice into something closer to a synthesizer. Here, with the spoken chops, it makes the vocal part of the rhythm section rather than a melody.

The {{ <concept_link name="drop" /> }} is the moment the full groove returns after a breakdown; the publisher calls the drops of this track addictive, which makes them its hook.

## Artistic Context

Croatia Squad is a Swiss DJ and producer who started the project during five summers as a resident DJ in Croatia, and who names disco, funk and garage as roots. The debut, the Electric Masquerade EP, came out on Enormous Tunes in 2009, followed by records for Toolroom, Ministry of Sound and Sirup Music. The D Machine, released on 25 January 2016, was the first on No Definition; the YouTube credits name Christian Beat Hirt as composer and lyricist, as Christian Hirt is credited on {{ <track_link source="platinum-doug-move-on-original-club-mix.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

No chart record or long review documents the track's reception. Its afterlife is in the catalogue: a remix package by Illusionize & Visage Music, Clouded Judgement and Vision Factory followed in August 2016, and the vocal appeared as an a cappella tool for DJs. Play it loud and wait for the drop.
