## Genre and Mood

**Mount Sims** brought cool detachment to electroclash, and **Together Alone** distils it. It is electro and synth-pop seen through a cold, nocturnal lens, hypnotic and provocative, with a melancholic streak running under the gloss.

## Song Summary

The title says it all: the song is about closeness that does not quite connect, bodies in the same room and minds somewhere else. Mount Sims sets it over a sparse, metronomic drum machine and chilly analogue synths, with a vocal delivered in a flat, half-whispered croon that makes the intimacy feel clinical. The memorable quality is that emotional distance, which turns a song about loneliness into something oddly seductive. It is a song that sounds like neon reflected in a puddle: glamorous on the surface, and lonely the moment you look closer. Very few electroclash tracks committed this fully to sadness without losing the beat.

## Musical Breakdown

The track is minimal and repetitive by design, its power in the loop.

### Intro

A cold drum-machine pattern and a buzzing synth line set the tone, clipped and mechanical.

### Verse 1

The vocal slides in, detached and deliberate, sketching the scene of two people sharing space without sharing much else.

### Chorus

The title phrase returns as a hook, cool and repetitive, more mantra than release.

### Main Section

Synth layers shift and pulse around the beat, keeping the hypnotic loop alive without breaking its spell.

### Outro

The track fades on its machine pulse, as if the lights in the club have gone up and nobody has moved.

## Artistic Context

Mount Sims is the project of Matthew Sims, who was signed to DJ Hell's International DeeJay Gigolo label after Hell reportedly saw one of his early performances. "Together Alone" appears on UltraSex, released in 2002, a concept album about technology and sexuality, and it is part of the track list of the U.S. edition on Emperor Norton Records. The album placed him in the thick of the electroclash moment, alongside Gigolo labelmates and the wider Berlin and New York scenes.

## Impact and Reception

UltraSex, and singles like "How We Do", made Mount Sims a fixture of early-2000s electroclash, a scene that looked back to 1980s synth-pop and forward to the clubs of the new century. "Together Alone" captures its emotional core better than most: stylish, cold and a little sad. Two decades on, its chilly minimalism sounds less like a period piece and more like a blueprint for much of the dark synth-pop that followed. Play it late, alone or together.
