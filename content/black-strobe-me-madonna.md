## Genre and Mood

**Black Strobe**'s **Me & Madonna** is electroclash at its source: electro and electronic dance music with the shadows of 1980s British pop in it. It is dark, and it is ironic. Resident Advisor wrote that anyone who thought the band really believed Madonna was in love with them had missed the point.

## Song Summary

Black Strobe were two Parisians, the producer Arnaud Rebotini and the DJ Ivan Smagghe, and Me & Madonna was their breakthrough single, released in 2002 on Output Recordings, the label of Trevor Jackson. They recorded it with the Parisian DJ Jennifer Cardini, who sings it with Rebotini. The version on this page comes from Kitsuné's reissue, Kitsuné: Me & Madonna, released on 6 December 2010 with Rebotini credited as composer.

### Main Section

Reviewing the single in January 2003, gullbuy heard a bass accent that recalled New Order and two voices, Cardini's and Rebotini's. A later gullbuy review added that the original leaned towards the guitar-based side of New Order, while the remix by Two Fairlight Bitches gave it an electro 1980s vibe. Its verdict: the immediate gratification of Me and Madonna is hard to beat.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }} was the early-2000s scene that brought 1980s electro, synth-pop and cold wave back to the clubs with a knowing wink. According to Wikipedia, this single made Black Strobe instrumental in the movement's rise in the UK.

The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} is where the New Order comparison lives: gullbuy heard a New Order accent in the bass. Listen for whether it carries a melody of its own or only the pulse under the voices.

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} reshapes a track for another floor. Exclaim! wrote that Me and Madonna was remixed to death; The Twelves made one that Kitsuné released alongside the original.

## Artistic Context

Resident Advisor described the band's early singles as a postmodern take on British electronic cold wave with Warp-style bleeps, industrial dynamics and a heavy dose of irony. Smagghe left in 2006; Rebotini carried on, later scoring films such as {{ <track_link source="arnaud-rebotini-curiosa.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Exclaim! called it an international club hit that cast the band as a sleeker, darker {{ <track_link source="fischerspooner-emerge.md" text="Fischerspooner" /> }} and pushed Smagghe to the front of the electroclash DJ circuit. Drowned in Sound called it a groundbreaking dance anthem. Play it loud and decide how serious it is.
