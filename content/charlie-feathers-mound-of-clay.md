## Genre and Mood

**Charlie Feathers** sings **Mound of Clay** as a rockabilly man who started in country: Discogs files the album under rockabilly and rock and roll, with folk and country as its other genre. Raw and gritty are the feelings this page files it under, but critics disagree on its temperature. Drowned in Sound called it boisterous; the New Zealand Herald heard deadbeat country cool. Both can be true of two and a half minutes.

## Song Summary

The song is not Feathers's own. The album credits it to Roy Acuff, whose Beneath That Lonely Mound of Clay Hank Williams also recorded. Feathers kept coming back to it: MusicBrainz lists several recordings under his name, among them one of over four and a half minutes on his 1974 album Good Rockin' Tonight, and the short one heard here, also issued in 1999 on the collection This Little Show.

## Musical Breakdown

### Main Section

No source describes this recording passage by passage, and Norton's liner notes, as one reviewer complained, do not give recording details for every track, so this page cannot say when it was made, who plays on it or how it unfolds. What the sources do give is the voice. Wikipedia describes Feathers as a master of shifting emotional and sonic dynamics, with a theatrical, hiccuping style; the Austin Chronicle called his voice uniquely jolted. Listen for that voice against the plain shape of an old country song.

## Concepts in Focus

Mound of Clay is a {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }}, and a good one to learn from. A cover keeps the words and the melody of another writer and changes everything else: phrasing, tempo, attitude. Steve Terrell, reviewing the series, wrote that Feathers puts his own crazy stamp on the songs he covers, and that some are barely recognisable.

The recording reached most listeners through a {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }}. Norton's three volumes, Wild Side of Life, Honky Tonk Kind and Long Time Ago, marked the tenth anniversary of Feathers's death by gathering one-off singles, LP cuts, home demos and live recordings. Three years later MGMT chose Mound of Clay for their Late Night Tales mix, beside {{ <track_link source="cheval-sombre-troubled-mind.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Feathers, born in Holly Springs, Mississippi, in 1932, started as a session musician at Sun, and recorded singles for Flip, Sun, Meteor and King; the Austin Chronicle notes that Sam Phillips never warmed to him. He died in 1998. The same Norton volume ends with his version of Folsom Prison Blues; compare Cash making another writer's song his own in {{ <track_link source="johnny-cash-hurt.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Over time, as Wikipedia puts it, Feathers's standing was greatly elevated: the Rockabilly Hall of Fame recognised him, Quentin Tarantino used two of his songs in Kill Bill, and singers such as Lux Interior of the Cramps learned from his voice. Listen to Mound of Clay twice and choose: boisterous or deadbeat cool.
