## Genre and Mood

**Basement Jaxx** put **Hot 'n Cold** near the end of Kish Kash, and it is one of the album's oddest dance tracks: electronic, built for the floor, with a sultry vocal and a restless, playful surface. Critics could not agree on what it resembled. Ink19 heard a fragmented Latin pop schmooze, Drowned in Sound a badly mangled Prince number, Treble something between a club grind and an industrial freak-out.

## Song Summary

The song is about the push and pull its title names, and the production acts it out. Treble's Andrew Van Baal, who gave it a whole column in 2004, described a strictly metered beat machine and a "supersexy" vocal holding down sounds that would otherwise fly apart: metallic tings, warbled voices and syncopated bleeps that make the track itself feel hot one moment and cold the next. Felix Buxton and Simon Ratcliffe wrote, produced, programmed and mixed it; the vocals are credited to Emily Oldfield and to Buxton himself.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

According to Treble, a gurgle comes before the vocal, an odd little sound that warns you this will not be a plain house track.

### Main Section

The beat is the anchor, metered and unbending, while metallic tings punctuate its forward motion. Over it sits a polished, chrome-like vocal, and around it a rush of warbled voices on breathy syllables.

### Climax

Van Baal heard the peak as the acceleration of an old factory grinding machine, the point where the sexy and the mechanical meet. No source describes how the track ends, so listen for yourself whether it cools down or simply stops.

## Concepts in Focus

The steadiness Treble praised is the work of a {{ <concept_link name="drum machine" /> }}, or of programming that behaves like one. A programmed beat does not drift or tire, and that is what lets Basement Jaxx pile odd sounds on top without losing the dancer. The grid holds, so the decoration can be wild.

Those decorations work through {{ <concept_link name="syncopation" /> }}. The boops and baps Van Baal describes as syncopated land between the beats rather than on them, so they pull against the metered pulse. That tension is where the hot and the cold come from.

Hot 'n Cold also had a second life as a {{ <concept_link name="soundtrack" /> }} cue: it was licensed to the soundtrack of the basketball video game NBA 2K15. For the album's punk-leaning side, hear {{ <track_link source="basement-jaxx-siouxsie-sioux-cish-cash.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Kish Kash, the third Basement Jaxx album, came out on XL Recordings on 20 October 2003. Hot 'n Cold is its twelfth track of fourteen. Wikipedia's account of the album notes a move away from groove- and sample-driven production toward songwriting, and Pitchfork heard fused fragments of disco alongside electro, acid, Bollywood and new wave. The record opens with {{ <track_link source="basement-jaxx-good-luck.md" /> }}, a very different way in.

## Impact and Reception

Kish Kash reached number 17 in the UK, and it won the first Grammy for Best Dance/Electronic Album, in 2005. Hot 'n Cold was never a single and drew split notices. Ink19 called it great; Drowned in Sound, in a mixed review, heard Buxton's vocal turn as a mangled Prince song; Treble said it could think of no other song that sounds quite like it. Put it on loud and decide which of them heard it right.
