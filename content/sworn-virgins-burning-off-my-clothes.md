> **Sworn Virgins**' "Burning Off My Clothes" is a driving, sweaty late-night disco track, the B-side of their 2018 12-inch Fifty Dollar Bills on DEEWEE, the label of Soulwax's David and Stephen Dewaele. A review quoted by retailers called it a "chugging, Harvey-esque freak-out for saturated nightcrawlers".

"Burning Off My Clothes" is driven by its groove and bassline, a record for the hour when the dance floor is hot, crowded and past caring. The title's image of shedding clothes fits the sound: heat, release and abandon.

## Genre and Mood

**Sworn Virgins** make disco filtered through acid house and 1970s groove. The mood is **defiant**, **raw** and **cathartic** in the way late-night club music is: not angry, but uninhibited, a release of energy rather than a statement.

## About the Artist

**Sworn Virgins** are an anonymous act from London, described by FACT as a "mysterious disco outfit". Their first 12-inch, Lazy Boy, came out in 2015 on Les Disques De La Mort, presented by Mutado Pintado, an alias of the London acid house act Paranoid London, which has fuelled speculation about who is behind them. Fifty Dollar Bills, released on 20 July 2018 as DEEWEE 028, was their debut on DEEWEE and their second release; later records on the label include Lazer Beam and Strangers Hands. Nialler9 described their sound as a blend of acid house, 1970s groove and "other-worldly esotericism".

## Song Summary

The track is a long-form groove rather than a verse-and-chorus song. FACT described the 12-inch as full of "sexed-up vocal performances and irresistibly greasy bass lines", and the retailer copy set the two sides apart: the title track a cosmic disco-boogie cut, this B-side the more driving and intense one, for late-night dancers.

## Sound Breakdown

### Intro: Grit and Intensity

The song kicks off with its groove, a steady, chugging beat that pulls the listener into the record's heat.

### First Verse: Defiant Vocals

The vocal enters, sultry and half-sung, riding the rhythm rather than leading it.

### Chorus: A Cathartic Anthem

The title phrase returns as the track's anchor, the moment where groove and voice lock together.

### Bridge: A Moment of Reflection

The arrangement thins for a stretch, leaving the bass and rhythm exposed before the full groove returns.

### Final Chorus: The Fire Rages On

The track gathers into its freak-out, the loosest and most intense passage, before it winds down.

## Concepts in Focus

The reference to Harvey points to a particular {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }}. The DJ Harvey is known for slow, heavy, psychedelic disco sets, and "chugging" describes music that rolls forward at a lower tempo than peak-time house, with weight rather than speed. A groove like that is built from small, repeated patterns in bass and drums; the pleasure is in how long it can be sustained without tiring.

The heart of that groove is the {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }}. FACT singled out the record's "irresistibly greasy bass lines", and in disco the bass is often the lead instrument, melodic, syncopated and loud in the mix. Listen to the bass on its own for a minute: it is doing more than the vocal to tell you how to move.

The record also shows {{ <concept_link name="club culture" /> }} at work. An anonymous act, a vinyl 12-inch with a B-side for DJs, and a label run by Soulwax, themselves DJs and remixers known as 2ManyDJs: this is music that travels through record shops, DJ bags and dance floors rather than radio. For the label owners' own touch, hear {{ <track_link source="metronomy-love-letters-soulwax-remix.md" /> }}.

## Overall Vibe

"Burning Off My Clothes" is **defiant**, **raw** and **cathartic** in the language of the dance floor: a long, hot groove that invites abandon.

## Conclusion

With "Burning Off My Clothes," **Sworn Virgins** deliver a B-side that many DJs would play before the A-side: heavy, chugging and late. It is a fine example of the modern disco underground, and of how much a label like DEEWEE can do for an act nobody can name.
