## Genre and Mood

**Dreadzone** put **The Warriors** on Once Upon a Time, their fifth album, which Discogs files under breakbeat, dub and breaks; the track's earlier 12-inch is filed under breaks and tech house. The feelings named here, groovy and energetic, come from those dancefloor styles rather than from a review: no source we found describes how The Warriors itself sounds.

## Song Summary

The Warriors is the album's tenth track and, at 6:20, one of its longer ones. The French release on Discogs calls it a mostly instrumental track and credits its writing to the band's Greg Roberts and Ben Balafonic alone; no singer, MC or guest player is credited on it. Its history starts before the album: in 2002 the Double Seven label issued a 12-inch called The Warriors, credited to Dreadzone vs. Mafia Tone, written and produced by the same two men and recorded at Dreadzone Central in London.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the track's structure, so this breakdown stays with what the credits and the catalogues establish.

### Main Section

The weight falls on the rhythm and the production. Roberts and Balafonic are credited on the album with production and arrangement, and the styles both releases are filed under point to broken, syncopated drums. With almost no words, the groove has to carry the track's six minutes on its own.

## Concepts in Focus

The first idea is the {{ <concept_link name="breakbeat" /> }}: a drum pattern built from broken, syncopated loops instead of a steady kick on every beat. Dreadzone came to it from dub and sound-system culture, and the British CD of the album carries the label name Functional Breaks.

The 2002 single shows the {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }} at work. Its two sides held a Breaks Mix and a House Mix of the same track by the same artists, one for each kind of dancefloor. Discogs does not say which of them, if either, became the album version.

The {{ <concept_link name="dj mix" /> }} carried the title further. MusicBrainz lists The Warriors, credited to Mafia Tone and Dreadzone, on Tayo's Next Level Breaks mix in the Y4K series in March 2003, and a track called The Warrior on Ministry of Sound's Breaks 03 that September, both two years before the album came out.

## Artistic Context

Dreadzone formed in London in 1993 around the former Big Audio Dynamite drummer Greg Roberts. Ben Balafonic joined in 2001, and the band then recorded Once Upon a Time, released in 2005 on the independent Functional label. The album's artwork printed the titles of The Warriors and {{ <track_link source="dreadzone-here-we-go.md" /> }} the wrong way round; the French release corrects them, noting that Here We Go is the one you can recognise by its lyrics.

## Impact and Reception

The Warriors is not among the album's singles, and no review singles it out. Rebel Base, which recommended the album to every reggae fan, named only {{ <track_link source="dreadzone-ska-con-queso.md" /> }} and First Steps as the instrumentals that keep the ambient flavour of the band's early records. Play The Warriors after those two and listen for what a Dreadzone track keeps when the voices step back.
