## Genre and Mood

**Moderat** open their first album with **A New Error**, a track between techno, minimal and the wider electronic field that the trio's music is filed under. The mood is melancholic and hypnotic, but not still: PopMatters heard its momentum as more of a march than a swagger.

## Song Summary

A New Error is built from a few elements. The machine beat is, in PopMatters' words, very simple and drum-machine-derived; the synths carry a slightly detuned wobble that the reviewer heard as the error of the title; and an anthemic buzz bass recalls the Human League's The Dignity of Labour. Drowned in Sound called it the bubbling melodic analogue chug of the opener, the exception on an almost uniformly bleak record. AllMusic heard it range from stern EBM sounds to filter disco and schaffel, all within this one track.

## Concepts in Focus

The album was made, PopMatters reported, with a bunch of 1970s {{ <concept_link name="analog synthesizer" text="analogue synthesizers" /> }}, yet still sounds like a computer record from 2009. On A New Error the reviewer heard the slight detuning of the synths as the error of the title: the drift that analog circuits are known for, turned into the song's character.

The {{ <concept_link name="drum machine" /> }} is used with deliberate modesty. According to PopMatters, the beat on this track draws attention away from the rhythm for the only time on the album. With the drums kept simple, the ear goes to the bass and the synths.

A New Error later reached a new audience as {{ <concept_link name="soundtrack" /> }}: Xavier Dolan used it in his 2012 film Laurence Anyways. Compare the patient, nocturnal layering of {{ <track_link source="lulu-rouge-lulu-theme.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Moderat joined the Berlin duo Modeselektor, Gernot Bronsert and Sebastian Szary, with Sascha Ring, who records as Apparat. Their first sessions produced the EP Auf Kosten der Gesundheit in 2003; years later they returned to a Berlin studio together and made this self-titled debut, released on BPitch Control on 11 May 2009. Albums II and III followed on Monkeytown.

## Impact and Reception

Moderat was well received, with 7.6 out of 10 from Pitchfork and 7 from PopMatters, and in 2009 Resident Advisor's readers voted Moderat the number one live act of the year. A New Error has stayed in their sets, with live recordings released since. Play it at night, and let the beat march you somewhere.
