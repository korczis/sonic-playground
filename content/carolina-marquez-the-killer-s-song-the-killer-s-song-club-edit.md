## Genre and Mood

**Carolina Márquez** built **The Killer's Song** from a film score and pointed it at the dancefloor. It is dance music with a house pulse, and its moods pull in different directions: playful in its la-la refrain, sultry in its lyric, and eerie in the melody underneath, which began life as a thriller's theme. This page reviews one of its several versions, named in the label's digital release The Killer's Song - The Killer's Song Club Edit.

## Song Summary

The song rests on Bernard Herrmann's music for the thriller Twisted Nerve, a score known for an eerie whistling tune that Quentin Tarantino used in Kill Bill: Volume 1 in 2003. Wikipedia credits The Killer's Song with turning that music into an international hit in 2004, and Herrmann shares the writing credit with Márquez and Raffaello Marchesini. The lyric, printed with the official video, is a love song to a DJ: the king of the night, a deejay in her dreams, a lover who plays or stops the music. Its refrain is mostly wordless, so the borrowed melody does the hooking.

## Concepts in Focus

The song is a lesson in the afterlife of a {{ <concept_link name="soundtrack" /> }}. Herrmann wrote the tune for one film; Tarantino placed it in another, and within a year a dance single had carried it into clubs. Kill Bill revived Nancy Sinatra the same way: compare {{ <track_link source="nancy-sinatra-bang-bang.md" /> }}.

The lyric is about {{ <concept_link name="club culture" /> }} itself, its night, its DJ and its play and stop. Wikipedia notes that the remixes of the follow-up, Vol. II, were heavily played in Ibiza.

The Club Edit is an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }}. Canada's Ultra issue of 2004 lists a radio version, an extended mix, a club mix, this club edit and three remixes; in 2007 the edit closed Márquez's album Angel de fuego (10th Anniversary).

## Artistic Context

Carolina Márquez was born in Bogotá in 1975, left Colombia for Spain in 1986, studied drama in Barcelona and moved to Venice in 1991; she is a singer, songwriter, dancer and producer based in Rome. She records for the Italian label Do It Yourself, which released The Killer's Song; EMI marketed it in Latin America and Ultra in the United States.

## Impact and Reception

The song became her international breakthrough, and, by Wikipedia's dating, in 2005 The Killer's Song Vol. II followed with remixes by Euro DJs including the German duo The Disco Boys.
