## Genre and Mood

**Tweak** is the Swiss producer Cyril Boehler, and **Fathorn** is the first track of his 2001 album Hybrid Organics. The genres here follow the record: nu-jazz, the scene of compilations such as Bazaar Nu-Jazz that carried the track, and downtempo and broken beat, which Discogs gives for the album. Groovy and adventurous are this page's reading; Boehler's own summary of the record is wild jazzy beats and alpine horns.

## Song Summary

Fathorn runs 5:53 and is credited to Boehler as writer, with one guest: Robert Morgenthaler on alphorn. The album credits name Boehler as its producer, mixer and programmer and as the player of keyboards and sampler. Straight Ahead, a Zürich label, released Hybrid Organics on CD and LP in 2001. In December 2017 Boehler reissued it digitally as Hybrid Organics (Expanded), with remixes by Raw Deal, Sequel and Beanfield and bonus tracks; that is the edition this page is about.

## Musical Breakdown

No review describes Fathorn part by part, so this page draws no map of it and claims no tempo. The sources give its two ingredients: programmed beats of the kind Discogs files as broken beat and drum n bass, and an alphorn played by a jazz musician. Listen for how the horn enters, whether it carries a melody or long held tones, and how the beat makes room for an instrument built for valleys rather than clubs.

## Concepts in Focus

An {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }} of Fathorn came out the same year: Straight Ahead's 2001 label compilation, also called Straight Ahead and compiled by Alex Dallas, carries a version of 6:02 against the album's 5:53.

The track lived on in the {{ <concept_link name="dj mix" /> }}: MusicBrainz lists it inside the mixed compilation Mood 2 Chill in 2001, and on the compilations Club Siesta Popular and Bazaar Nu-Jazz in 2002. That was how much nu-jazz of the period reached listeners.

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} followed in Raw Deal's Freedom Time mix, on Places and Spaces in 2004; Raw Deal also remixed for the 2017 expanded edition.

## Artistic Context

Boehler also records under other names, which Discogs lists as Perpetual Reverb, Zwicker and Icy-2. Morgenthaler, born in Bern in 1952, is a jazz trombonist who also plays alphorn, didgeridoo and conch; he played in Carla Bley's bands in 1985 and 1986, has played alphorn jazz with Günter Baby Sommer and the Swiss Horns, and has repeatedly been named trombonist of the year by the magazine Jazz 'n' More since 1997.

No review or chart figure for Fathorn was found. Its reception is written in its travels instead: a label compilation, mixed and unmixed compilations across two years and a remix three years on. Play it and listen for the moment the alphorn arrives.
