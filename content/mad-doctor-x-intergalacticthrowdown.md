## Genre and Mood

**Mad Doctor X** opens his 1997 album with **Intergalacticthrowdown**, a six-minute track that Discogs files under hip hop and electronic music, in the styles trip hop and downtempo. No published review describes how it sounds, so its two feelings rest on that classification and on the company it later kept: hypnotic and groovy are the plain words for a long cut by a hip hop DJ that a downtempo selector chose for a late-night mix. ## Song Summary

The track opens Picnic With The Greys, released in Britain by Freskanova on CD, with thirteen tracks, and on double vinyl. Its titles lean on science fiction and comics: Hyperion, Mad Molecules, Supernatural Jazz, A Tribute to Bruce Banner. The 1997 CD lists it as Intergalactic Throwdown; the run-together spelling used here also appears in Discogs' listing of the 2003 compilation that carried it further. ## Concepts in Focus

Mad Doctor X is a DJ first, and {{ <concept_link name="turntablism" /> }}, using the turntable as an instrument, is his trade: his Discogs biography credits him with all the scratches on the Freestylers' records of the time, and Wikipedia lists him as their featured turntablist. Whether this track carries scratching no source says.

Its second life came through a {{ <concept_link name="dj mix" /> }}. In 2003 Nightmares on Wax placed it twelfth in his LateNightTales selection, between Tranquility Bass and Dusty Springfield's Spooky; in that mix it runs 5:41, blended into its neighbours.

That mix is a {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }} of a particular kind: in the Late Night Tales series an artist selects and mixes the music, and the volumes often close on a spoken story. ## Artistic Context

Mad Doctor X is Jason Carter, a London hip hop DJ who first came up around 1990 with the Blapps Posse and Don't Hold Back, then recorded for Ninja Tune as DJ Toolz and the London Funk Allstars before his albums as Mad Doctor X on Freskanova.
