## Genre and Mood

**Tears For Fears**' **Mad World** is new wave and synth-pop from 1982, and its mood lives in a contrast: Songfacts hears upbeat, forward-leaning music under a darkly serious lyric. It is melancholic in its words, bittersweet in that tension and introspective in its point of view, a young man watching the world from a window.

## Song Summary

Roland Orzabal wrote it at 19 on acoustic guitar, living above a pizza restaurant in Bath and looking out over the city: "these people go on their merry way to work and the whole rat race thing." He later joked that he should have called it Bourgeois World. The lyric draws on Arthur Janov's primal therapy, the source of the band's name; its most famous line, about dreams of dying being the best, follows Janov's idea that such dreams release tension. Orzabal sang the first attempts and did not like them; when Curt Smith took the vocal, "suddenly it sounded fabulous."

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the song bar by bar, so this follows what its makers and critics describe.

### The Rhythm

Orzabal wanted a new wave song in the vein of Girls on Film: "it had this rhythm that I stole." Songfacts describes the result as driven by a synth bass, with drum machines forming the rhythm.

### The Voice

Smith said the song worked better with his voice because it is "more melancholic, darker." Cash Box heard it grow "from a soft dance tune to a shy Britisher's confessions of his darkest dreams."

### The Last Chorus

Smith's ad lib in the final chorus became a famous mondegreen. In 2010 he explained it: "Halargian world," after Halarge, an imaginary planet invented by a producer during the sessions.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="drum machine" /> }} was part of the band's toolkit from the start. Their keyboard player Ian Stanley owned a Roland Jupiter-4 and a Roland CR-78 drum machine, and Orzabal arranged his songs on those machines; the sources do not say which unit plays on the finished single.

Mad World is also one of the great examples of a {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} remaking a song. Michael Andrews and Gary Jules recorded it for Donnie Darko in 2001 with piano and voice in place of synthesisers and heavy percussion, and in 2003 it was the UK Christmas number one. Hear it again as {{ <track_link source="timmy-trumpet-gabry-ponte-mad-world.md" /> }}.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, the band's first, was directed by Clive Richardson at Knebworth House: Smith stares out of a window while Orzabal dances on a jetty by the lake, and the birthday party is Smith's mother's.

## Artistic Context

Mad World was meant to be a B-side for Pale Shelter until the record company heard a single in it. The band re-recorded it with producers Chris Hughes and Ross Cullum, and it became the first single from The Hurting, released in March 1983.

## Impact and Reception

Mad World reached number three in the UK in November 1982, the band's first hit and the twelfth best-selling single of the year there, and their first international success. Watch Orzabal dance on that jetty.
