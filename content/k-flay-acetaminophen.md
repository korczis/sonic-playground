## Genre and Mood

**K.Flay**'s **Acetaminophen** is hip-hop made with electronic tools and an indie attitude. It is energetic and gritty, and its humour is irreverent: a party song whose title is the painkiller it expects to need. Space City Rock heard it as the dance tune of the EP it closes, driven by brutal, buzzy synth lines.

## Song Summary

The song is the fifth and last track of K.Flay's self-titled EP, released on 12 October 2010 on iTunes and her own website; it runs 2:46. Its lyric, in the review's words, is all about the hedonistic party life. If the night keeps up, the narrator will need acetaminophen, the common painkiller, the next morning, and says plainly that it does not matter. The joke is in the title: the hangover cure named before the night is over.

### The Beat and the Synths

The buzzing synth lines are what set the song apart on the EP, pushing it towards the dance floor.

### The Voice

K.Flay raps and sings on the EP; the review heard her voice as husky and her flow as limber.

### The Production

Everything here is hers: the reviewer stressed that she made the beats, the lyrics and the production herself.

## Concepts in Focus

The EP is a {{ <concept_link name="self-release" /> }}. Space City Rock listed it as self-released and quoted K.Flay's own note that she wrote and produced it in a basement; antiMusic reported it on sale through iTunes, her website and her shows. The reviewer still found it as clean and professional as if the best in the business had produced it.

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} is what makes this track a dance tune. Where other songs on the EP, by the review's account, are lonely and bitter or rapid-fire, Acetaminophen leans on a synth that buzzes rather than glows.

K.Flay's {{ <concept_link name="rapping" /> }} sits beside her singing rather than against it. That mix, later the core of {{ <track_link source="k-flay-high-enough.md" /> }}, is already here.

## Artistic Context

K.Flay is Kristine Flaherty, who, by Wikipedia's account, began making music at Stanford because she found much radio hip-hop misogynistic, and released her first mixtape, Suburban Rap Queen, in 2005. antiMusic described her in 2010 as a San Francisco-based, Chicago-raised musician mixing indie rock with hip-hop, on her first US tour supporting Passion Pit. Space City Rock named her influences as Liz Phair, Mary J. Blige and her father's classic rock records.

## Impact and Reception

The EP was a small release, but the review called it a mini pop masterpiece, catchy and clever. In 2012 K.Flay signed with RCA. Play this one loud, and early in the evening.
