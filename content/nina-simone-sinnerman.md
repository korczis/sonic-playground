## Genre and Mood

**Nina Simone**'s **"Sinnerman"** is jazz, soul and blues at once: a spiritual about judgment played as a ten-minute chase, intense from its first bars.

## Song Summary

The song follows a sinner trying to hide from divine justice on Judgment Day, only to hear the rock cry out that there is no hiding place. Simone learned it as a child at the revival meetings where her mother, a Methodist minister, used it to make people confess. On Pastel Blues the credit reads simply "arranged by Nina Simone", and her arrangement stretches it past ten minutes.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro: The Chase Begins

Shmoop hears the opening as a frenetic hi-hat beat under a piano figure that repeats like a freight train barrelling into a tunnel, with the guitar echoing the piano's fast lines.

### Verses: Nowhere to Run

Simone's voice cuts above the noise. uDiscover Music hears a pulsing drum, a rock-and-roll guitar, plinking piano and rhythmic clapping.

### Call and Response: Power

About two minutes in, Shmoop notes, Simone begins calling out "Power" over and over in a gospel manner, answered by a quiet chorus. uDiscover places the first great release nearly four minutes in.

### Breakdown: Hands and Rhythm

Then the piano drops out. Guitar, bass and drums carry on alone, and handclaps enter, scattered at first, then more frequent as the piano finds its way back.

### Climax: Voice and Piano as One

Piano and voice merge into a second crescendo that rises and falls; Simone begins to moan.

### Outro: The Sudden Stop

Shmoop hears a last breakthrough near the nine-and-a-half-minute mark, sustained by the piano for almost another minute. Then the song simply stops.

## Concepts in Focus

The Power section is {{ <concept_link name="call and response" /> }} in its oldest sense: a leader calls, a congregation answers, as at her mother's revival meetings.

The stripped-down passage is a {{ <concept_link name="breakdown" /> }} before dance music named it: the piano falls away, rhythm and claps hold the tension, and its return lands harder. No wonder house producers return to this record, as in {{ <track_link source="nina-simone-felix-da-housecat-sinnerman.md" /> }}.

"Sinnerman" is also a lesson in the {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }}. Les Baxter's orchestra recorded it in 1956 with Will Holt singing, and the Weavers reshaped it in 1959. Simone's arrangement eclipsed them, and later covers such as {{ <track_link source="belako-sinnerman.md" /> }} are covers of hers.

## Artistic Context

Simone was a Juilliard-trained classical pianist, and in her Greenwich Village years she often closed her concerts with this song. Pastel Blues, recorded in New York with Al Schackman and Rudy Stevenson on guitar, Lisle Atkinson on double bass and Bobby Hamilton on drums, came out on Philips on 1 October 1965, the year of Malcolm X's assassination and the Selma-to-Montgomery march, where Simone performed.

## Impact and Reception

AllMusic called "Sinnerman" a frantic, explosive tour de force that dwarfs everything else on the album, and Pitchfork ranked Pastel Blues 21st among the best albums of the 1960s. It has been sampled for Talib Kweli's Get By and Hozier's Nina Cried Power and scored the gallery theft in The Thomas Crown Affair. Set aside ten minutes and let it chase you.
