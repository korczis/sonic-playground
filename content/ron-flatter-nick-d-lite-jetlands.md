## Genre and Mood

**Ron Flatter** and **Nick D-Lite**'s **Jetlands** is electronic music filed here under deep house, the first style Flatter's own biography names when it describes his DJ sets. No review we found names the track's genre, so the labels stay broad. The feelings come from the one description we have, Forced Exposure's note on the compilation Jetlands opens: melancholic melodies, subtle sunshine and blissful energy. Melancholic and bittersweet are the closest words for that mix.

## Song Summary

Jetlands is a long track, seven minutes and 52 seconds in the version on Spotify, which dates it 18 October 2013, and on Schneeweiß II, the Stil vor Talent compilation released in late November 2013 that places it first. The release the yearbook names, Jetlands und Wolken, adds a German word to the title: Jetlands and clouds. What it sounds like in detail is not described in any source we could find, so this page describes less than usual.

## Musical Breakdown

### Main Section

No source walks through the track. What can be said is its scale: nearly eight minutes is a club length, made to be mixed in and out of rather than played as a song. Listen for how it fills that time, and whether Forced Exposure's sunshine or its melancholy arrives first.

## Concepts in Focus

The track's history raises the question of what a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} is. In December 2012 the channel Majestic Casual posted a track of almost the same length titled Red Mystery - Jetlands (Ron Flatter & Nick D-Lite Edit), linking to a free download on Flatter's SoundCloud. An edit usually reshapes an existing track for DJs; a year later Stil vor Talent's compilation listed Jetlands as the duo's original mix. The sources do not explain how the two relate.

Credits differ too. Most releases list Ron Flatter & Nick D-Lite as equals, while the 2014 compilation About: Berlin, Vol. 6 credits Ron Flatter feat. Nick D-Lite, as if Nick D-Lite were a {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}. A credit is a claim about who made the music; here the releases disagree.

## Artistic Context

Ron Flatter holds a residency in his hometown of Halle (Saale); his biography counts more than 26 EPs and singles, the 2011 album Growth Rings and more than 50 remixes. The sources say little about Nick D-Lite. Schneeweiß II shows {{ <concept_link name="club culture" /> }} leaving the club: Oliver Koletzki compiled it for Stil vor Talent together with Schneeweiss, a restaurant in Berlin's Friedrichshain, one of whose owners came from the city's early club scene, and opened it with Jetlands before tracks by Stimming, Kellerkind and Koletzki himself, the producer of {{ <track_link source="oliver-koletzki-through-the-darkness.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Jetlands travelled through compilations: MusicBrainz lists it on Schneeweiß II, #afterhour, Volume 2 and About: Berlin, Vol. 6, all within a few months. It did so with almost no critical paper trail. Give it its full length, and decide for yourself where the sunshine is.
