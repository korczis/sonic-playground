## Genre and Mood

**Gin Wigmore**'s **Poison** is blues with soul in its voice and pop in its finish, the kind of record Gravel & Wine was built from. Femme Metal Webzine heard a blues-tinged tune filled with melancholy; the New Zealand Herald's TimeOut reviewer heard a lament so dramatic it bordered on too much, sung by a character, a southern woman scorned. Defiant, theatrical and melancholic are this page's names for the pull between those two readings.

## Song Summary

Poison is the third of eleven tracks on Gravel & Wine, released in New Zealand on 7 November 2011, and runs 3:07. Wigmore wrote it with Butch Walker, who produced the album, and with Dan Wilson. The TimeOut review described the woman in the song as strong, devious and furious, a part that suits Wigmore's powerful delivery. No source sets out the lyric line by line, so this page leaves the details of the grievance to the song itself.

### Main Section

The TimeOut reviewer found the instrumentation and arrangements so massive and visceral that you could imagine a whole set, almost a whole musical, built around the song. Among the album's backing vocals the same review pointed to a low and physical "huh, yah" riff in Poison. MusicBrainz credits guitars to Walker and Fran Capitanelli, bass guitar to Jake Sinclair, and background vocals to all three.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="stage persona" /> }} is a character a singer steps into. The TimeOut review heard exactly that here: Wigmore sounding as if she was having enormous fun playing a role, the wronged woman of a southern story.

The {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }} decides how big a song feels. On Poison, by that review's account, it is big enough to carry a musical. Compare the piano and strings of the album's ballad, {{ <track_link source="gin-wigmore-if-only.md" /> }}, also written with Wilson.

A {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }} is a short repeated figure, usually played on guitar. The "huh, yah" the Herald heard is a riff sung by the backing voices instead of played.

## Artistic Context

Wigmore, born in Auckland in 1986, spent about two months in Mississippi and Alabama, in her words the real southern heart of America, before making Gravel & Wine; she said so to Lydia Jenkin of the New Zealand Herald in an interview the Otago Daily Times published. She then recorded with Walker in Santa Monica, his band The Black Widows playing. Wilson, her co-writer, fronted Semisonic and co-wrote Adele's Someone Like You.

## Impact and Reception

Poison was never a single; the album's were Black Sheep and Man Like That. Gravel & Wine debuted at number one in New Zealand, went double platinum there and reached number 87 on the Billboard 200 after its US release in 2013. Reviewers picked it out all the same: the Waikato Herald named it with Man Like That as top shelf. Turn it up and let the arrangement fill the room.
