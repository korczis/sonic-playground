## Genre and Mood

**Apocalyptica** play **Nothing Else Matters** the way they play everything by Metallica: four cellos, no guitars, no drum kit and no singer. Where their versions of thrash songs turn bows into riffs, this one stays with a ballad, a neoclassical cover of a love song, melancholic and romantic by nature rather than by any added effect.

## Song Summary

Nothing Else Matters is Metallica's power ballad, the third single from their 1991 album Metallica. James Hetfield wrote it on tour in 1990, homesick, as a love song to his girlfriend; at first he was reluctant to show it to the band. Apocalyptica first recorded it for Inquisition Symphony in 1998. This is a different recording: one of three never-before-released instrumental tracks, Blabbermouth reported in May 2016, added to the 20th-anniversary remaster of Plays Metallica by Four Cellos, issued on 22 July 2016. Wikipedia lists it there as the 2016 Version, 4:49, and the band's own upload calls it a bonus track of that remaster. A digital edition of the compilation Amplified carries it too.

## Musical Breakdown

No review describes the 2016 recording in detail, so this part follows the song's shape and leaves the cello parts to your ears.

### Intro

Metallica's original opens with an E minor arpeggio, beginning with the open low E and then the open G, B and high E strings of a guitar. Listen for how a cello, tuned and played very differently, takes that opening over.

### Main Section

Without Hetfield's voice, the melody of the verses and the chorus has to move into the quartet. Metallica's own 1999 live version with the San Francisco Symphony showed that the song survives being handed to strings; here there are only four.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} can be recorded more than once. Apocalyptica's 1998 take, released by Universal on Inquisition Symphony, runs 4:46; this one, which Reflections of Darkness calls a newly recorded version, runs 4:49.

An {{ <concept_link name="arpeggio" /> }}, a chord played one note at a time, is what makes the song recognisable in its first seconds: a guitarist picking open strings in turn. On a cello it becomes a different gesture.

A {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }} usually gathers older recordings. Amplified, first issued in 2006 as a retrospective, appears on MusicBrainz in a digital edition whose third track carries this recording's 2016 code, DERK41600024, one number after the 2016 Battery's DERK41600023 ({{ <track_link source="apocalyptica-battery.md" /> }}).

## Artistic Context

Apocalyptica began in 1993 at the Sibelius Academy in Helsinki as four cellists playing Metallica, and their 1996 debut, Plays Metallica by Four Cellos, held eight such covers. Nothing Else Matters was not among them. Its 2016 recording joined that album two decades late, alongside Battery and Seek & Destroy, on the remaster that Reflections of Darkness lists under Odyssey Music Network.

## Impact and Reception

Of the three bonus tracks, Reflections of Darkness named only one: this newly recorded Nothing Else Matters. The band's earlier, 1998 version had a second life of its own in 2022, when, as Loudwire reported, it played over the closing montage of the third episode of Netflix's Wednesday. Listen to both.
