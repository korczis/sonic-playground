## Genre and Mood

**The Prodigy**'s **Invisible Sun** is where The Day Is My Enemy slows down and looks into the dark. It is electronic music near trip-hop and dubstep: NME filed it under dubstep, and Sonicabuse heard it closer to Massive Attack's Mezzanine than to The Fat of the Land. The feelings are dark, hypnotic and haunting; Talkhouse called it ominous and crawling, The Line of Best Fit spooked out.

## Song Summary

The album was the first on which Keith Flint and Maxim worked on the songwriting with Liam Howlett, yet Invisible Sun is credited to Howlett alone. Flint sings it, with background vocals by Tim Hutton. Talkhouse described its lyrics as typically nursery-rhyme-like, about a special sun at once lit and invisible, a star and a shadow on other stars, shining where there is no path. The embed is the album recording, uploaded by the band.

## Musical Breakdown

No source gives times; Talkhouse describes how the track begins and builds.

### Intro

Talkhouse heard it start with an incongruously modern trap beat pattern, the kind of thing that falls out of music software if you open it too fast.

### Main Section

Then, in Talkhouse's account, Howlett adds distorted guitars and a chanting hook, and the trap beat falls into step, marching in uniform. Sonicabuse heard waves of guitar over a beat it called hypnotic; The Line of Best Fit heard the track cut back the layers and slow the pace down.

### Chorus

The List heard a more sinister, brooding energy here, buried beneath a shouted chorus of limited sense.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is the phrase built to lodge in memory, and here it is a chant. Talkhouse heard a chanting hook like Twisted Sister's; The List heard the same chorus as shouting with little sense. One hook, opposite verdicts.

{{ <concept_link name="distortion" /> }} overdrives a sound until it turns harsh. In Talkhouse's account the distorted guitars change the track, turning a modern trap pattern into something regimented; listen for the guitars arriving and the beat changing character.

A {{ <concept_link name="deep cut" /> }} is an album track that never became a single, often where a record takes its risks. Sonicabuse heard a newfound maturity in the band's songwriting here, and the perfect breather after a relentless opening.

## Artistic Context

The Day Is My Enemy was released by Take Me to the Hospital and Cooking Vinyl on 30 March 2015, the Monday before Easter; Talkhouse's reviewer noted the date but wrote that the invisible sun would have raised the question of its meaning anyway. On the CD it is the thirteenth of fourteen tracks, before Wall of Death; on the 2LP edition Sonicabuse reviewed, it sits on side B. Its companion in calm is {{ <track_link source="the-prodigy-beyond-the-deathray.md" /> }}. Flint died in 2019.

## Impact and Reception

The album debuted at number one in the UK. Invisible Sun drew praise even where the album did not: Metal Hammer counted it among the album's real high points, Sonicabuse called it brilliant, and The Line of Best Fit heard a creeped-out piece of Timbaland-era thrill.
