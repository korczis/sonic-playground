## Genre and Mood

**Funkerman**'s **Coming Home** is house music with one foot in the jazz age. Indie Shuffle heard it as upbeat and electro-swing inspired, in the company of Parov Stelar and Goldfish; Dancing Astronaut called it a time machine of a track. It is groovy, playful and nostalgic by design, and this page is about its radio edit, the three-minute version cut from the club mix.

## Song Summary

Everything turns on a vocal sample. Dancing Astronaut described a coarse, vintage voice dropped into a modern house breakdown, and the label, Can You Feel It Records, says the track samples Mel Tormé's Comin' Home Baby. Indie Shuffle had the same identification from a reader. The label's own summary is that it brings the funk and the vibes and carries the soul as well as the house. Ardie van Beek, who is Funkerman, produced it with his label partner Raf Jansen; the single came out on 15 December 2014.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the radio edit section by section, and the video here is the 6:14 original mix, not the edit. What follows sticks to what the sources describe.

### The Sample

The old voice is the hook. Listen to its grain, the coarse, vintage quality Dancing Astronaut heard against the clean modern production.

### The Groove

Around it runs a house groove that the label heard as funk and Indie Shuffle as electro-swing.

### The Edit

The radio edit keeps the idea in about three minutes instead of six. Which bars it drops, no source says.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} here reaches back through three layers of history. Comin' Home Baby was written by Ben Tucker as an instrumental and first recorded by the Dave Bailey Quintet in 1961; Bob Dorough added words, and Mel Tormé's 1962 recording reached number 36 in the US and number 13 in the UK. Tormé himself had doubts, calling it a minor-key blues with trite, repetitive lyrics. Repetition is exactly what a house loop wants.

A {{ <concept_link name="radio edit" /> }} is the short version of a long track, made for radio and playlists. The label's promo CD paired the Radio Edit with the Original Mix: one for radio, one for DJs. Compare another house radio edit with a jazz-age air in {{ <track_link source="bakermat-goldfish-marie-plassard-games-continued-feat-marie-plassard-radio-edit.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Funkerman, born in Breda in 1975, is a Dutch house DJ and producer who runs Flamingo Recordings with Raf Jansen and Fedde Le Grand. He reached the international charts with Speed Up, which got him signed to Defected, and recorded with Le Grand as F to the F. In 2011 he and Jansen started Can You Feel It Records, which released Coming Home and, in 2015, a remix by Crazibiza.

## Impact and Reception

In 2015 a version of Coming Home just under three minutes long turned up on dance compilations such as De Maxx Long Player 33 and Deep Sounds 4: The Very Best of Deep House. Put on the radio edit, then Tormé's original, and hear where the house comes from.
