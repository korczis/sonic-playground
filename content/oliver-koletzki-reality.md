## Genre and Mood

**Oliver Koletzki**'s **Reality** is the quiet last word of an album otherwise made for clubs, festival stages and radio. It sits at the electronic, downtempo end of his work, built, as the label describes it, on piano and a spoken voice, and the mood that follows is reflective and calm: a door closing softly rather than a final peak.

## Song Summary

Reality is the thirteenth and last track of I am OK, Koletzki's fifth studio album, released on 16 May 2014. His label describes it in a single sentence: the echoing piano rolls and spoken word of Reality bid a gentle farewell, as Oliver quite literally walks us out of his unique musical world. On an album of eight collaborations, it is one of four tracks with no credited guest. Apple Music lists Koletzki as composer, producer and mixing engineer, with Stefan Thomas also mixing, and a second songwriter, Edward Charles Oliver, without saying what he wrote.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="spoken word" /> }} means a voice that talks or recites over the music instead of singing a melody. Placed at the end of an album full of singers, from Fran to HVOB's Anna Müller, a speaking voice changes the register: it addresses the listener directly rather than performing a song.

An echo in production is usually made with a {{ <concept_link name="delay" /> }}, which repeats a sound after a set time, often several times and fading. No source says how the echo on these piano rolls was made, but the effect the label describes is the kind a delay gives: each phrase seeming to sound again as it fades, which suits a farewell.

## Artistic Context

Koletzki, born in Braunschweig in 1974, broke through in 2005 with Der Mückenschwarm on Sven Väth's Cocoon and co-founded Stil vor Talent the same year. I am OK came out on the label with distribution by Universal Music. The label framed it as a return to his roots and to the club after more pop-oriented albums, with nods to disco, old-school hip-hop, UK garage, dub techno and deep house, and with a hand-drawn cover by Chrisse Kunst showing him at thirteen in nerd glasses. Up in the Air with Fran, it says, picks up where {{ <track_link source="oliver-koletzki-fran-hypnotized.md" /> }} left off.
