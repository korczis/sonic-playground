## Genre and Mood

**D12** made **American Psycho II** as a sequel to their own horror piece, and it is hip-hop and rap at their darkest and most theatrical: eerie in sound, provocative in word and dark from the first bar.

## Song Summary

The 2001 American Psycho, produced by Eminem for Devil's Night, set D12's serial-killer imagery to a foreboding beat. Three years later the crew returned to the idea with Dr. Dre behind the desk. HotNewHipHop called the sequel an equally compelling counterpart to the original and heard it as D12 and Dre leaning into their darker urges. The beat, produced by Dr. Dre with Mike Elizondo, who also played guitar and keyboard on it, rests on what the same piece describes as an eerie guitar riff and equally haunting strings. Over it, four members of D12 take a verse each, and B-Real of Cypress Hill joins as the guest.

## Musical Breakdown

No source times the track, so the parts below follow the published credits and a lyric transcription rather than a clock.

### Intro

The beat sets the scene. Joe Hanson of The Chicago Maroon heard it in 2004 as eerie and James Bond-ish: the tension of a spy film rather than the shock of a slasher.

### Chorus

A lyric transcription credits the hook to B-Real, and MusicBrainz lists him as a guest vocalist on the recording. His high-pitched, nasal style, the voice Cypress Hill was built around, cuts across the beat in a way no D12 voice does.

### Verses

Swifty McVay opens the run, Kuniva follows, then Bizarre, who also rapped on the 2001 original, and finally Eminem. HotNewHipHop joked that Bizarre's verse this time leaves out one particularly gruesome image from his first. The Maroon review's own headline asked for a little more Eminem on the album.

## Concepts in Focus

American Psycho II is a {{ <concept_link name="posse cut" /> }}: one beat, several rappers from the same crew, each given a verse to make his mark. HotNewHipHop noted that Proof is the only member of D12 never to appear on an American Psycho track: whoever is on the record competes with the others on equal ground.

The {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} here comes from outside the crew. B-Real brings a voice from another group and another city to a Detroit record and gives the chorus a sound the verses never have.

The track is also a lesson in what a {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} contributes. HotNewHipHop says this beat was Dr. Dre's only contribution to D12 World, and the credits show he mixed it too. Compare its cinematic chill with the original's foreboding Eminem production in {{ <track_link source="d12-american-psycho.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

D12, the Detroit crew around Eminem, released D12 World on 27 April 2004 on Shady Records and Interscope Records. American Psycho II is its eighteenth track, running 3:44, and its writing credits name the four rapping members, B-Real, Dr. Dre and Elizondo. Its other producers included Eminem, Kanye West and Hi-Tek.

## Impact and Reception

D12 World entered the Billboard 200 at number one with about 544,000 copies sold in its first week, also topped the UK albums chart and went double platinum in the US, though its reviews were mixed. The Maroon called American Psycho II excellent. Listen to the original first, then this one, and hear how a change of producer changes the fear.
