## Genre and Mood

**Earl** recorded **All That Glitters** for Tongue Tied, the 2017 album on which she turned to jazz, and it sits where jazz meets pop. Critics heard an electro-swing influence across the album, and CelebMix heard a 1930s sound in this song in particular. The feelings chosen here, sultry and nostalgic, follow that: a voice in an old-fashioned setting, looking back at the glamour of another era.

## Song Summary

All That Glitters is the second of twelve tracks on Tongue Tied, released by BMG on 28 July 2017; its official video appeared on 5 July, while the album was still on pre-order. Earl has described the song as a "cautionary tale" about her double life as a dancer to pay legal bills, as a 2026 profile by Curious Goldfish puts it. The title is the first half of an old proverb, and the song leaves the listener to supply the rest. No review describes its arrangement in detail, and no source consulted here names its writers or producers, so this page does not.

The one description a reviewer gave is CelebMix's: a 1930s sound, which the review said was inspired by Earl's past collaboration with Lana Del Rey. The album around it, the same review said, is jazz with a modern twist, twelve songs each with its own sound and outstanding beats. ## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="swing" /> }} is a rhythmic feel, pairs of notes played unevenly so the music lilts, and it gave its name to the big-band era. When Earl signed with BMG in 2015 she moved toward jazz citing Billie Holiday, Duke Ellington and big-band swing. Listen for whether this song's beat leans into that lilt.

A {{ <concept_link name="stage persona" /> }} is the self an artist presents in music. Born Kate Joy Smithson in Anchorage, Alaska, she made her 2005 debut with a folk-leaning sound and recorded for years as Kate Earl; for this chapter she became simply Earl, and Curious Goldfish describes the change as a rebrand toward jazz, blues and vintage soul.

## Artistic Context

Before Tongue Tied, Kate Earl had made albums for The Record Collection, Universal Republic and Downtown Records, then went independent and self-produced Ransom in 2014. In 2015 she was one of six acts chosen from 200,000 entries for the BBC Introducing stage in Hyde Park. The album's title single, an uptempo electro-swing track, was nominated as Radio 2's Record of the Week.

## Impact and Reception

The song's great audience came five years later. According to Curious Goldfish, Britney Spears posted a video featuring All That Glitters in 2022, sparking a viral explosion that the profile says resulted in over five billion streams, and the profile calls the song the "phoenix" moment that allowed her to reclaim her career.
