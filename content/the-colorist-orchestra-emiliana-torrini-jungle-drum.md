## Genre and Mood

**The Colorist Orchestra** and **Emilíana Torrini** play **Jungle Drum** as indie pop handed to a small orchestra that draws on world music: joyful, playful and, in the strings, euphoric. One reviewer summed up the arrangement as finger-clicking, foot-stomping joy.

## Song Summary

Jungle Drum was Torrini's biggest hit, a 2009 single written with her producer Dan Carey. Here it is rebuilt from the ground up. NPR's Michelle Mercer heard homemade percussion and a line doubled on piano and bass deepening the groove, with strings accentuating the euphoria, and noted that even when the players break the song down, they never lose its original dancing beat. At 2:46 this version runs about half a minute longer than the original single.

The beat comes from the ensemble's percussion, played on traditional and self-designed instruments, and the song's pulse survives the change.

The strings push the song's euphoria further, and when the band breaks the song down, the dancing beat stays.

## Concepts in Focus

This is an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }}: the same singer and the same song, a different arrangement. Play it next to the 2009 single and listen for how much of its mood came from production rather than melody.

It is also a {{ <concept_link name="live recording" /> }}. The album captures the collaboration on stage, strings and woodwinds played live.

Its {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} shows that a dance beat lives in how parts lock together, not in a drum kit: percussion and a doubled bass line keep the song moving. ## Artistic Context

The Colorist is a Belgian ensemble founded in 2013 by the multi-instrumentalists Aarich Jespers and Kobe Proesmans to reinterpret singers' songs, a method it calls an acoustic live remix or inverse karaoke. Its eight players use percussion, prepared piano, bass, bass clarinet, violin and viola alongside stones, mirrors and glass bowls. Torrini, its dream collaborator, toured with it from 2015; Rough Trade released the live album on 9 December 2016.

## Impact and Reception

The original Jungle Drum spent eight weeks at number one in Germany. Blogcritics called this reworking one of the album's liveliest tracks, and the partnership continued with a studio album, Racing the Storm, in 2023. Start here, then play the original.
