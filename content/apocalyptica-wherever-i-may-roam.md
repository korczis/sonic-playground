## Genre and Mood

**Apocalyptica** play **Wherever I May Roam** as four cellists and nothing else: no voice, no drum kit, no amplifier. The cover sits between neoclassical chamber music and symphonic metal. The Green Man Review found it spooky, ghostly and haunting: dark, eerie, and intense once it gathers speed.

## Song Summary

Wherever I May Roam is Metallica's song about life on the road, written by James Hetfield and Lars Ulrich during years of near-constant touring, and released in 1992 as the fourth single from their 1991 album Metallica. Its original opens on a gong and a sitar, with a twelve-string bass adding the crack on accented notes. Apocalyptica recorded it as the seventh of eight covers on their 1996 debut, where it runs 6:09. This page plays that performance in the album's 2016 remaster, uploaded by the band.

Metallica's gong and sitar have no place in a cello quartet. Teen Ink heard the cover start slowly; no source says what the players put in place of those sounds, so listen for how they set the scene.

### Climax

Teen Ink describes the track building to its climax with a fast beat: the point where the bowing turns into rhythm and the cover comes closest to metal.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} can keep every note and change every sound, and this one has to. A sung road song becomes an instrumental.

That makes it a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }}. The original's colour comes from a gong, a sitar and a twelve-string bass; four cellos must find it elsewhere, in how high, how low and how hard they play.

The shape of the track is a {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }}. Without a singer, six minutes hold together by starting slowly and gaining pace toward the end.

The 2016 edition is an exercise in {{ <concept_link name="remastering" /> }}: the 1996 performance mastered again, which Reflections of Darkness heard as cleaner, with the details brought forward. Compare {{ <track_link source="apocalyptica-the-unforgiven.md" /> }}, another song from the same Metallica album.

## Artistic Context

Apocalyptica began as four classically trained cellists from Helsinki's Sibelius Academy: Eicca Toppinen, Paavo Lötjönen, Max Lilja and Antero Manninen. After a 1995 show, a label employee invited them to record; Mercury released Plays Metallica by Four Cellos on 10 May 1996, produced by Pekka Ritualuoto. The remaster followed on 22 July 2016 on Harmageddon Records, with three bonus tracks.

## Impact and Reception

In Louder, Stephen Dalton heard this track and The Unforgiven relocate Metallica's doom-drenched western fantasies to the bohemian salons of central Europe. Metallica's own single had reached number two in Finland, Apocalyptica's home, and the album went platinum there.
