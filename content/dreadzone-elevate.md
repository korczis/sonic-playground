## Genre and Mood

**Dreadzone** placed **Elevate** in the middle of their 2005 album Once Upon a Time, the sixth of twelve tracks. Discogs files the album under breakbeat, dub and breaks, and Resident Advisor gave the track's 12-inch the same two words, breakbeat and dub. The mood here is groovy and uplifting, a reading that rests on that classification and the title rather than on a published description, because no review walks through the album version itself.

## Song Summary

Elevate runs 5:15 on Once Upon a Time. The album credits its production, arrangement and electronics to Greg Dread and Ben Balafonic, its vocals to Earl Sixteen and MC Spee, and Steve Roberts on guitar, bass and keyboards on this track. In March 2006 Functional Breaks issued it as a 12-inch single with two remixes and no album version, and Resident Advisor's Karl Palma wrote that the release stayed true to the band's dub-infused breakbeat. His review also names Earl 16's rap as part of the original track.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="breakbeat" /> }} means drums that break the steady kick into syncopated patterns, the kind first looped from the instrumental breaks of funk records.

{{ <concept_link name="dub" /> }} is the other half, the Jamaican art of remaking a record at the mixing desk with echo, space and parts that drop in and out. It runs through the band's whole history; hear it next to {{ <track_link source="dreadzone-iron-shirt.md" /> }} from the same album.

The single is a lesson in the {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }}. Mafiatone, which Resident Advisor calls Dreadzone's alter ego, made a chilled, trippy version on a smooth breakbeat with a soulful female vocal added in the background. Roxiller went harder: a heavy breakbeat, the original's vocals and Earl 16's rap, beefed up by a chugging guitar riff. The reviewer called it rocking and picked it as his favourite.

## Artistic Context

Dreadzone formed in London in 1993 around Greg Roberts, the former Big Audio Dynamite drummer billed as Greg Dread. Once Upon a Time came out in 2005 on the independent Functional label; Rebel Base heard a rougher sound on it, shaped by the band's time as DJs on the English club circuit. Dubwiser Records has since reissued the album.

## Impact and Reception

Elevate made no recorded chart run. Its reception is the remix single and its review, and a festival film from 2008. Play the album version first, then imagine what Roxiller and Mafiatone each pulled out of it.
