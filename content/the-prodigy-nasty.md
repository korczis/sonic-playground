## Genre and Mood

**The Prodigy**'s **Nasty** is the band announcing itself again: electronic, aggressive, energetic and defiant, a breakbeat track in the line of their 1990s records. Sonicabuse heard it harking back to Music for the Jilted Generation, and Consequence of Sound a nu-rave scorcher in the vein of Invaders Must Die. It is big beat that sounds exactly like its title, which is the praise and the complaint.

## Song Summary

Liam Howlett wrote it with Keith Flint, Tim Hutton and Nick Halkes, and Flint and Maxim share the vocals, with Simon "Brother Culture" Fajemisin and background vocals by Hutton. Songfacts describes the song and its video as an attack on the widening gulf between classes in the UK, and reports Flint telling Q that a friend had called him "Mr. Nasty". The embed above is the album recording; the official video runs shorter.

## Musical Breakdown

No source gives times, and only fragments describe its order; what follows is what the reviews and Howlett say.

### The Start

Consequence of Sound heard the track commence as some industrial rock epic and quickly morph into a nu-rave scorcher.

### The Body

Sonicabuse heard punk-infused vocals, sampled guitars and skittering beats that recall Pendulum's brutal reworking of "Voodoo People". DIY heard Flint as manic and unhinged as ever while a maelstrom of frenzied electronic sounds clatters around him. Of the middle section, Howlett told Q only that it has "some trippy Winston Churchill vibe".

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="breakbeat" /> }} is a syncopated drum pattern in the style of a funk break, and it drove The Prodigy's rave records; the skittering beats Sonicabuse heard are what tie Nasty to them.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Oliver Jones and produced by Better Feeling Films, mixes mainly rotoscoped animation with live action. An urban fox, the cover star of the album, slips under a fence towards a girl's home; her father gathers hunters, horses and shotguns, and the ending is, in Exclaim!'s words, a transformative affair. Songfacts reads the fox as a reference to Conservative plans to re-legalise fox-hunting.

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} EP followed on 2 February 2015, with versions by Zinc, Spor and Onen.

## Artistic Context

Released on 12 January 2015 by Take Me to the Hospital and Cooking Vinyl, Nasty was the lead single from The Day Is My Enemy, the band's sixth album, and their first single since "Take Me to the Hospital" in 2009. The title track followed two weeks later, and the fox came back in the video for {{ <track_link source="the-prodigy-wild-frontier.md" /> }}. Flint died in 2019.

## Impact and Reception

The single reached number 23 on the UK Dance chart and 98 on the UK Singles chart; the album debuted at number one in the UK. Critics split hard: DIY found it just what a Prodigy single called Nasty should sound like, while Drowned in Sound called it so tired it barely has a pulse, and Metal Hammer could mistake it for an Invaders Must Die b-side. Decide for yourself, loud.
