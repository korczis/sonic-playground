## Genre and Mood

**Dreadzone** open their 2005 album Once Upon a Time with a 57-second intro and then **King Dub Rock**, the first full track. Discogs files the album under breakbeat, dub and breaks and the single under dub and breaks, and that is the frame here: electronic dance music cut from Jamaican dub. The feelings named here, groovy and energetic, rest on that classification and the single's subtitle, In The Party, because no review walks through this track.

## Song Summary

King Dub Rock runs 5:28. The release credits its writing to the band's core of the time, Greg Roberts, his brother Steve Roberts and Ben Balafonic, and the album credits Greg Dread and Balafonic with production, arrangement and electronics. Yet while the album names a vocalist on most of its tracks, Earl Sixteen or MC Spee, it names none for King Dub Rock, and Steve Roberts' guitar, bass and keyboards are credited on four other tracks, not this one. In November 2005 Functional Breaks issued it on 12-inch and CD as King Dub Rock (In The Party), backed by a remix by Tom Real vs. The Rogue Element.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes King Dub Rock section by section, so this breakdown stays with the credits and leaves the rest to your ears.

### Main Section

The rhythm is where the two labels meet: breakbeat drums under the space of dub. On the neighbouring single, Once Upon a Time (In Jamaica), Resident Advisor heard a percussive breakbeat over a deep, dubby bassline; whether King Dub Rock is built the same way no review says.

### The Remix

The single's other side hands the track to Tom Real vs. The Rogue Element, with additional production by Ben Medcalf and T. Jenkins. No published review describes it.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="dub" /> }} is the Jamaican art of remaking a record at the mixing desk: parts drop out and return, echo and reverb open space around the drums and bass. The word sits in this track's title. Hear how the band handles it on a sung track with {{ <track_link source="dreadzone-elevate.md" /> }}.

{{ <concept_link name="breakbeat" /> }} means drums that break the steady kick into syncopated patterns, a style first looped from the instrumental breaks of funk records. Discogs gives the single exactly two styles, dub and breaks.

The {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} on the B-side uses one album track twice: as the band made it and as another producer heard it.

## Artistic Context

Dreadzone formed in London in 1993 around the former Big Audio Dynamite drummer Greg Roberts; Ben Balafonic replaced co-founder Tim Bran in 2001. Once Upon a Time, their fifth studio album, came out on the independent Functional label. In a 2005 interview with Rebel Base, Roberts called it a much more vocal album than its predecessors, less reliant on samples and shaped by the band's deejaying, with only about three instrumentals. Steve Roberts died in October 2006.

## Impact and Reception

King Dub Rock made no recorded chart run, and no review of the single has surfaced. Rebel Base heard the album as a clear evolution of the band's sound, rougher than before. Put the album on from the top and let the intro lead you into it.
