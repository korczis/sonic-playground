## Genre and Mood

**Matt Maeson**'s **Put It on Me** is alternative and indie rock with a heavy, purposeful drive. Indie Band Guru, reviewing the EP it opens, described Maeson's sound as intense yet hypnotic and his music as raw; this song is the intense side of that, a hard-hitting beat under a voice that offers to carry someone else's pain.

## Song Summary

The chorus is the song's argument. Maeson sings that if there is nothing but pain, or no one else to blame, it should be put on him, and he says it again and again; the review called it a catchy chorus that insistently demands. The words lean on religious imagery, as the review says much of his writing does: hung high and dry, the way, the life and the truth. Apple Music credits the song to Alex Hope, James Flannigan and Maeson.

## Musical Breakdown

### Main Section

The review describes the song's engine: a steady compound beat that hits hard every time. No source we found walks through the verses, so this page does not describe them.

### Chorus

The repeated offer to take the blame is the hook, and Maeson's voice, in the review's words, sails over high notes, hinting at vulnerability but rooted in strength.

### Outro

As the review describes it, synth riffs and guitar lines come to a head in a brief instrumental interlude, and that passage carries the song to its peak in the final lines.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="time signature" /> }} tells you how to count a song. A compound beat, the review's term here, divides each main beat into three instead of two, the way 6/8 or 12/8 do; no source gives this song's exact signature. The result is a rolling, heavy pulse, the same family of feel as the 12/8 of {{ <track_link source="imagine-dragons-believer.md" /> }}.

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is one plain demand, blame it on me, and the song is built so that it returns until it sticks. Its force comes from repetition and from the oddness of the request.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} was released with the EP on 27 April 2018, directed and edited by Cody LaPlant and produced by Jordan Larson. At about three and a half minutes it runs slightly longer than the three-minute-22 recording.

## Artistic Context

Maeson, born in 1993 and raised in Gloucester, Virginia, grew up in a family ministry: his parents played in Christian metal bands, he was kept away from secular rock on the radio until he was a teenager, and he took up guitar at 15 and performed with the ministry at biker rallies and in prisons. Signed to Neon Gold and Atlantic, he released The Hearse, six songs, a year after the EP Who Killed Matt Maeson. His debut album Bank on the Funeral followed in April 2019.

## Impact and Reception

Indie Band Guru heard the whole EP as powerfully honest, its songs heavy with a raw honesty. In 2019 his debut single Cringe, first released in 2016, spent four weeks at number one on Billboard's Alternative Songs chart. Put It on Me is the door into it. Play it loud, and count in threes.
