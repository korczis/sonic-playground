## Genre and Mood

**Masomenos** recorded **G 3rd eye** as one stretch of a longer journey: the seventh part of The Third Eye, an album mixed as a single continuous piece. Discogs files the album under house and minimal, and the duo's Bandcamp tags it deep, dub, house, minimal and techno. The duo call their own style colourful, playful and psychedelic, and the feelings here, playful and hypnotic, take that and the repetition minimal house is built on as their guide.

## Song Summary

The Third Eye came out in 2009 on Welcome To Masomenos, the duo's own label, as a mixed CD credited as produced and mixed by Adrien de Maublanc, and as a digital album. On streaming services its twelve tracks carry letters instead of names, A to L. On the duo's Bandcamp the seventh track, of the same length, 5:13, is called Percute / Juhuu: one piece of music handing over to the next. No review describes what Percute sounds like, so this page describes what the titles and lengths say.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="dj mix" /> }} joins tracks so that each flows into the next, and The Third Eye is built that way from start to finish. The point of a mixed album is that its divisions are partly arbitrary: a track number marks a place in a flow, not the start of a new song.

That makes this an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }} too. Percute also came out at full length, 7:56, on Third Eye 3/4, one of four twelve-inches drawn from the album, beside Ningessera and Sambatic. The mixed album keeps 5:13 of the flow, transitions included.

## Artistic Context

Masomenos are Joan Costes, a graphic designer and DJ, and the producer and director Adrien de Maublanc, from Paris. Their name comes from the Spanish más o menos, more or less, which they heard on a trip to Mexico. Their albums of those years were titled Costes présente; Joan Costes is the daughter of the founder of the Hôtel Costes in Paris, whose own compilation series carried records such as {{ <track_link source="gabin-doo-uap-doo-uap-doo-uap.md" /> }}. Beside the music they make installations that join image, sound and light.
