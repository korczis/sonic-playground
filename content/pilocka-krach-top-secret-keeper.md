## Genre and Mood

**Pilocka Krach** made **Top Secret Keeper** the second track of an album her critics heard as full of humour: techno and electronic music with a synth bass her label calls aggressive. The feelings are playful and gritty, the play of a song about codes and the grit of that bass.

## Song Summary

The press text of her label Monika Enterprise says the track takes up the baton from the opener, Oh Yeah Voll Krass, and sets itself inside a game that plays with codes; it predates the current preoccupation with whistleblowers, the text adds, and is therefore prophetically great. Its sound: an aggressive synth bass and early disco acid vocal-synth snippets. The credits name Deborah Spitz as singer. No source sets out the lyric, so this page does not paraphrase it.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} is the lowest melodic line of a track, and in electronic music it often does the work a riff does in rock. Here it is a synthesizer, and the label's adjective, aggressive, asks you to listen to its attack and grain as much as to its notes.

Best Of is not a {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }} in the usual sense, though it is named like one. It was Pilocka Krach's first album, and its credits say she wrote and produced all eleven tracks at Pank Angeles Studios in Berlin between 2009 and 2014. Three had come out before on limited 7-inch singles and one on an EP; the label names no earlier release for Top Secret Keeper, so on the sources this album is where it first appeared. DMC World's Ben Hogwood called it brave to call a first album Best Of.

## Artistic Context

Pilocka Krach was born in Berlin-Köpenick, grew up in Berlin-Mitte and releases on her own label, Greatest Hits International. Best Of came out on 22 May 2015 on Monika Enterprise, mastered by Mike Grinser at Manmade Mastering, with artwork by Ian Anderson of The Designers Republic. The track before it is {{ <track_link source="pilocka-krach-lian-ferdinand-oh-yeah-voll-krass-feat-lian-ferdinand.md" /> }}, whose baton, the label says, this one takes up; the same album holds {{ <track_link source="pilocka-krach-my-name-is-fame.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Groove's Christin Bolte heard live hits gathered on one record, from an artist who turns every song into a hit, and music rooted in provocation rather than theory. Hogwood gave the album four out of five and heard a looser, lo-fi approach that makes some tracks sound more human. HHV Mag's Kristoffer Cornils found her over-the-top manner more effective in short formats than over a whole LP. At almost five minutes it is one of the longer tracks; play it loud and listen for the code.
