## Genre and Mood

**Dr. Dre**'s **Ackrite** is West Coast hip-hop from the far end of his 1999 album 2001, a rap track that Okayplayer heard as playful, threatening and melodic all at once. The mood moves between those words, light on its feet and dark in what it says.

## Song Summary

Ackrite is the twentieth of 23 tracks on 2001, credited to Dr. Dre featuring Hittman and running 3:39. It was written by Andre R. Young, Melvin Charles Bradford and Brian Bailey and produced by Dr. Dre and Mel-Man, with Dre and Richard "Segal" Huredia credited as mixing engineers. The title is the phrase "act right" as it is spoken, and Edge Induced Cohesion reads the lyric as a call to women to act right, backed by threats of violence toward women who are not willing. That subject is the reason this is not an easy listen.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes the beat of Ackrite in detail or gives times, so this part describes how 2001 was made and leaves the details of this track to your ears.

### The Beat

Time's Josh Tyrangiel described every Dre track as beginning with Dre behind a drum machine: he programs a beat, asks the musicians in the room to play along, and refines what he likes. On 2001, Scott Storch recalled, he played keyboards, Mike Elizondo bass guitar and Dre the drum machine. The album used fewer samples than The Chronic, and its sample credits list none for Ackrite.

### The Verses

Hittman carries the track. Okayplayer's Peter A. Berry wrote that he did his thing here, even this late in the album, and called him an underrated figure in the whole story of 2001.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} is usually a guest. On 2001, Hittman is closer to a regular: Wikipedia lists him among the artists with several appearances, and Berry admitted he is on the album far more than he remembered.

{{ <concept_link name="rapping" /> }} is rhythm and pitch as much as words. Berry's three adjectives describe a delivery: playful in its timing, melodic in its line, threatening in its content. Listen for how a flow can sound easy while the lyric is anything but.

## Artistic Context

2001, Dr. Dre's second solo album, came out on 16 November 1999 on Aftermath and Interscope, seven years after The Chronic. Produced mainly by Dre and Mel-Man, it expanded The Chronic's G-funk with sparser beats; AllMusic's Stephen Thomas Erlewine heard ominous strings, soulful vocals and reggae, and called the album sonically first-rate. Dre told The New York Times he planned it like a film, entertainment first, with a touch of dark comedy. Hittman is also among those noted as having written lyrics Dre used on the album.

## Impact and Reception

Ackrite was not released as a single, and critics treat it as a deep cut. Okayplayer's 2024 ranking placed it 14th among the album's songs. In Soul In Stereo's 1999 Rewind conversation, published in 2024, both writers ranked it 16th of 17, though one of them called Hittman the unquestioned MVP of the project. Hear it in the album's late run, after Housewife and before Bang Bang.
