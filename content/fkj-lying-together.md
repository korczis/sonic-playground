## Genre and Mood

**FKJ** introduced himself with **Lying Together**, a track that mixes deep house, electronic production and funk warmth. Its mood is romantic, groovy and chill, a slow-burning piece of music that sounds like a lazy, sunlit afternoon. There is nothing aggressive here; even the beat feels like it is lounging.

## Song Summary

"Lying Together" is built from a small set of warm elements: chopped vocal fragments, filtered chords, a soft but steady beat and an elastic bassline. FKJ loops and layers them with the patience of a musician who plays every instrument himself, and the result feels intimate rather than club-sized. The memorable detail is the way the vocal snippets drift in and out, like overheard words from a conversation in the next room. The groove sways rather than pounds, and the sound has the slightly worn, analogue texture of a record played on a good system in a small room. It is house music built for headphones and late breakfasts as much as for clubs.

## Musical Breakdown

The track unfolds gently over four and a half minutes, building its groove one layer at a time.

### Intro

Filtered chords and a soft beat open the track, warm and slightly hazy, setting the relaxed tempo.

### Build-up

The bassline joins, rounded and funky, and percussion fills out the groove as the filter slowly opens.

### Main Section

Chopped vocal fragments and the full chord progression take over, the groove settling into a warm, hypnotic loop.

### Bridge

A breakdown pulls back the beat, leaving the chords and voices floating before the groove returns.

### Outro

The layers fade back to the filtered chords, ending the track as softly as it began.

## Artistic Context

FKJ, short for French Kiwi Juice, is the alias of French multi-instrumentalist Vincent Fenton, who plays guitar, bass, keys and saxophone and is known for building tracks live, layer by layer. "Lying Together" was released on the Paris label Roche Musique, which describes it as the neo-house track that first revealed him to the public. Roche Musique's catalogue of French producers mixing house, funk and soul gave him a natural home, and his later records and live shows developed the same blend into something more ambitious, often with him moving between instruments on stage.

## Impact and Reception

The track helped establish FKJ and the Roche Musique sound, a blend of French house, funk and soul, and it remains a staple of his live sets years later. It earned its place here for its warmth and intimacy. Put it on when you want to slow everything down.
