## Genre and Mood

**Gorgon City** and **Erik Hassle** made **FTPA** the after-hours corner of an album built for the floor. It leans from deep house towards R&B and electronic slow-burn: sultry, longing and darker than its neighbours. Complex heard nods to Balearic house, R&B and 1990s dance-pop, and EarMilk called it the duo's try at a jazzy R&B record.

## Song Summary

FTPA is the fifth track on Sirens, the 2014 debut album of the North London duo Kye Gibbon and Matt Robson-Scott. Complex reported that Hassle co-wrote it while on tour in Los Angeles. Two reviewers connected its title to Peaches' song Fuck the Pain Away: Skiddle said it shares an admittedly softened version of the name but not its jerky sexuality, while God Is In The TV called it a seedy, somewhat claustrophobic take on it. To Skiddle, Hassle's voice added a darker undercurrent to the theme of love that dominates the album, in a track that sounded like the despair of a break-up.

## Musical Breakdown

No source maps the track bar by bar; these are the parts reviewers described.

### Intro and Groove

The arrangement is minimalist. Complex heard a devouring sub-bass under gliding piano chords that sprawl lazily across the track, and Skiddle added strings and shuffling drums.

### The Vocal

Hassle's voice is the centre: yearning, for Complex; Skiddle heard an icy drawl that reminded it of Frank Ocean.

### The Drop

The West Review singled out a tremendously measured drop and a drum pattern it compared to I See You Baby, saying the track does not necessarily conform to typical house.

### Outro

Towards the end, the same review noticed toots from a trumpet.

## Concepts in Focus

FTPA shows what a {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} can change. Gorgon City built Sirens around guest singers, and the Swedish singer-songwriter Hassle, who has also co-written for Shakira and Rihanna, here pulls the duo away from pop house towards something closer to R&B.

The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} works mostly below melody: a sub-bass felt as much as heard, which is why the sparse arrangement still hits hard.

And the {{ <concept_link name="drop" /> }} is the opposite of a festival release. It arrives measured, so the track keeps its mood instead of breaking it. Compare the album's opening track, {{ <track_link source="gorgon-city-coming-home.md" /> }}, which Complex heard without a big build or heavy drop at all.

## Artistic Context

Sirens came out on 6 October 2014 on Virgin EMI and Black Butter and reached number 10 on the UK Albums Chart. Its singles, Ready for Your Love with MNEK and Here for You with Laura Welsh, were top-ten hits in the UK; FTPA was an album track. The credits list Gibbon and Robson-Scott as producers, mixing engineers and drum programmers, with Hassle, Daniel Ledinsky and Jonny Coffer among the writers.

## Impact and Reception

Opinion split. Skiddle called it easily the album's high point, with a hint of Massive Attack; The West Review counted it among the best tracks; aAh! Magazine named it the worst song, a good one that is much slower than the rest of an up-tempo album and feels misplaced. Play it late, when the room has thinned out.
