## Genre and Mood

**Em Harriss** and **Dials** made **Are Things Still Burning** a small song about an enormous loss. It belongs to indie pop and to the singer-songwriter tradition: a first-person story told through ordinary details. The feelings are longing and melancholy, the homesickness of someone who may have no home to go back to.

## Song Summary

The narrator is far from Earth, up in a fortress, as the last lines reveal. She cannot imagine life on Earth now, and instead of asking about the catastrophe she asks about her bedroom and the tree she planted in the backyard. A few days before the single came out, Harriss posted a short acoustic original of the song on her YouTube channel and explained that it was episode three of her first acted science-fiction podcast, a mix of stories and music. The released version, distributed through CD Baby on 25 February 2016, is credited to Em Harriss and Dials; her own upload of it credits Diamond in a Lotus. No source I found says how the two names relate, and no review describes the production, so this review stays with the song's words and form.

## Musical Breakdown

### Verse 1

The opening lines set the distance: Earth is unimaginable now, and the questions are domestic, a bedroom and a backyard tree.

### Chorus

The title becomes the refrain, a question repeated rather than answered: are things still burning?

### Verse 2

Memory takes over. A mother kills black widows with an old broom and makes the neighbours laugh; the narrator lies in the blossoms when the sky goes black.

### Outro

The last lines reveal where she is, a cold fortress like a prison cell with bad lighting, and turn the question into a wish to go home.

## Concepts in Focus

The song is a clear case of {{ <concept_link name="verse-chorus form" /> }} used for storytelling. The lyric posted with the official upload marks the chorus between two verses: each verse brings new images, the chorus brings the same question back, and the repetition becomes the narrator's anxiety.

It also lives as an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }}. The acoustic original lasts under a minute and a half; the single runs past three minutes with a collaborator credited beside the singer. Play both and listen to what the full version adds around the same words.

## Artistic Context

Soundcharts describes Em Harriss as an Oregon native, a songwriter, vocalist and multi-instrumentalist whose music draws on lo-fi, pop and indie; her SoundCloud page calls her a West Coast singer-songwriter from Los Angeles. Diamond in a Lotus, whose SoundCloud tagline is Future Everything, worked with her again in 2025 on Hold On To Me.

## Impact and Reception

The song has no press history that I could find; Soundcharts lists it among her top songs. Its strength is its scale: the end of the world told through a bedroom, a tree and a broom. Listen for those three things.
