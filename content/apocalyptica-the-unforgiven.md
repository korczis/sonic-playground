## Genre and Mood

**Apocalyptica** play **The Unforgiven** as four cellists and nothing else: no singer, no drum kit, no amplifier. It sits where neoclassical chamber music meets symphonic metal, a cover that keeps the darkness of Metallica's ballad and loses most of its anger. Teen Ink found it mellow and sad, and that is the mood: melancholic rather than furious.

## Song Summary

The Unforgiven was the second single from Metallica's 1991 album Metallica, credited to James Hetfield, Lars Ulrich and Kirk Hammett, with Hetfield's lyric about one man's struggle against those who would subdue him. Ulrich described it as an attempt at a ballad with the dynamics reversed: heavy, distorted verses and a softer, melodic chorus on clean electric and acoustic guitars. Apocalyptica recorded it as the fourth of eight covers on their 1996 debut, where it runs 5:23 against Metallica's 6:27. This page plays that 1996 performance in the album's 2016 remaster.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} can keep every note and change every sound. The Unforgiven tests the idea well, because its original depends on a contrast of sound: distortion in the verses, clean guitars in the chorus.

That contrast becomes a question of {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }}. On four cellos the arranger cannot switch a pedal; heaviness and softness have to come from how hard and how low the players bow, and from who plays what. The Green Man Review's division of roles is the starting point: listen for which cello is the singer and which the bass.

As an {{ <concept_link name="instrumental" /> }}, the cover drops Hetfield's words and keeps the shape of his melody, which may be why it reaches listeners as sad rather than angry.

Finally, {{ <concept_link name="remastering" /> }}: the 2016 edition gave the 1996 recordings a new master, which Reflections of Darkness heard as cleaner, with the details brought forward.

## Artistic Context

Apocalyptica formed in 1993 at Helsinki's Sibelius Academy: Eicca Toppinen, Paavo Lötjönen, Max Lilja and Antero Manninen, a cello quartet that began by playing Metallica. Mercury released Plays Metallica by Four Cellos on 10 May 1996, produced by Pekka Ritualuoto. The remaster followed on 22 July 2016, issued by Harmageddon Records under licence to OMN Label Services, with three bonus tracks.

## Impact and Reception

Reviewers singled this one out. The Green Man Review called The Unforgiven and Creeping Death incredible, and in Louder Stephen Dalton heard it, with Wherever I May Roam, carry Metallica's doom-laden western fantasies into the salons of central Europe. Metallica's single had reached number four in Finland, the quartet's home. Hear the original, then this, and listen for where the anger went.
