## Genre and Mood

**Franco**'s **Rock Enredo** puts its argument in its title: rock, and the enredo, the theme song of a samba school's parade. It is samba rock, the Brazilian blend of samba with rock, soul and funk, playful in its premise, groovy in the dance music it belongs to and, by one critic's ear, bold in its sound. Beyond that critic, we found no description of the record, and this page keeps to what is documented.

## Song Summary

Continental released Rock Enredo in 1976 as the A-side of a 7-inch single, with Meu Idioma É Samba, "my language is samba", on the B-side. Farofafá's guide to samba rock credits it to São Beto and Voltaire and calls it a manifesto fusing samba and rock, hesitant in its words but bold in its sound. The lyric, as the guide quotes it, wonders whether samba is giving way to rock and jokes about electrifying the samba school's percussion. Continental pressed it again in 1980 on a four-song 7-inch with Guitarreiro and Black Samba. Atração's Samba Rock Vol.1, released on 1 October 2007, collects it as its eighth track at 2:54; whether that is the 1976 take is not documented.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="samba-enredo" /> }} is the song a samba school writes each year for its carnival theme, sung by a lead voice and the marching school over a huge percussion section. Naming a song Rock Enredo imagines that parade with electric instruments, which is the joke and the manifesto at once.

{{ <concept_link name="syncopation" /> }}, accents between the beats, is what samba is built on; rock leans on a steadier backbeat.

The {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }} kept the record alive: Samba Rock Vol.1 places it four tracks after Franco's {{ <track_link source="franco-o-rock-do-rato.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Discogs identifies this Franco as Franco Scornavacca, a Brazilian rock and samba rock singer who played in the group Os Brasas, and dates his death to 15 September 2018 in São Paulo. The sources disagree on his birth: Discogs gives 1948 in Paraná, while Farofafá calls him Italian-born and settled in Rio Grande do Sul, a samba rock pioneer whose early recordings include Carolina, from 1970. After a spell of traditional samba, Farofafá writes, Rock Enredo was his return. He later managed the duo Zezé di Camargo & Luciano, Farofafá adds, and was the father and manager of the members of the pop trio KLB.

## Impact and Reception

We found no chart record. What Farofafá does record is the song's long life: of the single's two sides, Rock Enredo is the one that survived at underground samba rock dances. The compilation's upload sits on a YouTube channel for the Congolese bandleader Franco, and MusicBrainz credits the recording to him, so this page carries no video. Listen past the mix-up to the parade.
