## Genre and Mood

**La Roux**'s **Reflections Are Protection** comes from the deep end of the duo's debut album, synth-pop and electro-pop built from the same 1980s-minded palette as the hits around it. The mood the critics heard on that record was cool and melancholic, a voice held at a distance over machines, and nostalgic by design: its reviews kept naming the 1980s bands it drew on.

## Song Summary

The song is the album's tenth track, 4:19 long, written by Elly Jackson and Ben Langmaid and produced by the two of them. It was not released as a single, and no review, interview or annotation found for this page describes its lyric or its arrangement. What can be said is where it was made and by whom: it was recorded at Westpoint in London, engineered by Ian Sherwin and mixed by Serban Ghenea. The rest is for the listener to hear.

### The Album Around It

Resident Advisor called the record the finest and most tuneful synth-pop album of the year, packed with three-minute electro nuggets, and singled out how often the songwriting struck on practically every track. Hot Press heard vintage 1980s beats and a singer who sounds like a robot who has just had its heart broken.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="deep cut" /> }} is an album track that was never a single. On an album whose singles included In for the Kill and the number one Bulletproof, a song like this one is heard mostly by people who play the record through, and that changes how it is heard: against the singles, as part of a sequence.

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} is the album's main instrument, and critics heard it as deliberately old-fashioned: Resident Advisor compared its synths to mid-career Human League, Hot Press to the Human League and Depeche Mode, and Pitchfork called the result icy but irresistible throwback pop.

The credits also show a division of labour typical of big pop records. Jackson and Langmaid wrote and produced, but the final {{ <concept_link name="mixing" /> }} went to Serban Ghenea, a specialist engineer brought in to balance the parts into the finished stereo track, assisted by John Hanes and Tim Roberts.

## Artistic Context

La Roux were Elly Jackson, the singer whose red hair gave the act its name, and the producer Ben Langmaid, who had started out together as an acoustic duo before turning to electronic music. Their debut, La Roux, came out on Polydor on 26 June 2009, reached number two in the UK and was shortlisted for the Mercury Prize. Langmaid left in 2012, and Jackson has carried the name since.
