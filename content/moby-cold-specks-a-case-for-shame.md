## Genre and Mood

**Moby** and **Cold Specks** made **A Case for Shame** an elegiac song, in Hot Press's word: electronic and downtempo in its make-up, soul in its voice. It is melancholic and soulful, and its haunting quality is what NPR heard as Moby's warm-but-dark sensibility meeting an idiosyncratic voice.

## Song Summary

The song pairs Moby's electronics with Cold Specks, the stage name of the Somali Canadian singer-songwriter Ladan Hussein, whose own music has been described as doom-soul. The Quietus heard a velvety groove swathed in ambient keys, luminous harmonies and Cold Specks' signature bluesy drawl. It is credited to Moby and Hussein and was produced by Mark "Spike" Stent, and at six minutes it takes its time. The lyric's subject is in the title; no source quoted here interprets the words beyond it.

### Main Section

The groove is velvety, in The Quietus's word, and swathed in ambient keys; Hot Press grouped the song with the album's elegiac tracks, with their synth washes, plaintive piano and dreamy ambience.

### Vocal

Over that bed, the voice is the centre. NPR heard soulful and vulnerable contributions from Cold Specks, her voice's idiosyncratic beauty meshing with Moby's warm-but-dark sensibilities.

## Concepts in Focus

As the {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}, Cold Specks gives the song its voice and shares its writing credit. Moby heard her 2012 debut, I Predict a Graceful Expulsion, through their shared label, Mute, and invited her to sing; the two recorded this song and Tell Me in November 2012, while she was touring the UK. She called the session very free, collaborative and creative.

{{ <concept_link name="ambient texture" /> }} is the song's ground: the ambient keys and synth washes the reviews describe set an atmosphere rather than drive a beat.

The song also exists in an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }}. Moby made a quieter remix himself, the Under the Manhattan Bridge Version, and offered it as a free download from his website; the single's releases add an All We Ever Wanted Version and remixes by Thom Alt-J, Honor Detroit, Spartaque and Sharam Jey.

## Artistic Context

Moby recorded Innocents, his eleventh studio album, in his apartment between January 2012 and June 2013, working for the first time with a co-producer, Stent. Seven of its twelve tracks are collaborations, with Wayne Coyne, Mark Lanegan, Damien Jurado and Skylar Grey among the guests. Little Idiot and Mute released it on 1 October 2013; A Case for Shame, its first official single, came out on 1 July.

## Impact and Reception

Innocents drew mixed reviews, with a Metacritic score of 60, but Sputnikmusic picked A Case for Shame as one of its essential cuts. Moby directed the video himself in a pool at his Los Angeles home, an afterlife of people hiding because of shame; the video uses a shorter cut, while the player above holds the full album version.
