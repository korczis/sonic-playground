## Genre and Mood

**Red Hot Chili Peppers** recorded **Show Me Your Soul** for the film Pretty Woman and made it funk rock. Wikipedia files it under that genre, and the band's own story explains its energy: it replaced a song judged too slow for the film. No published review describes the recording in detail, so the feelings here, energetic and groovy, rest on that classification and that story rather than on a critic's ear.

## Song Summary

The song was made to order. In a late-1989 interview with Thrust, John Frusciante said it was written in two hours, because the song first written for the scene in Pretty Woman was rejected as too slow. The band recorded it in November 1989 during a break in their Mother's Milk tour, not in the album's sessions as is often assumed, with Norwood Fisher of Fishbone producing alongside them and Billy Preston on keyboards. It closes the Pretty Woman soundtrack, released by EMI on 13 March 1990.

## Musical Breakdown

No source maps Show Me Your Soul section by section, so this breakdown follows the players the credits name rather than a verse-and-chorus map.

### The Rhythm Section

Flea on bass and Chad Smith on drums carry the funk that gives the song its classification; Frusciante plays guitar.

### The Voices

Anthony Kiedis sings lead, and Flea and Frusciante are both credited with backing vocals.

### The Guest

Billy Preston plays keys, a guest who would return to the band's records fifteen years later on Warlocks.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="soundtrack" /> }} song is written for a scene before it is written for a record, and this one shows the pressure: a first attempt failed, the second was finished in two hours. The soundtrack sold: the Pretty Woman album reached number four on the Billboard 200 and went triple platinum in the US.

It was also a {{ <concept_link name="b-side" /> }}: in the US and the UK it backed the single Taste the Pain, and in Australia a belated release of Knock Me Down. A promotional single was issued too, and the song reached number ten on Billboard's Modern Rock Tracks chart.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Bill Stobaugh and released on 14 February 1990, shows the band in front of a bluescreen. It later turned up on Beavis and Butt-Head, and in 2024 the band re-released it on their Vevo channel, the version on this page.

## Artistic Context

In 1989 the line-up was new: Frusciante and Smith had joined after the death of Hillel Slovak and the departure of Jack Irons, and Mother's Milk, released in August 1989, was their first album together. When the band left EMI for Warner, EMI's 1992 compilation What Hits!? became the only full-length album of theirs to include Show Me Your Soul, as track eleven.

## Impact and Reception

The band never played the song live; they mimed it once, on the television special Save the Planet on 4 April 1990. Its life has been on radio, on a best-selling soundtrack and on one greatest-hits album. Put it on and hear what two hours of writing produced.
