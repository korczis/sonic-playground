## Genre and Mood

**Hindi Zahra**'s **Don't Forget (Remastered Version)** is a quiet, swaying song from an album critics heard as a jazz-folk hybrid with roots far beyond France: Moroccan folk songs, gypsy guitar, the bluesy side of jazz. The feelings are longing and warmth, a plea made gently rather than desperately.

## Song Summary

Don't Forget is the tenth of the eleven songs on Handmade, Zahra's 2010 debut on Blue Note, where it runs 3:47. Its title is its message: a request not to be forgotten. M Music & Musicians heard Zahra on this album as a teacher of less experienced lovers, with At the Same Time teaching cruelty and Don't Forget tenderness. This remastered version, issued on Oursoul Records in February 2011, runs a second longer.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes this song part by part or gives times, so this breakdown follows what the reviews say about it and about the album around it.

### Main Section

The BBC's Colin Irwin called the song lilting and heard in it a touch of the McGarrigles, the Canadian folk sisters Kate and Anna. M Music & Musicians noted that Zahra keeps the album's instruments acoustic and its tempos breezy. Irwin also heard her voice recall Lena Horne, Billie Holiday and Astrud Gilberto across the record.

### Into the Album's Close

Don't Forget leads into Old Friends, the album's last song. Blogger Hardie K heard the pair reveal a side of Zahra hidden until then: slower but not moody, in Hardie K's words, trippy and dreamy instead.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="remastering" /> }} explains the title. A remaster prepares a finished recording again at the final stage, adjusting loudness, tone and dynamics, while the performance stays the same. This is the 2010 recording with a new polish, not a new take.

The {{ <concept_link name="unplugged" /> }} version is the instructive comparison. The 2011 deluxe edition of Handmade added six stripped-down recordings, Don't Forget among them; that take runs about 2:40, more than a minute shorter. Hear how the song survives without its studio frame, as {{ <track_link source="hindi-zahra-beautiful-tango-unplugged.md" /> }} does with the album's opener.

## Artistic Context

Zahra was born in Khouribga, Morocco, in 1979 and moved to Paris at fifteen. She is a self-taught multi-instrumentalist who sings mostly in English and sometimes in Shilha. Irwin found the album's production overly sophisticated, a Norah Jones-style supper-club setting, but praised its many-coloured influences, from Moroccan folk songs to New York bars.

## Impact and Reception

Critics noticed the song even in passing. Time Out Chicago's 2011 preview, glowing about Zahra, named her plea not to be forgotten the silliest thing she does. Irwin called Handmade a hugely likeable and frequently transfixing album, and it went gold in France.
