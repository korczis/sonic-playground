## Genre and Mood

**Gorillaz**' **Phoner to Arizona** opens The Fall without a song in the usual sense. Wikipedia files it as techno; it is electronic, experimental and mostly instrumental, groovy enough for Vulture to call it funky as hell, and playful in the way PopMatters heard the whole album as impulsive work-shopping.

## Song Summary

Damon Albarn recorded the track in Montreal on 3 October 2010, on the first day of the album's recording, during the North American leg of the Escape to Plastic Beach tour, entirely on his iPad. It is mostly instrumental, with distorted vocals; Albarn wrote it, and Stephen Sedgwick recorded and mixed it. The video on this page, posted by the band on 22 December 2010, runs about half a minute longer than the 4:14 album track, so it is not the exact album recording.

## Musical Breakdown

No source used here describes the track part by part, so this follows what reviewers heard in it as a whole.

### Intro

How the track opens is not described in the sources.

### Main Section

Luke Turner of The Quietus heard a hooted refrain, seemingly from a Digital Owl app, over thick beats and treated vocals. Amos Barshad of Vulture heard synth burbles and keyboard noodles; Dylan Nelson of PopMatters, slow hip-hop beats that plod forward with the soothing aimlessness of a long bus ride. The album's credits list Albarn's synthesizers on most tracks, but not on this one.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="lo-fi" /> }} names music that keeps the marks of modest means, and The Fall is filed under it. Albarn recorded most tracks in a single day, without later overdubs, and called the album "a kind of diary of my experience in America".

{{ <concept_link name="distortion" /> }} here works on the voice. The vocals are distorted, treated in Turner's word, on a track that is otherwise mostly instrumental; listen for how much of the voice is still words.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} came before the album: a compilation of footage and images from the tour and the band's current phase, which Vulture called a mesmerizing fast cut of tour scenes, the cartoon characters and old-timey planes.

## Artistic Context

Albarn made The Fall over 32 days in October and November 2010, mostly alone, with few guests; many titles name the places where the tracks were recorded, and {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-amarillo.md" /> }} was made in Texas on 23 October. The album was streamed free and given to fan-club members on 25 December 2010, and Parlophone released it physically on 19 April 2011.

## Impact and Reception

Consequence of Sound previewed eighteen seconds of the track and called it the album's first single; the physical release was led instead by Revolving Doors and Amarillo. Turner called the track a deft first stroke. The Fall drew mixed reviews and peaked at 12 in the UK. Start the album here, then compare {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-el-manana.md" /> }}.
