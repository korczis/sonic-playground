## Genre and Mood

**Gorillaz**' **Amarillo** is trip-hop and electronic music at its quietest: a slow song that critics nevertheless heard as euphoric, dreamy in its reverb and melancholic in the way The Fall as a whole drifts. The cartoon band is almost absent; what remains is Damon Albarn, a few machines and a guitar.

## Song Summary

Amarillo was recorded in Amarillo, Texas, on 23 October 2010; the word is Spanish for yellow. Albarn wrote it and is credited with vocals, synthesizer and vocoder, and Mick Jones of The Clash plays guitar. NME, as Wikipedia quotes it, called it a slow yet euphoric song in which Albarn's reverb-laden voice evocatively recalls America's vast interior, and one of the more fully formed songs on the album.

### Main Section

The voice carries the song, wet with reverb in NME's hearing, alongside Albarn's synthesizers and vocoder and Jones's guitar. ## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="vocoder" /> }} imposes the shape of a voice on a synthesizer's sound. The Fall's sessions used a Korg vocoder, and the album credits Albarn with vocoder on this tenth track and the two around it, The Snake in Dallas and The Speak It Mountains; listen for whether the voice passes through it.

{{ <concept_link name="reverb" /> }} is the sound of a space, real or simulated, around a voice. NME's word for Albarn's voice here was reverb-laden, in a song it heard as a British songwriter taking in one of the world's largest open spaces; the echo can stand in for that distance.

## Artistic Context

Albarn made The Fall on an iPad during the North American leg of the Escape to Plastic Beach tour, over 32 days in October and November 2010. Most tracks were written and recorded in a single day without later overdubs, and Albarn described the album as "a kind of diary of my experience in America"; many titles name the places they were made. It went out free to stream and to fan-club members on 25 December 2010, and Parlophone released it physically on 19 April 2011.

## Impact and Reception

The Fall drew mixed reviews, 67 on Metacritic, and peaked at 12 in the UK; Kara called it more like a Damon Albarn solo album than anything. Amarillo was singled out: Kara counted its lilt among the most beautiful songs Albarn has recorded, and Erik Burg of Beats Per Minute heard in it Albarn's skill as a producer. It was released on 14 March 2011 as a double A-side with Revolving Doors. Play it after {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-de-la-soul-feel-good-inc.md" /> }} and hear how small Gorillaz could become.
