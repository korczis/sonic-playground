## Genre and Mood

**Gorillaz** gave the tenth track of Demon Days, **All Alone**, to two guests and let them pull it in opposite directions. **Roots Manuva** makes it gritty hip-hop; **Martina Topley-Bird** carries it somewhere ethereal. Around them sits the album's darkness, and the song travels from one mood to the other in about three and a half minutes.

## Song Summary

PopMatters called it the most impressive moment on the album: a track that starts as a dirty, skittery hip-hop joint and turns into an ethereal cloud of lovely. Grammy.com hears Roots Manuva's verse as an attack on those who manipulate the Bible's teachings for their own ill-gotten gains. The backing was partly recorded in Africa, and the two guests also recorded another song for the album, Snakes and Ladders, that was left off.

## Musical Breakdown

No source gives times or a section-by-section map, so these notes follow the reviews and the credits.

### Verse

The rap comes first, over a beat PopMatters called dirty and skittery. The NZ Herald heard Roots Manuva's flow as fluid and fiery; Grammy.com labelled the mix of styles a ska-grime fusion.

### Arrangement

Simon Tong plays guitar, and a small string section, two violins, two violas, a cello and two double basses, is credited on this track.

### Coda

The ending changes the weather. Martina Topley-Bird's voice takes over, and the hip-hop track becomes, in PopMatters' words, a lovely coda that proved Gorillaz could still surprise on an album full of surprises.

## Concepts in Focus

Gorillaz are a cartoon band built on the {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}: Damon Albarn and Jamie Hewlett's project keeps its fictional members on screen and invites real voices into the songs. Here both guests are credited, and Roots Manuva, under his real name Rodney Smith, is also a co-writer.

{{ <concept_link name="rapping" text="Rapping" /> }} is rhythmic speech over a beat, and critics judge it by flow, the way the words ride the rhythm. The NZ Herald's verdict is about exactly that: a fluid and fiery flow.

A {{ <concept_link name="coda" /> }} is a closing passage after a song's main material. Most codas wind down; this one changes the song's mood from street-level grit to something airy. Compare the album's own ending, {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-demon-days.md" /> }}, where a choir lifts the darkness.

## Artistic Context

Demon Days, released by Parlophone on 23 May 2005 in the UK, was produced with Danger Mouse; Albarn described it as a loose concept album about the world in a state of night. Roots Manuva, Rodney Smith, grew up around Stockwell, London, and released his debut album Brand New Second Hand on Big Dada in 1999. Topley-Bird first came to attention as the featured voice on Tricky's Maxinquaye, and her solo album Quixotic was shortlisted for the 2003 Mercury Prize.

## Impact and Reception

All Alone was never a single, but critics picked it out: Robert Christgau named it one of the album's highlights alongside DARE. Listen for the moment the rap gives way to the singing.
