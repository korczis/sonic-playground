## Genre and Mood

**Lee Moses** cut **Bad Girl (Pt. 1)** in 1967, and it is deep soul, the rough and powerful end of 1960s R&B that his singing came to stand for. The feelings are raw, intense and full of longing. The Spanish label Vampisoul, reissuing it, called it raw, passionate and incredibly moving, soul screaming at its very best.

## Song Summary

The song is about falling for a woman everyone warns you about, as The Indy Review put it. It was produced by Johnny Brantley and released by Musicor as a single split in two, Part I on one side and Part II on the other. The reissue notes call it Moses's own composition; a later digital release credits L. Moses and B. Fears. Part 1 runs about two and a half minutes.

## Musical Breakdown

No source times the record, but several listeners describe its shape.

### The Slow Start

The reissue notes describe a slow build-up: the song gathers itself before it lets go.

### The Band

Horns and guitars accompany Moses's wailing, in The Indy Review's description, and a reader of the Burning Wood blog remembered the drums above all: every beat is just a crash.

### The Release

After the build comes what Vampisoul called a torrent of passionate, raw emotion delivered with throat-shredding vocals. The blogger Sal Nunziato heard a pleading screamer of soul.

### The Turn of the Record

Part 1 ends where the A-side ends. The song continues on the B-side, and fans have since stitched the two halves together into one version.

## Concepts in Focus

The record is built on a {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }}, but not the kind electronic music uses. Nobody describes anything filtered or layered in; the build belongs to the singer, a slow start and then a voice pushed until restraint gives way.

The single also shows an older use of the {{ <concept_link name="b-side" /> }}. Instead of pairing two songs, Musicor put one song on both faces, so a listener had to turn the record over to hear how it went on.

Behind it is a {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} who shaped much of Moses's recorded work. Johnny Brantley co-wrote and produced his first single, My Adorable One, in 1965, produced Bad Girl, and produced his only album, Time and Place, in 1971.

## Artistic Context

Vincent Lee Moses was born in Atlanta in 1941 and led the Showstoppers, once the house band at the Royal Peacock club, before moving to New York in the mid-1960s to work as a session musician. His guitar playing was called funky and compared to Jimi Hendrix, a contemporary on the same club scene. His three Musicor singles of 1967 all failed commercially. Time and Place, released on Maple in 1971 with members of the Ohio Players, failed too; Moses went home to Atlanta, played locally, never recorded again, and died in 1998.

## Impact and Reception

The record's afterlife has been longer than its life. Castle Music gathered his recordings in 2007, Git It Records reissued the two parts in 2010, Vampisoul pressed them again as a 45, Colemine Records collected his singles and rarities in 2019, and a 2020 documentary took the title Time and Place. The Indy Review called Bad Girl a near perfect track. Play Part 1, then turn it over.
