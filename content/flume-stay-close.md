## Genre and Mood

**Flume** placed **Stay Close** sixth on his 2012 debut album, between On Top and Insane. Wikipedia files the album under wonky, electronica, experimental and downtempo; this page names electronic, downtempo and future bass, the term the catalogue already uses for Flume's Holdin On. The feelings, sultry and warm, come from two reviews: XLR8R heard the track as glowing, PopMatters as a sexy number.

## Song Summary

Stay Close runs 2:56 and is credited to Harley Streten alone. That makes it unusual on an album built around guests: Chet Faker, Jezzabell Doran, T.Shirt and Moon Holiday each appear on a track nearby, and Holdin On is built on a sample. Here no voice and no sample is credited, so what you hear is the producer's own work, made, like the whole album, on the first laptop Streten ever bought.

## Musical Breakdown

No review walks through Stay Close in order, and this page does not invent a structure for it. What the sources give are impressions of the whole.

### Main Section

XLR8R's Patric Fallon called it glowing and beat-driven, a track that shows off Streten's confidence in the studio, and heard in it a more mature, tasteful palette than the album mostly uses. PopMatters' Darryl G. Wright wrote that it bubbles up and turns out a sexy number that makes speakers wiggle seductively.

### The 2013 Version

On the 2013 deluxe edition, Stay Close returns at 2:44 with Boldy James, Alexander Spit and Aaron Cohen credited. The Line of Best Fit found that version a touch drab.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} shapes a recording from idea to finished track. In electronic music the producer is often the whole act, and Stay Close is that idea in miniature: one writer, one producer, one laptop.

The deluxe edition makes it an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }}. The original leaves the space open; the 2013 version fills it with voices, and the two side by side show how much a track's identity depends on what sits on top of the beat.

A {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} brings a voice and an audience. Flume's debut is full of them, and {{ <track_link source="flume-holdin-on.md" /> }} shows the other route, a borrowed soul voice cut into pieces.

## Artistic Context

Flume is Harley Streten, born in Sydney in 1991. He was signed in 2011 through an original-artists competition run by the Australian label Future Classic, whose founder Nathan McLay helped release his first EP, Sleepless; Future Classic released the album Flume on 9 November 2012. He finished it on a low-budget trip to London.

## Impact and Reception

The album debuted at number two on the ARIA chart, behind One Direction's Take Me Home, later reached number one, and won Australian Album of the Year at the 2013 J Awards; Metacritic averages its reviews at 73. Stay Close was not among its singles. Play it loud enough for the speakers to move.
