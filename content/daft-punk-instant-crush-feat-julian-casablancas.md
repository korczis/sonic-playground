## Genre and Mood

**Daft Punk** and **Julian Casablancas** made **Instant Crush** the Random Access Memories song that sounds most like a rock band: synth-pop and rock played by real drums, bass and guitars, with the singer of the Strokes pushed through a vocoder. The mood is melancholic and longing, a bittersweet look back at something that never happened.

## Song Summary

It began with a childhood memory of Daft Punk's, meeting a girl and missing the chance to be with her. Casablancas first wrote lyrics around it as Summer Crush, with help from Paul Williams, found them flat and improvised new lines instead. Musically it is two Daft Punk demos joined into one: Casablancas used one as the verse and the other as the chorus.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

No source we found describes the opening bars. The credits name the players: Nathan East on bass, John JR Robinson on drums, Quinn on percussion, Daft Punk on rhythm guitar, keyboards and synthesizers.

### Main Section

The verses carry the vocoder voice. At The Singles Jukebox, Madeleine Lee heard an Is This It outtake that is no knockoff, Jonathan Bradley a subdued echo of Digital Love; Edward Okulicz felt the song come alive in its dreamy chorus.

### Guitar Solo

Midway, Casablancas takes a guitar solo. Katherine St Asaph heard it release all the feeling the song had been building; Jer Fairall liked how spacey it is and wished it lasted longer.

### Outro

The album version runs 5:37, and Daniel Montesinos-Donaghy wrote that nearly six minutes felt like eight; the radio edit is about three and a half.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="vocoder" /> }} is the song's signature and its argument. The critic John Balfe found Casablancas's vocal substantially fed through one and still appropriately Strokes-ish; Katherine St Asaph found the voice barely audible over its processing. Guy-Manuel de Homem-Christo admitted it was not Casablancas's usual register and said that is what excited them.

As a {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}, Casablancas did far more than sing: he co-wrote, co-produced and played the {{ <concept_link name="guitar solo" /> }} himself. Compare Panda Bear on {{ <track_link source="daft-punk-doin-it-right-feat-panda-bear.md" /> }}, where the guest voice sits over machines alone.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Warren Fu, borrows from The Steadfast Tin Soldier and The Twilight Zone: two museum wax figures, a Napoleonic soldier who looks like Casablancas and a peasant woman, fall in love, are separated and melt together in a basement fire. Casablancas sings on a circular stage like the one in {{ <track_link source="daft-punk-around-the-world.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Daft Punk met Casablancas in 2010, while working on Tron: Legacy, through their friend Warren Fu, who also designed the album's artwork. Fans of the Strokes, they gave him an instrumental demo; recording ran until 2012. Columbia released it as the album's fourth single on 22 November 2013.

## Impact and Reception

Rolling Stone ranked it 58th among the best songs of 2013. It reached number four in France and 20 on Billboard's dance and electronic chart, went platinum in the US, Canada, Mexico and New Zealand, and Natalie Imbruglia covered it in 2015. Listen for the solo, then for what the vocoder hides.
