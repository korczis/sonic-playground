## Genre and Mood

**Ian Brown**'s **Eternal Flame** is an alternative-rock singer's song on an R&B frame. The beat comes from hip-hop, the strings from an orchestra, the voice from the Stone Roses. It is confident and relaxed at once, groovy rather than loud: NME heard swagger on the album, the New Zealand Herald laid-back funkiness.

## Song Summary

Eternal Flame is the fifth track of The World Is Yours, Brown's fifth solo album, released by Fiction and Polydor in 2007. The credits name Brown and Emile Haynie as writers; Haynie also produced and programmed it and co-mixed it with Tim Wills, and Simon Hale arranged the strings. It runs four minutes and sixteen seconds. Its one memorable comparison came from NME, which heard it a panther's whisker away from Family Affair, the Mary J. Blige single produced by Dr. Dre.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the song section by section or gives times, so this guide follows what the album's reviewers heard.

### The Beat

Brown likes hip-hop, NME wrote, so he brought in Emile, a New York hip-hop producer, to handle the beats, and laid the producer's hip-hop nous down in lush layers. That is the frame the comparison with Family Affair points to.

### The Voice

Across the album, the New Zealand Herald heard Brown's slothful singing style and his lackadaisical drone driving things along, and heard surging breakdowns that would not be out of place on a modern R&B tune.

### The Strings

The album used a 30-piece orchestra, and NME heard the orchestral backdrop intertwine the songs like ivy, so that the record sounds like one continuous piece of music. The Observer heard John Barry-esque strings across the set.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} can change a singer's genre. Haynie, from Buffalo, had started out producing rappers, and in the later 2000s worked with Eminem and Kanye West; here he gives a former Stone Roses singer the groove of an American R&B record.

The {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }} is the other half. A string arranger decides what the orchestra plays and when, and Hale's strings set Eternal Flame inside the album's continuous orchestral texture rather than on top of it.

An {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }} came with the deluxe edition: a bonus disc of orchestral mixes of all twelve tracks, Eternal Flame among them. Comparing the two is a lesson in what a new mix can change in a finished song.

## Artistic Context

Brown sang with the Stone Roses from 1983 until they split in 1996, then went solo. For this album he also enlisted Andy Rourke of the Smiths, Paul Ryder of Happy Mondays, and Steve Jones and Paul Cook of the Sex Pistols, with Sinéad O'Connor singing on two tracks.

## Impact and Reception

Eternal Flame was not a single, and reviews of the album were mixed, with a Metacritic score of 58. The New Zealand Herald found its lyric clichéd, while the Guardian said the album draws its strength from sounds rather than words. Listen for the sounds.
