## Genre and Mood

**Ben Khan**'s **waterfall** is the quiet one: an instrumental near the end of an album otherwise full of warped electro-pop, funk and coiled tension. It sits at the downtempo edge of his sound, and the feelings are dreamy and atmospheric rather than urgent. The Guardian placed it among the strong slower moments that arrive after the clouds break.

## Song Summary

waterfall is track fourteen of fifteen on Khan's self-titled debut, released by Dirty Hit on 10 August 2018, and runs 3:43. There is no lyric to summarise. The National Student, reviewing the album, called it refreshingly unique and described what it is built from: acoustic strums meeting electric production, in a track that is purely instrumental. The Dutch site Written in Music counted it among the album's most impressive moments and called it dreamy and breathy. Hold on to that pairing of hand-played guitar and electronics.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through waterfall section by section or gives times, so this guide follows the layers the reviews describe.

### The Guitar

Acoustic strums are the human part of the track; listen for how they keep moving while everything around them is shaped electronically.

### The Production

The National Student heard electric production meeting those strums. Khan, the same review reported, recorded organic sounds such as guitar with a field microphone for this album, aiming for something unintimidating and beautiful.

### The Air

Written in Music's word for it was breathy: the track leaves room around its sounds instead of filling every corner.

## Concepts in Focus

An {{ <concept_link name="instrumental" /> }} gives the whole job to the arrangement. On an album full of Khan's voice and of what the Guardian called loaded lyrics about honey and tempestuous weather, waterfall takes the words away and lets the guitar and the electronics carry the mood alone.

It works as a {{ <concept_link name="deep cut" /> }}: placed late on the record, it is found mostly by people who play the album through. Yet several reviews named it among the best things on it; The Skinny's three-star review listed it as one of three tracks to hear first, beside {{ <track_link source="ben-khan-monsoon-daydream.md" /> }}.

The album's sound is close to {{ <concept_link name="lo-fi" /> }}: the Guardian heard an appealingly raw, bedroom-recorded quality, with producer Flood adding little big-budget gloss. A guitar caught on a field microphone fits that description.

## Artistic Context

Khan is a London producer and singer whose EPs 1992 and 1000 came out in 2014 and 2015; The Skinny wrote that he then largely disappeared before releasing this debut on his own terms. The National Student reported that films such as Blade Runner played silently while he made it. On the album, waterfall comes straight after the 55-second ruby1strecording and just before the closer, {{ <track_link source="ben-khan-warriors-rose.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

For a fourteenth track, waterfall drew more praise than its place suggests. Ben Beaumont-Thomas of the Guardian called it an excellent instrumental and paired it with the loping {{ <track_link source="ben-khan-the-green.md" /> }}; Alexander Smail of The Skinny and Leon Pouwels of Written in Music both singled it out. Play the album's last two tracks in a row.
