## Genre and Mood

**Nicolas Repac**'s **Swinging in the Rain** is electro-swing, as its Bandcamp page tags it, in its most handmade form: a jazz record made by machines out of other people's records. It is playful in the way a cartoon soundtrack is playful, nostalgic by design, since its raw material is the sound of the 1930s, and quirky in every borrowed voice.

## Song Summary

The title bends Singin' in the Rain into swing, and the track keeps the reference: a sample-tracking database lists a dialogue sample from Moses Supposes, the number Gene Kelly and Donald O'Connor perform in the 1952 film. Around it, the album's own notes describe what Repac was after: Harlem nights, the Thirties, jungle fever, meowing muted trumpets, long-playing records with their breaths and creaks, assembled into modern, repetitive jungles.

At just under five minutes it is the longest piece on an album where several tracks last barely a minute.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} is the whole method here. Dandelion Radio calls Swing-Swing the sample-based solo outing of Arthur H's guitarist, and Repac explains the appeal: pairing pieces from different decades allows meetings between people who would never have met. A Hollywood musical of 1952 and a Paris studio of 2004 meet in this track.

The album's notes speak of repetitive jungles, which is another way of describing a {{ <concept_link name="loop" /> }}: a fragment repeated until it becomes a groove, with change coming from what is added and taken away.

The title promises {{ <concept_link name="swing" /> }}, both the jazz of the 1930s the album evokes and the lilting rhythm it is named after. Compare how {{ <track_link source="caravan-palace-lone-digger.md" /> }} later turned the same era into club music.

## Artistic Context

Repac is a French guitarist and producer, for years Arthur H's guitarist, who spent more than a decade on electronic production before returning to the guitar; Shazam's biography names Carl Stalling and Raymond Scott among his influences. Swing-Swing came out in 2004 on Nø Førmat!, as catalogue number NOF2; Swinging in the Rain is its second track. La Grande Roue followed in 2007, Black Box in 2013 and Rhapsodic in 2021.

## Impact and Reception

Dandelion Radio heard tunes at times playful and comic, at others tough, dark and very film noir, and the album has stayed in print: it is still sold on Bandcamp, with a heavy white vinyl edition. Listen to it as a question the album asks out loud: do machines have a soul?
