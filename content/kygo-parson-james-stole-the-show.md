## Genre and Mood

**Kygo** and **Parson James** made **Stole the Show** a tropical house hit out of a breakup song. It sits where deep house meets pop: piano, orchestral percussion and a flute-like lead around a young singer's voice. It is filed as bittersweet, dreamy and uplifting, because the lyric says goodbye while the music, in one reviewer's word, is summery.

## Song Summary

James wrote the song as a ballad. As he told V Man, quoted by Songfacts, it is a metaphor for a relationship that has run its course, pictured as a triumphant theatre show that has to end: it is time, he said, to close the curtains. He added that it was about a man he was not really in a relationship with, though they pretended to be. Kygo turned the ballad into tropical house and produced it; MusicBrainz also credits Kyle Kelso as producer and Mark Ralph with the mix. The writers are Kygo, James, Kelso, Michael Harwood and Marli Harwood.

## Musical Breakdown

EDMTunes described the track in March 2015; no source gives times, so the parts follow that review.

### Intro

The song builds with James singing practically a cappella while Kygo's classical piano slowly builds alongside orchestral percussion.

### Build-up

Cymbals smash, in the review's words, marking the transition into Kygo's summery sound.

### Verse 2

The second verse shows what EDMTunes called Kygo's darker side.

### Drop

From there the track progresses into the tropical drop. The Fader, writing about this song, called Kygo the guy who slays you with a digi-flute.

## Concepts in Focus

Stole the Show is a {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} record in which the guest is the author. James co-wrote the song and sings his own lyric; Kygo leads the credit, and the arrangement is his. Compare it with {{ <track_link source="kygo-conrad-sewell-firestone.md" /> }}, built the same way.

Its {{ <concept_link name="drop" /> }} is reached, not slammed into: EDMTunes heard the song progress into it beautifully after the darker second verse.

It also exists as an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }}: James's own original, produced by Kelso and released by RCA on 21 August 2015. The Fader found it just as huge, and Kygo called it simply beautiful. There is also a 3:31 radio edit, and the official video, directed by Saman Kesh for the 2015 YouTube Music Awards and telling of astronauts who crash-land on Earth, runs longer than the record.

## Artistic Context

Kygo, Kyrre Gørvell-Dahll, is a Norwegian producer and pianist from Bergen. Stole the Show came out on 23 March 2015 and later appeared on his debut album Cloud Nine. Parson James, born Ashton Parson in Cheraw, South Carolina, sang hymns in church as a child and moved to New York at 17. This was his first single, and RCA signed him after it.

## Impact and Reception

Stole the Show went to number one in Norway, Sweden and France, two in Germany and 24 in the UK, and was certified diamond in France and platinum in the US and the UK. Wikipedia calls it Kygo's biggest success after Firestone. Later that year they played it at the Nobel Peace Prize Concert. Listen for the moment the piano gives way to the flute.
