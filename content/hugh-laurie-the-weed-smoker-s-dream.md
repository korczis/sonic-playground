## Genre and Mood

**Hugh Laurie** recorded **The Weed Smoker's Dream** for Didn't It Rain, his second album of old American blues and jazz, and then gave the lead vocal to the singer Gaby Moreno. It is a cover of a 1936 blues with a jazz band's clarinet and trombone around it, and the feelings named here, sultry and relaxed, follow the critics: one heard Moreno as positively sultry and soothing on it.

## Song Summary

The song comes from the Harlem Hamfats, a Chicago band assembled to make records, who recorded it for Decca in 1936 with a credit to Kansas Joe McCoy and Herb Morand. It is about a marijuana smoker looking back on money and chances he let slip. McCoy later rewrote it from the woman's side as Why Don't You Do Right?, a hit for Lil Green in 1941 and for Peggy Lee with Benny Goodman in 1942. Laurie's version goes back to the first title and credits McCoy alone as writer.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through this recording part by part, so the breakdown keeps to the credits and to what reviewers heard.

### Main Section

Moreno sings the lead, with Jean McClain on backing vocals. The Copper Bottom Band plays around her: David Piltch on double bass, Jay Bellerose on drums, Elizabeth Lea on trombone, Robby Marshall on clarinet, Patrick Warren on accordion and keyboards, Larry Goldings on Hammond organ, Greg Leisz and Kevin Breit on guitars and mandolins, and Laurie on piano and guitar. Critics at Large found the track particularly poignant.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} is a new recording of a song by someone other than its first performers. This one reaches back past the famous Peggy Lee standard to its earlier, rougher form.

It is also a lesson in the {{ <concept_link name="blues" /> }} as a living repertoire. Wikipedia describes the song, in its later form, as a minor-key twelve-bar blues with a few chord substitutions; the Hamfats themselves mixed blues with dixieland and swing.

The {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} here is Joe Henry, who produced the album and assembled the Copper Bottom Band around Laurie. Critics at Large heard an ensemble greater than the sum of its parts.

## Artistic Context

Laurie, the English actor and musician, recorded Didn't It Rain at Ocean Way in Hollywood in January 2013, after his first blues album, Let Them Talk. This time he branched further into other Southern US and South American styles, including tango, and shared the microphone with Moreno, McClain and Taj Mahal. The album was released by Warner Bros. in the UK on 6 May 2013 and in the US that August.

## Impact and Reception

The album drew mixed reviews: Beats Per Minute found the band too often polite, and the NZ Herald thought a few tracks too polite to be remarkable, while praising the band, the guests and the authentic arrangements. This song was one of the bright spots for both Beats Per Minute and Critics at Large.
