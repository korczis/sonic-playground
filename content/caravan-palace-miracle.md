## Genre and Mood

**Caravan Palace** made their name by setting 1930s swing on club beats, and **Miracle** is where they let most of the swing go. Electro Swing Thing's interview with the band describes the single as largely trading their trademark jazz for dreamy electronica; Atwood Magazine heard a driving dance number. Energetic, upbeat and a little dreamy, it still belongs to electro swing by family more than by sound.

## Song Summary

Miracle was the first single from the band's fourth album, Chronologic, released in February 2019, more than six months before the album. The lyric is about brotherly love: a call to act like a brother, help one another and treat every day as a miracle. Zoé Colotis sings it. Redbrick's reviewer was struck by its synth bass, electronic beats and trippy lyrics, and Electro Swing Thing's reviewer called it bluesy, positive and energetic, with great use of samples throughout.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through Miracle in order, so this breakdown follows the layers the reviews and credits name.

### The Voice

Atwood Magazine heard Colotis's vocals as fast and playful, and took the single as the first sign of the album's lean towards pop.

### The Low End

Synth bass and electronic beats carry the track, the elements Redbrick picked out. The band's programmers are credited as Charles Delaporte, Hugues Payen, Antoine Toustou and Arnaud Vial, who also mixed it.

### The Played Parts

MusicBrainz credits Paul-Marie Barbier with piano and Victor Raimondeau as a guest on saxophone: acoustic threads that tie the track back to the band's jazz.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} is the clearest sign of change. Where the band once built tracks around swing rhythm, here a synth bass holds the floor, the sound that Redbrick heard first.

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} remains part of their method: a Chronologic reviewer heard samples used throughout the track, though no source names them, so this page does not guess.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} took a path of its own. Directed by the animation studio Double Ninja, who also made {{ <track_link source="caravan-palace-lone-digger.md" /> }}, it shows Spanish conquistadors meeting a civilisation they encounter, and YouTube restricted it to adults. The band told The Times the visuals bear no relation to the lyric or to their brief; Colotis said they would take style over views any day.

## Artistic Context

Caravan Palace, a Paris band, released their first album in 2008, naming Django Reinhardt and Daft Punk among their influences. For Chronologic they spent two years in separate Paris studios, trading ideas by email, and the hip-hop they listened to every day shaped the final sound more than the acoustic album they had first considered. It came out on 30 August 2019 and reached number 6 on the US dance albums chart.

## Impact and Reception

Electro Swing Thing's reviewer admitted he would not have recognised the band without knowing, yet called the track a brilliant start to the album. Listen to it beside Lone Digger and hear how far one band travelled in four years.
