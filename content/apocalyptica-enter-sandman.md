## Genre and Mood

**Apocalyptica** open their first album with **Enter Sandman**, Metallica's 1991 single, played by four cellos and nothing else. It is a cover pitched between neoclassical chamber music and symphonic metal: no drums, no amplifier, no voice. The mood stays intense and dark, because the song's menace lives in its riff.

## Song Summary

Metallica released Enter Sandman as the lead single and opening track of their self-titled fifth album. Its lyric is about nightmares and a child's bedtime fears, with a child's prayer in the breakdown. Apocalyptica's 1996 version is instrumental, so the words and the prayer are gone. It lasts 3:41, almost two minutes shorter than Metallica's recording; no source says which passages the quartet left out.

## Musical Breakdown

The reviews describe this recording only in general terms, so what follows sets them against the original's structure.

### Intro

Metallica's introduction is built on an E minor chord. Listen for how the cellos set it up and for the moment the riff takes over.

### Main Section

The engine is Kirk Hammett's riff: two bars, the first played three times before the second answers it, a pattern Lars Ulrich suggested. Here, the Green Man Review explains, the four players divide melody, lead guitar, bass line and rhythm between them. Louder's Stephen Dalton heard the track sweep along on strident col legno strokes, teasing out new textures and countermelodies from the original.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} lives by what it keeps. Apocalyptica keep the notes and lose what made the original sound like metal, so the composition has to carry what distortion and volume used to.

That composition is mostly one {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }}: Ulrich called Enter Sandman a one-riff song, since its sections all grow from that figure.

The detail Dalton singled out is {{ <concept_link name="col legno" /> }}, playing with the wood of the bow. Struck against the strings, the stick gives a dry, clicking attack, part note and part drum: one way four string players can supply a drummer's rhythm.

Finally, {{ <concept_link name="remastering" /> }}: this upload belongs to the 2016 edition of the album, which Apocalyptica describe as remastered. Unlike {{ <track_link source="apocalyptica-battery.md" /> }}, a new recording added to that edition, Enter Sandman is the 1996 performance with its sound prepared again.

## Artistic Context

Apocalyptica began as four classically trained cellists from Helsinki's Sibelius Academy who were also metal fans. Plays Metallica by Four Cellos, eight instrumental Metallica covers produced by Pekka Ritualuoto, came out on Mercury in 1996 and went platinum in Finland and gold in Germany and Poland. Enter Sandman is its first track; the twentieth-anniversary edition followed on 22 July 2016.

## Impact and Reception

Not everyone thought this was the right song for cellos. A Sputnikmusic reviewer felt it was chosen for recognition and handled competently rather than inspired, and the Green Man Review found it less moving than other tracks. Twenty years on, Louder heard sturdy, imaginative reworkings and singled this one out for its textures. Hear Metallica's version first, then this one, and listen for what the bows add.
