## Genre and Mood

**Ben Khan**'s **a.t.w (against the wall)** is electro-pop with funk and soul in its bloodstream, and the track on his debut where an acoustic guitar steps forward. Clash, announcing it in June 2018, called it a banger saturated in summer energy. Groovy, energetic and bold are the feelings.

## Song Summary

The track appeared online at the start of June 2018, two months before the album. Clash heard him matching his barbed, fluoro-soaked electronics with elements of acoustic guitar, and described the result as an experiment that merges the organic with the digital to make something that does not truly exist in either. No review spells out the lyric, so this page leaves it alone. What stays with listeners is that collision of textures, and the chord movement under it: XSNOIZE, reviewing the album, singled out the song's murmuring progressions.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} here is a texture with edges, not a backdrop. Barbed is Clash's word, and a good one to listen with: notice how the synth tones bite, and how the acoustic guitar sounds beside them.

A {{ <concept_link name="live recording" /> }} came in July 2018, when Khan shared a full live take of the song, his first-ever live clip, according to Clash, which heard it as strong, muscular and with a visceral edge. Comparing it with the studio version shows which parts of the song were built and which can be performed.

The {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} matters even on a one-man record. Grimy Goods reported that Khan played every instrument on the album, yet it was produced with Flood, known for his work with PJ Harvey, Depeche Mode and Nine Inch Nails, mixed by Spike Stent and mastered by Dave Cooley. Hear the album's opener, {{ <track_link source="ben-khan-2000-angels.md" /> }}, made the same way.

## Artistic Context

Ben Khan is a London-based producer and singer. a.t.w (against the wall) is the fifth of fifteen tracks on his self-titled debut, released by Dirty Hit on 10 August 2018, two places before {{ <track_link source="ben-khan-the-green.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

A music blog heard the song as Khan channelling Jai Paul, admitted the copycat claims held some water, and still said it delivered everything fans of that sound had been waiting five years for. XSNOIZE, giving the album 8 out of 10, simply said it loved the track. Play the studio version, then the live take, and listen for where the wall is.
