## Genre and Mood

**Nils Ohrmann** plays **In Ya Face (Original Mix)** as no-nonsense tech house, rooted in house and stripped back with a minimal touch. It is groovy, hypnotic and playful, a track that teases the crowd while keeping them locked to the floor.

## Song Summary

As the title suggests, the track has attitude. Ohrmann builds it around a springy, rubbery bassline, tight drums and a cheeky vocal hook that pops up like a taunt across the mix. The production is lean and precise, every element placed to keep the groove rolling, with small changes in percussion and filters doing the work that a lesser track would leave to big breakdowns. At over six minutes it is made for DJs, designed to be mixed, layered and ridden for as long as the room demands.

## Musical Breakdown

Like most club tracks, it develops through layering and subtraction rather than verses and choruses.

### Intro

A crisp kick and shuffling hats lay down a DJ-friendly foundation, giving space to mix in.

### Build-up

The bassline slides in, bouncy and elastic, and the percussion starts to fill out.

### Main Section

The vocal hook arrives, playful and a little mocking, riding over the groove as the track settles into its hypnotic stride.

### Bridge

A short break pulls the bass away, leaving drums and vocal snippets to tease before the groove returns.

### Climax

Everything locks back in at full power, the bass and hook bouncing off each other in the track's most physical stretch.

### Outro

The elements peel away again, ending on drums so the next record can slip in.

## Artistic Context

Nils Ohrmann is a house producer at the heart of the Arms & Legs label, where he has repeatedly shared records with Daniel Steinberg. "In Ya Face" opened the Tumbleweed EP, released on Arms & Legs in April 2012, a four-track record shared with Steinberg that also included "Higher Noon", "Money Talks" and "Gokart Disco". The track later returned in a Kristin Velvet remix on Ohrmann's Bon Voyage EP.

## Impact and Reception

"In Ya Face" has stayed in circulation well beyond its release, revisited in remix and still a natural pick for DJs thanks to its instantly recognisable hook and its bounce. It represents a strand of Berlin-connected house that is fun first and serious second, without ever sacrificing groove. It is here because it still works: drop it in a mix and watch the floor respond. Turn it up and let it get in your face.
