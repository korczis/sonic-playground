## Genre and Mood

In **A Fistful of Peanuts**, **Spacemonkeyz** turn a **Gorillaz** hit into dub with a reggae lilt, a remix that does not try to be a hit again. The mood is hypnotic and relaxed: the version leans on its bass and lets the song drift past.

## Song Summary

The track is Spacemonkeyz' reworking of Clint Eastwood, the lead single of Gorillaz's 2001 debut, written by Damon Albarn and Teren Jones, better known as Del the Funky Homosapien. The original was already half in Jamaica: Albarn said the name came to them while recording there and listening to dancehall, and that he admired the actor, Sergio Leone and Ennio Morricone. The new title keeps the western joke, swapping the dollars of Leone's A Fistful of Dollars, the first of his westerns with Eastwood, for peanuts. Kenn Rodriguez of The Daily Lobo heard the song refitted as reggae and called it a delight, one of the few well-known Gorillaz songs on the album where the remixing works.

## Musical Breakdown

No timed analysis of this version has been published, so the parts below follow what reviewers describe rather than a clock.

### Intro

The Daily Lobo describes the album's working method as standard dub technique: drum loops, heavy bass and a lot of backward tape effects.

### Main Section

The reviewer found that the remixes turned pop songs into airy, bass-heavy versions that still closely resembled their originals.

### Outro

The standard album version runs 5:53. The digipak edition hides a longer one, the More Peanuts version, at 6:39, so that edition ends on this song.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="dub" /> }} began in Jamaica as a way of remixing a record by stripping it to drums and bass and treating the rest with echo, reverb and drop-outs. Spacemonkeyz apply that idea to a pop single, and the bass and the space around it become the subject.

Each track on Laika Come Home is a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} of a song from the debut, renamed: Jungle Fresh is 19-2000, P.45 is {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-5-4.md" /> }}, and this is Clint Eastwood. Compare the way the {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-19-2000-soulchild-remix.md" /> }} took its original somewhere brighter.

The longer take is a {{ <concept_link name="hidden track" /> }}: unlisted, placed on the limited digipak, for listeners who let the album play on.

## Artistic Context

Spacemonkeyz grew out of a dub remix of Tomorrow Comes Today by Darren Galea, who also played turntables in the Gorillaz live band; Albarn liked it and asked him to remix the whole debut. The group's named members are Galea, Richie Stevens and Gavin Dodds; in the band's fiction they were space-test monkeys who took the tapes from Kong Studios without permission. Laika Come Home came out on 1 July 2002 on Parlophone in the UK.

## Impact and Reception

The album reached number 6 on the US dance albums chart and drew three stars from AllMusic and seven out of ten from NME. The track lived on in another place: a sample of it plays at the start of the {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-de-la-soul-feel-good-inc.md" /> }} video. Put the original on first, then this, and listen for what is no longer there.
