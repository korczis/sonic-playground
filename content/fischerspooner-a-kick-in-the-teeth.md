## Genre and Mood

**Fischerspooner**'s **A Kick In The Teeth** is the moment on their second album where the electroclash duo sounds most like a pop group. It is synth-pop and electro-pop built from electronic parts, but the mood is warm and dreamy, carried by a chorus of hushed, stacked voices rather than by the beat.

## Song Summary

The song began in an unglamorous place. According to the band's own account in the album's release coverage, Casey Spooner found the chorus words in a randomly worded spam email while caught in teeth-grinding personal anxiety during the making of the record. Linda Perry added melodic input, Tony Hoffer, known for his work with Beck and Air, added production touches, and the band said the lush vocal approach was influenced by Steve Miller and Pink Floyd. Its words were found rather than written, then sung as if they meant everything.

## Musical Breakdown

No source times the sections; this follows what the band and critics described.

### Verses

PopMatters' Patrick Schabe heard the album start to stretch beyond its icy synth tones here: the track is built on computer tones and processed beats, yet arrives with a full-fledged pop presence that he found closest to Pet Shop Boys.

### Chorus

The chorus is the song's signature: nine extremely soft vocal tracks of Spooner and his longtime backing singer Lizzy Yoder layered together. John Brainlove of Drowned in Sound listed the warm glow of this chorus among the album's highlights.

### Fade-out

The ending dissolves into scrambled beats, which Schabe called well beyond anything Steve Miller's Space Cowboy ever attempted.

## Concepts in Focus

The chorus is a clear case of {{ <concept_link name="double tracking" /> }} taken far past doubling. Two singers, Spooner and Yoder, recorded nine extremely soft vocal tracks between them, and the band's account calls the layered result a superhuman sound: no single take is loud, but together they fill the chorus.

The credits show how the {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} role spread out on Odyssey. Fischerspooner share production with Tony Hoffer, who worked with Fischer at Sunset Sound in Los Angeles, and Nicolas Vernhes, the engineer who recorded the duo's virtual band of session players in Brooklyn; the French producer Mirwais helped with the album's final touches.

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} is still the floor of the track, as it was on {{ <track_link source="fischerspooner-natural-disaster.md" /> }} from their first album, but PopMatters heard this song stretch beyond the icy synth tones of Odyssey's opening tracks.

## Artistic Context

Fischerspooner, Warren Fischer and Casey Spooner, were known as an electroclash duo and performance troupe from New York before this album. Odyssey, released on 5 April 2005 in the US on Capitol, took more than two years and, by the band's account, stretched them to their breaking point. For the first time Spooner reached outside the duo, to Linda Perry and Susan Sontag, and the album shifted towards synth-pop.

## Impact and Reception

Odyssey drew a Metacritic score of 70 and reached number two on the US Top Electronic Albums chart. A Kick In The Teeth was released in November 2005 as a single paired with All We Are. Listen for the chorus, then for the beats coming apart in the fade.
