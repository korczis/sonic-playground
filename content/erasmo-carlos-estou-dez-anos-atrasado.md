## Genre and Mood

**Erasmo Carlos** opened his 1970 album with **Estou Dez Anos Atrasado**, "I am ten years behind", and the record around it is where the Jovem Guarda rocker turned toward soul and samba. Filed today under samba rock and Brazilian soul, it is groovy and warm, a sound Prensa's Marcelo Pereira heard full of brass, strings and full-bodied bass and guitars.

## Song Summary

The song was written by Erasmo Carlos with Roberto Carlos, his songwriting partner since the 1960s, and it is the first track of Erasmo Carlos e os Tremendões, recorded in 1969 and released by RGE in 1970, with arrangements by the maestro Chiquinho de Moraes. No source this page found reads the lyric beyond its title, so the title is all this page claims: a man saying he is a decade late. The version embedded here is the one on the compilation Samba Rock Vol.1, released by Atração in October 2007; no source says which master the compilation used.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the song bar by bar. Pereira's description of the album, which names this track as its example, gives its layers.

### The Brass

Brass sections, the soul colour that Pereira ties to 1970, the year Tim Maia released his first album and Erasmo and Roberto were, in his words, crazy about soul music.

### The Strings

String-quartet arrangements beside the brass, part of what Pereira calls the precious arrangements of Chiquinho de Moraes.

### The Rhythm Section

Full-bodied basses and guitars, the rock side of the record.

### The Voice

Erasmo's own voice in front: a former teen idol over an arrangement this full.

## Concepts in Focus

The track is a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }}. The song is a Carlos and Carlos composition, from the team that wrote Erasmo's Jovem Guarda hits, but its sound, brass sections and a string quartet over bass and guitars, is the work of an arranger, Chiquinho de Moraes.

Its later life runs through the {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }}. Samba Rock Vol.1 opens with {{ <track_link source="cleo-galante-voce-vai-ver-que-eu-tenho-razao.md" /> }}, places this song ninth of fourteen and closes with Jorge Ben Jor's Berenice. Samba rock, Wikipedia explains, began as a dance culture at São Paulo parties where DJs played samba beside rock and African-American music, and compilations like this one keep its old records in circulation.

## Artistic Context

Erasmo Carlos, born Erasmo Esteves in Tijuca, Rio de Janeiro, in 1941, learned his first guitar chords from his childhood friend Tim Maia and became, with Roberto Carlos and Wanderléa, a face of the 1960s Jovem Guarda, nicknamed Tremendão. Pereira writes that the end of that scene left him adrift until "Sentado à Beira do Caminho" put him back in the charts in 1969 and freed him to experiment. Erasmo Carlos e os Tremendões, which also holds "Coqueiro Verde", his first samba-rock recording according to Wikipedia, was the result. He died in November 2022.

## Impact and Reception

Portuguese Wikipedia counts the album among the most important records of his career. Pereira reports reasonable chart results and, more than that, attention to Erasmo's reinvention, enough for Polygram to sign him. Put the song on and listen for the horns arriving over the guitars.
