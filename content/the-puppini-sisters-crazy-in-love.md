## Genre and Mood

**The Puppini Sisters** take **Crazy In Love**, the 2003 hit often credited with making Beyoncé a solo superstar, and play it as if it had been written for a jazz trio decades earlier. It is a cover, filed under jazz and vocal pop, and its mood is playful and exuberant: less a homage to Beyoncé than a game with her song, played at full energy.

## Song Summary

Beyoncé's original, released in 2003 with a verse by Jay-Z, was written by the two of them with Rich Harrison and built on a horn hook sampled from the Chi-Lites' 1970 song Are You My Woman (Tell Me So), which earned Eugene Record a writing credit. Its lyric is about a romantic fixation, and critics singled out its "uh-oh, uh-oh" hook. No review of the cover mentions those horns or the original's beat; the Boston Herald described an upright bass and violin treatment, the Guardian an exuberant jazzy deconstruction. In place of one diva there are three voices.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through this recording section by section; the reviews describe its instruments, its solo and its changes of style, and so does this breakdown.

### Main Section

The three singers carry the song in the close harmony the group is built on, backed by the trio's band; George Graham, reviewing the album, named its rhythm section as Nick Pini on bass, Martin Kolarides on guitar and Henry Tyler on drums. To his ear the tune travels through everything from hot jazz to what seems like French cabaret.

### Violin Solo

The Von Pip Musical Express singled out a violin solo by Stephanie O'Brien, then one of the three singers, and heard the whole track as foot-stomping and almost Romany in flavour.

## Concepts in Focus

As a {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }}, Crazy in Love shows how much of a hit lives in its production. The reviews describe new instruments in place of the original's sample and beat, yet melody and lyric remain recognisably Beyoncé's.

In place of a single diva comes close {{ <concept_link name="vocal harmony" /> }}, the Andrews Sisters' sound that gave the Puppinis their name. A hook written for one voice becomes an ensemble part.

The third idea is {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }}: who plays what. Setting a 2003 hit for upright bass and violin is what moves it towards the hot jazz Graham heard. Compare the treatment of a sixties hit on the same album, {{ <track_link source="the-puppini-sisters-spooky.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Marcella Puppini founded the trio in London in 2004, inspired by the animated film The Triplets of Belleville, and Universal Classics and Jazz signed them in 2005. Their second album, The Rise and Fall of Ruby Woo, released on 1 October 2007, mixes old standards, contemporary hits recast in the Andrews Sisters' style, among them Crazy in Love and Walk Like an Egyptian, and five originals. Martin Terefe produced this track.

## Impact and Reception

The album entered the UK chart at number 73 and reached the top ten of the US jazz albums chart. The Guardian counted Crazy in Love among its most daring and successful tracks; AllMusic found the formula wearing thin. The Real Tuesday Weld later remixed the cover. Play it after Beyoncé's original and listen for what three voices and a violin do with that hook.
