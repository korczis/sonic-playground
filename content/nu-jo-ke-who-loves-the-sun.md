## Genre and Mood

**Nu** and **Jo.Ke** made **Who Loves the Sun** a defining record of the organic, open-air end of deep house, with the slow sway of downtempo built into its bones. The mood is uplifting, hypnotic and relaxed, the sound of the sun going down over a dancefloor that has no intention of stopping.

## Song Summary

At more than nine minutes, the original mix is a long, warm journey rather than a quick hit. Jo.Ke's gentle, almost sing-along vocal carries the song, framed by a loping groove, earthy percussion and simple, glowing melodic phrases that repeat until they feel like a mantra. It shares its title with a Velvet Underground song, but its spirit is entirely its own: unhurried, communal and full of light.

## Musical Breakdown

The track unfolds slowly, giving each element room to settle before the next arrives, the way a long sunset changes colour by degrees.

### Intro

Soft percussion and a steady, rounded kick start the journey, with warm textures drifting in like heat haze over a field.

### Build-up

The bass and melodic elements gradually join, the groove becoming fuller and more rolling while staying relaxed.

### Verse 1

Jo.Ke's voice enters, intimate and unforced, singing as if to a few friends gathered close rather than a crowd.

### Main Section

The heart of the track is a long, locked groove where voice, percussion and melody circle each other, hypnotic in its repetition and gentle in its pace.

### Bridge

The rhythm eases off and the atmosphere opens up, a breath of space before the groove gathers again.

### Outro

The elements fade one by one over the closing minutes, the melody lingering like the last warmth of the day.

## Artistic Context

Who Loves the Sun was released on Bar 25 Music, the label that grew out of the legendary Berlin club Bar 25, and it captures the label's open-air, sun-soaked aesthetic. It later appeared on the collection We Love The Sun. Its success led to a series of new versions, including a Bar 25 Music edit and a remake with the producer Jake Hall, with Jo.Ke singing again.

## Impact and Reception

The original became a slow-burning phenomenon, passing hundreds of millions of streams by most counts and becoming a fixture of festival sunsets and chill-out playlists. It earns its place here as proof that dance music does not need to rush to move people. Find nine free minutes, ideally outdoors as the light turns golden, and give it the whole running time.
