## Genre and Mood

**The Game** and **50 Cent**'s **"Hate It or Love It"** is hip-hop dressed in R&B and old soul: warm and reflective, a look back at hard beginnings over a beat its producers wanted to sound sun-kissed.

## Song Summary

Songfacts sums up the theme as what the two rappers had to overcome to succeed. The Game wrote while being driven to 50 Cent's Connecticut mansion in early 2004, during the sessions for The Documentary, and wrote the final verse first, working backward so that tiredness would not weaken the later verses. 50 Cent wrote and raps the chorus. The beat is built on "Rubber Band", from the Trammps' 1975 Legendary Zing Album, and its writers share the credit.

## Musical Breakdown

### The Beat

Cool of the production duo Cool & Dre found the Trammps record at a mom-and-pop store in Tallahassee and kept returning to "Rubber Band" because it felt so nostalgic. He chopped it on an MPC with a swing like 50 Cent's "21 Questions" and added the hi-hat sample that became the duo's signature; his partner Dre cut it so the guitars and horn had more punch, and a bass player came in to record a part.

### Chorus

50 Cent's hook carries the title. Stylus's Scott McKeating thought he "manages to steal the show".

### Verses

The Game raps the verses. Songfacts notes a nod to Tupac's "Brenda's Got a Baby"; no source we found walks through them line by line.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} is the song's foundation: a 1975 soul record about heartbreak and exhaustion, as Okayplayer describes it, recut into a beat Cool & Dre wanted to sound "luxurious and glamorous, but also sticky and sun-kissed".

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} came from the guest. According to Okayplayer, 50 Cent already had it in mind and lacked the right beat until G-Unit's Sha Money XL put this one in his hands, after it had sat unsold for two years.

The {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} credit has a twist. Dr. Dre heard the song, asked 50 Cent to give it to the Game, and altered the production; Cool & Dre said "Dre brought it to life", yet Interscope's Jimmy Iovine could not tell the final version from the original beat.

## Artistic Context

It was the third single from the Game's album The Documentary, released in 2005 on Aftermath, G-Unit and Interscope. Okayplayer frames it as the beginning and end for the pair: the G-Unit general, 50 Cent, cut the Game off over a perceived lack of loyalty, after the Game would not condemn Jadakiss and Fat Joe for teaming up with Ja Rule on "New York".

## Impact and Reception

It spent five weeks at number 2 on the Billboard Hot 100, kept from the top by 50 Cent's own "Candy Shop", topped the R&B/Hip-Hop chart and reached number 4 in the UK. Stylus called it "a great piece of warm soul-fuelled hip-hop". It was nominated for two Grammys, and Pitchfork ranked it 93rd among the best songs of the 2000s. A sequel with Snoop Dogg followed in 2026. Play it and listen for the soul record underneath.
