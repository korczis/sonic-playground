## Genre and Mood

**Gigi D'Agostino**'s **Bla Bla Bla** is Italo dance at its most stripped down: a beat, a voice and almost nothing to understand. Hypnotic, playful and energetic, it turns a sung line into rhythm, and the joke of the title is that the words stop mattering.

## Song Summary

The vocal is a sample. D'Agostino took a line from "Why Did You Do It", a 1975 single by the British rock band Stretch, and used it again and again; the Italian Wikipedia describes the track as a continuous repetition of that phrase, remixed and distorted in various ways for its full four minutes and fifteen seconds. The writing is credited to D'Agostino and Stretch's guitarist Kirby Gregory, and D'Agostino produced it. Released as a single on 17 May 1999, it also closes the first disc of his double album L'Amour Toujours.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the track bar by bar, so this follows what the sources say it is made of.

### The Sample

One sung line about what someone has done to the singer, cut up and distorted until it reads more as syllables than as a sentence.

### The Repetition

The phrase runs through the whole track. What changes, in the Italian Wikipedia's account, is how it is treated: remixed and distorted differently as the track goes on.

### Other Versions

L'Amour Toujours carries the song three times: this version, the Drammentenza Mix and the Dark Mix on the second disc, Beats for the Feet.

## Concepts in Focus

Bla Bla Bla is a clean example of {{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }}. Stretch's single was, according to Wikipedia, a direct attack on Mick Fleetwood for his failure to join the band on an ill-fated US tour; twenty-four years later its opening line became the raw material of an Italian dance hit, its anger drained into texture.

The track is also a lesson in the {{ <concept_link name="loop" /> }}. With one phrase repeating, interest has to come from what is done to it, the same principle that drives {{ <track_link source="kruder-dorfmeister-johnson.md" /> }} at a far slower tempo.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Andreas Hykade and Ged Haney at Studio Film Bilder in the style of the Italian cartoon La Linea, follows Baba, a boy with a floating head and no arms, walking towards a shark that multiplies and changes direction. The studio says it and the video for The Riddle were fixtures on MTV and VIVA for months.

## Artistic Context

Born in Turin in 1967, D'Agostino started as a DJ in 1986 playing Italo disco. L'Amour Toujours, his second album, came out in 1999 on two discs, Chansons for the Heart and Beats for the Feet, and also produced "The Riddle", "La Passion" and the title song later reused in {{ <track_link source="dynoro-gigi-d-agostino-in-my-mind.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Bla Bla Bla reached number three in Austria, four in Germany and fifteen in France, went gold in Germany and platinum in Austria, and was certified platinum in Italy in 2021. Play it and try to catch the words before they dissolve.
