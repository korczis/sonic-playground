## Genre and Mood

**Modus**, with **Ľubomír Stankovský** at the microphone, made **Ty, ja a môj brat** a pop rock song of the 1980s that people still know by heart. The feelings chosen here, catchy and nostalgic, come from what the song became more than from any description of its sound: no critic we found describes the recording, so this page says what is documented and leaves the listening to you.

## Song Summary

The title means "you, me and my brother". Ján Lehotský, the band's leader, wrote the music and Kamil Peteraj the words, as for every track on the 1984 album Najlepšie dievčatá, where the song is the fourth track and runs 4:02. It was not written to be a hit single. Its singer was the drummer, Stankovský, and its listeners renamed it after a phrase of the refrain: the song became popular as Sklíčka or Sklíčka dotykov.

## Musical Breakdown

No source we found walks through the arrangement, so this breakdown follows what the credits and the press say rather than a timeline.

### The Voice

Stankovský sings lead and plays drums. Peteraj remembered him as a very good drummer with an "iron rhythm" who also sang very well.

### The Band

The recording credits Lehotský on keyboards, Vladimír Kaššay on bass guitar and Jiří Vana on guitar, with Lehotský and Kaššay on backing vocals.

### The Refrain

Somewhere in the refrain is the phrase that took over the title. Listen for it and you will hear what the public decided the song was called.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is the part of a song that sticks, and this one stuck so well that it renamed the song: the press of 2024 still says it "zľudovel", passed into common use, as Sklíčka rather than under its printed title.

A {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }} is how a 1984 album track reaches later listeners. The upload here comes from GOLD, an Opus compilation of 2005, where the title carries the nickname in brackets; the same recording also sits on later collections.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} came last. The song had none until 2023, almost forty years after release, which says how far it travelled on radio, records and voices alone. ## Artistic Context

Stankovský, born in Bratislava in 1951, co-founded Modus in 1967 with Ján Baláž and Miroslav Žbirka and played drums with the band until 1973. Lehotský joined in 1972 and became its leader. Stankovský returned in the 1980s and was one of its lead singers from 1983 to 1986, beside Lehotský and Marián Greksa. Najlepšie dievčatá, the band's fifth studio album, came out on Opus in 1984 and was reissued on CD and LP in 2024.

## Impact and Reception

Ty, ja a môj brat became one of the biggest hits of 1980s popular music in Czechoslovakia and, about forty years on, is still among the best-known Slovak songs; the TASR news agency called it the dominant mega hit of its album. When Stankovský died in March 2024, at 72, the headlines named him as the singer of Sklíčka dotykov. Play it and listen for the line everyone remembers.
