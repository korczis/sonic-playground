## Genre and Mood

**Louisahhh**’s *Love Is a Punk (Vitalic Remix)* is a collision of **techno**, **industrial**, and **punk**, exuding a **rebellious**, **intense**, and **raw** atmosphere. It is a track for the darker, harder corners of electronic music, where techno and punk share an attitude.

> Reviewers of the album heard industrial music with electro distortions, techno and rock, and Louisahhh cited Garbage and Nine Inch Nails as influences she shared with its producer, Vice Cooler.

## About the Artist

**Louisahhh** is Louisa Pillot, a New York-bred DJ, vocalist and producer based in Paris. She was one half of NYCPARTYINFO, has made music as Louisahhh since 2011, and co-founded the label RAAR with her friend Maëlstrom, "a techno label for punk rockers, a punk rock label for techno-heads". The Practice of Freedom, produced with Vice Cooler, was her debut album and the first album release of the HE.SHE.THEY. label, on 12 March 2021.

Vitalic, the French producer Pascal Arbez-Nicolas, whose distorted electro has been a club staple since the early 2000s, remixed the album's opening track for the deluxe edition released on 5 November 2021, alongside remixes by Spencer Parker, AnD, Rebekah, Man Power, Imogen, Bobby Nourmand and others.

## Song Summary

*Love Is a Punk (Vitalic Remix)* opens with a brooding atmosphere, setting a tense mood with distorted synths and sparse percussion, where the original album version opens with marching drums and the voice straight away. Louisahhh's commanding vocal emerges with a mix of defiance and vulnerability, and Vitalic's production layers beats and evolving textures around it.

## Sound Breakdown

### Intro: Brooding Ambiance

The track begins with ominous synths and minimal percussion, creating a sense of unease. This restrained start builds anticipation, drawing the listener into the emotional gravity of the song.

### First Drop: Rebellion Unleashed

When the beat drops, it does so with relentless energy. Syncopated rhythms and distorted basslines create a chaotic yet structured momentum, embodying the rebellious spirit of the track.

### Midsection: Emotional Intensity

The middle section amplifies the tension, with Louisahhh’s voice taking center stage. Her delivery oscillates between raw vulnerability and commanding authority, while Vitalic’s production layers evolve to maintain an intense, immersive atmosphere.

### Climax: Punk Meets Techno

As the track crescendos, all elements collide in a frenzy of sound. The pounding rhythms, jagged synths, and impassioned vocals reach a peak of emotional and sonic intensity, leaving the listener breathless.

### Outro: Sudden Silence

The track ends abruptly, with the layers of sound disappearing into silence. This unexpected conclusion mirrors the song’s themes of chaos and control, leaving the listener in a state of reflection.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} begins from the original recording's parts, and the most important part here is the voice. Vitalic keeps Louisahhh's vocal and replaces the rest: the original's marching drums give way to a long, slow-building club structure, and a three-minute song becomes a track of almost six minutes that a DJ can mix. The deluxe edition of The Practice of Freedom gathers twelve such remixes, each a different reading of the same songs.

{{ <concept_link name="distortion" /> }} is the shared language of the original and the remix. Distortion clips and saturates a sound until it grows harsh overtones; in rock it comes from overdriven amplifiers, in techno and electro from pushed synthesizers and drum machines. Vitalic built his name on that grit, and here it plays the role guitars play in punk: the sound of something pushed past its limits on purpose. Compare it with his own {{ <track_link source="vitalic-cocaine.md" /> }}.

## Overall Vibe

*Love Is a Punk (Vitalic Remix)* merges the raw energy of punk with the industrial edge of techno. It is **intense**, **rebellious** and **raw**, and it shows how a remixer can keep an artist's voice while moving her song into his own territory.

It is built for a club system, where the slow opening and the hard drop make the most sense.

## Conclusion

In *Love Is a Punk (Vitalic Remix)*, Louisahhh and Vitalic meet halfway between punk and techno. Listen to the album version first, then this remix, and notice how much of the song survives the change of form.
