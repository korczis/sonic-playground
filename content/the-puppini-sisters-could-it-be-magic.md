## Genre and Mood

**The Puppini Sisters**' **Could It Be Magic** is a cover in a jazz idiom, filed by Apple Music as easy listening, and it takes a 1970s pop ballad into the world of a 1940s vocal trio. The mood is sultry, the word The VPME's reviewer used, alongside seductive; theatrical, because George Graham heard a "theatrical torch song"; and romantic, because The Guardian heard it voicing "the love Barry certainly never dares to name".

## Song Summary

Barry Manilow wrote the music, inspired by Chopin's Prelude in C minor, Opus 28, Number 20, and Adrienne Anderson the words; Manilow imagined it as one great crescendo that would build like Hey Jude. The Puppinis keep the song and change its frame: three voices in close harmony, a fourth singing bass, strings, and Martin Terefe producing.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes this recording bar by bar, so this follows its credits and what critics heard.

### The Trio

Marcella Puppini, Kate Mullins and Stephanie O'Brien sing the three-part harmonies the group is known for; Nick Pini is credited as bass vocalist beneath them.

### The Strings

Marcella Puppini and David Davidson are credited with the string arrangement. Graham noted that a string quartet joins the trio on a few of the album's tracks.

### The Character

Graham called it a theatrical torch song and "another example of the group's musical resourcefulness"; The VPME heard it as sultry, seductive and sexy, and preferred it to Take That's version.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} can change what a song means. Could It Be Magic has been a bubblegum single by Featherbed in 1971, Manilow's own slow ballad in 1973 and 1975, a disco track for Donna Summer in 1976 and a pop-dance hit for Take That in 1992. Here, in George Graham's words, it becomes a theatrical torch song, led by voices in harmony.

{{ <concept_link name="vocal harmony" /> }} is the group's whole method. The Puppini Sisters are a close-harmony trio, named in tribute to the Andrews Sisters, and they reimagine contemporary songs in that 1940s style. Marcella Puppini has said they like to sing modern, risqué subjects in a quaintly old-fashioned way, the way 1940s songs hid innuendo in innocent words.

## Artistic Context

Marcella Puppini founded the group in 2004, inspired by the film The Triplets of Belleville, and Universal Classics and Jazz signed them in 2005. The Rise and Fall of Ruby Woo, their second album, was released in the UK on 1 October 2007, recorded at Air, RAK, Kensaltown and Little Big Room studios and in Nashville. It mixes covers such as Walk Like an Egyptian and Crazy in Love with five original songs, and is named after a MAC lipstick.

## Impact and Reception

The album reached number 73 in the UK, number 9 on Billboard's Jazz Albums chart and number 43 in France. The Guardian singled out its daring covers, this one among them. Listen for the three voices moving as one.
