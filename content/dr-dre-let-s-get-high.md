## Genre and Mood

**Dr. Dre**'s **Let's Get High** is hip-hop and rap, and in Okayplayer's 2024 ranking of the album it is the song on 2001 that sounds most like the early-1990s G-funk Dre had made famous. It is carefree in its subject and groovy in its beat, a short party track on an album NME heard as full of mournful atmospherics.

## Song Summary

The title says what the song is about: getting high, one of the drug-use themes Wikipedia lists among the album's subjects. It is a posse cut, credited to Dr. Dre with Hittman, Ms. Roq and Kurupt, and Okayplayer describes the four rappers trading verses with a lively interplay that gives the song the feel of a party. The beat, produced by Dre and Mel-Man, is built on samples of two funk records, and the whole thing is over in under two and a half minutes.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through Let's Get High section by section, so this follows how it was made.

### The Beat

The album's liner notes credit samples of Backstrokin' by the Fatback Band and High by Skyy, and the track's writing credits name Randy Muller, Bill Curtis and Johnny Flippin beside the rappers.

### The Players

MusicBrainz credits Sean Cruse on guitar, Mike Elizondo on bass and Scott Storch and Camara Kambon on keyboards, with Dre and Richard "Segal" Huredia mixing.

### The Verses

Dre, Hittman, Ms. Roq and Kurupt share the microphone; Mel-Man and Scruncho add vocals. The writing credits also name Marshall Mathers, better known as Eminem, one of the album's documented ghostwriters.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} is the oldest tool in this track. Wikipedia notes that 2001 used fewer samples than The Chronic, its new beats sparser; Let's Get High still builds on two funk records, which is why the writing credits run to nine names.

Each guest here is a {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}: Hittman and Kurupt appear several times on the album, Ms. Roq only briefly, and this track gives all three a turn.

The {{ <concept_link name="drum machine" /> }} is where every Dre track started, according to Time's Josh Tyrangiel: Dre programs a beat in a room full of trusted musicians, asks them to play along and refines what he likes. Scott Storch remembered the same sessions: him on keyboards, Elizondo on bass, Dre on the drum machine.

## Artistic Context

2001 was Dr. Dre's second album, released on 16 November 1999 by Aftermath and Interscope as the follow-up to The Chronic, with eighteen guests. It was first announced as Chronic 2001, and the marijuana leaf beside the title on the cover is a nod to that name. Compare {{ <track_link source="gang-starr-full-clip.md" /> }}, another producer-led rap record of 1999.

## Impact and Reception

Let's Get High was never released as a single, but radio airplay took it onto Billboard's Hot R&B/Hip-Hop chart. The album debuted at number two on the Billboard 200 with 516,000 copies in its first week and has been certified six times platinum; AllMusic's Stephen Thomas Erlewine called it sonically first-rate. Play it loud and count the voices.
