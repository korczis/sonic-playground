## Genre and Mood

**Carte Contact** open the soundtrack album of Jeune Femme with **Like a Dog**, a song that belongs to two worlds at once: the film's music and a small Paris band's indie pop. A 2019 concert listing described the band as a home-studio project making bittersweet, danceable garage pop that turns its torments into mockery, which is where this page's playful and bittersweet tags come from. No published review describes the song itself, so what follows keeps to the credits and the film.

## Song Summary

The label's own upload names the authors: Julie Roué composed Like a Dog and Angèle Chiodo wrote it, and Carte Contact perform it for Léonor Serraille's film. It runs 3:38 and is the first of eleven tracks on the soundtrack album, which Milan released digitally in October 2017. Roué is also the film's composer, so the song stands beside her own score on the same record.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="soundtrack" /> }} album collects the music of a film, and Jeune Femme's mixes several kinds: licensed recordings, songs by other artists and original music. Like a Dog sits in the middle category, a band's song placed at the head of the record.

A {{ <concept_link name="film score" /> }} is the music a composer writes to the film itself. Here the two roles meet in one person: Julie Roué wrote the score and composed this song, and the concert listing credits her synths as part of Carte Contact's sound. ## Artistic Context

Jeune Femme, released in English as Montparnasse Bienvenue, is Léonor Serraille's first feature: Laetitia Dosch plays Paula, who returns to Paris after years in Mexico and is left with no job and no support. It premiered at Cannes in 2017, where it won the Caméra d'Or for best first film, and opened in France on 1 November 2017. Roué trained as a sound engineer at the École Louis Lumière, where she began composing for student films. Carte Contact later released the EP L'élite de la nation in 2020.

## Impact and Reception

Like a Dog was never reviewed on its own, and no chart run is recorded; its audience came through a prize-winning debut film. Listen to it first, then to Roué's score, and hear one composer in two roles.
