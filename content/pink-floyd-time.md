## Genre and Mood

**Pink Floyd**'s **Time** is progressive rock with a psychedelic band's patience: introspective in its words, melancholic in its harmony and intense once the guitar takes over. Rolling Stone's Loyd Grossman heard it in 1973 as a country-tinged rocker with a strong guitar solo, a description that does not prepare you for the clocks.

## Song Summary

Time is the fourth track of The Dark Side of the Moon, released in March 1973 by Harvest in the UK and Capitol in the US. Roger Waters wrote the lyric after realising in his late twenties that he was no longer preparing for life but already living it; the music is credited to all four members, the only song on the album that is. This page is the 2011 remaster of the album, six minutes and fifty-three seconds long. The video is the band's official audio upload, which does not say which master it uses.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

Clocks chime and alarms ring, then Nick Mason plays about two minutes of rototoms over a bass tick-tock.

### Verse 1

David Gilmour sings over F sharp minor, A and E, with his guitar and Richard Wright's keyboards panned to opposite sides.

### Bridge

The texture thickens: Wright takes the lead, Gilmour harmonises, and four female singers add oohs and aahs.

### Guitar Solo

Gilmour solos over the verse and bridge progressions.

### Outro

After a last verse and bridge, an F major chord over a held B bass slides into Breathe (Reprise) in E minor.

## Concepts in Focus

The verse sits in F sharp {{ <concept_link name="minor key" /> }}, and its {{ <concept_link name="chord progression" /> }}, F sharp minor to A to E and back, never settles far from home. The bridge moves to a D major seventh and an A major ninth, chords with an added note that changes their colour, and its second half walks down through C sharp minor and B minor to E major.

The {{ <concept_link name="guitar solo" /> }} is the passage Grossman singled out in 1973. Gilmour plays over the same progressions the voices used, so the solo comments on the song instead of leaving it.

## Artistic Context

Pink Floyd recorded the album at EMI Studios, now Abbey Road, from June 1972 to February 1973, with Alan Parsons as engineer. Parsons had recorded the clocks in an antiques shop as a quadraphonic test, not for the album, and offered the tape when the band was working on the song. Wright plays Farfisa organ, Wurlitzer electric piano and an EMS VCS 3 synthesiser; Waters plays bass; Mason plays drums and rototoms.

## Impact and Reception

Time came out in the US in February 1974 on the B-side of Us and Them. Billboard ranked it ninth among Pink Floyd's greatest songs, Louder fifth, and the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame lists it among the 500 Songs that Shaped Rock and Roll. Start with the clocks, and do not skip the two minutes before the first word.
