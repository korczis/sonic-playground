## Genre and Mood

**Dead or Alive** released **You Spin Me Round (Like a Record)** in November 1984, and it remains one of the purest statements of Hi-NRG: synth-pop and disco pushed to a hard, relentless pulse. It is energetic, bold and theatrical, mechanical and wildly human at once.

## Song Summary

The lyric is a pursuit that turns on the pursuer: the singer sets his sights on someone, warns them he is coming, then admits in the chorus that he is the one being spun around like a record. Pete Burns said he wrote the melody over patterns borrowed from Luther Vandross's I Wanted Your Love and a Little Nell record, though the Guardian's account credits Stock Aitken Waterman with bringing the song to the sessions. The Guardian called the result taut, alien and utterly majestic.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the record bar by bar; this follows the lyric's sections and what the reviews say of them.

### Verses

Burns opens in his commanding baritone over a wholly synthetic backing of drum machines and synthesizers.

### Pre-Chorus

The singer asks to be let in, then issues his warning: playful flirtation with a glint of menace, in the Guardian's hearing.

### Chorus

With the title hook the power shifts: the predator becomes the one spinning, flustered and loving it.

### Climax

Toward the end Burns holds long climactic notes, the part the Guardian called euphoric.

## Concepts in Focus

The song shows what a {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} can be. Mike Stock, Matt Aitken and Pete Waterman ran their studio as the Hit Factory, and this was their first UK number one. Waterman called it techno-disco, new technology playing Motown, and the trio said they wanted nothing in their productions to sound like a recognisable instrument.

That sound rests on the {{ <concept_link name="drum machine" /> }}. MusicRadar places the Linn drum, the cold, digital PPG Wave synthesizer and an early Waveterm sampler behind the Hi-NRG trilogy this single belongs to, and the Guardian hears in it the language Kraftwerk and Giorgio Moroder invented, now codified.

The third ingredient is a {{ <concept_link name="stage persona" /> }}. In the video, directed by Vaughan Arnell and Anthea Benton, the band are tied up while Burns, in backcombed hair and a glittery eyepatch, darts free. The Guardian calls the record one of the great queer pop anthems; compare the very different 1984 statement of {{ <track_link source="bronski-beat_small_town_boy.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Dead or Alive had worked in various line-ups since 1980 around Burns. Their label, Epic, was lukewarm: Burns said he took a loan of 2,500 pounds to fund the recording and that the band paid for the video themselves. After tense sessions, finished according to the Guardian in a 36-hour marathon, it became the lead single of the second album, Youthquake, in 1985.

## Impact and Reception

The single took seventeen weeks to climb from number 79 to number one in the UK in March 1985, reached number 11 in the United States and topped the charts in Ireland, Canada and Switzerland. It returned to the UK top five in 2006, was interpolated in Flo Rida's Right Round in 2009, and has topped critics' rankings of SAW's records. Put it on loud and let it spin you.
