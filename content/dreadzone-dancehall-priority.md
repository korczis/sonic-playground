## Genre and Mood

**Dreadzone** placed **Dancehall Priority** near the end of their 2005 album Once Upon a Time, just before the closing First Steps. Discogs files the album under breakbeat, dub and breaks, Apple Music under electronic, and the band's own history is reggae and sound-system culture. No published review describes the studio recording, so its energy is told here by the stage: in 2023 a Brighton reviewer called it a fine bouncing track.

## Song Summary

Dancehall Priority runs 5:43, the eleventh of twelve tracks on Once Upon a Time. Its title names dancehall, the Jamaican music named after the dance halls where local sound systems played records. The album credits its production, arrangement and electronics to Greg Dread and Ben Balafonic and its vocals to Earl Sixteen and MC Spee; no source says which voice carries this track, and this review does not guess.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through Dancehall Priority section by section, so this breakdown stays with what is documented: the band's sound on the album and the track's life on stage.

### The Record

Rebel Base heard a rougher Dreadzone on Once Upon a Time, a sound that fits MC Spee's vocal work, with Earl Sixteen's velvet reggae voice still striking the imagination.

### On Stage

Live, the track bounces. At the Concorde 2 in Brighton in 2023 the reviewer credited Greg Dread's drumming, rock solid and pounding like a heartbeat.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="dub" /> }} is Dreadzone's foundation: the Jamaican practice of remaking a record at the mixing desk, opening space with echo and dropping parts in and out. The band's sound system ran the Dubwiser club night in London from 1998 to 2001, and Dubwiser later became the name of their own label.

{{ <concept_link name="breakbeat" /> }} is the other half of the album's classification, drum patterns built from broken, syncopated loops rather than a steady kick on every beat. Hear how it sits beside {{ <track_link source="dreadzone-iron-shirt.md" /> }}, from the same album.

The track also exists as a {{ <concept_link name="live recording" /> }}. Live at Sunrise, the band's 2006 live album, carries a version about five minutes long, and Rebel Base wrote that it made the listener long to see Dreadzone live.

## Artistic Context

Dreadzone formed in London in 1993 around Greg Roberts, the former Big Audio Dynamite drummer, credited and billed as Greg Dread. Earl Sixteen, a Jamaican reggae singer whose career goes back to the 1970s, joined in 1995, and MC Spee in 2000. Once Upon a Time came out in 2005 on the independent Functional label; Dubwiser Records has since reissued it digitally.

## Impact and Reception

Dancehall Priority was never a single, and no chart run is recorded. Its reception is its staying power: it was played at the Concorde 2 in 2021 and again at the band's thirtieth-anniversary show there in 2023. Play the studio version loud, then imagine Greg Dread's drums under it.
