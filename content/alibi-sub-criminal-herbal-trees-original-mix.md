## Genre and Mood

**Alibi** and Subcriminal's **Herbal Trees** is jungle by its label's own classification, released on a label that carries the genre in its name. Jungle is the direct precursor of drum and bass, and the feelings here, energetic and groovy, are those the style is made for. ## Song Summary

In Da Jungle Recordings released the track on 18 April 2015 as a digital record, catalogue number IDJR212, with Herbal Trees by Alibi and Subcriminal as the first track and Dani DK's Wine Up Your Body (Whine in the label's track list) as the second. The label's own upload spells the title Herbal Tree; the shops and streaming services call it Herbal Trees and list this version as the Original Mix, which runs 4:53.

### The Breaks

Jungle's engine is the breakbeat: rapid, syncopated drum loops, often sampled from funk and jazz records, the Amen break most famously.

### The Bass

Underneath runs the other half of the genre, deep basslines carried over from dub, reggae and dancehall.

### The Voices

Jungle producers have long borrowed vocal samples and melodies from the same Jamaican sources. Listen for whether this track does.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="breakbeat" /> }} is jungle's defining sound: a drum pattern lifted from an older record and cut up so that kick and snare skip around the beat rather than marching on it. Compare {{ <track_link source="dj-patife-sambassim-dj-patife-remix.md" /> }}, where the same kind of break carries a samba melody.

The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} is where jungle shows its Jamaican roots. One early record often credited with laying the groundwork, We Are I.E., did it with a ragga bassline, and the genre kept that weight under its fast drums.

Both depend on {{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }}: the breaks, and often the voices, come from other records, reused as loops and chops. How much of Herbal Trees is sampled no source says.

## Artistic Context

Jungle grew up in the UK in the early 1990s out of breakbeat hardcore, the British rave scene and Jamaican sound-system culture; ragga jungle, its most reggae-heavy form, became a major style in 1994 and 1995, and drum and bass emerged from it in the middle of the decade. A 2015 release filed as jungle places itself in that older line. The name Alibi is shared by more than one drum and bass act, and no source we found says which made this record; Spotify credits the pair as Alibi and Sub Criminal, the label as Alibi and Subcriminal.
