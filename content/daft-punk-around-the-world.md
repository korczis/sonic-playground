## Genre and Mood

**Daft Punk** made **Around the World** out of almost nothing, and it became one of the founding records of French house. House and electronic minimalism meet a funky, bouncing bassline in a mood that is hypnotic, groovy and iconic: seven minutes that feel like a single, endless, perfectly timed loop.

## Song Summary

The lyric is the title, sung by a vocoder voice again and again; on the album version it is famously repeated well over a hundred times. Around it the duo builds a groove from a rubbery bassline, a crisp drum machine, filtered keyboard riffs and a few disco-flavoured accents. The trick is that the repetition never feels static. Small changes in filter, layering and emphasis keep pulling your ear back in.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

Muffled, filtered drums and bass emerge as if heard from outside the club, the sound slowly opening up as you walk through the door.

### Build-up

The bassline steps forward in full, round and elastic, and the beat locks in with it. It is already irresistible, and the vocal has not even started.

### Main Section

The robot voice begins its chant, "around the world", over and over, riding the groove like another instrument rather than a singer.

### Bridge

Mid-track the arrangement shifts its weight, the bass and keyboard riffs trading places, a breakdown that changes the colour without breaking the spell.

### Climax

All the elements lock together at full strength, the filter wide open and the chant relentless, a peak reached through accumulation rather than any sudden drop.

### Outro

The track slowly pulls its layers back, closing the filter until the groove fades away like the club door swinging shut behind you.

## Artistic Context

The song comes from *Homework*, the 1997 debut album by Thomas Bangalter and Guy-Manuel de Homem-Christo, which helped put French house on the global map. Its video, directed by Michel Gondry, is as famous as the track: troupes of dancers dressed as robots, skeletons, mummies, athletes and swimmers each move to one instrument, turning the arrangement into choreography.

## Impact and Reception

"Around the World" was a major international hit, top ten in the United Kingdom, and it remains one of the defining electronic tracks of the nineties, still played everywhere from festival main stages to wedding parties. It is on this playground as proof that minimalism can be maximal fun. Put it on, let the bassline take over and count how many times you hear the title before you stop counting.
