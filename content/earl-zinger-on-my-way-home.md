## Genre and Mood

**Earl Zinger**'s **On My Way Home** is downtempo for the walk back after a long night, chillout with a wink. The only contemporary description we found is one line from Luna Kafé, which called it quite chilled and old-school Tom Waits-y: relaxed in pace, nostalgic in manner, and playful because of who is supposedly singing.

## Song Summary

The song is the second track and first full song of Put Your Phazers on Stun Throw Your Health Food Skyward, released by !K7 in February 2002. It runs about two and a half minutes. No review describes its arrangement or lyric in detail, so this page describes less and points you to what can be checked: its mood, its singer and its place in the record.

## Musical Breakdown

### Mood

Chilled and old-school are the two words on record, with Tom Waits as the comparison. Listen for what in the song might have reminded the reviewer of him, and decide whether it holds.

### Placing

It follows an intro of just over a minute and is followed by Song 2wo, a dub version of Blur's Song 2. The album moves between dub, funk and crooner ballads, and this chilled song is the first of them.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="stage persona" /> }} is an invented identity a musician performs as. Earl Zinger came with a biography that Luna Kafé laid out with delight: a crooner in 1940s New York, a friend of Burroughs, Ginsberg and Kerouac, the man who advised Robert Zimmerman to call himself Bob Dylan, later a guest of Peter Tosh and Lee Perry in Jamaica. None of it is true. The reviewer called it prank time, and the joke is part of the music: a modern record sung by a man pretending to be an old one.

An {{ <concept_link name="interlude" /> }} is a short piece between an album's main songs. This album uses many: according to its MusicBrainz track list, several of its eighteen tracks last about a minute or less, among them two intros, Ringa Dinga Zinga and Egyptian in the Red. On My Way Home is the first real song after that opening frame.

## Artistic Context

Earl Zinger is Rob Gallagher, the original vocalist of Galliano, the acid jazz group that was the first act on Eddie Piller and Gilles Peterson's Acid Jazz label and the first signing to Peterson's Talkin' Loud. After Galliano broke up in 1997, Gallagher formed Two Banks of Four and recorded solo under names including Earl Zinger. He was also among the collaborators on Koop Islands, the 2006 album by the Stockholm duo Koop.

## Impact and Reception

Luna Kafé found the album humorous, colourful and partly quite a party, even if not all of it holds water. No other review of the song was found. Put it on for the last stretch home.
