## Genre and Mood

**Santigold**'s **Disparate Youth** sits where new wave, electronic pop and art pop meet: a rolling beat, sleek keyboards and stabs of guitar under a voice that stays cool while the lyric urges the listener on. The feeling is defiant and uplifting at once, a call to step out of a world someone else built, delivered calmly enough to sound certain rather than angry.

## Song Summary

Santigold told Pitchfork, as Spin reported, that the song "is about the youth creating their own world" instead of accepting the broken one handed to them. It is credited to Santigold, Nick Zinner of the Yeah Yeah Yeahs, who plays guitar on it, and Ricardo Johnson, and produced by Santigold with Ricky Blaze. She told The Quietus how it began: Blaze sent her a track on which "the bulk of the track was there", and she "just sang the melody in one go".

## Musical Breakdown

### The Beat

Spin's Devon Maloney pointed to the rolling beat as the heart of the song, and Stereogum's Tom Breihan called it "a catchy and bottom-heavy new-wave track". No source maps the arrangement section by section.

### Keyboards and Guitar

Michael Cragg of The Guardian, as Wikipedia summarises the review, heard sleek new-wave keyboards mixed with frantic bursts of guitar, and Spin noted the stabs of guitar.

### The Vocal

Cragg also praised Santigold's intriguingly emotionless delivery, and Maloney called her melody an earworm.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} can hand a singer most of a song before she writes a note. Here Ricky Blaze's track came first and Santigold's melody followed in one take.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, co-directed by Santigold and Sam Fleischner, was shot on a shoestring budget in Portland Parish, Jamaica: she rides a motorcycle through a colonial-era town, a speedboat off the coast, and walks into a jungle with young locals. It was the second video made for the song; she chose not to release an earlier one inspired by A Clockwork Orange.

The song's long life is a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="sync licensing" /> }}. It sold Direct Line insurance on British television from 2012 to 2014, an instrumental version scored a Honda advertisement, and it turned up in The Vampire Diaries, Atypical, Good Girls, the trailer of Unorthodox and the games Forza Horizon and NBA 2K16. Compare {{ <track_link source="lenka-everything-at-once.md" /> }}, which an advertisement also carried.

## Artistic Context

Santi White, born in Philadelphia in 1976, sang in the punk band Stiffed and worked in A&R before her 2008 debut Santogold; the name changed to Santigold in 2009. Master of My Make-Believe, her second album, was recorded in 2010 and 2011 partly in Jamaica and released by Downtown and Atlantic in spring 2012. Disparate Youth followed Big Mouth as a single that February. Her voice already turns up in {{ <track_link source="amanda-blank-a-love-song.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Cragg's verdict was enthusiastic. The song reached number 20 on Billboard's Rock Songs chart and number 26 in Germany but only 96 in the UK, where it has since gone gold, as it has in Germany. Listen to how little the voice has to push.
