## Genre and Mood

**MGMT**'s **All We Ever Wanted Was Everything** is a close cover of a 1982 song by the English post-punk band Bauhaus. Its feelings come from the original's subject: David J of Bauhaus said the song evokes nostalgic memories of a time of innocence and naive yearning, and MGMT keep that dreamy, longing ache while letting a little more light in.

## Song Summary

Bauhaus put the song ninth on The Sky's Gone Out, released by Beggars Banquet in October 1982. SPIN's Marc Hogan described that original as a slow-burner with airy acoustic plucks, impressionistic lyrics and an anti-industrial theme, a song that already had plenty of neo-hippie tendencies. MGMT's recording lasts 4:25. Stereogum's Tom Breihan called it a slightly sunny but mostly very faithful cover, while Hogan heard the band go even more warped and pastoral with a trippy, effects-heavy take.

## Musical Breakdown

No published review or timed analysis walks through the cover part by part, so this section follows only what the critics single out.

### The Vocal

Andrew VanWyngarden sings the lead. Dom Gourlay of Drowned in Sound found the cover not very different from the original, except that the vocal lacks Peter Murphy's icy delivery: the same words, sung warmer and less forbidding.

### The Arrangement

The shape stays Bauhaus's; what changes is the surface, a haze of effects laid over a faithful outline.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} can copy a song or remake it; this one mostly copies, so the lesson lies in the small shifts, above all the singer. The same lyric in a warmer voice carries a different weight.

The cover was made for a {{ <concept_link name="dj mix" /> }}. Late Night Tales: MGMT runs twenty tracks chosen by the band, MusicBrainz lists a continuous mixed version, and this cover is the only recording MGMT contributed. It sits at number eleven, after Mark Fry's {{ <track_link source="mark-fry-song-for-wilde.md" /> }} and before Cheval Sombre's {{ <track_link source="cheval-sombre-troubled-mind.md" /> }}; Gourlay wrote that its mid-point placement splits this well organised mixtape in two intricate halves.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} is animated. Ned Wenlock directed it for Hoverlion, and it was released on 3 October 2011, the day the compilation came out.

## Artistic Context

Andrew VanWyngarden and Ben Goldwasser of MGMT compiled Late Night Tales: MGMT, the 25th volume of the series, released by Night Time Stories on 3 October 2011. Their selection runs from the Velvet Underground and Suicide to the Chills and Spacemen 3. Gourlay called the pair self-confessed Anglophiles with a penchant for Eighties independent rock, and DIY's El Hunt heard the cover as a post-punk re-imagining tucked away among gloomy selections. The song's writers are the four members of Bauhaus.

## Impact and Reception

Stereogum and SPIN premiered the cover in September 2011, Hogan pitching it at fans of the band's zonked-out second album. The compilation received 7.8 out of 10 from Pitchfork and four stars from AllMusic. Play the Bauhaus original right after, and listen for how much of the difference lives in the voice.
