## Genre and Mood

**Gregory Porter** blends jazz, soul and a touch of blues in **Don't Lose Your Steam**, and the atmosphere is uplifting from the first bar. It is an up-tempo song of encouragement, warm and unhurried at the same time, sung by a voice that sounds as if it has every confidence in you.

## Song Summary

Don't Lose Your Steam is a song about keeping your energy and commitment through whatever comes. Porter wrote it himself, and it shows his signature style: a big, rich baritone, a melody that sits comfortably in the voice and an arrangement played by a small acoustic band rather than built in a studio. The core message is resilience, staying committed to your goals and true to yourself, delivered like advice from someone who has been there.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro: Warm and Inviting

The song opens with piano and the rhythm section setting a warm, inviting tone, the band's acoustic sound immediately clear.

### Build-up: Soulful Expression

Porter's voice takes the lead, relaxed and conversational. The arrangement builds gradually, the band filling in around him.

### Main Section: Resilient Message

In the main section the refrain urges the listener not to lose momentum. The steady groove and the repeated title line reinforce the song's message.

### Climax: Powerful Vocals

The emotional peak shows Porter's range and dynamic control, his voice opening up as the band pushes behind him.

### Outro: Inspirational Conclusion

The song ends on an uplifting note, the band easing off while the message lingers.

## Concepts in Focus

Don't Lose Your Steam shows how a {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} can come entirely from players. The album's band, Chip Crawford on piano, Aaron James on bass and Emanuel Harrold on drums, with horns arranged by Kamau Kenyatta and Keyon Harrold, plays together in the room, and the feel comes from how the parts lock: bass and drums steady, piano comping in the gaps, the voice riding on top. The Guardian praised the album's warm spontaneity, as if it had been recorded in six days.

The song also had a second life as a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }}. The French producer Fred Falke, known for filtered French house, reworked it in 2016 into what one critic called a neo-disco makeover, and the remix was included on the album's deluxe edition. A remix like this keeps the voice and changes the beat, carrying a jazz singer onto dance radio. Compare the way a house producer rebuilds a soul vocal in {{ <track_link source="gorgon-city-hard-on-me.md" /> }}.

The official {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Nayip Ramos and produced by Decca Records France, appeared on the deluxe edition's DVD together with a making-of film and a "1 Mic 1 Take" performance, three ways of seeing the same song.

## Artistic Context

Don't Lose Your Steam was the lead single from Take Me to the Alley, Porter's fourth studio album, released on 6 May 2016 by Blue Note in the United States and Decca in Europe. It was recorded in Hollywood and New York in September and October 2015 with the producer Kamau Kenyatta, who had first worked with Porter in the mid-1990s when Porter was a student at San Diego State University. All songs on the album are Porter's own except where noted, and the Guardian's Alexis Petridis noted how proudly they display his gospel roots.

## Impact and Reception

Take Me to the Alley won the 2017 Grammy Award for Best Jazz Vocal Album and scored 81 on Metacritic, and Don't Lose Your Steam, with its video and Fred Falke remix, carried it to listeners beyond jazz. In a 2019 conversation with Christian McBride for WBGO and NPR, Porter discussed it among his most soul-baring songs. It remains one of the clearest introductions to his warm, encouraging style. Put it on when you need a push.
