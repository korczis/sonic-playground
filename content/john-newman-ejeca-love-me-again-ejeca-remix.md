## Genre and Mood

**John Newman** sang it and **Ejeca** remade it: **Love Me Again (Ejeca Remix)** takes Newman's 2013 debut single from northern soul pop into house. Nialler9, writing in May 2013, heard the Belfast producer at his brightest yet. Uplifting and groovy are this page's words for that brightness, with the longing of the original plea still in the voice.

## Song Summary

The original "Love Me Again", written by Newman with Steve Booker and produced by Booker and Mike Spencer, is a plea for a second chance, and it went to number one in the UK. Ejeca's version builds a new track around Newman's voice. Nialler9 named its three ingredients: piano house chords, cut-up vocal samples and bass synth notes. It runs 6:52, almost three minutes longer than the 4:00 of the original on the same release.

### Main Section

According to Nialler9, three things carry the remix. Piano house chords give it its brightness. Pieces of Newman's vocal are cut up and used as samples. Bass synth notes hold the low end. How the remix opens, where its breakdowns fall and how it ends, no source says.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} rebuilds a recording from its parts. This one was released with the single: the digital download of Love Me Again carried it as the fourth of five tracks, beside remixes by Kove, Gemini and Love Thy Brother. Compare the Kove version, {{ <track_link source="john-newman-kove-love-me-again-kove-remix.md" /> }}, which takes the same voice into drum and bass.

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} here means sampling the song itself. Cut-up vocal samples are the singer's own phrases chopped into short pieces and replayed as rhythm, so that a voice can become part of the beat as well as the melody on top of it.

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} supplies the bass. The bass synth notes Nialler9 heard are the house producer's foundation, a sound made electronically rather than played on a bass guitar.

## Artistic Context

Ejeca is Garry McCartney, a producer from just outside Belfast. He told We Are Soundspace in January 2014 that he grew up on jungle, rave and happy hardcore, that you can hear happy hardcore style pianos in some of his tracks, and that he made drum and bass before bringing the tempo down into house. His collaboration with Bicep, You, on AUS Music in 2012 and his tracks Horizon and Night Rays made his name in the 90s house revival, and he was ranked among Resident Advisor's top 100 artists of 2013.

## Impact and Reception

Nialler9 heard the remix as a male companion piece to Frank B's remix of Chain of Fools. Released in the remix bundle on 30 June 2013, it travelled with a single that finished 2013 as the 14th biggest of the year in the UK. Play it after the original and listen to where the piano takes the plea.
