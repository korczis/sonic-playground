## Genre and Mood

**ME & her** and **David Keno** made **Walking Boots** for the club. Discogs files Kitties Go Deep Vol. 2, the compilation that first carried it, under deep house and tech house, and Suara's #BeatportDecade Deep House, which carried it again a few months later, under deep house alone. Groovy is the plainest word for what those styles aim at. Carefree comes from Defected's copy for Bloomingdale 2014, which licensed the track among what it called summer anthems. No published review describes the track itself, so both feelings rest on how labels classified and placed it, not on a listener's account.

## Song Summary

Walking Boots runs six minutes and fifty-six seconds. Discogs lists it as the third track of Kitties Go Deep Vol. 2, a digital compilation the Spanish label Suara released on 10 February 2014 under the catalogue number SCOM013, and Suara's YouTube channel posted the track that week. Defected's notes credit it as written and produced by Me & Her & David Keno and published by Suara, and YouTube's art track credits both as composers and lyricists, so the track has words. I found no published lyrics, so this review does not describe them.

## Musical Breakdown

No published review describes Walking Boots section by section, and this review does not invent a map. The sources give its length, almost seven minutes, which is the length of a track made to be mixed: long enough for a DJ to bring it in and take it out, and for small changes over a steady pulse to carry it. Listen for how it begins and ends, and for where the words come in.

## Concepts in Focus

Much of the track's life has been inside a {{ <concept_link name="dj mix" /> }}. Defected placed it in the club-night half of Franky Rizardo's two mixes for Bloomingdale 2014, after the beach-day half, and the Austrian label Musicpark Records put it on Alpine Grooves Volume VII, a CD that DJ Hoody compiled and mixed in 2015. The digital edition of that compilation, which this page follows, carries the full track under Suara's own recording code. A mix shows how a club track is meant to be used: blended into the record before it and the record after it, rather than heard alone.

Its path also traces a {{ <concept_link name="club culture" /> }} of labels and compilations rather than charts. Suara released the track in its own series, then licensed it to Defected for a beach club's summer mix and later to an Austrian series. Compare {{ <track_link source="simion-roland-clark-lost-club-mix.md" /> }}, another 2014 Suara deep house release that travelled into a compilation.

## Artistic Context

Discogs describes David Keno, the alias of Daniel Andersson, as a DJ and producer born in Switzerland and now based in Germany, who founded the label Keno Records in 2006; his earlier Suara release, Regina, dates from 2010. MusicBrainz lists ME & her as a Swiss artist; Discogs has no profile text, and I found no source that says more. A year later David Keno turned up on Katermukke with {{ <track_link source="mat-joe-david-keno-shamoozey-early-morning.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Walking Boots is not a chart record; its reception shows in its licences. Within two years Discogs lists it on at least four Suara compilations, among them Meeeow! 2014 and Best Of Suara 2014, as well as on Defected's Bloomingdale 2014 and Musicpark's Alpine Grooves Volume VII. Play it as those mixes did, between two other records.
