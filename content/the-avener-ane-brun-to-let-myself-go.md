## Genre and Mood

**The Avener** and **Ane Brun** meet on **To Let Myself Go**, where deep house and electronic production wrap around a folk song's quiet heart. It is melancholic and hypnotic, but the title is the key: underneath the sadness there is a liberating sense of surrender.

## Song Summary

Ane Brun's original is a fragile song about finally allowing yourself to fall, to stop holding on and let feeling take over. The Avener keeps her voice almost untouched and builds a slow, rolling groove beneath it: a soft kick, a warm, round bassline, gentle percussion and patient melodic loops. The effect is like watching a private moment from a slight distance, as the beat carries the confession out into the open air. The memorable detail is the contrast between Brun's bare, trembling voice and the smooth, steady machinery around it, which never rushes her.

## Musical Breakdown

The rework follows the slow-burn logic of a sunset deep-house set, where patience pays off.

### Intro

A muted pulse and a few reverberant notes set a calm, open space, like the sky just before dusk.

### Verse 1

Brun's voice arrives intimate and unadorned, carrying the song's longing over a beat that barely raises its voice.

### Build-up

Bass and percussion gradually thicken, giving the vocal momentum without stealing its focus.

### Chorus

The title line becomes a gentle hook, the groove now fully in motion, the moment of letting go turned into movement.

### Outro

The layers ease back, leaving the voice and the pulse to drift away together into silence.

## Concepts in Focus

To Let Myself Go is a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} in the sense The Avener made his name with: a rework of an existing recording. Ane Brun wrote and recorded the song as the opening track of her 2005 album A Temporary Dive, a bare 3:19 folk song. The Avener keeps her voice and stretches the track past four minutes around it, so the song stays hers while the setting becomes his.

The new setting is a slow {{ <concept_link name="house groove" /> }}. A soft kick on every beat and gentle percussion give the confession a steady pulse, unhurried enough that the vocal never sounds pushed. A reviewer of the album described it as a lesson in smooth, laid-back house, with wobbly keyboard lines, twangy guitar licks and plenty of well-placed basslines.

That {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} is the warm centre of the track: round, rolling and close to the kick, it fills the space Brun's quiet voice leaves. For another fragile voice set on the same kind of groove, compare {{ <track_link source="niklas-ibach-hungry-remix.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

The Avener is the alias of Tristan Casara, a French producer and DJ from Nice, born in 1987, who broke through in 2014 with Fade Out Lines, his rework of The Fade Out Line by Phoebe Killdeer & The Short Straws, a number one in Germany, Austria and Spain. To Let Myself Go comes from his debut album The Wanderings of the Avener, released in France on 19 January 2015, a collection built largely on slow, melodic reworks of folk, blues and soul vocals, and it became one of the album's singles that year alongside Hate Street Dialogue and Panama. Ane Brun, born Ane Brunvoll in Molde in 1976, is a Norwegian singer-songwriter of Sami origin who has lived in Stockholm since 2001 and runs her own label, Balloon Ranger Recordings; A Temporary Dive, the album with the original, debuted at number one in Norway.

## Impact and Reception

The Wanderings of the Avener reached number 2 in France and made his sound one of the signatures of mid-2010s European radio and beach bars, the gentle, sun-faded deep house that took folk voices to new audiences. To Let Myself Go is one of its finest moments, respectful of the original and quietly transformative, and it can send listeners back to Brun's own catalogue, which is a fine thing for any rework to achieve. Play it at the end of a long day and let it do what the title says.
