## Genre and Mood

**Scissor Sisters** wrote **Filthy/Gorgeous** as a dance record with a rock band inside it: hi-NRG and disco in its pulse, pop rock in its guitars and drums. It is provocative by design, energetic from start to finish and playful about both, the slash in its title promising two songs at once.

## Song Summary

The song is the seventh track on the band's self-titled debut and was written by Jake Shears, Babydaddy and Ana Matronic, credited under their legal names, and produced by the band. Doug Rule of Metro Weekly called it a hard-rock dancing ditty and the album's true stunner. Rule heard Babydaddy's background in rock bands and dance production in it, stadium rock merged with disco, and linked the lyric to Shears's earlier work as a go-go dancer in Manhattan. Looking back in 2024, Attitude called it a kinky, high-energy stomper.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} can be cut for more than one audience, and this one was made twice. John Cameron Mitchell directed a full-length, raunchier version with semi-explicit scenes in a sex club, and an edited version in which those scenes are shorter, out of context or obscured, and some are gone. The UK CD single carried the "gorgeous" video and the DVD single the "filthy" one, so the title's two halves became two films.

The single also shows what a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} package does. Next to the 3:48 album version it offered the Paper Faces mix, 8:53 on the 12-inch picture disc, with a 4:26 vocal mix edit on CD, and the I Love You - See You Next Tuesday mix.

## Artistic Context

Scissor Sisters formed in New York in 2000 and produced their debut themselves; Polydor released it in the UK on 2 February 2004, and it became the UK's best-selling album of that year. The song was recorded at 5D Studios in Brooklyn and The Shed in New York and mixed by Neil Harris. On the same album, the band's falsetto disco cover {{ <track_link source="scissor-sisters-comfortably-numb.md" /> }} had been their breakthrough.

## Impact and Reception

Not every critic was won over: Noel Murray of The A.V. Club counted it among the album's trashy dance-floor throwaways. Released on 3 January 2005 as the album's fifth and final UK single, it reached number five, the band's first British top-five single, and number one on the UK Dance chart and the US Billboard Dance Club Play chart; it peaked at 29 in Australia and 13 in Ireland. Play it and decide which half of the title wins.
