## Genre and Mood

**HVOB**'s **Azrael** is melodic techno at its most minimal: a hypnotic, sultry track that lasts seven and a half minutes and spends most of them holding back. Vice heard the album it opens as deep, moody, melodic techno; Azrael is where that mood is set, and it is set through patience rather than force.

## Song Summary

Azrael opens Trialog, the Viennese duo's second album, released by Stil vor Talent in April 2015. Paul Wallner builds the track out of very little, and Anna Müller sings over it in the whispered voice that defines the band. Vice called it a primer on restraint: it does so much with single-note melodies and space that it is not until four minutes in that another note is introduced, let alone Müller's vocals. Textura heard sensuality and tension in the syncopated thrust of its rhythms and its percussive detail.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

The track begins with a single-note melody and room around it. No published account describes the opening sounds in more detail, so listen for how little is there and how long it is allowed to last.

### Build-up

For about four minutes the rhythm carries the track: the syncopated pulse and the percussive detail Textura singled out do the work that a chord change or a hook would do elsewhere.

### Main Section

Only after the fourth minute does a second note arrive, and only then Müller's voice. Vice's point is that this late entry frames the listening experience of the whole album; no source maps the remaining minutes, so this review does not.

## Concepts in Focus

Trialog is a {{ <concept_link name="concept album" /> }} of an unusual kind. HVOB made it with the artist Clemens Wolf and the visual collective lichterloh, and each of its ten tracks is tied to a physical process: tearing, mixing, bursting, melting, oxidising, breaking, imploding, etching, bending and burning. Azrael is tearing, and every piece exists as music, video and installation at once.

The track is also a study in the {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }} without a release. Instead of rising towards a drop, it withholds: a melody of one note, then space, then, four minutes on, a second note and a voice. The tension comes from {{ <concept_link name="syncopation" /> }}, accents that push against the pulse, so the groove feels alive even while the melody stands still.

The official {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, made by Clemens Wolf and lichterloh.tv, is part of the same work: it belongs to the process the track stands for rather than showing the band.

## Artistic Context

HVOB, short for Her Voice Over Boys, are Anna Müller and Paul Wallner, who formed the duo in Vienna in 2012 after playing together in the band Herbstrock. Trialog followed their self-titled debut of 2013. The track is written and produced by HVOB and mastered by Robin Schmidt. Compare {{ <track_link source="oliver-koletzki-through-the-darkness.md" /> }}, another Stil vor Talent release that takes its time.

## Impact and Reception

Vice called Trialog an album-of-the-year contender in April 2015, and C-Heads described Azrael as one of the strongest songs HVOB had ever made. Give it the full seven and a half minutes, and count how long the first note waits for company.
