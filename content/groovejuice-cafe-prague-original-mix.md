## Genre and Mood

**Groovejuice** made **Café Prague** for the club, and Discogs files it under tech house and minimal. Its label promised more than that: sounds never heard in a house track before, a hypnotic jazz piano and snare drums in the style of Gene Krupa, the swing-era drummer. Hypnotic, groovy and playful is this page's reading of that mix, an old colour set inside a steady modern pulse.

## Song Summary

Café Prague is the second release of 120dB Records, issued as a download on 22 January 2010 under the catalogue number DBD 002. Discogs credits Niels Reinhard as its producer, composer, writer and arranger. The "Original Mix" of the title is his own version, the first of three tracks on the release, and it runs six minutes and thirty-two seconds. It has no lyric: what it is about has to come from its title and its sound, and the label's own notes point to the piano and the snare.

## Concepts in Focus

The ground under Café Prague is the {{ <concept_link name="house groove" /> }}: a kick on every beat, at a tempo a room can dance to for hours. Here, according to its label, a piano figure and a snare from the swing era are set on top of it; compare how {{ <track_link source="caravan-palace-lone-digger.md" /> }} builds a whole club track from swing.

The single was born as a family of versions. The 2010 release carried two {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} versions beside the original: a rougher, electro-driven one by the label managers Greg Silver and Chico Chiquita, and Kevin Over's Czech Tech Mix, which the label said added deeper, funky grooves. In 2016 the label returned to it with new remixes by Patrick Hofmann, Lukas Greenberg and Promi5e.

## Artistic Context

The track belongs to a {{ <concept_link name="club culture" /> }} of small labels and download releases. 120dB Records, an EDM and house label in Mannheim, was founded in 2009, and Café Prague was its second release. Niels Reinhard had begun as DJ Groovejuice in Mannheim's student scene around 2004. The same year as Café Prague he scored a hit as half of Laserkraft 3D: Nein, Mann!, also on 120dB, reached number five in Germany.
