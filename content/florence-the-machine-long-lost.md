## Genre and Mood

**Florence + The Machine** recorded **Long & Lost** as the hush in the middle of a loud album. It is an alternative ballad with a soul undertow, built from a few guitars, a distant beat and a voice held back on purpose. The feeling is longing and melancholy, and the intimacy comes from how close and quiet Florence Welch sounds.

## Song Summary

Songfacts sets it beside the song before it on the album: in Delilah, Welch waits for a call that never comes; here, away in Los Angeles, she worries whether her lover will welcome her back when she comes home. She wrote it with Ester Dean, an American songwriter whose credits include Rihanna's Rude Boy and Katy Perry's Firework. Markus Dravs produced and programmed it, and the track lasts 3:15.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes the song section by section or gives times; what follows is what reviewers heard.

### Intro

The guitars set the tone first. Drowned in Sound called them flickering, DIY a lilting, sparse guitar groove; together they describe something that moves without pushing.

### Main Section

Welch sings low. DIY heard her take the vocal volume down and called the result possibly the album's strongest moment, where her true self bleeds through. Under her, Drowned in Sound heard a distant percussive lull rather than a drum part that drives.

## Concepts in Focus

An {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }} is also what is left out. On an album built for size, the reviewers here describe only a few guitars, distant percussion and a lowered voice; the space around the singer becomes part of the song.

A {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} can make that restraint possible. Dravs produced this track and most of the album; Welch told Hot Press he became a father figure who set her hours, down to when to have lunch, and that the quiet, meditative process was good for her.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} pairs the song with Queen of Peace as chapters 5 and 6 of The Odyssey, Vincent Haycock's film series for the album, shot on Easdale, a Hebridean island of fewer than a hundred people. It ends with Welch shipped off the island; Haycock said the last shot was done in a single take on a stormy barge at the very last seconds of light. Hear the same singer at full force in {{ <track_link source="florence-the-machine-delilah.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

How Big, How Blue, How Beautiful was the band's third album, released by Island on 29 May 2015. Welch made it after announcing a year-long break from music, a period she later said brought "a bit of a nervous breakdown"; recording ran mainly in London from February 2014. Long & Lost is its seventh track.

## Impact and Reception

Reviewers kept singling it out: Drowned in Sound called it a soulful standout, while Clash heard understated classic rock. The album entered at number one in the UK and the US and was shortlisted for the 2015 Mercury Prize. Play it after Delilah and listen to the volume drop.
