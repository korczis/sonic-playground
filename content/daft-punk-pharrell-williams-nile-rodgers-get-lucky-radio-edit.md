## Genre and Mood

**Daft Punk**, **Pharrell Williams** and **Nile Rodgers** built **Get Lucky** out of disco and funk, dressed it for the dancefloor and gave it a pop hook the whole world could sing. The mood is pure joy, groovy and celebratory, the feeling of a long night that is only just getting good.

## Song Summary

The song is about staying up until the sun comes up, chasing connection and luck through the small hours. Its construction is deceptively simple: one four-chord progression loops for the whole song, and everything else is feel. Nile Rodgers' chicken-scratch rhythm guitar locks into a live bass-and-drums groove, Pharrell sings with relaxed, airy confidence, and the robots arrive late with their vocoder chorus. The memorable detail is that guitar: crisp, endlessly repeating, impossible to sit still to.

## Musical Breakdown

The radio edit trims the album cut but keeps every essential move.

### Intro

The guitar riff enters almost alone, bright and clipped, and the rhythm section falls in behind it with a warm, unhurried pocket.

### Verse 1

Pharrell's vocal floats over the groove, conversational and smooth, setting the scene of a night that will not end early.

### Chorus

"We're up all night to get lucky" lands as a communal shout, simple enough for anyone to join in by the second time round.

### Bridge

The vocoder section turns the hook into a robotic chant, the moment the song becomes unmistakably Daft Punk.

### Outro

The groove rides on, voices and robots intertwined, until the edit fades with the party still going.

## Artistic Context

"Get Lucky" was the lead single from Random Access Memories, released by Columbia in 2013. For that album Daft Punk turned away from samples toward live studio musicians and the sound of late-1970s records, and bringing in Nile Rodgers, the Chic guitarist behind a generation of disco and pop hits, was central to that idea. Pharrell, then on the cusp of his own huge solo run, gave the song its voice.

## Impact and Reception

The single became a global phenomenon, topping the charts in the United Kingdom and many other countries, and it won Grammy Awards for Record of the Year and Best Pop Duo/Group Performance. More than a decade on, it still fills dancefloors at weddings and clubs alike, which says everything about how well its simplicity holds up. Put it on and see who starts moving first.
