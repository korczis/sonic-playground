## Genre and Mood

**Daft Punk**, **Pharrell Williams** and **Nile Rodgers** built **Get Lucky** out of disco and funk, with a touch of house in the beat, and gave it a pop hook the whole world could sing. The mood is pure joy, groovy and celebratory, the feeling of a long night that is only just getting good.

## Song Summary

Pharrell has said the lyric is about the good fortune of meeting someone and connecting at once, as well as about sexual chemistry; the setting is a night that lasts until sunrise. Its construction is deceptively simple: one four-chord progression loops for the whole song, at a relaxed 116 beats per minute, and everything else is feel. Nile Rodgers' chicken-scratch rhythm guitar locks into a live bass-and-drums groove, Pharrell sings with airy confidence, and the robots arrive late with their vocoder chorus. The memorable detail is that guitar: crisp, endlessly repeating, impossible to sit still to.

## Musical Breakdown

The radio edit trims the six-minute album cut to about four minutes but keeps every essential move.

### Intro

The guitar riff enters almost alone, bright and clipped, and the rhythm section falls in behind it with a warm, unhurried pocket.

### Verse 1

Pharrell's vocal floats over the groove, conversational and smooth, setting the scene of a night that will not end early.

### Chorus

The "up all night to get lucky" line lands as a communal shout, simple enough for anyone to join in by the second time round.

### Bridge

The vocoder section turns the hook into a robotic chant, and Daft Punk lay the pre-chorus hook and the chorus hook over each other: the moment the song becomes unmistakably theirs.

### Outro

The groove rides on, voices and robots intertwined, until the edit fades with the party still going.

## Concepts in Focus

The whole song runs on one {{ <concept_link name="chord progression" /> }}: Bm7, D, F#m7, E, repeated without a break. The violinist and composer Owen Pallett showed in Slate why it never goes stale: most of the time the song sounds as if it is in F-sharp minor, but each time the cycle returns to B minor the ear is briefly tricked into hearing a different key, "a musical Tilt-a-Whirl". Listen to the start of each new cycle and notice the small lurch. Chilly Gonzales pointed out that Daft Punk had used the same progression before, in {{ <track_link source="daft-punk-around-the-world.md" /> }}.

The foundation of the arrangement is Rodgers' guitar {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }}. The song began as a demo built around a Wurlitzer electric piano; Rodgers asked Daft Punk to mute everything except the drums, played until the duo were visibly pleased, and then Nathan East re-recorded the bass to fit his part. "Everybody else wound up re-playing to me," Rodgers said. Rodgers also noted that the title phrase plays off a guitar line he contributed.

Then there is the {{ <concept_link name="vocoder" /> }}, Daft Punk's signature since Discovery. Near the end their robot voices sing the hook, and Consequence described the result as voices becoming "another delicious layer in the instrumental gumbo". The human singer leads; the machines answer.

Finally, this page is the {{ <concept_link name="radio edit" /> }}. The 12-inch single of July 2013 carried three versions side by side: a ten-minute Daft Punk remix, the album version and this radio edit. Comparing them is a quick lesson in how arrangement length serves different listeners.

## Artistic Context

Get Lucky was the lead single from Random Access Memories, Daft Punk's fourth and final studio album, released by Columbia; the single came out on 19 April 2013 after two 15-second teasers aired during Saturday Night Live and a trailer was shown at Coachella. For that album Daft Punk turned away from samples toward live studio musicians and the sound of late-1970s records. They had first met Rodgers, the Chic guitarist behind a generation of disco and pop hits, at a New York listening party for Homework in 1997, and finally brought him into the sessions at Electric Lady Studios. Pharrell had remixed {{ <track_link source="daft-punk-harder-better-faster-stronger.md" /> }} as part of the Neptunes, and Daft Punk had co-produced N.E.R.D.'s "Hypnotize U" before he offered to work with them in any capacity. The song took about eighteen months to finish.

## Impact and Reception

The single topped the charts in France, where it was certified diamond, and in the UK, where it held number one for four weeks and was the second best-selling single of 2013 with over 1.3 million copies. In the United States it spent five weeks at number two, Daft Punk's first top-ten hit there. It won Record of the Year and Best Pop Duo/Group Performance at the 56th Grammy Awards, where Daft Punk performed it with Rodgers, Pharrell and Stevie Wonder. More than a decade on, it still fills dancefloors at weddings and clubs alike. Put it on and see who starts moving first.
