## Genre and Mood

**Gorillaz** end their second album with its title track, and **Demon Days** is the record's last word: alternative pop built on soul and gospel voices, produced, as one fan writer puts it, like dub reggae. It is dark in what it describes and uplifting in where it goes. Stereogum's Ryan Leas hears a final, surging plea carried on gospel melodies, and that turn from night towards light is as close to spiritual as Gorillaz get.

## Song Summary

Damon Albarn imagined Demon Days as a journey through the night, each track a confrontation with a personal demon, and this is where the journey stops. In its final moments, Leas writes, the song talks about seeking oblivion through drugs and entertainment before its plea to pick yourself up. The blog Gorillaz: Song By Song reads the lyric as a picture of cold, dark times and a damaged world, and its message as staying vigilant and turning around. The London Community Gospel Choir sings it with Albarn.

## Musical Breakdown

No timed analysis exists; this follows the fan blog and Audio Snobbery.

### Intro

The song grows out of the one before it, Don't Get Lost in Heaven, whose vocals transition seamlessly into it. An organ lays down the base, strings swell, and the choir's hummed voices come and go.

### Main Section

An electric guitar line played by Albarn takes over and sounds more hopeful. James Dring sets the tempo on a hi-hat, Jason Cox plays a distorted bass guitar, and a reggae-style piano joins them.

### Climax

Audio Snobbery hears a build-up of strings and sustained gospel singing. The choir takes the lead and repeats its line about the sun again and again.

### Outro

The other instruments fade away until only the organ is left.

## Concepts in Focus

Demon Days is a {{ <concept_link name="concept album" /> }}, in Albarn's words a loose one about the world in a state of night; he told MTV it was also an allegory. The title track is the morning that answers it. Compare how {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-bootie-brown-dirty-harry.md" /> }} uses another choir earlier in the same night.

The climax is a {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }} without a drop: strings and voices pile up while the groove stays where it is, so the intensity comes from accumulation.

The fan writer describes the production as {{ <concept_link name="dub" /> }} reggae under a gospel soul song, a huge wall of sound. Listen for the reggae-style piano and the bass under the choir.

## Artistic Context

Albarn and Jamie Hewlett wrote the song; the album was recorded in London in 2004 and produced mainly by Danger Mouse with the band, Jason Cox and James Dring. Its strings came from Demon Strings, led by Isabelle Dunn. Parlophone released Demon Days in the UK on 23 May 2005; it entered the chart at number one.

## Impact and Reception

The title track was never a single, but Stereogum notes that the celestial finale of Don't Get Lost in Heaven and Demon Days became a mainstay of the live show. It has closed the encore, the band filing out until only the choir is left on stage. Play it straight after Don't Get Lost in Heaven.
