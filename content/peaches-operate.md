## Genre and Mood

**Peaches**'s **Operate** is electroclash leaning towards dance-punk: a hip-hop beat with crunchy guitars and synths around it, and a performer at the centre. It is provocative and gritty, as most of her records are, and its chorus has an eerie edge that one reviewer found spine-chilling.

## Song Summary

Operate comes from Fatherfucker, Peaches' second album, which Wikipedia describes as more rock-oriented than The Teaches of Peaches. She wrote it with Sticky Henderson and produced it herself; on the album as a whole she wrote and programmed all the music. Reviewing the album for Gigwise in October 2003, Steve McQueen wrote that Operate is cleverly pieced together from a hip-hop beat, synths and crunchy guitars. What the lyric is about, beyond a chorus that unsettled that reviewer, no source used here explains.

The parts Gigwise names are the whole kit: the beat, the synths, the guitars and Peaches' voice. The album's credits add Gonzales on additional drums, the engineering of Cornelius Rapp at Studio Rapp in Berlin, and, on the label's digital release, Renaud Letang as mixing engineer. Spin's Laura Sinagra summed up the album's balance: for all its amped-up ax-grinding, Peaches can still shake it on the electro autobahn.

## Concepts in Focus

Operate is a compact example of {{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }}, the scene of the late 1990s and early 2000s that fused electro, synth-pop and new wave with punk attitude and a taste for performance. Peaches, a Toronto-born artist then based in Berlin, plays with that persona on the album's cover, where she wears a full beard.

Its widest audience came through a {{ <concept_link name="soundtrack" /> }}: it plays during the Halloween house party in the 2004 film Mean Girls, and it was also used in the film Waiting... and an episode of the TV series Las Vegas.

In 2017 it became a case study in {{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }}. When Taylor Swift released Look What You Made Me Do, some commentators suggested it sampled Operate; Alternative Press wrote that it clearly sounds like the Peaches song, W that its beat sounded similar but cleaner. Swift's liner notes credit a sample of Right Said Fred's I'm Too Sexy and do not list Operate. A resemblance is not a credit.

## Artistic Context

XL Recordings released Operate on 8 September 2003 as a limited double A-side with Shake Yer Dix, the first single from Fatherfucker, which followed on 23 September. The album's opener loops Joan Jett's Bad Reputation, and Iggy Pop duets on Kick It; compare {{ <track_link source="peaches-pickles.md" /> }} for the same artist twelve years on.

## Impact and Reception

The single reached only number 112 on the UK chart, while Gigwise called the album a total groundbreaker. The song outlived its chart run through film, and the 2017 debate brought its beat back into the conversation. Play it loud, and listen for how much of the track is attitude.
