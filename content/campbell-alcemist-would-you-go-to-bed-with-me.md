## Genre and Mood

**Campbell** and **Alcemist** turned **Would You (go to bed with me?)** into drum and bass: a remix of a 1998 pop hit, rebuilt for a 2020s rave. The mood is the original's, playful and provocative, now with the energy of a fast breakbeat behind it. The question is the same as in 1998; the room it is asked in is not.

## Song Summary

The song is one question, and the title spells it out. In 1998 David Lowe built Touch and Go's Would You...? around a sampled woman's voice asking it, words drawn from questions used in a 1978 psychology study, with James Lynch's trumpet and a Latin rhythm underneath. Campbell and Alcemist kept the question and changed everything around it: Songfacts calls their version a drum and bass update of Touch and Go's track. It lasts two minutes.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} reworks a recording into a new version, and this one shows how far the idea can stretch: the same words, a different decade, a different tempo and a different dance floor. Wikipedia describes it as a drum and bass remix that Campbell and Alcemist first played at the Parklife festival in Manchester in 2023.

The original itself was built on {{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }}: Lowe did not hire a singer for the line, he used a sampled voice. That voice is the {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }}, a phrase so short and so direct that it works on any beat. Compare the warmer jazz of {{ <track_link source="touch-go-tango-in-harlem.md" /> }} to hear what the remix leaves out.

## Artistic Context

Alcemist is a South East London drum and bass producer and DJ known for mixing UK garage, house and vocal-led drum and bass, with releases on DnB Allstars, Born On Road, Dread Recordings and Atlantic. Touch and Go's original came out on V2 on 26 October 1998, reached number three in the UK and was later included on the album I Find You Very Attractive.

## Impact and Reception

After its Parklife debut the remix was released by Atlantic and reached number 29 on the UK Singles Chart, where Songfacts counts ten weeks; a version featuring the singer Caity Baser followed. David Lowe's own site credits the rework with taking the song back into the Spotify charts. Play the 1998 original first, then this.
