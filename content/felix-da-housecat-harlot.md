## Genre and Mood

**Felix Da Housecat** put **Harlot** first on Kittenz and Thee Glitz, an album Wikipedia files under house, electroclash and electro house. The feelings named here, energetic and gritty, come from the two contemporary reviews that describe the track: one heard electro-house beats with an amazing energy, the other a fuzzed bassline.

## Song Summary

Harlot is the album's opening track, listed on the original release as Harlot (Intro) and running just over three minutes. Felix Stallings Jr. wrote, produced and arranged it; the voice is Melistar's, and the single's credits add Electrikboy on synthesizer and Dave the Hustler on the mix. Pitchfork read the album around it as a concept record about the price of fame and the lack of privacy that comes with it, and a track called Harlot opens it.

## Musical Breakdown

No source gives a timed account of the track, so this breakdown follows what the reviews heard rather than the clock.

### The Beat and the Bass

The track rests on its low end. Pitchfork's Paul Cooper heard a fuzzed bassline close to Thomas Heckmann's techno track Amphetamine; Gullbuy's reviewer heard electro-house beats with real energy.

### The Voice

Melistar sings over it. Cooper wrote that the album introduced her to the world, and heard her delivery as a blank imitation of Miss Kittin's vacant tone. Gullbuy simply called the track a terrific opener with her guest vocal.

### The Handover

As an intro, Harlot hands over to Walk With Me, the second of sixteen tracks on the original album.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }} joined 1980s electro and synth-pop to a detached, deadpan vocal style, and Kittenz and Thee Glitz is one of its landmark albums. Harlot shows the style before Miss Kittin appears at all: a flat, cool voice over cold electronics.

The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} is where its grit lives. A fuzzed bass can carry a track as much as a vocal hook can.

The single shows the {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} at work. City Rockers released Harlot on 15 October 2001 as an edit, the Thee Glitz Extended Mix and a seven-minute remix by Justin Robertson.

## Artistic Context

Felix Stallings Jr. belongs to the second wave of Chicago house; his first single came out in 1987 under the guidance of DJ Pierre. Kittenz and Thee Glitz came out in the UK on City Rockers in July 2001 and in the US on Emperor Norton in 2002; it was made with Tommie Sunshine, Miss Kittin, Dave the Hustler, Melistar and others, and Wikipedia says it mixes early-1980s electronic music with house.

## Impact and Reception

The Harlot single reached number 79 in the UK, between Silver Screen Shower Scene at 39 and What Does It Feel Like? at 66. The album scored 78 on Metacritic, won Felix Best Album at the Muzik Awards and stood at 73 in Resident Advisor's top 100 albums of the 2000s; Pitchfork gave it 4.2 out of 10.
