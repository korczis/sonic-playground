## Genre and Mood

**Adam Freeland** made his name as a breakbeat DJ, but **Do You** sounds like a rock band that has been plugged straight into a sound system. Breakbeat, electronic production and rock energy collide in a mood that is energetic, rebellious and gritty, three and a half minutes of sweat and distortion.

## Song Summary

The song throws its title at the listener like a challenge over a rhythm that keeps kicking forward. It opens with a distorted melodic synth line before live drums barge in, and Kurt Baumann's vocal rides on top with a rock singer's attitude. What makes it memorable is the tension between the two worlds: the precision of club production and the looseness of a drummer playing in a room.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

A bright, melodic synth figure sets the stage, already fuzzed at the edges, and you can sense that it will not stay pretty for long.

### Verse 1

Live drums crash in, heavy and propulsive, and the vocal enters with real bite. The groove has the push of breakbeat but the weight of rock.

### Chorus

The hook hammers the title home, band and synths stacked into a dense wall while the drums keep pushing. It is built for fists in the air.

### Bridge

The electronics briefly take the front again, a reminder that this is a producer's record before the band returns for the final run.

### Climax

Everything returns at full volume, the band and the machines finally working in the same direction, the energy at its most raw.

### Outro

The track stops almost abruptly, leaving a ringing silence that feels like the moment after a mosh pit clears.

## Concepts in Focus

Freeland comes from {{ <concept_link name="breakbeat" /> }}, the syncopated, funk-derived drumming of the breaks scene he helped lead. On Do You that background is translated into a band: instead of a sampled break, a live drummer (Tommy Lee, credited on the album) plays the push, so the rhythm keeps its breakbeat lurch but gains the heft and small irregularities of a person hitting skins.

The other lesson is how a {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} can stand in for a guitar. The track opens on a melodic synth line, and what makes it feel like rock rather than electronica is {{ <concept_link name="distortion" /> }}: the signal is driven until it grits and growls, gaining the harmonics and aggression rock fans expect from an overdriven amp. Resident Advisor heard the same strategy across the album, a synth roar in place of a guitar riff; compare it with {{ <track_link source="freeland-under-control.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Adam Freeland, born in Welwyn Garden City in 1973, started DJing in 1991 and became one of the best-known names of British breaks; he runs the Marine Parade label. On his albums he is credited simply as Freeland. "Do You" opens *Cope*, his second album, released on 8 June 2009 on Marine Parade after his move to Los Angeles. He wrote and produced it with Alex Metric, Kurt Baumann sings lead, and the album's guests included Tommy Lee, Twiggy Ramirez, Joey Santiago of Pixies and Devo's Gerald Casale. "Do You" followed "Under Control" as the album's second single on 29 June 2009.

## Impact and Reception

*Cope* split the critics: BBC Music and NME were positive, while Resident Advisor gave it two stars and doubted that its icy professionalism was the anti-consumerist statement Freeland intended. mxdwn's reviewer called "Do You" one of the best songs on the album, singling out the way its melodic synth distortion gives way to live drums. It belongs on this playground next to "Under Control", another moment where Freeland's breakbeat roots meet rock muscle. Turn it up loud and answer the question for yourself.
