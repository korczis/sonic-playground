## Genre and Mood

**Adam Freeland** made his name as a breakbeat DJ, but **Do You** sounds like a rock band that has been plugged straight into a sound system. Breakbeat, electronic production and rock energy collide in a mood that is energetic, rebellious and gritty, three and a half minutes of sweat and distortion.

## Song Summary

The song throws its title at the listener like a challenge over a rhythm that keeps kicking forward. It opens with a melodic synth line before live drums barge in, and the vocal rides on top with a rock singer's attitude. What makes it memorable is the tension between the two worlds: the precision of club production and the looseness of a band playing in a room.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

A bright, melodic synth figure sets the stage, almost pretty, and you can sense that it will not stay that way for long.

### Verse 1

Live drums crash in, heavy and propulsive, and the vocal enters with real bite. The groove has the swing of breakbeat but the weight of rock.

### Chorus

The hook hammers the title home, band and synths stacked into a dense wall while the drums keep pushing. It is built for fists in the air.

### Bridge

A brief breakdown lets the electronics take over again, the beat filtered and the tension coiled for the final run.

### Climax

Everything returns at full volume, the band and the machines finally working in the same direction, the energy at its most raw.

### Outro

The track stops almost abruptly, leaving a ringing silence that feels like the moment after a mosh pit clears.

## Artistic Context

Adam Freeland, credited simply as Freeland on his albums, was one of the leading figures of British nu-skool breaks and runs the Marine Parade label. "Do You" opens *Cope*, his second album, released in 2009 on Marine Parade, a record that leaned heavily toward live instrumentation and rock songwriting. He co-wrote the track with Alex Drury, better known as Alex Metric, and it was released as a single shortly after the album.

## Impact and Reception

*Cope* drew solid reviews in the music press, and critics singled out "Do You" as one of its strongest songs, praising the way the synth opening gives way to the live drums. It belongs on this playground next to "Under Control", another moment where Freeland's breakbeat roots meet rock muscle. Turn it up loud and answer the question for yourself.
