## Genre and Mood

**Hanayo**'s **Joe Le Taxi** is a cover that drags a French pop hit into the club. It sits between electro and electroclash, the scene of the early 2000s that dressed 1980s pop in raw machines. A Sputnikmusic reviewer heard the song given a hardcore electro-club bite; playful and energetic suit a cover that keeps its sweetness and adds teeth.

## Song Summary

Joe le taxi was written by Franck Langolff and Étienne Roda-Gil for Vanessa Paradis, who released it in April 1987. Its Joe is a Paris taxi driver who knows every little bar, every street, the Seine and its bridges, and has a taste for rum and Latin music; Xavier Cugat and Yma Súmac are name-checked. It spent eleven weeks at number one in France and the next year reached number three in the UK. Hanayo's version closes her album Gift, released on 23 November 2000, and is credited to her with the Cologne producer Jürgen Paape.

### Place on the Album

On the CD, Joe le taxi shares the last track with Hanayo's version of Kimigayo, the Japanese national anthem, which the same review called her spin on it. The digital edition gives Joe le taxi its own five minutes.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} can stay faithful or change a song's genre. This one moves a 1987 chart-topper into the world of electronic club music, made by a Japanese artist living in Berlin with a producer from Cologne's techno scene.

A {{ <concept_link name="mashup" /> }} lays one record's vocal over another's music. On As Heard on Radio Soulwax Pt. 2, the 2manydjs album Wikipedia calls the first official mash-up album, The Jets' a cappella Crush on You sits on Hanayo and Paape's Joe le taxi. The Dewaele brothers are also the band in {{ <track_link source="soulwax-e-talking.md" /> }}.

{{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }} is where the track travelled: MusicBrainz lists it on The Electroclash Mix of 2003, and on fashion-week and Colette compilations.

## Artistic Context

Hanayo, born Hanayo Nakajima in 1970, is a Japanese musician, photographer and artist. She trained as a geisha in Tokyo from 1989, formed the band Muscats with Merzbow in 1992, and moved to Berlin in 1997 to work with the director Christoph Schlingensief. Gift brought in guests including Merzbow, Kai Althoff, Terre Thaemlitz and Woodman; its German edition came out on Geist Records, a label of Digital Hardcore Recordings. Paape is a co-owner of Kompakt.

## Impact and Reception

In 2002 the German label Zeitgeist released Joe le Taxi as a single with a radio edit, an extended mix and a DJ S.P.U.D. remix. Wikipedia names Hanayo among the artists who have covered the song. Play Paradis first, then Hanayo, and hear thirteen years of pop pass by.
