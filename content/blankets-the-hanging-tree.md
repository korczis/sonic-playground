## Genre and Mood

**Blankets** takes **The Hanging Tree**, the folk song from The Hunger Games, and reimagines it as electronic music, a cover that keeps the haunting core and adds a dancefloor pulse. The mood is haunting, hypnotic and defiant: the eerie refrain of the original, now pushed forward by a beat.

## Song Summary

The song's lyrics come from Suzanne Collins' Mockingjay, a dark folk riddle about a meeting at a hanging tree at midnight, and its film version became a rebel anthem in 2014. Blankets' version understands that the melody is what made the original so powerful, simple, repetitive and unsettling, and builds around it with electronic production that turns its slow menace into momentum. It sits somewhere between a cover and a remix, honouring the song's chill while making it move.

## Musical Breakdown

The track follows the familiar arc of the song but trades the orchestral crescendo for an electronic build and release.

### Intro

The cover opens quietly, with the haunting melody presented over sparse, atmospheric sounds, keeping the song's folk eeriness intact.

### Verse 1

Romane Salomon's vocal carries the strange, riddling verses, close and intimate, while a programmed pulse begins to stir underneath.

### Build-up

Synths and percussion rise around the voice, the tension climbing as the refrain repeats.

### Chorus

The beat takes over and the melody becomes a hook, the defiance of the original translated into a driving electronic groove.

### Outro

The production recedes, returning the song to something close to its bare, unsettling beginning.

## Artistic Context

Blankets is a French act; the recording credits the producer Thibaut Fournier, who programmed and produced it, and the singer Romane Salomon. The Hanging Tree, released in March 2015 through Mercury Music Group, was presented as Blankets' first single, accompanied by an official video directed by Léo Schrepel for Baghera Films. It arrived in the wave of covers that followed the huge success of the version by James Newton Howard and Jennifer Lawrence, whose recording was written by The Lumineers' Jeremiah Fraites and Wesley Schultz from Collins' lyrics. The single was followed by remixes, including versions by Mozambo and Cherokee.

## Impact and Reception

The original's success opened the door for dozens of reinterpretations, and Blankets' version stood out by moving the song decisively into electronic territory rather than simply re-singing it. It belongs on this playground next to the original so you can hear how far a single melody can travel, from a riverbank in a dystopian film to the dancefloor. Listen to both and compare how each one builds its tension.
