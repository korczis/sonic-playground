## Genre and Mood

**Hindi Zahra**'s **Beautiful Tango (Remastered Version)** is a song where world music, folk and the intimacy of a singer-songwriter meet in a slow, swaying dance. It is romantic and soulful, sung so close and so softly that it feels like a private confession shared across a small, candlelit room.

## Song Summary

Despite its title, this is not a tango in the ballroom sense but a love song that moves like one, two people circling each other, drawn together and pulled apart. A lilting acoustic guitar with a hint of desert blues, soft hand percussion and gentle bass leave plenty of air around Zahra's husky, jazz-tinged voice. The memorable detail is her phrasing, unhurried and sensual, bending each line as if she were improvising it on the spot.

## Musical Breakdown

The arrangement is spare and organic, letting the voice lead every step.

### Intro

A gently plucked guitar sets a lilting, slightly melancholy rhythm, warm and inviting.

### Verse 1

Zahra's voice enters low and smoky, almost conversational, with light percussion keeping time underneath.

### Chorus

The melody opens up, her voice rising with a soft ache as the guitar strums more fully, the dance at its most intimate.

### Bridge

A short instrumental passage lets the guitar and percussion take over, the groove swaying like a slow walk at dusk.

### Climax

The final refrains bring a little more intensity, layered voices and fuller rhythm without ever losing the song's tenderness.

### Outro

The instruments fall back and the song closes on a gentle, lingering phrase, as if the dancers are reluctant to let go.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} for Beautiful Tango was directed by Tony Gatlif, the French-Algerian filmmaker known for films about Roma music and travelling cultures, and shot in Fès. It gave a new singer an instant visual world: a song about two people circling each other placed in the streets of an old Moroccan city, which tied her English-language song to her North African roots.

The version on this page is labelled remastered, which is a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="remastering" /> }}. A remaster reprocesses the finished recording at the last stage before release, adjusting loudness, tone and dynamics, often for a reissue. It does not change the arrangement, the performance or the balance between the guitar, percussion and voice; that would be a remix. Listen for the details a remaster can bring forward, such as the texture of the guitar strings and the breath in the voice, but expect the same song.

## Artistic Context

Hindi Zahra was born in 1979 in Khouribga, Morocco, into a family of Amazigh (Shilha) artists from the Souss, and moved to Paris at fifteen. Her mother and uncles introduced her to Gnawa music, reggae and Bob Dylan's folk; she has named influences from Cheikha Rimitti and Umm Kulthum to Billie Holiday, Nina Simone and Jimi Hendrix, and she started out in Paris as a soul backing singer. A self-taught multi-instrumentalist, she had written about fifty songs by 2005. Beautiful Tango first appeared on her 2009 EP and was released as a single the same year; it then opened her debut album Handmade, released by the jazz label Blue Note in January 2010. Its video was directed by Tony Gatlif and filmed in Fès. The remastered version presented here gives the original recording a fresh polish without changing its character.

## Impact and Reception

Handmade made Zahra one of the breakthrough voices of world music in France: it won the Prix Constantin in November 2010 and the Victoires de la Musique award for best world music album in February 2011, sold more than 135,000 copies, 75,000 of them in France, and took her on a tour of more than 300 concerts. Beautiful Tango was its calling card. It sits on this playground as one of the most seductive songs in the collection. Dim the lights and let it sway you.
