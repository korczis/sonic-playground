## Genre and Mood

**Gin Wigmore** teams up with **Suffa** and **Logic** on **Willing To Die**, a song that welds soul-singer grit to hip-hop verses and an alternative rock weight. It is defiant, intense and bold, an anthem of loyalty pushed to its absolute limit.

## Song Summary

The theme is right there in the title: devotion so complete that you would put your life on the line for it. Wigmore carries the hook with her trademark rasp, hard and fierce, while the two guest rappers bring their own takes on the same promise in the verses. The production is heavy and dramatic, built on booming drums, dark tones and a sense of rising pressure. The memorable detail is the way her voice cuts through the mix like a blade, raw enough to make the vow believable.

## Musical Breakdown

The track is structured like a hip-hop collaboration, with a sung hook framing the rap verses.

### Intro

A brooding, heavy groove sets a tense, cinematic stage before the first voice arrives.

### Verse 1

The first rap verse lands with weight and precision, adding a gritty, grounded perspective to the song's promise.

### Chorus

Wigmore's hook explodes over the beat, defiant and full-throated, spelling out exactly what is at stake.

### Main Section

The second guest verse switches up the flow and pushes the energy higher, two very different voices circling the same vow.

### Climax

The final return of the chorus brings everything together at full force, Wigmore's voice riding the loudest moment of the track.

### Outro

The song ends abruptly and powerfully, leaving the vow ringing.

## Artistic Context

"Willing To Die" appears on Blood to Bone, Gin Wigmore's third album, released in 2015. The New Zealand singer had already moved from the retro soul of Holy Smoke to the rougher blues-rock of Gravel & Wine, and this album pushed further into darker, heavier and more rhythm-driven sounds, with hip-hop as a strong influence. Suffa is a member of the Australian hip-hop group Hilltop Hoods, which makes the collaboration a natural meeting of two antipodean scenes.

## Impact and Reception

The collaboration showed how well Wigmore's voice works against rap verses, and it stands among the boldest moves on the album. It sits on this playground as an example of an artist refusing to stay in one lane. If you like your anthems loud and uncompromising, turn this one all the way up.
