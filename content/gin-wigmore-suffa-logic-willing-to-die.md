## Genre and Mood

**Gin Wigmore** teams up with **Suffa** and **Logic** on **Willing To Die**, a song that welds soul-singer grit to hip-hop verses and an alternative rock weight. It is defiant, intense and bold, an anthem of loyalty pushed to its absolute limit.

## Song Summary

The theme is right there in the title: devotion so complete that you would put your life on the line for it. Wigmore carries the hook with her trademark rasp, hard and fierce, while the two guest rappers, the American Logic and Suffa of Australia's Hilltop Hoods, bring their own takes on the same promise in the verses. The production is heavy and dramatic, and under it lies a sample of "Rosie", a work song from the Alan Lomax field recordings. The memorable detail is the way her voice cuts through the mix like a blade, raw enough to make the vow believable.

## Musical Breakdown

The track is structured like a hip-hop collaboration, with a sung hook framing the rap verses.

### Intro

A brooding, heavy groove sets a tense, cinematic stage before the first voice arrives.

### Verse 1

The first rap verse lands with weight and precision, adding a gritty, grounded perspective to the song's promise.

### Chorus

Wigmore's hook explodes over the beat, defiant and full-throated, spelling out exactly what is at stake.

### Main Section

The second guest verse switches up the flow and pushes the energy higher, two very different voices circling the same vow.

### Climax

The final return of the chorus brings everything together at full force, Wigmore's voice riding the loudest moment of the track.

### Outro

The song ends abruptly and powerfully, leaving the vow ringing.

## Concepts in Focus

Willing To Die is built on {{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }}: it reuses part of "Rosie", a work song sung by an African-American chain gang at the Mississippi State Penitentiary and recorded by the ethnomusicologist Alan Lomax in 1948. Because the sample is part of the composition, Alan and John Lomax appear in the writing credits next to Wigmore, her co-producer Stuart Crichton and the two rappers. The same recording was sampled by David Guetta on "Hey Mama" that year, a reminder that an archive sound can travel into very different songs.

The structure is classic hip-hop collaboration: a sung {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} framing rap verses. The hook has to be strong enough to hold the song together between two guest voices, and Wigmore's rasp makes it so; it returns as the anchor after each verse, the part a listener remembers. Compare her solo singing on {{ <track_link source="gin-wigmore-new-rush.md" /> }} from the same album.

## Artistic Context

"Willing To Die" was released on 6 June 2015 as a promotional single from *Blood to Bone*, Gin Wigmore's third album, and appears on it as a hidden track. The New Zealand singer recorded the album in about two weeks in August 2014 at Blakeslee Recording Studios in North Hollywood, produced it with Stuart Crichton, and wrote it after ending an engagement and moving from Australia to Los Angeles. She had already moved from the retro soul of *Holy Smoke* to the rougher blues-rock of *Gravel & Wine*, and this album pushed further into darker, more rhythm-driven sounds. Logic is the American rapper Sir Robert Bryson Hall II; Suffa, Matthew Lambert, is a member of the Australian hip-hop group Hilltop Hoods.

## Impact and Reception

*Blood to Bone* went to number 1 in New Zealand and number 13 in Australia. The collaboration showed how well Wigmore's voice works against rap verses, and it stands among the boldest moves on the album. If you like your anthems loud and uncompromising, turn this one all the way up.
