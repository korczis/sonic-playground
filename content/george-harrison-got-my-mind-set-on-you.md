## Genre and Mood

**George Harrison**'s **Got My Mind Set on You** is pop rock with a grin: a cover that turns a forgotten R&B single into a bright, upbeat, playful hit, catchy from its first line.

## Song Summary

Rudy Clark wrote the song, and James Ray recorded it in 1962 as I've Got My Mind Set on You; it never became a hit. Harrison heard it in 1963 while visiting his sister in Illinois and kept the song with him for almost a quarter of a century. In 1987 he recorded it at his home studio at Friar Park with Jeff Lynne as co-producer, and it became the lead single of Cloud Nine. It is the only song on the album he did not write or co-write.

## Musical Breakdown

No source maps the recording bar by bar; what is documented is how it was built.

### The Groove

Harrison said the song came about because Jim Keltner started playing that drum pattern and the song seemed to fit right on it. Mix tells how a kick and snare from Keltner's own recordings went into an E-mu Drumulator for a four-bar loop.

### The Band

Lynne added bass, an Oberheim OB-X synthesizer and an E-mu Emulator II; Harrison played electric and acoustic guitars; Jim Horn added saxophones and Ray Cooper percussion.

### The Voices

Harrison sang lead and, with Lynne, backing vocals that engineer Richard Dodd stacked, some looped and moved elsewhere in the song.

## Concepts in Focus

As a {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }}, Harrison's recording keeps the song and changes the feel: writing in Salon, Kenneth Womack heard Harrison tighten it up considerably, with a bright sound against Ray's jazzy original. Keltner's driving beat, he adds, is what reminded Harrison of the song. Compare {{ <track_link source="boney-m-sunny.md" /> }}, another 1960s soul song remade for a new decade.

The {{ <concept_link name="drum machine" /> }} here plays real drums: Keltner's sounds, loaded into a sampling machine. The final patterns were programmed during the mix; real drums were also recorded, Dodd recalled, but everyone was used to the demo sound by then.

The backing voices show {{ <concept_link name="double tracking" /> }}: on these sessions Dodd stacked up to nine vocal parts, sometimes triple- or quadruple-tracked, and Mix hears the result as Beatles-influenced but closer to Lynne's ELO.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} did the rest. In the second, directed by Gary Weis, Harrison plays a Telecaster in a study whose furniture sings and dances while a dance double performs his backflip; it earned three MTV Video Music Award nominations.

## Artistic Context

Harrison had stepped back from recording in the early 1980s to focus on his film company, Handmade Films. Cloud Nine, released on 2 November 1987 on Dark Horse, was his comeback, made with Keltner, Ringo Starr, Eric Clapton, Elton John and others; in 1988 he and Lynne began the project that became the Traveling Wilburys.

## Impact and Reception

Released in October 1987, the single reached number one in the US, Canada, Australia and Ireland and spent four weeks at number two in the UK. It was Harrison's third US number one and his last, and remains the last by a former Beatle as a solo artist. Watch the second video, then hear James Ray's original.
