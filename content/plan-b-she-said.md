## Genre and Mood

**Plan B**'s **She Said** is soul with a rapper's spine and a pop single's reflexes: a sung, high-register soul song in the 1960s mould, crossed with a rapped interlude that ties it to hip-hop, and the most successful single of its author's career up to then. The feelings are soulful and catchy on the surface, theatrical in the way it is staged, and dark underneath, because the sweet tune carries the moment its narrator's life falls apart.

## Song Summary

She Said is the second single and the fourth track of The Defamation of Strickland Banks, Ben Drew's 2010 concept album about a sharp-suited British soul singer who finds fame with bitter-sweet love songs and loses it when he is sent to prison for a crime he did not commit. In the album's story the song is the turn. The night out of the previous single, Stay Too Long, ends in a one-night stand; the woman turns out to be obsessed with Strickland's music and believes she is in love with him. He rejects her, she accuses him of rape, and the trial puts him in prison. The title is her claim, reported in his voice.

Drew described the episodes to Contactmusic.com, as Songfacts quotes him: She Said starts with Strickland back in the hotel, and suddenly he is in court and she is accusing him of something he has not done. The record sounds lighter than its plot. Songfacts calls it a finger-snapping tune, The Daily Telegraph heard a thumping Motown groove across the album, and the credits list a string section and a horn section around a small band.

The detail that makes it memorable is that contrast: a high, pleading melody, the kind a soul singer would write for a love song, used to tell the worst day of that singer's life.

## Musical Breakdown

No source times the passages of She Said, so this breakdown follows the parts the critics and the credits name rather than a clock.

### The Band and the Snaps

The pulse is the finger snaps that give the song its character, over a rhythm section of Richard Cassell on drums, Eric Appapoulay on bass and backing vocals and Tom Wright-Goss on guitar. The three of them share the writing credit with Drew.

### Sung Verses and Chorus

Drew sings the verses and the chorus as Strickland, in what Alexis Petridis of The Guardian called a high, aching croon that adds an appealing touch of self-doubt to the hardest-hitting lyrics. Martin Skidmore of The Singles Jukebox, who had not liked his singing before, heard it here as soulful and very feminine; Alex Ostroff praised a smooth upper register. Ian Mathers, who scored it low, found the same quality too much falsetto, along with too much musical restraint.

### Strings and Brass

Around the voice sit the orchestral colours. Sally Herbert arranged the strings, played by four violins, two violas and a cello; Jason Yarde arranged the brass and played alto and baritone saxophone, with tenor saxophone, trombone, two trumpets and flugelhorn beside him. Skidmore found the strings nice; Alfred Soto summed the record up as Adam Levine meeting a string section.

### Rapped Interlude

Once, Drew drops the character's croon and raps. Ostroff, who liked Plan B's nimble tongue on the mic, still found the interlude left-field without the album's context, and Iain Mew noted that its lyric brings in the Zutons' Valerie.

### Stops

Skidmore heard the song as thin and noted that it more or less stops in one or two places. Listen for those breaks: they are where the song's tension sits, between the sweetness of the tune and what the story is about.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="concept album" /> }} tells one story across its tracks. The Defamation of Strickland Banks runs from Love Goes Down, a love song to Strickland's girlfriend, through Writing's on the Wall, Stay Too Long and She Said, into prison with Welcome to Hell, Hard Times, The Recluse and Prayin', and ends in court again with What You Gonna Do, the verdict left open. Drew made the singles and their videos episodic, so each single is a chapter; She Said is the one where the story turns.

Strickland Banks is a {{ <concept_link name="stage persona" /> }}. Drew had abandoned a hip-hop follow-up to his debut and once planned an album half rapped by Plan B and half sung by his soul character; the label found that too confusing, so the sung half became this record. The persona let a rapper known for spectacularly violent soliloquies sing sweet soul in someone else's voice.

The {{ <concept_link name="falsetto" /> }} and the high head voice are what critics argued about. To Petridis the high register was the reinvention's strength; to Mathers it was too much. Listen to the chorus and decide whether the high voice sounds like pleading, seduction or fear.

{{ <concept_link name="rapping" /> }} returns only in the interlude, and that is its point: the album's original plan split rap and soul between two narrators, and She Said keeps a trace of that split inside one song. Compare a soul record that never leaves its band, {{ <track_link source="amy-winehouse-rehab.md" /> }}, and another song about a woman's claim and a man's denial, {{ <track_link source="michael-jackson-billie-jean.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Plan B is Benjamin Paul Ballance-Drew, raised in London, with Forest Gate in east London given as his origin. He taught himself guitar at 14, wrote R&B love songs, then turned to rap at 18; he has said he called himself Plan B because the sweet-boy singing never felt comfortable. His debut, Who Needs Actions When You Got Words (2006), made with Paul Epworth, Fraser T Smith and the Earlies, was a hard-edged hip-hop record, and his first top-ten single came in 2009 with End Credits, a collaboration with Chase & Status.

He wrote soul songs such as Love Goes Down while supporting the Roots on tour in 2006. Epworth's demo of Writing's on the Wall shaped the album's sound, but most of it was recorded with David McEwan and Eric Appapoulay at The Sanctuary in London, over more than two years. 679 Artists and Atlantic released She Said as a download and CD single on 24 February 2010, with remixes by 16bit and Shy FX. The album followed on 12 April 2010.

Daniel Wolfe directed the video, produced by Partizan according to IMVDb, with Plan B as Strickland in court after the events of the Stay Too Long video, Vicky McClure as his girlfriend and Kaya Scodelario as the woman who accuses him. Promonews described it as gritty but cut with fantasy and musical theatre: Chicago comes to Southwark Crown Court.

## Impact and Reception

She Said entered the UK Singles Chart at number 3 on 4 April 2010 and spent eight weeks in the top ten, returning to number 3 in May. It reached number 1 on the UK R&B Chart and number 2 in Ireland, his first top ten there, and charted in Austria, Belgium and Denmark. It was the sixteenth best-selling British single of 2010 and was later certified double platinum in the UK and gold in Germany and Italy. The album debuted at number 1 and, by 2018, had sold 1.4 million copies in the UK.

At the 2011 Ivor Novello Awards it won PRS for Music Most Performed Work, credited to Drew, Appapoulay, Cassell and Wright-Goss, on the night Drew also won the Album Award and Songwriter of the Year. Songfacts adds the Music Producers Guild's UK Single of the Year. At the 2011 Brit Awards, where he won Best British Male, he performed She Said in a medley with Prayin'. Critics were split, from three to eight out of ten on The Singles Jukebox, and Drew himself said he thought he had written a cool album track and found he had made a complete pop song, one he was proud of and half hated.
