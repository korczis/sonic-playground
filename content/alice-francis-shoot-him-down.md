## Genre and Mood

**Alice Francis** sings **Shoot Him Down!** in the style she built her name on: the swing and Charleston of the 1920s mixed with hip-hop and electro, a blend usually filed as electro swing. Discogs files the album under electronic and jazz, swing and electro. The mood is defiant and theatrical: heartbreak turned into a threat.

## Song Summary

The lyric, printed under the official video, is a revenge fantasy. The man in it is a ticket for a ghost train; he called her his baby girl while in bed with another woman. She wants to choke him, squeeze him and break his neck, and in the chorus, with nothing left but heartache, she is about to go insane and take her gun to shoot him down. Francis wrote the words and the music with the brothers Johann and Robert Niegl; Goldielocks and the Niegl Bros produced it, and Rob Kaspar mixed it.

## Concepts in Focus

Francis performs with a {{ <concept_link name="stage persona" /> }}: she is also known as Miss Flapperty, after the flappers of the 1920s, and names Josephine Baker and Django Reinhardt beside Beyoncé as influences. Electro Swing Thing describes her singing, sighing and purring between rap songs; the persona lets a murder threat be sung as a period piece.

The credits list scratches by Herman Schulte, so {{ <concept_link name="turntablism" /> }}, a hip-hop technique, sits inside a song dressed as a 1920s dance number.

The {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} is how many people found the song. Parov Stelar made his own version of the single and released it on his label, Etage Noir, with a club and a radio version beside two by Milk & Sugar. Francis says it went through the roof.

## Artistic Context

Alice Francis was born in Timișoara, Romania, to a Romanian mother and a Tanzanian father, and lives in Cologne with her band, Goldielocks and Chul-Min Yoo. St. James Ballroom, her debut, came out on 7 September 2012 through Universal Music, on the Boutique label; Shoot Him Down! was its first single.

## Impact and Reception

Francis says the album caused a big stir in the electro-swing scene, and Electro Swing Thing counts Shoot Him Down! among the band's hits and lists a tour with Parov Stelar among its milestones. Listen for the scratching under the swing.
