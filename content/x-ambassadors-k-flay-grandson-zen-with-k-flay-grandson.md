## Genre and Mood

**"Zen"** puts three acts on one song: **X Ambassadors**, with Sam Harris singing, Casey Harris on keyboards and Adam Levin on drums, and two guests, **K.Flay** and **grandson**, both singers and rappers. Wikipedia files it under indie rock, rap rock and pop rock. The feelings are cathartic and introspective: Sam Harris told Atwood Magazine the song felt timely and cathartic, and its subject is the inside of a stressed mind.

## Song Summary

The song was written soon after the COVID-19 lockdowns began in March 2020 and deals with mental health in quarantine: loneliness and the wish to get back to what used to feel normal. Harris summed it up as finding peace in chaos, and, in the label's words, the three wanted to talk about what stresses them out. The making of it matches the subject. According to the label, Harris sent a rough sketch to grandson, who wrote a verse; within 48 hours K.Flay had added one too; and everyone finished the track over FaceTime, Zoom, email and text. X Ambassadors produced it, and it was released on 15 May 2020.

## Musical Breakdown

Sources describe the song's materials and its ending, not every section, so the breakdown follows them.

### The Verses

Each guest brings a verse of their own, so the voice changes as the song moves between Harris, grandson and K.Flay.

### The Arrangement

The label hears kinetic beats and moody guitar work; Casey Harris plays keyboards and Adam Levin drums.

### The Ending

The chorus stops abruptly, and the song ends on zen-like music, a calm the rest of the track keeps out of reach.

## Concepts in Focus

The song shows {{ <concept_link name="verse-chorus form" /> }} shared between artists. The chorus is the common ground that comes back each time, while the verses are where each voice speaks for itself. The abrupt stop of the last chorus makes the form audible: you notice it because it is broken.

A {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} is credited beside the main act; here the title itself says "with K.Flay & grandson". Harris told Atwood Magazine he had suggested a supergroup; X Ambassadors had already worked with K.Flay on "Confidence".

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, released with the song, was directed by K.Flay with Evgenia Strukova. It uses a collage style to show each artist's quirks and anxieties in lockdown and ends with all three singing and dancing to the final chorus.

## Artistic Context

X Ambassadors released Zen through Kidinakorner and Interscope. K.Flay, born Kristine Flaherty, made her name with alternative singles such as {{ <track_link source="k-flay-blood-in-the-cut.md" /> }}; Harris counts her as part of his community in the Los Angeles alternative scene. grandson, Jordan Benjamin, is an American-Canadian singer and rapper whom Harris called politically active and a longtime favourite.

## Impact and Reception

Before release, a phone number played a woman asking callers whether they needed some zen. Afterwards, the #zenchallenge hashtag asked people in quarantine to focus on mental health, mindfulness and gratefulness, and also pointed to activist groups including Black Lives Matter. Wikipedia lists the song at number 24 on Billboard's Alternative Airplay chart. Play it with headphones and wait for the quiet at the end.
