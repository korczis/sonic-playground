## Genre and Mood

**D12** close the main part of D12 World with **Good Die Young**, and for once the crew best known for shock and comedy sounds melancholic and reflective. It is still hip-hop in the group's usual format, five rappers taking turns over one beat, but the subject is grief: the song was recorded in memory of Bugz, the member who was killed in 1999.

## Song Summary

It runs five minutes and fifty-six seconds, the twentieth song on the album and the last before the bonus track. The five members other than Eminem take part, and the Chicago Maroon called the result a moving tribute to a fallen member. PopMatters heard a tribute to those lost to the traumas of inner city life. The production, by Mr. Porter with Essman as co-producer, is built on a sample of Screen Kiss, a song from Thomas Dolby's 1984 album The Flat Earth.

## Musical Breakdown

No source gives times or a map of the song's sections, so these notes stay with the credits and the reviews.

### The Beat

The bed of the song comes from another world, a 1984 Thomas Dolby album track turned into a rap production. PopMatters found the result sluggish; whether the pace reads as dragging or as mourning is for you to decide.

### The Verses

Bizarre, Kon Artis, Swifty McVay, Kuniva and Proof carry the song. Eminem mixed it with Steve King but does not rap.

### The End of the Album

The Indiana Daily Student noticed that D12 finally get introspective on the disc's last song. The jokes come first; the grief is what the album leaves you with.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" text="Sampling" /> }} lets a producer lift a piece of an existing recording and build a new track on it. Screen Kiss was never a single, and Dolby's album had nothing to do with Detroit rap; a producer heard a mood in it and gave it a new subject.

A {{ <concept_link name="posse cut" /> }} usually shows off a crew's voices and boasts; here the same relay becomes a group remembering one of its own. Hear it against the title track {{ <track_link source="d12-d-12-world.md" /> }}, the same five members in their irreverent mode.

The {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} is himself a member: Mr. Porter is Denaun Porter, who also raps as Kon Artis.

## Artistic Context

D12 formed in Detroit in 1996 around Proof. Bugz, Karnail Pitts, was shot after a dispute at a picnic in May 1999, XXL recalls, and Eminem joined the core line-up after his death; the group recorded its debut, {{ <track_link source="d12-devils-night.md" /> }}, in his memory. D12 World followed on Shady and Interscope on 27 April 2004. It was the last D12 album with Proof, who died in 2006.

## Impact and Reception

The song was never a single, and the critics split. The Chicago Maroon was moved; PopMatters granted it good intentions but found it too sluggish and formulaic to hear more than once; the Indiana Daily Student doubted that a group clowning for so long could be taken seriously. Listen to Good Die Young after the rest of D12 World and decide whether the sincerity lands.
