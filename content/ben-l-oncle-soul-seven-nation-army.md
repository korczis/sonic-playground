## Genre and Mood

**Ben l'Oncle Soul**'s **Seven Nation Army** takes the White Stripes' stomping garage-rock riff and makes it a soul record. Aurgasm called it an uptempo soul cover and his sound French retro soul with a modern edge; The New LoFi heard soul, funk and reggae fused together. Groovy and upbeat, it trades the original's menace for movement.

## Song Summary

Jack White's song is built on a riff he played on a semi-acoustic guitar through a pitch pedal, an octave down so that it sounds like a bass, and that riff became a football chant sung across Europe. Ben l'Oncle Soul keeps the song and dresses it in the manners of 1960s soul, the register his whole debut album worked in. No source describes the arrangement in detail, so the best guide is the comparison: same words, same seven notes, different music around them. This page covers the remastered edition (Remasterisée) of the album track; the video carries the original 2009 release.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} keeps a song's melody and words and changes its performance. Here the change is a whole genre: a rock song from 2003 recast in the style of soul from four decades earlier, by a singer whose nickname came from the bow tie he often wore.

The {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }} is what makes the cover work. A short, memorable figure like White's survives any arrangement, which is why it carries a soul band here as easily as it carries a stadium, and why {{ <track_link source="the-white-stripes-seven-nation-army.md" /> }} and its many covers stay recognisable within a few notes.

{{ <concept_link name="remastering" /> }} is what separates this edition from the first release: the Spotify edition of the album labels the track Remasterisée. A remaster changes loudness and tonal balance and leaves the arrangement alone; no source says when or by whom this one was made.

## Artistic Context

Ben l'Oncle Soul, born Benjamin Duterde near Tours in 1984, first recorded the song for his debut EP Soul Wash: Lesson 1 in 2009, a set of six covers, and signed with Motown France, part of Universal Music. It then opened his self-titled debut album, released by Motown on 17 May 2010 and produced by Guillaume Poncelet and Gabin Lesieur, in French and English. The clip on his own channel credits Vincent Défossé and Benjamin Raimbault as directors.

## Impact and Reception

Released as the album's lead single, promotionally in March and commercially in September 2010, it reached number 16 in Wallonia and charted in Germany, the Netherlands and Switzerland, while the album went to number four in France and triple platinum there. Aurgasm called it a major hit of that summer. Play it beside the White Stripes' original and listen for what the soul version changes.
