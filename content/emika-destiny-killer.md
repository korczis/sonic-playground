## Genre and Mood

**Emika**'s **Destiny Killer** is electronic music at its most shadowed: dub in its space and experimental in its shape. The feelings named here come from the critics. Ibiza Spotlight heard eerie piano chords; Spectrum Culture heard confusion and discontent. Together they make the track dark and eerie, an ending that does not resolve.

## Song Summary

Destiny Killer closes DREI, the album Emika released on her own Emika Records on 4 May 2015 after writing it, reportedly, in a single two-week period and producing and mixing it herself. She described the record as being about freedom and coping with it: the madness of being alone, ambitions and insecurities, the perils of a limitless creative mind. Spectrum Culture heard this track as a statement of that theme, with dizzying electronics and interwoven lyricism standing for the confusion that artistic limits bring. It runs three minutes and 46 seconds.

Spectrum Culture adds the dizzying electronics and the way the words are woven into them rather than set apart.

## Concepts in Focus

To call a track dubbed out is to borrow from {{ <concept_link name="dub" /> }}, the Jamaican practice of rebuilding a record in the studio with echo, reverb and drop-outs. Ibiza Spotlight placed DREI in Emika's Berlin mix of dark dub pop and electro, and on this track the dub is in the space, not the reggae.

Because Emika produced and mixed DREI herself, the {{ <concept_link name="mixing" /> }} of that space is hers too: how loud the piano sits, how near or far the voice is placed.

In December 2017 she released an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }}, Destiny Killer (Instrumental), on Drei (Instrumentals & A Capellas). Hearing it shows how much of the confusion Spectrum Culture describes lives in the words.

## Artistic Context

Emika, born Ema Jolly in 1986, grew up in Milton Keynes, studied classical piano and composition, and moved to Berlin in 2006. After two albums for Ninja Tune she founded Emika Records, where the piano album Klavirni came out in January 2015; FACT counted DREI as her fourth album. The album opens with {{ <track_link source="emika-battles.md" /> }} and ends here.

## Impact and Reception

Ibiza Spotlight made DREI its album of the week, and ended its review on this track. It earns its place here as a closing statement made by one person from writing to mixing. Play it last, after the rest of the album, where the album puts it.
