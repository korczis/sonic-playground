## Genre and Mood

**Kelis**'s **Milkshake** is R&B with hip-hop production and a funk riff, sultry, playful and supremely confident. About.com called it a low-beat dance song; Slant Magazine called it thick and creamy. Its beat, a lone hand drum, set it apart from the drum-machine urban pop of its day.

## Song Summary

Milkshake was the lead single from Kelis's third album, Tasty, released in the US on 25 August 2003 by Star Trak and Arista. Pharrell Williams and Chad Hugo, the Neptunes, wrote and produced it; the idea came when the album's title was chosen, and Kelis knew right away it was good and wanted it as the album's first single. The chorus, built on the line about her milkshake bringing the boys to the yard, is a double entendre she never pinned down: she told The Observer the word means whatever people want it to, and the Associated Press that a milkshake is what gives women their confidence. It was recorded at Hovercraft Studios in Virginia Beach and mixed by Phil Tan in New York.

## Musical Breakdown

No source times the song section by section; these are the layers its description names.

### The Beat

Instead of a drum machine, the rhythm comes from a single darbuka, a goblet drum, playing a pattern close to belly dance.

### The Bell and the Riff

A sampled manjira, a pair of small hand cymbals, rings like a diner's order-up bell throughout, under a riff described as blaring synth-funk.

### The Chorus

The hook returns again and again, delivered in what The Guardian called Kelis's husky croon.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} here is one line, half nursery rhyme, half innuendo. MTV News called it a sexy, euphemistic playground rap: simple enough to chant, open enough to argue about.

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} supplies the bell. The manjira sample works as percussion and as a joke about the diner the song imagines. The song later became a source itself: the bridge of Doja Cat's Mooo! (2018) samples it, with the yard swapped for a farm.

The Neptunes show how much a {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} can make from very little: one hand drum, one bell, one riff and a voice. Stylus Magazine wrote that it might be remembered as their masterpiece. Compare their gentler work on the same album in {{ <track_link source="kelis-sugar-honey-iced-tea.md" /> }}.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Jake Nava, sets the song in a diner called Tasty's Yard, where Nas plays the cook and a milkshake machine ends up spraying the customers.

## Artistic Context

Tasty came out in December 2003 with producers including the Neptunes, Dallas Austin, Raphael Saadiq and Dame Grease. Slant heard Kelis as a post-millennium Donna Summer, with the Neptunes as a hip-hop Moroder and Bellotte.

## Impact and Reception

Milkshake reached number three on the Billboard Hot 100, her highest US chart position to date, number two in the UK and number one in Ireland. It was nominated for the Grammy for Best Urban/Alternative Performance; Pitchfork named it the eighth-best single of 2003. It reached Mean Girls in 2004 and a Gap campaign with Katseye in 2025. Turn it up for the bell.
