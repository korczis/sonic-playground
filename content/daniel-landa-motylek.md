## Genre and Mood

**Daniel Landa** packs a whole crime story into **Motýlek**, under three minutes of Czech rock from a singer Czech Wikipedia files under rock and folk rock. The mood is dark, since the story ends in a shoot-out, and romantic, since it is told as a love song to the woman who dies in it.

## Song Summary

The narrator addresses a girl he calls dangerous, after a bank job that went, he boasts, without much shooting. A roadside hotel she picked becomes a trap, the police come at dawn, she is shot, and he talks to her as she dies, half tender and half joking, before following her. What the arrangement sounds like, no source we found describes; the story is the documented part.

## Musical Breakdown

No review describes the music part by part, so this breakdown follows the published lyric, whose choruses share a closing line while their words change.

### Verse 1

The couple, the stolen money and the luck that, in the narrator's words, is right on their heels.

### First Chorus

The hotel by the road, a symbol of misfortune in his words, and a closing line, sung twice, about a night of fun in the white bed.

### Verse 2

He saw a car parked outside and got scared; now the police climb to the windows as in a horror film.

### Second Chorus

The refrain proper: the dive where the two are done for, in rough colloquial Czech, dawn coming and bullets flying. The closing white-bed line returns, now saying that they will shoot her there.

### Verse 3

She is hit. He tells her not to leave yet, hopes it does not hurt much and promises her a new pistol for her name day.

### Outro

After the refrain, the last lines: she looks asleep and no longer hears the sirens, and as shots crack through the dark he flies after her. The refrain closes the song.

## Concepts in Focus

Motýlek follows {{ <concept_link name="verse-chorus form" /> }}: verses move the story forward, and the chorus keeps its shape while everything around it falls apart. Its last line is the clever part: the white bed stays, but the night of fun becomes the place of the shooting.

A {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is the line a song is built to leave in your head. Here the obvious candidate is the refrain about the dive, which comes back three times, the last time to end the song.

Motýlek was one of three songs from Chcíply dobrý víly to get a {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, with Ztracení hoši and Včera mě někdo…; Warner Music Gold now publishes it as the official video. What it shows, no source we found describes.

## Artistic Context

Landa, born in Prague in 1968, graduated in 1988 from the Prague Conservatory's music and drama course and that year co-founded Orlík, an Oi! band whose skinhead image and some lyrics drew criticism. His solo career began with Valčík in 1993. Chcíply dobrý víly, his second solo album, came out in 1995 on Monitor-EMI, with Motýlek as its third track.

## Impact and Reception

Chcíply dobrý víly became Landa's best-selling album, more than 106,000 copies by 2000, with gold and platinum discs. Motýlek returned on Best of Landa in 2000 and in a live version on Vltava Tour in 2003.
