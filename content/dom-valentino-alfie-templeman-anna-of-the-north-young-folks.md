## Genre and Mood

**Dom Valentino**'s **Young Folks** takes Peter Bjorn and John's whistled indie-pop hit and rebuilds it as jungle, the breakbeat-driven ancestor of drum and bass. It is energetic where the original was easygoing, but it keeps the bittersweet, slightly nostalgic heart that made the song last.

## Song Summary

The song comes from Peter Bjorn and John's 2006 album Writer's Block: a duet in which two world-weary people meet in a bar and cautiously explore a connection. Valentino, a South London producer releasing his debut solo single in October 2023, gave the two parts to Alfie Templeman and Anna of the North and set them over what Clash's Robin Murray called crunching jungle breaks and heavy duty low end, a rugged re-work that leans toward sound-system culture.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the rework part by part; this follows what the coverage describes.

### The Rhythm

Where the original ran on a sparse beat with no cymbals, this version runs on fast, chopped breaks, the defining sound of jungle.

### The Bass

The low end does the heavy lifting, the sound-system weight Clash heard under the whole track.

### The Duet

Templeman and Anna of the North recreate the original's back-and-forth between two voices, and with it, in Clash's hearing, its bittersweet feel.

## Concepts in Focus

This is a {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} of the transforming kind: the melody and lyric stay, the genre changes completely. Compare it with the source, {{ <track_link source="peter-bjorn-and-john-young-folks.md" /> }}, and notice how much of a song's character lives in its rhythm.

That rhythm is the {{ <concept_link name="breakbeat" /> }}. Jungle grew out of chopped, syncopated drum breaks played fast, and Clash placed this record beside newer junglists such as Nia Archives, a sign that a new generation has taken the style up again.

It is also a lesson in the {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}. Valentino is the artist, but the voices belong to two guests, each with a career of their own, who step into a duet first sung by Peter Morén and Victoria Bergsman.

## Artistic Context

Before this single, Valentino worked behind other artists: The Line of Best Fit lists production for Greentea Peng, Unknown T, Ragz Originale and dexter in the newsagent, and a contribution to Skepta's Mercury Prize-winning album Konnichiwa. Alfie Templeman is an English singer-songwriter and producer born in 2003, known for indie and disco-tinged pop. Anna of the North is the project of the Norwegian singer-songwriter Anna Lotterud, an electropop and dream-pop artist whose deluxe edition of Crazy Life includes this collaboration.

## Impact and Reception

Clash greeted the full studio version as an overhaul one for the ages. Play it after the original and hear the same two shy voices suddenly running at sound-system speed.
