## Genre and Mood

**Florence + The Machine** named their third album after **How Big, How Blue, How Beautiful**, and the title track is its widest moment: indie pop and art rock that Bearded Gentlemen Music heard as lush, almost baroque pop. It is uplifting in sound and nostalgic in its words, a goodbye sung under an open sky, and that mix is what makes it bittersweet.

## Song Summary

The title comes from Los Angeles. In a 2015 NPR interview, the host noted that it refers to the city's skyline, and the lyric places the singer between a crucifix and the Hollywood sign. Welch told NPR that the year she wrote the album was one of unravelling and inner conflict, and that in writing it she came back to herself in a different way. Gigwise heard the song's nostalgia in a line about meeting someone in another life because this one was not enough. Welch wrote it with Isabella Summers, the Machine of the band's name, and Markus Dravs produced it.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

When the band first shared a fragment of the song in a teaser video in February 2015, Refinery29 described an extremely lush, minimal track. No source maps the opening in detail.

### Main Section

The arrangement is big on paper: the album credits list strings, French horns, trumpets, flugelhorn, flute, harp and piano on this track. uDiscover Music counted nearly two dozen stringed instruments and noted how smoothly the song moves between its quieter and louder segments.

### Outro

The ending is the song's signature. Bearded Gentlemen Music heard swelling strings and a long instrumental coda; Refinery29 heard the voice echo above a growing brass section before everything cuts out.

## Concepts in Focus

The song is a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }}. Welch said she chose Dravs partly because he was good with trumpets and she knew she wanted a brass section on the record. On this track the brass was arranged by Will Gregory, credited as Will Owen, the strings by Ali Helnwein, and Sally Herbert conducted. The Line of Best Fit called it one of Will Gregory's wonderful brass scores.

A {{ <concept_link name="coda" /> }} is a closing passage that follows a song's main material. Here it is long and wordless, so the feeling goes on after the lyric has ended.

The deluxe edition adds a {{ <concept_link name="demo" /> }} of the song, produced by Summers. Compare {{ <track_link source="florence-the-machine-caught.md" /> }} from the same album, where the band holds back instead.

## Artistic Context

The song is track three of How Big, How Blue, How Beautiful, released by Island Records on 29 May 2015. Welch has said she wanted the album to be big but gentle and warm, built more on live instruments, almost band-led, rather than a second Ceremonials.

## Impact and Reception

The album went to number one in the UK and the US and was shortlisted for the 2015 Mercury Prize. Opinions on the finale split: Gigwise found the explosion of brass, for once, unnecessary, while The Line of Best Fit called the track astonishing. Gigwise also saw Welch open her Radio 1 Big Weekend set with it. Play it loud and stay until the brass falls silent.
