## Genre and Mood

**Get Ready for the Young Folks** by **Controller 7** is a mashup: two existing records laid on top of each other until they sound like one. It sits between electronic production, hip-hop beat culture and 1960s soul, and its mood is energetic and nostalgic at once, a dance call from 1966 riding a groove from 2006.

## Song Summary

Controller 7, the San Jose producer Tommy McMahon, took the a cappella of the Temptations' "Get Ready" and set it over the rhythm section of "Young Folks" by the Swedish band Peter Bjorn and John. "Get Ready" was written and produced by Smokey Robinson as an up-tempo answer to a dance craze of the day and became a number one R&B hit in 1966; "Young Folks" was the first single from Writer's Block in 2006, recorded on a low budget in bassist Björn Yttling's small Stockholm studio, with a minimal beat inspired by Daft Punk's "Robot Rock" and borrowed bongos. The pleasure of the mashup is how naturally the Motown vocal sits in that loose, dry groove.

## Lyrics Breakdown

### Intro: Setting the Scene

The track opens on the Young Folks rhythm, the bare drums, bass and percussion that made the original sound homemade and light on its feet.

### Verse 1: The Call to Adventure

Eddie Kendricks's high lead voice from "Get Ready" enters. Heard over a twenty-first-century indie groove, the 1960s vocal sounds both older and fresher than it does in its original setting.

### Chorus: Embracing the Moment

The "get ready" refrain is a call to the dancefloor, and it lands on the Young Folks groove with uncanny timing. This is where the mashup convinces: two arrangements made forty years apart lock together.

### Verse 2: The Joy of Freedom

The second verse keeps the same balance, the Temptations' vocal riding a rhythm section that never changes its easy pace.

### Bridge: A Moment of Reflection

Where the vocal thins out, the Young Folks groove carries the track on its own, and you can hear the indie record underneath the soul song.

### Final Chorus: A Celebration of Youth

The refrain returns for a last round, and the record ends at about four minutes.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="mashup" /> }} works only when two recordings agree on tempo, key and phrasing closely enough that the listener stops hearing the join. Controller 7's Discogs entry describes side A exactly: the rhythm section of one record combined with the a cappella of another. A soul blogger writing in 2009 found it "eerie" how well the arrangements line up. Listen for the places where the vocal lines fall into the gaps of the groove, and for any moment where the two records seem to pull apart.

Mashups are a particular kind of {{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }}: instead of borrowing a fragment, they borrow whole parts. That is also why they live outside the usual channels. This one came out in October 2007 on a 7-inch single on the small label Token Recluse, in a limited edition of 500 copies, with a second mashup, "I'm Ready to Shake", on the B-side.

The vocal is led in {{ <concept_link name="falsetto" /> }} by Eddie Kendricks, the Temptations' high tenor, whose light, floating voice was Motown's sound for joy and longing. Against the dry, low-key Young Folks beat, that falsetto becomes the brightest thing in the track.

## Artistic Context

Tommy McMahon was born in San Jose in 1979, started playing drums in 1988, and by 1992 had moved on to pause-tape beats made from drums and an out-of-tune player piano. Get Ready for the Young Folks is not on his debut album Left Handed Straw, released on Anticon in 2000: it appeared on the 2007 Mash Up single on Token Recluse, which is also where MusicBrainz lists the recording.

## Impact and Reception

Mash Up was a limited pressing, and the track was passed around by music blogs such as Soul Sides in the late 2000s, the high point of the mashup era. Both of its sources were hits in their own right: "Get Ready" topped the US R&B chart, and "Young Folks" reached the top 40 in several European countries and Canada. Listen once for the Temptations, once for Peter Bjorn and John, and once for the place where they become one song.
