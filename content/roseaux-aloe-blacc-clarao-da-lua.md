## Genre and Mood

**Roseaux** and **Aloe Blacc** recorded **Clarao Da Lua** for an album of covers that crossed soul with blues, folk and jazz, and this one reaches further, to a song from Brazil. Its title means the glow of the moon, and the page files it as soul, a cover and Brazilian, dreamy and warm. ## Song Summary

The song was written by Almirzinho Gabriel, the composer credited both on Roseaux's track and on "Clarão De Lua" on Nazaré Pereira's 1988 album Ritmos Da Amazonia, which Discogs files under samba and forró. Roseaux's credits name three arrangers, Clément Petit, Emile Omar and Alex Finkin, and Finkin also produced, recorded and mixed it. The voice is Aloe Blacc's, as on every track of the album.

## Artistic Context

Roseaux was formed around Emile Omar, a former DJ and Radio Nova programmer who spotted Aloe Blacc long before his solo success, with Alex Finkin and Clément Petit. Qobuz says the quartet recorded its album of covers from the underground black music repertoire in 2007, but it came out only on 12 November 2012, after I Need a Dollar had made Blacc famous, on Fanon and Tôt ou Tard. Nazaré Pereira, born in 1940 in Xapuri, in the Brazilian state of Acre, lives in Paris; French Wikipedia singles out her Clarão de lua.
