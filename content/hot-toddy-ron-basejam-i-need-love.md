## Genre and Mood

**Hot Toddy** and **Ron Basejam** made **I Need Love** a disco record that refuses to hurry. It sits where nu-disco meets house, but at a pace below 100 BPM, and Juno Daily heard it as a dark, moody piece of disco-noir. Sultry is the tempo, longing the vocal, dark the colour.

## Song Summary

Hot Toddy is Chris Todd, a Nottingham-based guitarist and producer in the band Crazy P, and this was his first solo release in three years. The voice belongs to Ron Basejam, the alias of James Baron, with whom Todd founded Crazy P in the mid-1990s after they met at the University of Nottingham. The single came out on the Belgian label Eskimo in autumn 2009, with the original at five and a half minutes on one side and Morgan Geist's Love Dub on the other. Eskimo had already teased it on the compilation Cosmic Balearic Beats Volume 2; the upload above comes from that compilation, at the single's length.

## Musical Breakdown

No source gives times or a full structure; this guide follows Resident Advisor's review by Terry Fonseca.

### The Pocket

The track moves deliciously slow on purpose, he wrote, letting you luxuriate in each kick, hearing an imaginary drummer trying hard not to rush. It is a deep pocket: the beat sits back rather than pushing.

### The Vocal

That space gives room to Basejam's line, which Fonseca called a mantra, so that it takes on its full weight. Juno Daily heard a deep croon that takes the track to another level.

### The Lift

Then piano, synths and strings come in and amp things up to almost unbearable levels, a climax reached at walking pace.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} here is about patience. Slowing a disco beat below 100 BPM does not make it lighter: it lengthens every gap between kicks, which is why RA could luxuriate in each one. Compare the slow-motion pulse of {{ <track_link source="mario-basanov-we-are-child-of-love.md" /> }}.

A {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} usually brings a name from outside. Here the guest is the producer's bandmate, and the credit marks a role, singer rather than producer, more than a new partnership.

The B-side is a {{ <concept_link name="dub" /> }} in the dance-music sense. Morgan Geist's version, for RA, almost completely disregards the vocal and lets horns, kick drums and guitar lines carry the meaning; Juno Daily heard a deep, rumbling bassline bounce off twangy key stabs.

## Artistic Context

Crazy P built their sound from soul, disco and house, and Todd has remixed many artists as Hot Toddy. Eskimo, founded in Ghent in June 2000, has a sound Textura summed up as the loose, funky feel of space-disco with its feet on the ground; the label's five-disc box Eskimonde placed the Geist dub of this single second on its first disc.

## Impact and Reception

RA called the record histrionic enough to fulfil the promise of its clichéd title, and Juno Daily called it a slow-burning disco gem. A year later Juno Daily opened its review of Todd's album Late Night Boogie by recalling this single. Play it loud enough to hear the gaps between the kicks.
