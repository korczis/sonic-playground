## Genre and Mood

**Art Blakey & The Jazz Messengers** open **Moanin'** like a sermon. It is hard bop with the blues and the church in it, soulful and groovy, and Leonard Feather's original liner notes called its first chorus the quintessence of funk.

## Song Summary

Moanin' was written by the band's 22-year-old pianist, Bobby Timmons. According to Benny Golson, Timmons had only the opening eight bars, which he played between tunes, until Golson encouraged him to add a bridge. The quintet recorded it at Rudy Van Gelder's studio in Hackensack, New Jersey, on 30 October 1958, for Alfred Lion and Blue Note: Lee Morgan on trumpet, Golson on tenor saxophone, Timmons on piano, Jymie Merritt on bass and Blakey on drums. The tune is in F minor and runs over nine minutes; it reappears on the compilation The Best Of Art Blakey.

## Musical Breakdown

### Theme

The theme is a conversation: Timmons plays a short, simple phrase on the piano and the horns answer it, in what Feather called the classic call-and-response pattern.

### Bridge

The bridge brings the contrast Golson asked for; the New York Jazz Workshop points to its descending chord groups against the main theme.

### Solos

Morgan solos first, fanning out slowly in impact and intensity, Feather wrote, until by the first release he is swinging in a more complex fashion. Jazz Journal heard his burnished tone with smears, trills and half-valve effects, and Golson's rhapsodic chromaticism in contrast. Trumpet, tenor and piano take two choruses each, Merritt's bass one.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="call and response" /> }} is the heart of the tune. The New York Jazz Workshop traces it from Africa through work songs and gospel; Bob Blumenthal's 1999 notes speak of Timmons' affinity for the sanctified, the church sound.

After the theme comes {{ <concept_link name="improvisation" /> }}. Each soloist invents new melody over the tune's chords for a fixed number of choruses, and Morgan shows how a solo can build rather than start at full power.

The {{ <concept_link name="bridge" /> }} is the section the tune almost lacked. Without it, Moanin' would be one eight-bar idea repeated; with it, the return of the call and response sounds like coming home.

## Artistic Context

The session marked the Messengers' return to Blue Note. Golson, the band's musical director, had recruited his fellow Philadelphians and wrote most of the album, but Moanin' became its runaway hit, and the record, first issued as Art Blakey and the Jazz Messengers, took its name. Blumenthal heard the album give new emphasis to Blakey's backbeat.

## Impact and Reception

Moanin' was an immediate hit as an instrumental, NPR noted, and Jon Hendricks later wrote lyrics for Lambert, Hendricks & Ross. Jazz Journal places it in the burgeoning soul-jazz scene and cites over 360 recorded versions. The 1958 recording entered the Grammy Hall of Fame in 1998. Listen for the first answer from the horns.
