## Genre and Mood

**Feist**'s **My Moon My Man** in the **Boys Noize Classic Mix** takes a sly indie pop single and sets it on an electro and disco floor. Groovy and energetic where the original is wry and understated, it runs six minutes and forty seconds, almost twice the length of the song it comes from.

## Song Summary

The My Moon My Man Boys Noize Classic Mix is the work of Alexander Ridha, the German producer and DJ known as Boys Noize. In 2011 he told Clash how it began: he thought he was making a cool disco remix, played a new bass, programmed his 808, played Juno-106 keys and used a talk box. He never expected it to become a serious banger, and it did. MusicBrainz finds the same recording on the 2007 single, on Kitsuné Maison Compilation 4 that June, on later editions of The Reminder and on his own debut album, Oi Oi Oi, where it closes the record as a bonus track.

## Musical Breakdown

No source maps the remix minute by minute; these are the parts its maker and its reviewers name.

### The Groove

A new bass line, which PopMatters called buoyant, over a programmed 808: the foundation Ridha built before anything else.

### The Voices

Feist's vocal from the original, facing off, in PopMatters' words, against an omnipresent robot voice.

### The Build

A series of slowly building organs, as PopMatters heard them, with the Juno-106 keys Ridha says he played.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} reworks a recording's parts, and this one shows how much can be replaced while a song stays recognisable. Ridha kept Feist's voice and rebuilt nearly everything around it; PopMatters found his production left just enough room for her vocal to carry the song. Compare the piano-led original, {{ <track_link source="feist-my-man-my-mood.md" /> }}.

The beat comes from the {{ <concept_link name="roland tr-808" /> }}, the drum machine of 1980 whose sounds run through electro and hip-hop; Ridha's "my 808" is the drum programming under the whole mix.

The real hook is the {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }}. In dance music the lowest line often carries a track as much as the vocal, and here a bass Ridha played himself does what Gonzales' piano did in the original: it sets the mood under Feist's words.

## Artistic Context

Boys Noize founded his label, Boysnoize Records, in 2005, and released Oi Oi Oi on 25 September 2007; the Feist remix closes it, and PopMatters noted that it and an I-Robots remix were the album's only vocal tracks. The remix also introduced him to Gonzales, Feist's co-writer, who loved it. That meeting led to Gonzales' album Ivory Tower, which Ridha produced and co-wrote, and later to their duo, Octave Minds.

## Impact and Reception

PopMatters, cool on the rest of Oi Oi Oi, called the Feist remix easily its most memorable track, and BBC Music's Ian Wade pointed listeners to it as a chance to hear what Ridha does with other people's music. Give it all six minutes and forty seconds, then go back to the original.
