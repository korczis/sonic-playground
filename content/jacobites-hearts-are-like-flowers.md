## Genre and Mood

**Jacobites**' **Hearts Are Like Flowers** sits where mid-1980s indie rock meets folk rock: guitars, a rhythm section and the boozy, weary-eyed romantic songwriting Wikipedia credits the band with. Its feelings are bittersweet, melancholic and heartbroken, though one admirer heard it as heartbreak you can dance to.

## Song Summary

Dave Kusworth wrote it, and it appeared on Robespierre's Velvet Basement, the Jacobites' second album, released by Glass Records in August 1985. Picking it for AllMusic's 2021 tribute to Kusworth, Michael Burbo of Encore Records in Ann Arbor called it a standout example of his ability to express mixed emotions with few words: lush and melodic, with poignant, wistful lyrics full of regret and resignation, a beautiful, aching song that moves with a great rhythm section. This page plays the 2019 remaster issued by Easy Action and Troubadour.

### The Sound of the Album

A Sputnikmusic reviewer heard Robespierre's Velvet Basement as a heady alloy of jangle pop, psychedelic guitar and old country, a double album of despairing country made by two veterans of post-punk. AllMusic dates its sessions from September 1984 to May 1985, in Birmingham and Leamington Spa.

### The Song

Burbo's description is the closest to a portrait: melody first, then words that say little and mean much, carried by bass and drums with enough momentum to move to.

## Concepts in Focus

Hearts Are Like Flowers is a {{ <concept_link name="deep cut" /> }}: never a single, it found its listeners through people who played the whole record, from fans in record shops to musicians.

It also travelled by {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }}. In 2011 MGMT placed it on Late Night Tales: MGMT, between Dave Bixby's {{ <track_link source="dave-bixby-drug-song.md" /> }} and The Chills' Pink Frost; Drowned in Sound heard the two bring the compilation's love affair with the mid-1980s back to the fore.

And the version heard here is a {{ <concept_link name="remastering" /> }} from 2019. The album had been conceived and recorded as a double album, which Secretly Canadian's 2002 reissue restored on two discs; Easy Action's 2019 master is the version heard here.

## Artistic Context

Nikki Sudden, formerly of Swell Maps, and Kusworth, of the Subterranean Hawks, formed the Jacobites in Birmingham in 1982; Wikipedia names their influences as the Faces, Bob Dylan, glam rock and above all the Rolling Stones. Paul Westerberg of the Replacements later steered Twin/Tone towards their first two albums, which the label drew on, with their first EP, for the American compilation The Ragged School.

## Impact and Reception

The Jacobites stayed a cult band, with their following above all in Germany and mainland Europe. Sudden died in 2006 and Kusworth in 2020; AllMusic's tribute shows this song is part of how Kusworth is remembered. Listen to it late at night, then play the rest of the album.
