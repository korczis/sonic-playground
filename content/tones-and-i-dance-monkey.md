## Genre and Mood

**Tones and I**'s **Dance Monkey** is pop and electro-pop with a street performer's instincts: catchy, playful and energetic enough to stop people walking past.

## Song Summary

Toni Watson wrote the song about busking. She told the radio host Smallzy that it is about the pressure she felt to keep entertaining people on the street, who would shout for more or simply leave, and that if "dance for me" is replaced with "sing for me", it is pretty literal. She recalled writing it alone in a dark closet in about thirty minutes. Konstantin Kersting produced and mixed it in his Brisbane studio, and it was released on 10 May 2019.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

There was no demo. Watson played Kersting the song as she performed it live, built from loops, and its main eighth-note keyboard line, which she played on a Korg Minilogue, opens the record.

### Verse 1

No source walks through the verses. What Kersting describes is the bass Watson played on a MIDI keyboard, which he layered from Moog synthesizers and swung to make it more jagged.

### Build-up

In the pre-chorus the kicks arrive, with an 808 added underneath.

### Chorus

The first chorus is deliberately lean: a kick, the bass line and a snap. The second becomes a double chorus with backing vocals, guitar and keyboard.

### Bridge

The middle eight passes through a vocoder.

### Climax

The final chorus adds what a loop station could not: big group vocals that Watson recorded with friends, guitars and more keyboards.

## Concepts in Focus

Dance Monkey began as a {{ <concept_link name="loop" /> }} performance. After she lost her retail job, Watson bought an RC300 loop station and busked up and down Australia's east coast with it. In the studio Kersting kept that skeleton and expanded it to 19 keyboard tracks, from a piano sample to a steel drum, so that they would make one cohesive sound.

Her busking version already had a {{ <concept_link name="four-on-the-floor" /> }} kick in its drop; on the record Kersting layered five kick sounds in the chorus. The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is the chant itself, sung back to the crowd that demands it.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Liam Kelly and Nick Kozakis, casts Tones as Mr. Tones, an elderly man who sneaks out of a care home with friends for a dance party on a golf course. For another 2019 Spotify giant, compare {{ <track_link source="the-weeknd-blinding-lights.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Watson grew up on the Mornington Peninsula near Melbourne and moved to Byron Bay in 2017 to busk, where she met her manager, Jackson Walkden-Brown. She signed to Bad Batch Records in February 2019; Dance Monkey was her second single and appeared on her debut EP, The Kids Are Coming.

## Impact and Reception

The song topped the charts in more than 30 countries, spent 24 weeks at number one in Australia and eleven in the UK, a record there for a female artist, and was the first song by a woman to pass three billion streams on Spotify. It won Best Pop Release at the ARIA Awards, yet Watson has said she loathes it a lot of the time. Listen for the busker inside the hit.
