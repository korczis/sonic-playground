## Genre and Mood

**Ian Brown**'s **Save Us** is an alternative-rock singer's protest song dressed in orchestral strings and hip-hop beats. It is defiant in its words and intense in its intent: a former Stone Roses singer calling on someone, anyone, to save the world from the people running it.

## Song Summary

Save Us is the fourth track of The World Is Yours, Brown's fifth solo album, released by Fiction and Polydor in 2007; on the album it runs 4:58. The credits name Brown and the New York producer Emile Haynie as its writers. Its lyric is a list of pleas: Drowned in Sound quoted lines asking to be saved from imbeciles who think they rule the world, from hypocrites and from warmongers. musicOMH noted that it also turns on those who ignore the suffering of African children. In a Guardian interview before the release, Michael Odell reported that the album tackles South America's street children and the plight of Africa.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through Save Us section by section or gives times, so this guide follows the layers the album's reviewers heard.

### The Beat

NME wrote that Brown likes hip-hop, so he brought in Haynie to handle the beats. Drowned in Sound heard pre-programmed beats as the album's base.

### The Strings

Hot Press heard a big string section on every track of the album; Drowned in Sound heard strings laced with occasional drama when a chorus cuts in.

### The Pleas

The repeated call to be saved is the song's hook. Listen for how each line names a new target.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="protest song" /> }} takes a public stand. Brown's targets here are broad, the powerful in general rather than one war or government, which is why musicOMH compared his tone to Bono's.

Its lyric works like a {{ <concept_link name="list song" /> }}: instead of a story, a series of targets, one after another, each introduced the same way. Repetition makes the plea feel bigger with every line.

The {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }} is what critics argued about. NME heard the orchestral backdrop intertwining the songs like ivy; Hot Press found the strings so constant that little else could make its mark. A bonus disc of the deluxe edition carries an orchestral mix of Save Us. The next track, {{ <track_link source="ian-brown-eternal-flame.md" /> }}, uses the same frame for a love song.

## Artistic Context

Brown had moved from the Stone Roses to a long solo career. For this album he enlisted Andy Rourke of the Smiths, Paul Ryder of Happy Mondays, and Steve Jones and Paul Cook of the Sex Pistols, with Sinéad O'Connor on two songs and a 30-piece orchestra, as NME counted it.

## Impact and Reception

The reviews that mentioned Save Us were mixed. musicOMH's John Murphy called the sentiments laudable but the result banal; Drowned in Sound's Dom Gourlay felt the music reduced the hard-hitting words to a laboured routine. Caroline Sullivan of the Guardian said the album drew its strength from sounds rather than words. Listen once for the words and once for the strings.
