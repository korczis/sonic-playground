## Genre and Mood

**Basement Jaxx** made their name with house, but **Living Room** is not a house record. Crack Magazine calls it an indie rock jam, and the mood is quirky, playful and energetic: a short, odd song that seems to have wandered in from another band's rehearsal room.

## Song Summary

Living Room is the thirteenth of fourteen tracks on Kish Kash, the duo's third album, released by XL Recordings on 20 October 2003. Felix Buxton and Simon Ratcliffe wrote, produced, programmed and mixed it themselves, and Buxton sings the lead, one of three songs on an album full of guests where he does. At 2:25 it is the shortest full song on the record, a sketch next to the seven-minute closer that follows it.

### The Voice

Buxton's vocal is the track's centre: not a hired soul singer or rapper, but one of the two producers stepping up to the microphone.

### The Band Sound

Crack Magazine heard a proto-Metronomy indie rock jam; Silent Uproar compared it to a Frank Black experiment. Both point to guitar-band music rather than the dance floor.

### The End

It is over almost as soon as it starts, and the album moves straight on into Feels Like Home.

## Concepts in Focus

Basement Jaxx work as a production duo, and the {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} is the role they usually keep: on most of Kish Kash they build the track and bring in a voice, from Siouxsie Sioux to Dizzee Rascal. Living Room reverses that. The credits name Buxton as performer, producer, programmer and mixing engineer, so the person shaping the sound is also the one singing. Compare {{ <track_link source="basement-jaxx-hot-n-cold.md" /> }}, which sits just before it and is also Buxton's own vocal.

Living Room is also a {{ <concept_link name="deep cut" /> }}. It was never a single, yet reviewers kept naming it. Pitchfork's Mark Pytlik, describing the album's collage of genres, listed "whatever the hell the incredible 'Living Room' is"; Crack Magazine, looking back twenty years later, counted it among the deep cuts that made Kish Kash more consistent than the duo's earlier albums despite fewer hits.

## Artistic Context

Kish Kash was the follow-up to Remedy and Rooty. Piero Scaruffi heard it as more sombre, with a bluesier mood, and grouped Living Room with If I Ever Recover and Feels Like Home. Silent Uproar placed it as the counterweight to Tonight, the track with Phoebe that it found less convincing. The album was nominated for the 2004 Mercury Prize and won the first Grammy Award for Best Dance/Electronic Album.

## Impact and Reception

Living Room was never a single; its reputation is the one critics gave it, an oddity worth hearing. It shows how wide the duo's idea of an album was. Play it straight after {{ <track_link source="basement-jaxx-good-luck.md" /> }} and hear how far Kish Kash travels between its first track and its thirteenth.
