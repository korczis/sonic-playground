## Genre and Mood

**Jalen Santoy**'s **Foreplay** is hip-hop with a jazz heart: a rap song led by a saxophone, which Audible Treats called a breezy, minimalist summer jam. The mood is relaxed on the surface and bittersweet underneath, because, as Santoy has said, the lyric is about discontent in a relationship.

## Song Summary

Foreplay came out in March 2015, according to the press release for its video, and became Santoy's breakout. The same release describes compelling verses, a beautifully harmonised chorus and an unforgettable saxophone riff; The Hundreds heard the song as an exploration of love and uncertainty set to swelling alto saxes. Santoy himself has said that, lyrically, there is clear discontent within the relationship. The release credits the production to Jordeaux in its text, though its headline names Tim Stacks. The version embedded here is the official audio, published in 2020, the year the song appeared on the album II Shepherds; it runs 2:52.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} here comes with a puzzle. The 2016 press release says the saxophone riff is borrowed from Bobby Hebb's 1966 hit Sunny; the artist biography on Shazam says the song is built on a sample of Happy by the jazz saxophonist Stanley Turrentine. The two accounts disagree, and comparing the records is the way to find out.

A {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }} can carry a whole song. Foreplay is short and spare, and almost everything written about it starts with the saxophone.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, written and directed by Arthur Gallery and premiered by The FADER in October 2016, follows four loosely connected people in dreamy slow motion and pulsing colours. Santoy said the colour is meant to contrast with the discontent in the lyric. For another jazz-coloured groove under a rapper, hear {{ <track_link source="tom-misch-de-la-soul-it-runs-through-me.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Santoy was born in Charleston, South Carolina, and raised in Charlotte, North Carolina. Foreplay became the lead single of his EP Charlie Eastern, released on EMPIRE on 18 November 2016 and which shares its name with a rap group founded in Charleston that his cousin was part of; he said he lost that cousin to police brutality while making the project. In February 2020 he released II Shepherds, a 15-track album that includes the song.

## Impact and Reception

Foreplay grew slowly and kept growing: nearly six million streams by the time the video came out, and 152 million on Spotify by late 2021, according to Clture, which called it his jazzy single. Put it on and follow the saxophone.
