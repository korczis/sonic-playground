## Genre and Mood

**D12**'s **Girls** is hip-hop as a settling of accounts: rap at its most aggressive and provocative, and defiant, one rapper turning on a band that had been his friends. It is also the least typical track on Devil's Night, because the crew whose name is on the album does not take part.

## Song Summary

Girls is a diss track aimed at Limp Bizkit and their DJ, DJ Lethal, at Dilated Peoples and at Everlast. Credited to D12, it is in Wikipedia's words a hidden track by Eminem. Eminem produced it and wrote it with Luis Resto, who plays keyboards, with Jeff Bass on bass and DJ Head on drum programming. It contains a sample of Limp Bizkit's own Rollin'. The title is the insult: Eminem said in an interview that he had dissed Limp Bizkit because "they're little fucking girls."

### Intro

On the CD the song begins after the last listed track; on the clean pressing it was mistakenly listed as track 18, after Revelation.

### Main Section

One voice carries the track over Resto's keyboards, Bass's bass line, DJ Head's programmed drums and the Rollin' sample.

### Outro

No source describes the ending. The track runs five minutes and thirty-five seconds.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="diss track" /> }} names its target, and this one names four. Eminem's account, retold by Louder, starts with his feud with Everlast: Limp Bizkit offered to take his side and record with him, Durst asked him to write verses, and Lethal offered to help. On the day of the session, Eminem said, Durst cited a toothache and Lethal said he was unsure because he was still friendly with Everlast. The last straw, he said, was an MTV interview in which Lethal called him the better MC but said Everlast would beat him in a fight.

A {{ <concept_link name="hidden track" /> }} has no place in the listing. Girls closes Devil's Night as track 19, after the album has, on paper, ended; compare {{ <track_link source="amy-winehouse-amy-amy-amy.md" /> }}.

Here {{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} turns on its source: the credited sample of Rollin' takes the target's own hit and uses it against the band.

## Artistic Context

Devil's Night, D12's debut, came out on 19 June 2001 on Shady Records and Interscope, the first album on Eminem's Shady label. The Detroit crew recorded it in memory of their member Bugz, killed in 1999. Loudwire places the feud after Eminem's appearance in the video for Limp Bizkit's Break Stuff in early 2000. Hear the crew together in {{ <track_link source="d12-devils-night.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Devil's Night debuted at number one on the Billboard 200 to mixed reviews. NME's Ted Kessler called it Eminem's most misogynistic, homophobic, violent and anally fixated trip to date. Durst later said in a Reddit AMA that he understood why Eminem was upset, mainly with Lethal, and called him one of the best rappers of all time. In 2024 Eminem named Limp Bizkit in Guilty Conscience 2, which Loudwire read as settling the beef. Listen with the backstory in mind.
