## Genre and Mood

**Hindi Zahra**'s **Ahiawa - Unplugged** sits where world music, folk and acoustic performance meet: a song sung in Berber, recorded without the studio layers of her debut album. It is filed here as intimate and soulful because of what an unplugged session is, a voice close to the listener with little between them, rather than because a critic described it; we found no review of this recording.

## Song Summary

Ahiawa is credited to Hindi Zahra as lyricist and to her, Abdenour Djemaï and Thomas Naïm as composers, and she produced it. Lyric sites list its language as Berber. On Handmade the Moroccan-born singer, whose family are Chleuh Berbers from the Souss, sang mostly in English, and she has explained why: English gave her distance from what she says, and singing her love stories in her mother tongue would be very hard. In Berber, she said, she tells different stories, such as the story of her parents in Oursoul. The words of Ahiawa are not translated in any source we found, so this review does not guess at them.

## Musical Breakdown

No review or session note we found describes the performance part by part, so this breakdown stays with what is documented.

### The Session

Ahiawa opens the Unplugged Session, six tracks added to the 2011 deluxe edition of Handmade and recorded at Studio Ferber in Paris on 28 March 2011, according to the release's notes. On the CD it runs 4:51.

### The Voice

What carries the track is Zahra's voice in her family's language. Listen to how it phrases the Berber words without the rhythm section and electric colours of the studio album around it.

## Concepts in Focus

An {{ <concept_link name="unplugged" /> }} recording strips a song down to acoustic instruments and voice. The term spread with MTV's series of acoustic concerts in the early 1990s, and the idea is the same here: take away the production and the melody and the phrasing have to do all the work. Djemaï and Naïm, the two co-composers, are credited as guitarists on the original Handmade.

Ahiawa is also a {{ <concept_link name="bonus track" /> }}. It is not on the 2010 album; it arrived on the deluxe edition, issued digitally as Handmade (Bonus Version) on 29 April 2011, and on Until the Next Journey, released that July. Bonus tracks show what an album left out, and here they add a language the original used only on a few songs, such as {{ <track_link source="hindi-zahra-imik-si-mik-remastered-version.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Hindi Zahra was born in Khouribga, Morocco, and has lived in France since 1993. Handmade, which she produced, arranged and mixed herself, came out on Blue Note in 2010; Songlines heard in it traditional Berber styles and desert blues alongside jazz, folk and reggae.

## Impact and Reception

Handmade won the Prix Constantin in November 2010 and the Victoire de la musique for world music album in February 2011, weeks before this session, and it went gold in France. The deluxe edition came out after those awards. Put it on quietly and let the voice lead.
