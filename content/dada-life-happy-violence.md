## Genre and Mood

**Dada Life** built their name on electro house that refuses to be solemn, and **Happy Violence** says so in its title. It is big-room dance music, energetic, euphoric and playful: the joke is part of the track. This page is about the vocal radio edit, the version that sits on the album.

## Song Summary

Happy Violence first came out on 29 August 2011 on So Much Dada, the duo's own new label, as a single without a singer. Two months later, on 31 October, it returned as a vocal version, with an official video and a pack of remixes. That vocal radio edit, about 3:48 long, is track six of The Rules of Dada (2012). Olle Corneér explained the title to Club Glow: bringing your brain to a club or a rave is an epic fail, and the aim is to wake up the next day with bruises, smiling like a child.

## Musical Breakdown

No review we found walks through this edit section by section or gives times, so the parts below say only what the releases themselves show.

### Intro

As a radio edit, this version is built to arrive quickly; the vocal extended mix on the same release runs 5:36, with room for a DJ to mix in and out.

### Main Section

The sung part is what separates this version from the August single. Magnetic Magazine later heard the melody of the album's {{ <track_link source="dada-life-everything-is-free.md" /> }} as somewhat similar to this one.

### Outro

The edit ends inside four minutes; the extended mix and the remixes carry it further.

## Concepts in Focus

Happy Violence is a lesson in the {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }}. The same track exists as the original single, a vocal version, a vocal extended mix, a vocal dub mix and an instrumental, all by the duo themselves.

The {{ <concept_link name="radio edit" /> }} is the shortest of them. The {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} pack went further: Kaskade, Swanky Tunes, Uppermost and Caveat each reworked it, and Dirty Smart noted that Caveat was a 16-year-old producer.

There are two official videos. The first, from August 2011, was directed by Sam Sabawi. The second, for the vocal version, was directed by Leo Horsfield; Dancing Astronaut described it as a mythical office that begins celebrating when Dada Life Inc stock goes through the roof. The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} does what the title does: it tells you not to take the track too seriously.

## Artistic Context

Dada Life are Olle Corneér and Stefan Engblom, a Stockholm duo formed in 2006 and named in homage to the absurdist Dada movement; they are known for the humour of their live shows. The Rules of Dada, released on 16 October 2012 by So Much Dada together with Universal, credits Happy Violence to the two of them alone.

## Impact and Reception

Wikipedia's singles table records no chart position for Happy Violence. Its reach shows elsewhere: Dancing Astronaut reported that Kaskade played it on both of his nights at Roseland Ballroom, and in 2012 Your EDM counted it among the hits that put Dada Life on the map. Play the radio edit, then the extended mix, and listen to how much the same idea can stretch.
