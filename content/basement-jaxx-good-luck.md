## Genre and Mood

**Basement Jaxx**'s **Good Luck** is filed as electronic music, but its makers meant it to be something else: "a rock 'n' roll song which didn't even sound modern". It is dance music only in its origins, a rock song in its riff and its voice, and it is defiant and bold, in Crack Magazine's words "a stunning, bombastic rock-opera" that hands the spotlight to its singer.

## Song Summary

Good Luck opens Kish Kash, the duo's third album, and it was a deliberate opener. "We had to kick it off with something," they said; "whatever we chose people would say, 'That isn't house music.' Who cares?" The song is a kiss-off, sung by Lisa Kekaula of the American rock 'n' soul band the BellRays, set to a guitar riff, drums, backing singers and a sixteen-piece orchestra.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the song section by section, so this follows the three layers its makers described.

### The Riff

It nearly did not happen. "Initially, Lisa sounded like a diva and we didn't want that," the duo recalled. With two hours left before she flew back to America, Simon Ratcliffe strummed what they called an AC/DC riff, some words were scribbled down, and suddenly they had their song.

### The Orchestra

Ratcliffe wanted "to make a beautiful song" and could not see how, until their manager suggested an orchestra. Sixteen players of the London Session Orchestra recorded the strings.

### The Voice

Kekaula sings lead over Nathan Curran's drums and four backing singers. Buxton called it the hardest track on the album to finish: "it took a long time to get it to its finish point."

## Concepts in Focus

Kekaula is a {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} in the strongest sense: she co-wrote the song with Buxton and Ratcliffe, and it was her voice that the duo had to rethink the track around. Crack wrote that the song cedes the spotlight to her.

The {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }} is what turned it from a diva track into rock 'n' roll; listen for the guitar against the orchestra.

## Artistic Context

Felix Buxton and Simon Ratcliffe made Kish Kash between March 2002 and August 2003 in their new Brixton studio, wanting an approach less reliant on grooves and samples and more focused on songwriting. XL Recordings released it on 20 October 2003. For the house side they returned to a decade later, hear {{ <track_link source="basement-jaxx-galactical.md" /> }} from Junto (2014).

## Impact and Reception

Released as a single on 5 January 2004, Good Luck reached number 12 in the UK, number two on the US Dance Club Play chart and was nominated for Best Dance Recording at the 47th Grammys; Kish Kash won the first Grammy for Best Dance/Electronic Album. An instrumental version opened the BBC's Euro 2004 coverage and took the single back to number 14. Turn it up and wait for the strings.
