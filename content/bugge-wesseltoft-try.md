## Genre and Mood

**Bugge Wesseltoft**'s **Try** is nu-jazz at its quietest, jazz made with electronics and heard far beyond jazz, on chillout compilations from Café del Mar to Saint-Germain-des-Prés Café. Its feelings are intimate and relaxed: one voice, a keyboard and a soft pulse, what the Breath of Life blog called graceful and effortless.

## Song Summary

Try is a duet. The voice belongs to the Norwegian singer **Sidsel Endresen**, credited with Wesseltoft as its writer and producer, and the track first appeared on their joint album Out Here. In There., released by Jazzland in May 2002. Writing for Kalamu ya Salaam's Breath of Life in 2006, Mtume ya Salaam called it beautifully minimalist: nothing but Wesseltoft picking out a chord here and there on his keyboard, backed by the muffled thump of electronic drums.

## Concepts in Focus

The track shows what a {{ <concept_link name="soundtrack" /> }} placement can do. The reviewer first heard Try on KCRW, the Los Angeles station, and a couple of years later it turned up in the film Prime; the recording appears on the film's 2005 soundtrack album and on lounge compilations from Verve Today 2002 to Café del Mar Dreams 8.

It is also a lesson in credits. On Out Here. In There. the track belongs to Sidsel Endresen & Bugge Wesseltoft; on later releases, including the Café del Mar upload embedded here, Endresen is listed second, like a {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}, and the title on this page names Wesseltoft alone. She sings every word. Compare {{ <track_link source="marc-moulin-into-the-dark.md" /> }}, another keyboardist's record of the same years.

## Artistic Context

Wesseltoft, born in Porsgrunn in 1964, released his records on his own Jazzland label in the 1990s, and the first album of his band New Conception of Jazz won a Spellemannprisen in 1996. With Endresen he made Nightsong, Duplex Ride and Out Here. In There., which won the 2002 Spellemannprisen in the open class. In 2016 he gathered twenty years of old and new songs, originals, re-recordings and remixes, on Somewhere in Between, with Try on its first disc.

## Impact and Reception

The Irish Times called Wesseltoft a nu-jazz pioneer whose sparse, electronically polished music stays warm and human, and Try is that description in four minutes. Play it quietly, late.
