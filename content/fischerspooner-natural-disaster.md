## Genre and Mood

**Fischerspooner** put **Natural Disaster** at the end of their debut album, and critics kept picking it out. It is electroclash in its most danceable form: electro-pop built from synthesizers, groovy rather than cold, catchy enough that PopMatters called its beat flat-out contagious, and, in a phrase from Hot Press, oddly emotive.

## Song Summary

Casey Spooner and Warren Fischer wrote the song, and they produced it with Nicolas Vernhes. On the original nine-track edition of #1 it is the closing track, and on the later reissue it moved to tenth place, still at 4:46. The reviews describe its feel more than its form: Gary Numan-ish, according to PopMatters; the album's resident groover with a little more bounce, according to the Daily Nexus. No source quoted here discusses the lyric, so this page leaves the words to your own listening.

## Musical Breakdown

No review walks through Natural Disaster section by section; the parts below follow what the critics heard.

### The Beat

The track rests on a dance beat that PopMatters gave as its example of the album's flat-out contagious side. The comparison to Gary Numan points to the cool synth-pop of the turn of the 1980s.

### The Groove

The Daily Nexus heard more bounce here than elsewhere on the record. Listen for how the rhythm pulls the track forward.

### The Close

As the original album's last track, it is the note #1 ended on. Drowned in Sound heard the record regain its potential with it.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }} took the minimal, early-1980s synth-pop sound back to the clubs of the early 2000s. PopMatters described Fischerspooner taking exactly that sound, and Natural Disaster shows the dance side of it. Its sibling {{ <track_link source="fischerspooner-emerge.md" /> }} shows the anthem side.

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} is the band here. Drowned in Sound noted that #1 brings New York cool without a guitar in sight, and PopMatters credited Fischer's compositions and synth arrangements with making the album interesting.

The {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} credit is shared: Fischerspooner and Nicolas Vernhes. When artists co-produce, the outside producer's role is usually to shape the sound with them; the sources do not say how the work was divided.

## Artistic Context

Fischer and Spooner formed Fischerspooner in New York in 1999. #1 first appeared in 2001 in a limited run on DJ Hell's International DeeJay Gigolo Records, and was later reissued with a different track listing, reaching the United States through Capitol in 2003. It is the album of Emerge, The 15th and L.A. Song.

## Impact and Reception

#1 received mostly favourable reviews, with a Metacritic score of 70, though Pitchfork was harsh. Natural Disaster was never a single, but Hot Press counted it among the album's three scorchers, beside Emerge and Horizon. Play it after Emerge and hear the same duo in a looser mood.
