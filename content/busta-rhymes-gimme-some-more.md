## Genre and Mood

**Busta Rhymes** released **Gimme Some More** on 26 October 1998, seven weeks before its album: hip-hop and rap at a sprint, energetic, intense and, once you see the video, playful. Wikipedia files it under hardcore hip-hop and notes that it is often considered the summit of his rhyming style.

## Song Summary

Busta wrote it with George Spivey, better known as DJ Scratch, who produced it. The beat rests on a sample: the violin riffs under the main rhythm come from the opening theme Bernard Herrmann composed for Alfred Hitchcock's Psycho in 1960. Over it Busta packs three verses and a chorus in which every line is answered by the title, and the whole thing is over in two minutes and thirty-nine seconds. It was recorded in 1998 at Soundtrack Studios in New York.

## Musical Breakdown

No source times the song, so this walk-through follows the lyric's structure and the sources.

### Intro

Busta opens with a story about a childhood fall in which he bumped his head; the video turns it into its cartoon opening.

### Verse 1

The first verse comes in at full pace, a list of boasts in short, end-rhymed lines, with the sampled violins in the beat beneath it.

### Chorus

The chorus is call and response: Busta asks, and the answer comes back each time as gimme some more.

### Outro

After the third verse and a last chorus the track simply ends, short enough that the video, at almost three minutes, runs longer than the album version.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} lets a producer build a beat from someone else's recording, and DJ Scratch reached for one of the most famous string themes in film. The sampled violins carry a theme written to put a cinema audience on edge into a rap single.

The sampled figure works as a {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }}: a short, repeated phrase that drives the song. Listen to how the rapping locks to it.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Hype Williams with Busta credited as Busta Remo, is shot through a fisheye lens in fast motion. A child playing young Busta is chased through a house by a blue monster with yellow eyes, intercut with Busta and the Flipmode Squad as a boxer, a stockbroker, a miner, a pimp and more; NPR remembered him rapping as a boxer, a cowboy and a zoot-suited gangster. It was nominated for Breakthrough Video at the 1999 MTV Video Music Awards.

## Artistic Context

Extinction Level Event: The Final World Front followed on 15 December 1998 on Flipmode and Elektra, the third album of an apocalyptic run after The Coming and When Disaster Strikes..., whose single {{ <track_link source="busta-rhymes-jamal-put-your-hands-where-my-eyes-could-see-feat-jamal.md" /> }} had also been recorded at Soundtrack Studios. The album reached number 12 on the Billboard 200 and went platinum.

## Impact and Reception

Gimme Some More reached number five in the UK, where it went silver, and number 29 on the US R&B/Hip-Hop chart, and it was nominated for the Grammy for Best Rap Solo Performance in 2000, losing to Eminem's My Name Is. Turn it up and watch the video.
