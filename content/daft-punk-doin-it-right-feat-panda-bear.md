## Genre and Mood

**Daft Punk** and **Panda Bear** made **Doin' it Right** the quiet heart of Random Access Memories: electro and electro-pop stripped to a few electronic parts, uplifting in what it says and bittersweet in how it says it. Pitchfork called it a terrifically uplifting bit of electro-pop; Beats Per Minute heard it as perhaps the most doleful thing the duo had put their name to. Both are right, and the loop makes it hypnotic.

## Song Summary

On an album built around veteran session players, this is the exception. Daft Punk called it the record's only purely electronic piece: a modular synthesizer they played themselves, a drum beat and a vocoder loop, with no band at all. Over that sparse frame Noah Lennox of Animal Collective sings a promise that everybody will be dancing and doing it right. It was also the last song recorded for the album, an idea that came late in the Paris sessions, almost on a whim.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

The robots go first. NME heard a Trojan Robot opening, the vocoder loop and the beat alone before Panda Bear emerges.

### Main Section

Lennox's voice enters and takes over, ranging across about two octaves. The chord progression never changes, so the song moves through its voices, the human one against the machine chant.

### Break

At one point the vocoder stops while Panda Bear sings a line alone, which Pitchfork took to be for emphasis.

### Outro

Beats Per Minute described the track coming round in a glorious full circle, and its mood as the comedown after the night of your life.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="vocoder" /> }} here is not a lead singer but part of the rhythm section. Its chant repeats as a {{ <concept_link name="loop" /> }}, so the same robotic phrase works as beat, hook and backdrop at once. Where {{ <track_link source="daft-punk-give-life-back-to-music.md" /> }} uses the vocoder over a live band, here it carries almost the whole arrangement.

As a {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}, Panda Bear is unusually dominant. GQ's Zach Baron called it effectively a Panda Bear solo track that happened to be on a Daft Punk album, and Lennox said he aimed for a point perfectly in the middle of the two acts. He recorded his part in three days, far faster than his usual pace.

## Artistic Context

Lennox first heard Daft Punk through Michel Gondry's video for {{ <track_link source="daft-punk-around-the-world.md" /> }}. The duo turned down his requests to remix them, then about a year and a half later invited him to the album sessions in Paris. Random Access Memories came out on Columbia on 17 May 2013, written here by Thomas Bangalter, Guy-Manuel de Homem-Christo and Lennox and produced by Daft Punk.

## Impact and Reception

Pitchfork named it Best New Music and the album's strongest statement of purpose; PopMatters, dissenting, called it the record's only letdown. Sent to American alternative radio as the album's third single on 3 September 2013, it reached number 17 on Billboard's dance and electronic chart and went gold in the US and Canada. Darkside remixed it within weeks for their unofficial Daftside album. Listen late at night, when the dancing is over.
