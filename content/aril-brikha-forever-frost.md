## Genre and Mood

**Aril Brikha**'s **Forever Frost** is techno in the Detroit line, the tag its label puts on it beside electronic, ambient and techno. Its mood comes less from the beat than from melody: the record distributor Forced Exposure describes a journey in melody built in layers. A supporter on the label's Bandcamp page put the feeling in four words, techno with a soul, and the title supplies the climate, cold, clear and long.

## Song Summary

Forever Frost is the title track of a three-track record: Forever Frost, Tundra and Glaciär / For Minou, all written and produced by Brikha. It came out on Art of Vengeance as a German 12-inch (AOV 003) in April 2011 and digitally on 18 April; Discogs also lists an earlier self-released digital version from 2010. At eight minutes it is the longest of the three, and according to Forced Exposure it tries to fit as many melodies as possible into one track without going over the line.

## Musical Breakdown

### Main Section

No timed analysis of the track exists that I could find, so this page does not say what happens at which minute or how the track opens and closes. What the sources describe is its method: melodies laid over one another, each added layer one more voice in the same cold landscape. Traxsource heard in it a return to the sound of Brikha's Winter EP, released on Kompakt in 2007, and Clone, announcing the 12-inch, wrote that Aril takes a turn in style on this second Art of Vengeance release.

## Concepts in Focus

Brikha's instrument is the {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }}. In an interview with Ableton he recalls getting a keyboard at seven and later buying a Roland System 100, TR-808, TB-303 and Yamaha CS-5 as a bundle in the mid-1990s; the chord of his first classic, Groove La Chord, came from an Ensoniq SQ80. No source lists the gear used on Forever Frost, but this is the toolkit his music grew from, and here the stacked melodies carry the emotion.

He is also his own {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }}: Discogs credits him alone for writing and production, and the record came out on a label that shares its name with his debut EP. One person decides how many layers are enough.

## Artistic Context

Brikha was born in Tehran in 1976 and grew up in Jönköping, Sweden. As a teenager he listened to Depeche Mode, Nitzer Ebb and Kraftwerk, and friends told him that what he was making was called Detroit techno. His first major release, the Art of Vengeance EP with Groove La Chord, came out in 1998 on Fragile, a subsidiary of the Transmat label. Hear another producer of that lineage in {{ <track_link source="anthony-rother-brainshaker.md" /> }}, and Kompakt's melodic techno in {{ <track_link source="kolsch-grey.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

I could find no press review of the record, but Kompakt had greeted the Winter EP in 2007 as a long-awaited dream come true, and Forever Frost shows the same patience. Give it all eight minutes and count the layers.
