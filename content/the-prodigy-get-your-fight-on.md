## Genre and Mood

**The Prodigy**'s **Get Your Fight On** states its intent in its title: electronic, big beat with a rock edge, aggressive, defiant and energetic. NME filed it with the album's dance-rock, and Sonicabuse heard something brutal, Keith Flint and Maxim screaming the refrain over a clattering backdrop that only Liam Howlett could build without straying far from his roots as a dance artist.

## Song Summary

No source describes the lyrics. Eight writers are credited: Howlett and Maxim, Tim Hutton and Nick Halkes, Joe Erskine and Luca Gulotta of KillSonik, who also share the production, and Jari Salo and Paul Malmström, credited through a sample of Pepe Deluxé's "Salami Fever". Flint and Maxim share the vocals. The embed is the album recording, uploaded by the band; the official video is a little shorter.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the track in order or gives times. The reviews describe two layers.

### The Vocals

Sonicabuse heard the two singers screaming the refrain. DIY singled the track out as Maxim's showcase and a highlight of the album.

### The Percussion

Drowned in Sound heard it as "Take Me to the Hospital", the band's 2009 single, in demo form, right down to the exact same flickering percussion sample.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} reuses a piece of an existing recording inside a new one, and when a composition is sampled its writers share the credit. MusicBrainz records that this track samples "Salami Fever", a Pepe Deluxé recording first released in 2003 on Beatitude; no source says which part is used or where it sits. Hear the group on its own terms in {{ <track_link source="pepe-deluxe-before-you-leave.md" /> }}.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, released in July 2015, is built from the stage. Exclaim! described the band's fast-paced, high-energy, probably seizure-inducing live show spliced with footage of the band looking spooky and delivering the vocals into a close-up camera; Entertainment.ie saw the three members interspersed with intense live footage.

A {{ <concept_link name="live recording" /> }} captures a performance in front of an audience, and this track got one: the album's expanded edition adds Get Your Fight On live at Alexandra Palace in 2015, eleven seconds longer than the studio cut.

## Artistic Context

The Day Is My Enemy, the band's sixth album, came out on 30 March 2015 on Take Me to the Hospital and Cooking Vinyl. Get Your Fight On, its eleventh track, was released as a promotional single on 26 March, the day after Rhythm Bomb. In July it also appeared on the EP The Night Is My Friend, announced for 31 July on Cooking Vinyl. All five writers of {{ <track_link source="the-prodigy-wild-frontier.md" /> }} are credited here too. Flint died in 2019.

## Impact and Reception

The album debuted at number one in the UK. The title drew as much comment as the music: The List cited it, with Nasty, as an aggressive title on what it found a conservative album, and Drowned in Sound called the track toothless, picturing a punch-drunk former champion swinging wide. Sonicabuse and DIY heard the opposite. Play it next to {{ <track_link source="the-prodigy-nasty.md" /> }}, where Flint and Maxim also share the mic, and decide.
