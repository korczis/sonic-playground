## Genre and Mood

**Kings of Convenience** made **Misread** the way they made most of their records: two voices, acoustic guitars and as little else as possible. It sits where indie folk meets indie pop, an acoustic song that is calm on the surface and introspective underneath. Drowned in Sound's Gareth Dobson heard it as sparse, calm and hypnotic.

## Song Summary

Misread is a song about love and about getting the other person wrong, as its title says, and the review of the single heard in it a lyric about loneliness and honesty. The arrangement is built from very little: what Dobson called gently clipped guitars, sprinkles of piano and gentle strings, which he compared to Robert Kirby, the arranger behind the strings on Nick Drake's Five Leaves Left and Bryter Layter. Treble's Jeff Terich made the same comparison from the other side, calling Misread one of the album's outstanding songs and one of the most jazz-touched, more Bryter Layter than the rest.

## Musical Breakdown

No review we found walks through Misread section by section, so this one follows its layers rather than inventing a structure.

### The Guitars

The ground of the song: short, clipped acoustic guitar figures that keep time on their own, so that the song moves without a drum kit.

### Piano and Strings

The colour around them: piano touches dropped between the guitar lines, and strings that stay gentle instead of swelling into a climax.

### The Voice

The lead vocal sits close and soft above the guitars. Dobson credits the words to Erlend Øye, singing about love and about not understanding the person he loves.

## Concepts in Focus

Treble described the whole album as fingerpicked acoustic guitars, sparse arrangements and hushed vocal harmonies. {{ <concept_link name="fingerstyle guitar" /> }} means plucking the strings with the fingers rather than strumming with a pick, so one guitarist can play a bass note, a chord and a melody at once. With two guitars played that way, clipped, muted notes can do the work a hi-hat would do elsewhere.

{{ <concept_link name="vocal harmony" /> }} is the other half of the duo's sound: two voices singing different notes together so they sound a chord. Kept quiet, as on this album, harmony is less a big chorus than a sense of two people thinking aloud at the same time.

## Artistic Context

Kings of Convenience are Eirik Glambek Bøe and Erlend Øye from Bergen, Norway, schoolmates who formed the duo in 1999. Their debut, Quiet Is the New Loud (2001), lent its name to a small movement of acoustic musicians in the pop underground. Misread was the first single from their second album, Riot on an Empty Street, released in June 2004 ahead of the album, which came out on 21 June on Source in Britain and Astralwerks in the United States. The duo produced it with Davide Bertolini and wrote every song themselves; Feist sings on two tracks. For the Bergen scene around Øye, hear {{ <track_link source="kakkmaddafakka-your-girl.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Misread reached number 15 in Italy and number 83 in the UK. The album went to number 2 in Norway and number 3 in Italy, where it stayed on the chart for a year, and Metacritic counts its reviews as generally favourable. Drowned in Sound gave the single 8 out of 10 and called the duo the two smartest boys this side of Belle and Sebastian. It is a lesson in how little a song needs. Put it on quietly and let it work.
