## Genre and Mood

**Gorillaz** open their 2007 collection D-Sides with **68 State**, a piece of electronic music rather than a song: no chorus to wait for, just a groove and its gadgets. Energetic and dark are this page's words for what the critics heard, and alternative is where a virtual band from the Demon Days era naturally sits.

## Song Summary

68 State first appeared in May 2005 as a B-side of Feel Good Inc., on the B side of the UK 7-inch picture disc and on a European digital single, at 4:48. Reviewers describe it as an instrumental. MusicBrainz credits the writing to Gorillaz and the production to Jason Cox, James Dring and the band, with Cox engineering and mixing and Dring on programming; the YouTube Topic upload's metadata names Damon Albarn as producer and Danger Mouse as programmer instead, so the credits depend on which source you read.

## Musical Breakdown

No review walks through 68 State section by section or gives times, so this breakdown keeps to what the sources name.

### The Electronics

entertainment.ie heard an instrumental opener crammed with electronic bleeps and gadgetry, and Spectrum Culture filed it among the album's infectious electro sketches.

### The Groove

PopMatters' Evan Sawdey called it a kinetic, slithering dance song, equally suited to staring out of a train window late at night or to backing a space shooter game.

### The Mood

Afisha heard it as energetic and sinister, and DeBaser's reviewer as something straight out of an 80s cop show.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="b-side" /> }} is the second track of a single, once literally the other side of the record. Afisha's point is that this one plays against its A-side: where {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-de-la-soul-feel-good-inc.md" /> }} is laid back, 68 State is restless.

As an {{ <concept_link name="instrumental" /> }}, it leaves the mood entirely to sound. That is why reviewers reached for such different pictures, a train ride, an arcade game, a television chase: without words, the listener supplies the scene.

A {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }} of B-sides gathers what fans of an era might have missed. D-Sides collected the B-sides, remixes and bonus tracks around Demon Days plus unreleased tracks from the same sessions, and put this one first.

## Artistic Context

Gorillaz are the virtual band of Damon Albarn and Jamie Hewlett. D-Sides, released on 19 November 2007 in the UK by Parlophone and in the US by Virgin, followed Demon Days (2005); the same Feel Good Inc. single also carried {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-spitting-out-the-demons.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Reviews of D-Sides singled it out: PopMatters and entertainment.ie both wrote about it as the opener that sets the tone, and Spectrum Culture still called it infectious in 2015. Play it after Feel Good Inc. and hear the other side of the same single.
