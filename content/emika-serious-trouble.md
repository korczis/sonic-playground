## Genre and Mood

**Emika**'s **Serious Trouble** is electronic music turned towards electro-pop and R&B. The feelings named here come from the critics: alluring and sultry, after The Electricity Club's "moody allure" and Ibiza Spotlight's "seductive R&B pop"; dark, like the dark and brooding world Ibiza Spotlight heard across the whole album.

## Song Summary

Serious Trouble is the eighth of eleven tracks on DREI, Emika's 2015 album, and runs 3:47. The sources say little about the song on its own: Ibiza Spotlight placed it in the album's later stretch, with What's the Cure and Without Expression, where the record nudges towards seductive R&B pop, and The Electricity Club, looking back in 2018, wrote that its moody allure recalled Róisín Murphy. The music and lyrics are Emika's, and as on the rest of DREI she produced and mixed the track herself; the official upload credits its mastering to Hue Jah Fink.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through Serious Trouble from start to finish, so this breakdown stays with what the critics heard.

### Main Section

This is where the R&B turn belongs, a different pull from {{ <track_link source="emika-my-heart-bleeds-melody.md" /> }}, which the same review called electro-clash inspired. Under it, expect the palette critics heard across DREI: soft bass beats and dark futuristic sounds for Ibiza Spotlight, electroclash-era bass and beats with soaring synths for Piccadilly Records.

### Outro

How the track ends is not described. On the album it hands over to Destiny Killer, which Ibiza Spotlight called a dubbed-out soundscape with eerie piano chords.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="mixing" /> }} is the stage where every recorded part is balanced into one finished track: how loud the voice sits against the bass, where each sound is placed, how much space surrounds it. On DREI Emika did it herself: as Ibiza Spotlight put it, she writes, produces, mixes and sings all of her own material. If the voice in Serious Trouble seems to hold back, that restraint is also a mixing decision.

{{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }} is the scene of the late 1990s and early 2000s that crossed 1980s electro and new wave with deadpan cool. Piccadilly Records heard its bass and beats on DREI. Serious Trouble, by the critics' account, keeps some of that machinery but leans towards R&B pop, which may be why the comparison with Róisín Murphy came to mind.

## Artistic Context

Emika is Ema Jolly, born in Milton Keynes in 1986 to a Czech mother from Příbram and based in Berlin since 2006. After two albums on Ninja Tune she founded Emika Records in 2014; DREI, which FACT reported she wrote in a single two-week period, came out on it on 4 May 2015. She described the album as being about freedom, and about coping with it. Its opener is {{ <track_link source="emika-battles.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

The sources mention Serious Trouble only inside reviews of DREI, never as a song on its own. Yet when The Electricity Club looked back on DREI three years later, it named Miracles as a key highlight and Serious Trouble for that allure. Play it next to {{ <track_link source="roisin-murphy-primitive.md" /> }} and decide whether the allure is the same.
