## Genre and Mood

**Axwell**'s **Feel The Vibe** is house music in its most direct form: a long, physical dance record built for the moment a club stops thinking and starts moving. Electronic to the core, it is euphoric, energetic and relentlessly groovy, a track that measures its success in raised hands rather than clever ideas.

## Song Summary

This is the original, long version, the one that circulated in clubs from the summer of 2004 before the vocal single existed. Without a sung topline to lean on, it does everything with groove and patience: a pumping four-to-the-floor kick, a rolling bassline, filtered layers that open and close, and builds that keep promising more and then deliver it. Seven minutes long, it is a DJ's tool and a crowd's release at the same time.

## Musical Breakdown

The structure is classic mid-2000s house: long blends at each end, and the energy shaped by filters rather than by verses and choruses.

### Intro

A bare kick and percussion run for DJ-friendly bars, so the record can slide in under whatever came before.

### Build-up

Bass and harmonic layers arrive behind a closed filter, muffled at first, then slowly brightening as the sweep opens and the room starts to feel where it is going.

### Main Section

The full groove locks in: driving, bouncy and bright, with the hook riding on top of the bass. This is the stretch that made the record a peak-time favourite.

### Bridge

A breakdown strips the beat away, leaving the melodic layers hanging in the air while the tension builds for the return.

### Climax

The kick slams back in with everything open, the most euphoric minute of the track.

### Outro

Layers peel away until only rhythm remains, ready to be mixed out.

## Artistic Context

Axwell is the Swedish producer Axel Hedfors, later one third of Swedish House Mafia. He made "Feel The Vibe" in 2004; it came out on Nero Records and then on his own Axtone label, and when the record was picked up for a UK release, the singer-songwriter Tara McDonald wrote and sang a topline for it. That vocal version, "Feel the Vibe ('Til the Morning Comes)", followed in 2005.

## Impact and Reception

The 2005 single reached number one on the UK dance chart and number 16 on the UK Singles Chart, and the track became one of the records that made Axwell a headline name long before Swedish House Mafia filled stadiums. Two decades on it still sounds like a warm-up for something big. Turn it up loud, and give it the full seven minutes.
