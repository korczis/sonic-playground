## Genre and Mood

**Florence + The Machine** close their third album with **Mother**, almost six minutes of art rock that turns psychedelic on its way out. It starts as a prayer and ends as a storm, and the feelings on this page follow that path: spiritual in its words, intense in its build, cathartic when the guitars finally break loose.

## Song Summary

Florence Welch wrote Mother with Paul Epworth, who produced it and, according to the album's credits, played its drums, bass, guitar, synths, organ and percussion himself. The lyric opens with a prayer that turns into a plea, apparently to Mother Nature, ending in the wish to be turned into a tree; The Guardian's review of the album picked out that same image of Welch invoking Mother Earth. The music takes its time. Sputnikmusic heard a slow unfurling, remarkable less for its eventual ferocity than for the path it takes to get there.

## Musical Breakdown

No source times the song's sections; the reviews describe its opening groove, its last chorus and its finale.

### Intro

No review describes the opening in detail. Spin called the song a bluesy organ shuffle, and that groove is its floor.

### Main Section

The verses carry the prayer and the plea. At first, Sputnikmusic thought, the song seems to squeeze everything the album had missed into one last track.

### Climax

During the last chorus a roiling guitar surfaces, angry and buzzing, which the same reviewer compared to The Jesus and Mary Chain rather than to Kate Bush. Rolling Stone heard meaty psych guitars and full-on rock shouts: a howl-at-the-moon finale.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="distortion" /> }} is the sound of the ending. An overdriven guitar clips and grows harmonics, and Sputnikmusic described the song soaring through a cloud of distortion. After a prayer, that grit reads as release.

The song is a long {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }}. It gathers over nearly six minutes, so the guitar in the last chorus lands as the end of a journey. Compare {{ <track_link source="florence-the-machine-how-big-how-blue-how-beautiful.md" /> }}, which also saves its peak for the end but reaches it with strings and brass.

Mother also had a place in a {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} series. Vincent Haycock directed The Odyssey, a chain of videos that follows Welch's journey to find herself again after a heartbreak, and Mother serves as the connecting scene of its seventh chapter, before {{ <track_link source="florence-the-machine-delilah.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

How Big, How Blue, How Beautiful was released by Island Records on 29 May 2015, with Markus Dravs producing most of it; Mother, its eleventh and last track on the standard edition, is Epworth's only contribution. Rolling Stone heard in it Welch's love of Jefferson Airplane, and Spin heard in its blues a hint of 1970s Rolling Stones boogie.

## Impact and Reception

The album went to number one in the UK and on the US Billboard 200, and critics mostly praised its cohesion, production and Welch's singing. Mother was not among its singles, yet for Sputnikmusic its hypnotic heights were the reason to listen to the band at all. Play it last, the way the album does, and let it take its time.
