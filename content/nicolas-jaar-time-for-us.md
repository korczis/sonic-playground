## Genre and Mood

**Nicolas Jaar** made his early name with **Time For Us**, a track that mixes minimal, deep house and experimental electronic music. Its mood is hypnotic, melancholic and intimate, a dance record that feels like a slow, swaying confession. It is club music that wants to be listened to as much as danced to.

## Song Summary

"Time For Us" begins as a dance track at a modest tempo, with a sultry, crooned vocal, dusty textures and a hypnotic groove. Then it does something rare for club music: it slows down dramatically, sinking into a long breakdown at a much lower tempo before finding its way back. That shifting pace gives the track an emotional, almost cinematic arc, and it is the detail that made it so memorable, and so hard to mix. The production is warm and slightly lo-fi, with the human touch of a young producer experimenting freely, and the vocal sounds like a voice remembered rather than recorded.

## Musical Breakdown

The structure is unusual, a dance groove that slows into a reverie and returns.

### Intro

A soft, minimal beat and dusty textures set the mood at a measured dance tempo.

### Main Section

The vocal enters, sultry and crooned, riding the hypnotic groove with a hint of melancholy.

### Bridge

The tempo drops sharply into a long breakdown, the track slowing to a swaying, almost dreamlike pace where every element seems suspended.

### Climax

The groove gathers itself and builds again, the return of the beat feeling earned and emotional. It is a lesson in tension and release played out over minutes rather than bars.

### Outro

The track fades gently, the textures and vocal dissolving into silence.

## Artistic Context

Nicolas Jaar is a Chilean-American producer who was still a student when he released "Time For Us" in 2010 on Wolf + Lamb, the New York label at the heart of a slow, soulful house scene. The EP paired it with "Mi Mujer", and Zev of Wolf + Lamb described the track as Jaar moving from dance music toward songwriting, a direction confirmed by his 2011 album *Space Is Only Noise*. His mix of Latin American influences, deep house rhythm and experimental structure quickly made him one of the most talked-about young producers of the time.

## Impact and Reception

Reviewers praised the track's catchiness despite its unusual shifts in tempo, and it became a famous set-closer, its tempo drop so steep that DJs rarely tried to mix out of it. It is here because it shows how much emotion can live in a minimal groove. Listen all the way through the breakdown.
