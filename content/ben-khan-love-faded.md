## Genre and Mood

**Ben Khan**'s **love faded** sits between electro-pop, funk and electronic production, like the rest of his debut album, but leans harder on a guitar. Gritty, groovy and faintly futuristic, it sets a rough guitar against bright machine sounds.

## Song Summary

"love faded" is the tenth of fifteen tracks on Ben Khan, released by Dirty Hit on 10 August 2018. The writing is credited to Benedict Khan alone. The title states its theme plainly; no review this page found reads the lyric further. What reviewers noticed is the collision of sounds: Epigram's Bethany Marris picked it as an example of Khan drawing on an unusual variety of sources, scanner bleeps married to distorted blues guitar, under the dystopian undercurrent she heard across the whole album.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the track section by section. What follows are the layers the reviews name, not a map of its bars.

### The Guitar

A distorted, bluesy guitar in Epigram's account, which The National Student heard as funk guitar: one of the album's common threads, most prominent, it says, here, on {{ <track_link source="ben-khan-a-t-w-against-the-wall.md" /> }} and on {{ <track_link source="ben-khan-the-green.md" /> }}.

### The Electronics

Bleeps that Epigram compared to a supermarket scanner, and around them what The National Student called electronic production adding depth and pace, with oscillating beats that command attention.

### The Voice

Soft vocals over that guitar, in The National Student's words. The voice stays gentle while the guitar stays rough.

## Concepts in Focus

The guitar is a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="distortion" /> }}: overdriving a signal until it clips adds harmonics, grit and presence. Here the roughest sound in the song is also the most old-fashioned one.

Calling that guitar bluesy points back to the {{ <concept_link name="blues" /> }}, whose phrasing runs through rock and soul. Written in Music notes that Khan listened to old blues as a teenager, alongside seventies rock and nineties grunge, and tried to play the songs that inspired him on guitar; a record full of futuristic synths still carries that apprenticeship.

The album was produced by Flood, known for his work with PJ Harvey, Depeche Mode and Nine Inch Nails, and it shows how a {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} can choose restraint. The Guardian's Ben Beaumont-Thomas heard a raw, bedroom-recorded quality and wrote that Flood brought little big-budget gloss, leaving Khan's singular vision in front.

## Artistic Context

Ben Khan is a London producer, singer and songwriter. He emerged in 2014 with the EP 1992, followed it with 1000 in 2015 and then, as Epigram tells it, withdrew from music until April 2018, when the opening track "2000 angels" announced the album. According to The National Student, Khan wanted the record to marry its produced electropop with organic sounds such as guitar, which he recorded with a field microphone.

## Impact and Reception

"love faded" drew only passing mentions, but warm ones: XS Noize's Gemma Harewood called it another must-hear addition to the set, and The Line of Best Fit gave the album 8.5 out of 10. DIY was cooler, finding the record wildly inventive but largely muddled. Play it loud enough to hear the scanner and the blues guitar sharing one room.
