## Genre and Mood

**Kelis** and **Terrar** built **Good Stuff** from R&B, hip-hop and funk. It is a groovy, alluring song of desire, and its confidence, as Freaky Trigger heard it in 2000, lies in a beat that swaggers.

## Song Summary

Albumism heard Kelis on Good Stuff as flushed with desire. Pharrell Williams and Chad Hugo, the Neptunes, wrote and produced it at Master Sound in Virginia Beach, and Clash described its funk bass wobbles under Kelis's husky delivery. The song also features a young Virginia rapper credited as Terrar, the first stage name of Pusha T. Freaky Trigger summed it up: no twists, but with a beat that swaggering it hardly needs any.

## Concepts in Focus

Good Stuff is a textbook {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} credit. Terrar was not yet a name: he and his brother would become Clipse, and he would later be famous as Pusha T. The Neptunes, fellow Virginians who had helped the brothers sign to Elektra, put him on their new singer's single, which is how producers have long introduced their circle. Hear the same voice with his brother on {{ <track_link source="n-e-r-d-clipse-loser.md" /> }}.

The song's {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} is what critics noticed first. The Neptunes loved, in Freaky Trigger's words, clockwork beats and wobbly bass, and on Good Stuff it is the bass that Clash singled out. A bassline like this is a hook in its own right.

The UK single also shows how a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} serves a different audience. Next to the album version came radio edits, a Forces of Nature mix and Junior Vasquez's eleven-minute Transatlantic Mix, the same song rebuilt for clubs.

## Artistic Context

Kelis Rogers grew up in Harlem and studied at the LaGuardia High School of Music & Art before a friend introduced her to the Neptunes, who helped her sign to Virgin. Her debut Kaleidoscope, produced entirely by them, came out in the US on 7 December 1999. Its first single, Caught Out There, reached number four in the UK; Good Stuff, track two, with Terrar on vocals, followed as a UK single on 5 June 2000.

## Impact and Reception

Good Stuff reached number 19 in the UK and number six on the UK hip-hop and R&B chart, and number three in Iceland. Kaleidoscope sold poorly in America but went gold in Britain, where Freaky Trigger called it the most convincing pop-soul record for ages. Play it loud enough to feel the bass.
