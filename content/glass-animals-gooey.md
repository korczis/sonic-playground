## Genre and Mood

**Glass Animals** found their signature sound on **Gooey**, a slow-motion fusion of indie pop, alternative R&B grooves and gentle psychedelia. It is dreamy, playful and strikingly intimate, a love song that feels like it is being whispered from inside a humid greenhouse.

## Song Summary

Gooey is about infatuation in its sweetest, silliest form, all sticky metaphors and murmured invitations, with Dave Bayley singing about peanut butter vibes as if it were the most romantic image in the world. The arrangement is spare and tactile: finger-snap beats, soft guitar, woozy keys and a bassline that oozes rather than drives. The detail that makes it memorable is the space; every sound seems to drip slowly into place. Nothing is rushed; the tempo stays low and the mix stays close, so the track feels less like a performance and more like a mood you step into.

## Musical Breakdown

The song unfolds like a lazy afternoon, each section only slightly warmer than the last.

### Intro

A muted guitar figure and soft clicks set a jungle-at-dusk atmosphere, sparse and inviting.

### Verse 1

Bayley's breathy vocal arrives close to the ear, half-sung and half-sighed, over a loping, hip-hop-leaning beat.

### Chorus

The hook swells with layered harmonies and a deeper bass, the closest the song comes to a release, sweet and slightly delirious.

### Bridge

The arrangement melts into a hazy interlude of echoing voices and shimmering keys, letting the groove breathe.

### Climax

The final chorus gathers all the textures together, warm and enveloping without ever raising its voice.

### Outro

The beat dissolves and the last vocal line lingers, as soft as the song began.

## Artistic Context

Glass Animals are an Oxford band led by singer, songwriter and producer Dave Bayley. Gooey was the lead single from their debut album ZABA, released in 2014 on Paul Epworth's label Wolf Tone, with Epworth co-producing. The song came with an EP of remixes, including a much-loved version by Gilligan Moss, and it defined the lush, exotic, slow-groove aesthetic of the whole record.

## Impact and Reception

Gooey became the band's breakout, building a loyal following through blogs, streaming and word of mouth long before their later global success, and it remains a fan favourite in their live sets. It is on this playground because few songs make softness feel this addictive. Slow down, put on good headphones and let it stick.
