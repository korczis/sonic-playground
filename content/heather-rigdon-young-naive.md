## Genre and Mood

**Heather Rigdon** sings **Young & Naïve**, the title track of her 2007 debut, as small-group jazz: one voice, a piano, a bass and drums, under three minutes long. It is intimate and relaxed, a singer taking her time; her biography places her between traditional and contemporary jazz.

## Song Summary

Rigdon wrote the song with Cliff Goldmacher, its producer, and the songwriter Tom Kimmel. The credits list a compact band: Catherine Styron on piano, David Francis on bass and Jim White on drums, with Goldmacher also recording and mixing it and Jim DeMain mastering it. What makes it memorable, by the one detailed review found, is the singing rather than the arrangement: MusicRow's Robert K. Oermann heard in this track a distinctive, off-the-beat phraser and a languid stylist with a charming way of handling a minor-key melody. ## Concepts in Focus

The central idea is {{ <concept_link name="phrasing" /> }}: how a singer places words and notes against the pulse. Calling Rigdon an off-the-beat phraser means she does not land squarely on each beat, a habit of many jazz singers. Her biography compares her singing to Peggy Lee and Chris Connor.

Oermann heard her play with a melody in a {{ <concept_link name="minor key" /> }}, the scale whose lowered third gives music a darker colour than a major key; listen for how that colour sits with her relaxed delivery.

The third idea is the {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }}. Goldmacher co-wrote the song, produced, recorded and mixed it, and the release is credited to his own production company, distributed through TuneCore.

## Artistic Context

According to her biography, Rigdon was raised in Texas by parents who toured as religious singers; her father was an ordained Pentecostal preacher, and as a child she sang hymns, gospel and sacred songs with little access to secular music. After school she moved to Nashville, met Goldmacher, who produced her debut and wrote many of its songs, and became a regular at the Bluebird Café. Young & Naïve came out in 2007; MusicBrainz also lists a Japanese edition issued in 2009 by T.A.C.S Records.

## Impact and Reception

The record was a small, independent release; the notice found for it is Oermann's MusicRow column of 4 February 2009, which gave Rigdon its DisCovery Award and called her promising in the extreme. Her biography mentions her alongside Melody Gardot and Roberta Gambarini. Listen for where each phrase lands against the beat.
