## Genre and Mood

**Anita Ward**'s **Ring My Bell** is disco at its most economical: a dance record built on one electronic drum sound, a chiming motif and a voice. It topped the American disco and soul charts as well as the pop chart in 1979, and its mood is groovy and playful, with a sultry edge that Billboard later acknowledged by placing it among its 50 sexiest songs of all time.

## Song Summary

Frederick Knight wrote and produced the song, first as a teenybopper number for the eleven-year-old Stacy Lattisaw about children talking on the telephone. When Lattisaw signed with another label, Knight gave it to Ward with more adult lyrics, though this origin story is disputed. The lyric now has a woman inviting her partner to relax with her after a hard day at work. Some heard sexual suggestion in it; Knight said he deliberately kept the words clean to give Ward a clean-cut image. The detail everyone remembers is the Synare, an electronic drum playing a high, decaying tom tone at the start of every bar.

## Musical Breakdown

This page carries the album and twelve-inch version, 8:11 against the 3:30 of the seven-inch. No source maps those eight minutes part by part, so the notes below stay with what reviewers described.

### Intro

Billboard described a whooping sound accenting the first beat, the Synare tone that announces the song before the voice does.

### Main Section

Guitars play around the edges of the drums, chimes answer the title line, and Ward sings the invitation over a groove that never gets crowded.

## Concepts in Focus

The Synare is one of the first {{ <concept_link name="electronic drums" /> }}: a pad that triggers a sound generator instead of a drum shell. Wikipedia places its falling tom on the first beat of every bar; PBS's Marco Werman heard it on the fourth and compared it to the Space Invaders video game. Either way it works as a {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} that needs no words.

The {{ <concept_link name="extended mix" /> }} explains this page's length: the twelve-inch single, made for clubs and DJs, gave dancers more than twice the time of the radio edit.

## Artistic Context

Ward, born in Memphis, studied psychology at Rust College and was working as a schoolteacher when Knight, the owner of her label Juana, recorded her debut album Songs of Love, released through TK in 1979. She did not like the song at first and recorded it at Knight's insistence. It became her only major hit. The streaming album that carries this version is a 2005 reissue on 601 Music.

## Impact and Reception

Ring My Bell reached number one in the US, the UK and Canada, went platinum in the US and earned Ward a Grammy nomination for Best Female R&B Vocal Performance. It reappears in lists of the best dance songs, from VH1 in 2000 to Billboard in 2025, and in 2026 Aitch reworked it as RMB (Ring My Bell). Play the long version and let the first beat of every bar do its work.
