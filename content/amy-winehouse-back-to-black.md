## Genre and Mood

**Amy Winehouse**'s **Back to Black** is soul and R&B in mourning dress. Written in D minor and produced to sound like a 1960s girl-group record, it is heartbroken, melancholic and dark.

## Song Summary

The song grew out of Winehouse's relationship with Blake Fielder-Civil, who left her for an ex-girlfriend. The lyric is full of hurt and bitterness about a man who went back to what he knew, while the narrator stays strong and goes back to black, a darkness read as drinking and depression. Mark Ronson, who co-wrote and produced it, said he played her Shangri-Las records, built a little piano riff that became the verse chords, then added a kick drum, a tambourine and tons of reverb. That sketch is the whole idea of the record: old-school soul with a much darker heart.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

musicOMH's John Murphy compared the opening to Martha and the Vandellas' Jimmy Mack, then noted how quickly the song goes somewhere much darker than that Motown hit.

### Verse

Ronson's piano riff carries the verses, with drums, bass and guitar around it and the reverb-soaked tambourine on the beat.

### Chorus

The title line arrives in the chorus. Ronson asked her to change a line there so it would rhyme; she refused, saying it was what came out.

### Outro

The credits add violin, viola and cello sections to the band. No source describes the ending, so listen for what the strings and the voice do in the last bars.

## Concepts in Focus

Back to Black is a modern study in the {{ <concept_link name="wall of sound" /> }}. Reviewers noted the production's Wall of Sound quality, and the BBC's Matt Harvey heard Phil Spector and Scott Walker in what he called a tortured monster of a track. Ronson's ingredients, piano, kick, tambourine and much reverb, are the ones that made Spector's girl-group singles sound huge and distant at once.

The song is in a {{ <concept_link name="minor key" /> }}, D minor, which casts a shadow over the girl-group frame. Compare it with the brighter, defiant {{ <track_link source="amy-winehouse-rehab.md" /> }} from the same album.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Phil Griffin and shot in black and white in Stoke Newington and Abney Park Cemetery, stages a funeral procession for the relationship, with a grave marked for the heart of Amy Winehouse; that shot was edited out after her death in 2011.

## Artistic Context

Back to Black was the title track of Winehouse's second album, released by Island Records on 27 October 2006 and recorded with Ronson at Daptone and Chung King studios in New York and Metropolis in London. It was released as the album's third single in April 2007, after Rehab and You Know I'm No Good.

## Impact and Reception

The single reached number 25 in the UK in 2007 and re-entered at number eight after Winehouse's death in July 2011. Rolling Stone ranked it 79th on its 500 Greatest Songs of All Time, and Beyoncé with André 3000, Florence Welch and Ronnie Spector have covered it. Start with the first bars and hear how quickly the frame turns dark.
