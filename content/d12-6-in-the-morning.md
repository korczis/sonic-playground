## Genre and Mood

**D12** recorded **6 In The Morning** for D12 World, their second album, and it is hip-hop and rap at its most stripped down: a piano melody, drums, and the voices of the Detroit crew. The mood is defiant and gritty, aimed at the group's haters, yet one line in Eminem's verse argues for answering them with words rather than weapons.

## Song Summary

A Sputnikmusic reviewer described the beat as a very distinctive piano melody with nothing but drums backing it, and the song as being about the familiar, broad topic of haters. He found its individual rhymes by far the best on the album. Eminem's verse holds what he called possibly the only positive rap on the record, the line about lifting a pencil rather than a pistol when angry, which Plugged In summed up as a scathing rap being better than a gun. The writing is credited to Marshall Mathers, Denaun Porter, Von Carlisle and Ondre Moore, that is Eminem, Kon Artis, Kuniva and Swift, together with Luis Resto.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the track section by section; this breakdown follows the beat and the verses the reviews name.

### Intro

The piano melody sets the tone from the start, with drums as its only company; there is no bassline or sample that any source describes.

### Verses

The verses pass between the credited members. Eminem's carries the pencil-and-pistol line; the reviewer thought he was not quite as strong here as on Git Up. Swift's verse is the one he praised without reservation, so consistent that he could not pick a single highlight.

## Concepts in Focus

6 In The Morning works as a {{ <concept_link name="posse cut" /> }}: one beat, several rappers, each given a verse. A crew track like this is a showcase of contrasts, and a sparse beat makes those contrasts easy to hear.

The art itself is {{ <concept_link name="rapping" /> }}, words placed rhythmically against the pulse. With only piano and drums underneath, every rapper's flow sits in the open.

The {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} credit belongs to Eminem, with Luis Resto as additional producer. Resto, a keyboardist from the Detroit area, has worked closely with Eminem for years and co-produced Lose Yourself, which won an Academy Award.

## Artistic Context

D12 formed in Detroit in 1996, initiated by Proof. D12 World came out on 27 April 2004 on Shady Records and Interscope, recorded between 2002 and 2004 while Eminem was also making Encore. Before the album, the song had already leaked on Straight from the Lab, a 2003 bootleg of unreleased Eminem recordings, under the title Come On In. Compare its beat with the slower, heavier {{ <track_link source="d12-40-oz.md" /> }} from the same album.

## Impact and Reception

D12 World debuted at number one on the Billboard 200 and the UK Albums Chart, though critics were mixed. The Sputnikmusic reviewer ranked 6 In The Morning second-best on the album. Put it on and follow the piano under each voice in turn.
