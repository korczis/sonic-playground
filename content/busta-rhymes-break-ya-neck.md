## Genre and Mood

**Busta Rhymes** made **Break Ya Neck** as hip-hop at a sprint: rap over a funk-driven beat, energetic and intense from its first words, and bold in the way only a performer this sure of his speed can be. RapReviews filed it with the hardcore funk of the album Genesis, and its title is also its instruction: nod your head until it hurts.

## Song Summary

The song is about the music itself, a call to the listener to move. Its power lies in delivery. Busta's verses race over an uptempo beat produced by Dr. Dre with Scott Storch, and the song borrows a chant from the Red Hot Chili Peppers' Give It Away, which is why all four members of that band appear among the writers. Truth Hurts adds vocals. PopMatters called it an instant classic on sheer momentum and its opening vocal line alone.

### Intro

The song starts with that opening line and a beat that lands at once. Listen for how little time it spends setting up before the rapping starts.

### Main Section

Verses and chorus alternate at full pace. Busta said that when he heard Dre's uptempo beat he knew immediately how he would attack it, and Dre let him take the lead. The chorus came the other way round: Dre hummed the flow pattern and Busta put words to it.

No source describes the ending of the album version. In the official video, which runs longer than the 3:51 track, Hype Williams frames the song with its cast of guests.

## Concepts in Focus

Here {{ <concept_link name="rapping" /> }} is the main instrument: Busta's flow piles syllables and rhymes against the pulse. PopMatters even heard him doing a great Eminem impression.

An {{ <concept_link name="interpolation" /> }} re-records part of an existing song rather than sampling the recording. Here the Give It Away chant is performed anew, and its writers, Flea, John Frusciante, Anthony Kiedis and Chad Smith, share the credit. Hear the band themselves on {{ <track_link source="red-hot-chili-peppers-show-me-your-soul.md" /> }}.

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} shows how a producer can write without words. Busta told Pitchfork that Dre literally hummed the melody and flow of the chorus, giving him the navigation system to reach the destination.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Hype Williams and released on 16 October 2001, is a roll call of guests from Sean Combs to CeeLo Green.

## Artistic Context

Break Ya Neck came out on 25 August 2001 on Flipmode and J Records and became track ten of Genesis, Busta's fifth album, released that November. He recorded it at Record One in Los Angeles while he was shooting a Halloween film, sleeping in the studio for 48 hours with Dre and leaving with five songs. Compare his earlier sprint, {{ <track_link source="busta-rhymes-gimme-some-more.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

The single reached number 26 on the Billboard Hot 100, number 10 on the R&B/Hip-Hop chart and number 11 in the UK, and went platinum in New Zealand. PopMatters, unconvinced by the album, still singled it out. Turn it up and let it set the pace.
