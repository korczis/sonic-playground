## Genre and Mood

**Franco**'s **O Rock do Rato**, "the rat's rock", is filed today under samba rock, the Brazilian blend of samba with rock, soul and funk. Playful is the word its title suggests and groovy the word for the dance music it is collected with. Beyond its credits, though, it is barely documented: we found no review, liner note or biography that describes it, and this page says so rather than invent.

## Song Summary

The catalogues give the outline. Warner Music Brasil released O Rock do Rato in 1978 as the second of ten tracks on an album simply titled Franco, with Franco credited as the singer and Hélio Matheus as the composer; that version runs 3:18. The same album holds songs called Guitarreiro and Black Samba. Atração's Samba Rock Vol.1, released on 1 October 2007, collects it as its fourth track at 3:16, and MusicBrainz lists it on an earlier collection too, Humbatuque Apresenta Samba Rock, from 2003. Whether every copy is the same take is not documented.

## Musical Breakdown

### Main Section

No source describes the arrangement, so this is a guide to listening rather than a description. Wikipedia describes samba rock as built on drum kit, bass guitar, keyboards and brass with a strong groove, and with the electric guitar Jorge Ben brought to it. Listen for which of those this record uses, and for how the samba rhythm sits under a title that announces rock.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="syncopation" /> }} means accents where the ear does not expect them, between the beats or on the weak ones. Samba is built on it, and in samba rock it meets the steadier backbeat of rock and soul. On this record, listen for the tension between the two.

The song's afterlife comes from the {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }}. Samba Rock Vol.1 places it straight after {{ <track_link source="doris-monteiro-e-isso-ai.md" /> }} and includes a second Franco song, Rock Enredo. Compilations like this put records back in front of dancers decades after release.

## Artistic Context

Samba rock, Wikipedia explains, grew from block parties in the predominantly Black neighbourhoods of São Paulo, where DJs mixed American rock and roll and soul with samba; Jorge Ben is called its father. Hélio Matheus started out in the nightclubs of Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo, recorded his first single in 1968 and composed more than 150 songs. Wikipedia names a Franco Scornavacca among the singers he wrote for; no source we found confirms that he is the Franco of this record.

## Impact and Reception

We found no review and no chart record. What we did find is a name problem: the compilation's official upload sits on a YouTube channel called Franco OK Jazz - Topic, and MusicBrainz files the recording under a Franco from the Democratic Republic of the Congo, while the record is sung in Portuguese and comes from Warner's Brazilian catalogue. Listen past the mix-up to the record itself.
