## Genre and Mood

**Crookers** made **Mad Kidz** in the middle of the late-2000s electro-house boom, and it sits squarely inside it: electro sounds in a house frame, made for raves. The FADER, profiling the Milan duo that summer, described their music as artfully colossal house fit for brave new world raves, and that sets the feelings here: energetic and bold.

## Song Summary

Mad Kidz is the title track of a four-track EP that Southern Fried Records released in July 2008, alongside Embrace the Martian with Kid Cudi, Sveglia and Magic Bus. It runs almost five minutes, a length made for DJs rather than radio. No published review describes this track on its own, so this page does not invent a structure for it. What the sources do give is the duo's sound at the time: hip-hop spliced with house, synth-heavy, built on dramatic pauses and build-ups, with a weakness for car horns and exclamation-like hooks.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="club culture" /> }} is the world this track was made for and the world that named it. The FADER reported that fans online had tagged Crookers fidget house and blog house, labels born on music blogs and dancefloors, while Bot insisted it was just house music. The FADER even hoped they might lead the next big European dance movement after Justice; hear that act in {{ <track_link source="justice-vs-simian_we-are-your-friends.md" /> }}.

A {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }} raises tension before a release. The FADER singled out the duo's dramatic pauses and build-ups; on a dancefloor, the silence before the return can be as loud as the drop itself.

Crookers' name was made largely through the {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }}. The FADER listed their reworks of the Ramones, AC/DC, Daft Punk, Kid Cudi's Day 'n' Nite and the Chemical Brothers' {{ <track_link source="chemical_brothers_the_salmon_dance.md" /> }}. Their version of Day 'n' Nite, released as a single in December 2008, entered the UK Singles Chart at number two.

## Artistic Context

Crookers were Francesco "Phra" Barbaglia and Andrea "Bot" Fratangelo from Milan. They met, the FADER wrote, in the corner of a record shop where Bot sold leftfield electro and house; Phra was a former rapper, Bot a former graffiti writer. In 2008 they released three EPs of original tracks, Knobbers, Mad Kidz and E.P.istola. Bot left in 2012, and Phra carries on under the name.

The FADER promoted the EP in June 2008 with a free teaser ahead of a show at Webster Hall in New York. Play it loud, and listen for the pause before the return.
