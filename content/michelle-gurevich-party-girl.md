## Genre and Mood

**Michelle Gurevich** made **Party Girl** at home, and it sounds like it: an intimate, slow song between indie pop, dream pop and the slowcore and lo-fi pop she is usually filed under. The mood is reflective and melancholic, a song about nightlife sung from the morning after.

## Song Summary

"Party Girl" is built on Gurevich's voice, a low contralto delivered with a deadpan calm that can be tender and ironic in the same line. The song portrays the party girl rather than celebrating her: the thrill of the night is there, but so is the loneliness around it. It is the title track of her 2007 debut, released under the name Chinawoman, and it became one of her best-known songs.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro: Atmospheric Start

The song opens with a spare, unhurried arrangement. Its home-made sound is part of the mood: close, a little rough, nothing between the voice and the listener.

### Verse 1: Lyrical Exploration

As the first verse unfolds, Gurevich's low voice takes centre stage, sketching the persona of the party girl with a mix of longing and affectionate irony.

### Chorus: Emotional Resonance

The chorus sings the title slowly and plainly, which makes it sound less like an invitation and more like a description of someone seen from a distance.

### Bridge: Reflective Moment

Midway through, the song gives the voice more space, the arrangement staying minimal as she lingers on the picture she has drawn.

### Verse 2 and Outro: Continuing the Journey

The second verse keeps the introspective tone, and the track fades out gently, without a climax.

## Concepts in Focus

Party Girl is a fine example of {{ <concept_link name="lo-fi" /> }} recording. Gurevich started out recording at home; her first stage name, Chinawoman, was a joke she typed when GarageBand asked her for a band name. The modest means are audible and they suit the song: a polished studio production would make it glamorous, and the song is about the gap between glamour and what lies under it.

The song also shows how music and film can feed each other, the other side of a {{ <concept_link name="soundtrack" /> }}. Rather than being written for a film, Party Girl inspired one: the 2014 French film Party Girl by Marie Amachoukeli, Claire Burger and Samuel Theis takes its title from Gurevich's song, and it won the Caméra d'Or at Cannes that year. Her songs have been used on screen since, as {{ <track_link source="michelle-gurevich-lovers-are-strangers.md" /> }} was for the Latvian film Kolka Cool.

## Artistic Context

Michelle Gurevich was born in Toronto to Russian-Jewish immigrants: her father an engineer from Leningrad, her mother a Kirov ballerina. She grew up speaking Russian first and worked in film for about ten years before turning to music. She cites Alla Pugacheva, Leonard Cohen, Yoko Ono and Federico Fellini as influences. Party Girl was self-released on 1 April 2007; after criticism that a non-Chinese woman was using the name Chinawoman, she dropped it and has since released her albums under her own name, all of them self-released.

## Cultural Impact

"Party Girl" became one of Gurevich's most loved songs and helped build her largest audience, in Russia and Eastern Europe, where she toured regularly. Its afterlife in a Cannes-winning film shows how a quiet home recording can travel. It is a song to hear late, and slowly.
