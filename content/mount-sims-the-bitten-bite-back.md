## Genre and Mood

**Mount Sims** delivers **The Bitten Bite Back** as electroclash with a synth-pop pulse and a grudge to settle. The mood is dark, defiant and hypnotic: cold machines, a deadpan voice and a title that reads like a threat. It sounds like the moment the victim in a horror film stops running and turns around.

## Song Summary

The song turns revenge into a groove. Its premise is right there in the title, the wounded striking back, and Mount Sims plays it with the cool detachment that made him one of electroclash's more menacing figures. Instead of exploding, the track stalks: a tight drum machine, wiry synth lines and a vocal that sneers more than it sings. The effect is part dancefloor chant, part horror soundtrack, and wholly his own.

## Musical Breakdown

At just over three minutes, the track is lean and focused, built from a handful of hard-edged parts that lock together and refuse to let go.

### Intro

A clipped drum machine pattern and a buzzing synth bass set the scene immediately, cold and sparse, with no warm-up and no wasted bars.

### Verse 1

The vocal enters flat and close, delivered with a deadpan sneer. It sits right on top of the beat, half spoken and half sung, and the menace comes from how calm it sounds.

### Chorus

The title phrase becomes a chant, repeated over a brighter synth line that gives the song its pop backbone without softening its bite.

### Bridge

The arrangement strips back to the bass and drums for a few bars, a tense pause that makes the return of the hook hit harder.

### Outro

The synths keep circling as the track winds down, leaving the chant ringing like a warning in the dark.

## Artistic Context

Mount Sims is the project of the American producer and singer Matt Sims, who made his name on the electroclash scene of the early 2000s with records on International DeeJay Gigolo. The Bitten Bite Back first appeared on his 2008 album Happily Ever After, released on Hungry Eye Records, and later on its companion edition Happily Ever After... Again. By then electroclash's first wave had passed, and Sims was using its sleek, synthetic toolkit for something moodier and more personal.

## Impact and Reception

Mount Sims never chased the charts, and this track is a cult favourite rather than a hit, the kind of song passed between DJs and late-night playlist makers. That is exactly why it belongs here: it captures the darker, wittier side of electroclash, where attitude matters as much as melody. Play it loud and let the chant get under your skin.
