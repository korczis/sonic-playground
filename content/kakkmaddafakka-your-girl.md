## Genre and Mood

**Kakkmaddafakka**'s **Your Girl** is indie pop from a Bergen indie rock band at its most relaxed. A Dutch reviewer heard the album it comes from as light, easily digestible and often cheerful pop, and that is the mood here: carefree, playful and upbeat.

## Song Summary

Your Girl is the second track of Hest, the band's second album, and was one of its two singles after Restless. It runs three and a half minutes. What sets it apart on the record is the voice: Kakkmaddafakka had four lead singers, and on this song the lead belongs to Jonas Nielsen, the band's pianist. The Festivalinfo review counted it among the album's highlights and said it strongly recalls Friska Viljor.

## Concepts in Focus

Hest shows the stages a band record goes through after the playing is done. Erlend Øye produced it, shaping the arrangements and the sound; Jonas Verwijen did the {{ <concept_link name="mixing" /> }}, balancing the recorded parts into one stereo picture; and Bo Kondren mastered it for release. In a band with six members and several singers, the mix decides which voice and which instrument sit in front at any moment.

The single got a {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, released on 29 May 2011 and directed by Rick Hvalross, with Pål Rønnevik as director of photography and Siv Wangsmo as producer. A video gives an album track a second life as a single, and with it a face and a story that the audio alone does not carry.

## Artistic Context

Kakkmaddafakka formed in Bergen and is known for high-energy live shows. Hest came out on 25 February 2011 on Bubbles Records, the label of Erlend Øye of Kings of Convenience and The Whitest Boy Alive, who also produced it. The streaming credits name Axel and Pål Vindenes, Jonas Nielsen, Stian Sævig and Matias Tellez among its writers. Nielsen left the band in early 2014.

## Impact and Reception

Hest got four out of six from both Bergensavisen and NRK P3. The Festivalinfo review was cooler, calling it a nice album and no more, but it singled out Your Girl. The song is here for exactly that lightness: three and a half minutes of a band enjoying itself. Put it on when the day needs less weight.
