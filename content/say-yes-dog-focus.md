## Genre and Mood

**Say Yes Dog**'s **Focus** is synth-pop with an indie heart, uplifting and quietly confident. It is a song about pulling yourself together, and it does so without fanfare: a lyric in which the singer talks himself into trusting himself, and a beat that, in one reviewer's words, affirms the hope.

## Song Summary

Focus is a track from Plastic Love, the trio's debut album, released on 4 September 2015 by Diskodogs Records. Vanguard's review describes it as "another track about confidence in yourself and hope that once you declutter your mind of failure, you'll be able to do something good. That you have self-worth." Then it adds the musical point: "The beat here affirms the hope." The Topic upload names all three members beside the band and credits Aaron Ahrends as producer.

## Musical Breakdown

The reviews describe Focus as a whole rather than part by part, so this breakdown follows what they hear: a beat, a voice and the synths around them.

### The Beat

The trio's rhythm section is Pascal Karier on drums and Paul Rundel on bass, and the beat is where Vanguard heard the song's hope made audible.

### The Vocal

Ahrends sings the song's argument, clearing out failure to make room for self-worth, over that pulse.

### The Synthesizers

The keyboards frame the voice; how they change across the song is not described in the sources.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} makes sound electronically, shaping oscillators with filters and envelopes, and in synth-pop it often carries the harmony a guitar band would give to guitars. Vanguard wrote that the style of synth on Plastic Love "isn't revelatory" but is "laid down with a level of skill and expertise that makes for a really satisfying listen", and Black Plastic heard the same thing from another side: production that "favours function over form", electronics in support of the voice. Focus belongs to that album-wide approach.

The {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} is the felt quality of the rhythm, how drums and bass lock together. In Focus it does more than keep time: it is where the reviewer found the song's hope. The same trio made the album's first single, {{ <track_link source="say-yes-dog-stronger.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Say Yes Dog are Aaron Ahrends, Paul Rundel and Pascal Karier, two Berliners and a Luxembourger who met studying sound engineering and jazz at the conservatory in The Hague. As Black Plastic tells it, they formed out of necessity: unable to get tickets for Germany's Fusion festival, they sent in a video of themselves playing, won a slot and then had to write enough songs to fill a set. Those songs became the 2013 EP {{ <track_link source="say-yes-dog_a-friend.md" /> }}; Plastic Love followed, recorded in a studio in Haarlem.

## Impact and Reception

Vanguard called Plastic Love "an impressive piece of work" for a debut, an album "to share with friends, or to get you going for a night out", and Black Plastic compared the band to Hot Chip and Metronomy. Focus was not a single, but it holds the album's mood. Put it on when you need to clear your head.
