## Genre and Mood

**Marbert Rocel**'s **Ba Ba Bounce** comes from an album that Juno filed under broken beat and nu-jazz and that its reviewer called soulful techno, drawing heavily on the traditions of jazz. The mood is playful and carefree, with what Compost Records describes as the band's stroke of bittersweet pain.

## Song Summary

Ba Ba Bounce is the second of thirteen tracks on Catch A Bird, the Thuringian trio's second album, and runs a little over four minutes. Compost released it in August 2009 on a two-track single with Clappeople, in a radio edit of just under three minutes, before the album followed that autumn. Juno's Peter Carroll named both among the album's standout moments. The credits name a full band: Antje Seifarth sings, Alexander Binder plays bass, Philipp Milner synthesizer and Panthera Krause saxophone, while Marcel Aue programmed it.

## Musical Breakdown

No review walks through the track part by part, so this breakdown stays with what Juno heard and what the credits name.

### Main Section

Listen first for the rhythm Juno described as understated ska, then for the voice over it. Carroll wrote that Seifarth's insouciant delivery adds charm throughout the album, and here she sings words she co-wrote with Krause. How the credited bass, synthesizer and saxophone sit against the programmed beat is for your ears to follow.

## Concepts in Focus

Ska, which grew up in Jamaica, puts its accent on the {{ <concept_link name="off-beat" /> }}: the guitar or keyboard chop falls between the main beats, so the groove seems to skip rather than march. In house music the same place belongs to the open hi-hat. An understated ska beat in an electronic track borrows that skip without the full horn section.

The single carried a {{ <concept_link name="radio edit" /> }}, about a minute shorter than the album track. Radio edits usually reach the vocal sooner and repeat less, so the two versions side by side show what the band treats as essential.

Compost also put out an official {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} in 2010, credited as directed by Marbert Rocel themselves, with Spunk, Aue and Krause named as its writers. Compare the credits of {{ <track_link source="marbert-rocel-trenchcoat.md" /> }}, which share the programmer and the saxophonist but name no singer.

## Artistic Context

Marbert Rocel formed in Erfurt in 2004 and are based in Leipzig: Marcel Aue (DJ Malik), Robert Krause (Panthera Krause) and the singer Antje Seifarth (Spunk). Compost's video description says Aue had co-produced the records Weit Weg and Gute Musik for the pop-rap act Clueso, and that Seifarth sang backing vocals for Clueso. Their debut, Speed Emotions, came out on Compost in 2007; Catch A Bird followed in October 2009, with Bandcamp and Compost giving different days.

## Impact and Reception

Juno praised the album as lively and unpredictable and picked out this track by name; no source reports a chart place. Compost has also shared a live performance of it from Zauberbaum Camp.
