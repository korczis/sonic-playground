## Genre and Mood

**Ian Brown** opens his sixth solo album with **Stellify**, alternative rock with an indie singer's swagger and a pop song's economy. It is euphoric and confident, and, by Brown's own account, romantic: he called it kind of a love song, the first he had ever consciously set out to write.

## Song Summary

The title is a rare word, and that was the point. Brown told Clash that he tries to put an unusual word into each album's pop song; the one before had been solarised. Hot Press glossed it from the Latin as to be turned into a star. Brown described the song as probably about being a young guy. Its hook, in his words, is six piano notes repeating.

## Musical Breakdown

No source maps the album version section by section or gives times; what follows is what reviewers heard.

### Intro

The piano figure is the song's signature, and Hot Press heard the whole track as jaunty and keyboard-driven.

### Verses and Chorus

Gigwise heard staccato rhythms colliding head-on with a pounding bassline. The credits list three drummers, Inder "Goldfinger" Matharu, Robert Maxfield and Tim Hutton, with Richard Stubbs on bass guitar and Dave McCracken on keyboards.

### Brass

Gigwise singled out the euphoric brass and the chest-beating elation it aims for.

## Concepts in Focus

Stellify is a lesson in the instrumental {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }}. The six-note piano phrase is what Brown himself pointed to, ahead of any sung line.

The {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} here is also the co-writer. Brown told XFM that McCracken, newly signed to Jay-Z's Roc Nation, was asked to write for Rihanna; McCracken thought she would kill this song, then the next morning decided to keep it for Brown. Wikipedia tells it more briefly: Brown realised he had created a great sound and kept the track for himself.

The single also shows the {{ <concept_link name="radio edit" /> }} at work. The album version runs 4:57; the promo CD carried a single edit of 3:32, and the official video on Brown's VEVO channel also runs well under four minutes. Compare Brown's earlier work with a hip-hop producer in {{ <track_link source="ian-brown-eternal-flame.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Brown was the lead singer of the Stone Roses from their formation in 1983 until their split in 1996, then began a solo career with Unfinished Monkey Business in 1998. My Way, his sixth solo album, was recorded at Battery Studios in London with McCracken producing and released by Fiction on 28 September 2009. Stellify came out a week earlier, on 21 September, as its lead single.

## Impact and Reception

Stellify reached number 31 in the UK; Wikipedia notes it was still his last solo top-40 hit as of 2020. My Way reached number 8 on the UK Albums Chart and scored 72 on Metacritic. Drowned in Sound heard Stellify and Marathon Man as songs of self-affirmation and nocturnal wisdom. Start it and count the six notes.
