## Genre and Mood

**Camp Claude** are a French trio, and **Trap** is the song where their electro-pop leans hardest toward new wave and dream pop at once. Les Oreilles Curieuses placed the band's sound halfway between electro-pop, rock and new wave, and heard Trap as a condensed burst of what it called sky wave, as dreamy as it is catchy. Add the lyric's refusal and the feelings are dreamy, catchy and defiant.

## Song Summary

Trap is a refusal song: the singer will not fall into someone's trap, and the refrain says so plainly. Diane Sagnier wrote it with Leo Hellden and Michael Giffts, who produced it. It first appeared in August 2014 on the band's Hurricanes EP and returned, at the same two and a half minutes, near the end of their debut album Swimming Lessons in April 2016. The detail critics noticed was the voice: Benjamin Berton of Sun Burns Out wrote that Sagnier's vocal is worked on here and there, Trap included, to produce an old-fashioned echo.

### Main Section

Sagnier's echoing voice carries the refrain over a pulse Berton found madly addictive and strongly marked by dance music. IndieMusic called it a sensual electronic trap one would gladly get lost in.

### Outro

No source describes the ending; at 2:30 it arrives quickly, which suits a song critics heard as condensed.

## Concepts in Focus

The echo on the voice is a {{ <concept_link name="delay" /> }} effect or something close to it: the sound repeats a moment later and fades. Used lightly, it places a singer in a larger, older-sounding space, which is what Berton meant by an echo à l'ancienne.

The guitar and bass point back to {{ <concept_link name="post-punk" /> }} and its cold-wave branch. alter1fo heard the album as a big recycling of new wave; Sun Burns Out called it pop in the land of new wave, with a post-punk influence.

Trap has two {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} versions on the band's channel: a 2014 clip, described as a dream directed by Sagnier, and a 2015 official video that she filmed in northern Michigan as a stone-cold thriller.

## Artistic Context

Camp Claude are Sagnier, a Franco-American photographer and video maker, the Swede Hellden and the Englishman Giffts; the two men also played in Tristesse Contemporaine. Swimming Lessons came out on 8 April 2016 on Believe Recordings and reached number 183 in France. Hear the trio's later, quieter side in {{ <track_link source="camp-claude-old-downtown.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

The album divided critics, but Trap kept coming up. Les Oreilles Curieuses called it, with the title track, one of the record's real bombshells; Sun Burns Out called it beautiful. Play it twice: once for the refrain, once for the echo.
