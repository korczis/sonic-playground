## Genre and Mood

**Marcus Meinhardt** made **Endjoy** for the dancefloor of a club that no longer existed when it came out. Partyflock files his music under minimal, tech house and techno, and this page takes the first two; no review describes the track itself, so its feelings, groovy and hypnotic, are this page's reading of seven minutes of Berlin club music rather than a listener's account.

## Song Summary

Endjoy runs 6:55 and is the fourth of eleven tracks on the Dancefloor disc of Bar 25: Tage außerhalb der Zeit, the soundtrack to the documentary of the same name, released digitally on 3 May 2012, the day the film opened in German cinemas. The label wrote that it had worked for over a year with its closest musician friends on exclusive tracks for the film's final cut. The title reads like "end" folded into "enjoy"; the track has no lyrics that any source records.

## Concepts in Focus

Endjoy is a {{ <concept_link name="soundtrack" /> }} piece of an unusual kind: club music written for a film about a club. The documentary by Nana Yuriko and Britta Mischer follows the four owners of Bar 25, the club on the Spree that opened in 2004 and closed in 2010, where, as filmportal's synopsis puts it, time did not matter. The soundtrack split itself in two like the place: Dancefloor for the club tracks, Zirkus for fifteen songs from its cabaret, theatre and band side.

The track then lived on through {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" text="compilation albums" /> }}. A 12-inch maxi on the Bar 25 label followed in July 2012, and in 2018 Heinz Music opened 20 Years Best Of Marcus Meinhardt with it, the first of twelve tracks.

Behind both stands {{ <concept_link name="club culture" /> }}: Meinhardt is a DJ first, and a track like this is judged in a room at night.

## Artistic Context

Meinhardt comes from Thuringia and made his home in Berlin's club scene. Dachstock calls him one of its most important representatives, resident DJ at Kater Blau and earlier a regular at Katerholzig; most of his best-known tracks came out on Katermukke, and he founded his own label, Heinz Music.
