## Genre and Mood

**Gorillaz** and **Lou Reed** made **Some Kind of Nature** an alternative pop song with electronic means and a funk step, playful and quirky. The Quietus heard proto boogie-funk; Drowned in Sound heard something very, very jolly, very rinky dink, with Reed's deadpan in the middle of it.

## Song Summary

It is the ninth of Plastic Beach's sixteen tracks and runs 2:59. Gorillaz and Reed are credited with writing it. Reed sings and plays additional guitar, Gabriel Manuals Wallace adds drums, and a choir credited as The Purple, the People, the Plastic Eating People joins them.

Getting Reed took persistence. By Wikipedia's account, Albarn emailed him three times over a year and was turned down each time; on the fourth attempt he sent a demo of this song, and Reed answered more warmly. At Chung King Studios in New York, Reed let only Albarn into the session. Albarn recalled in Daniel Rachel's book Isle of Noises that Reed wrote the words in a cab journey uptown and back, "a very Lou Reed, New York thing to do", and returned with his thoughts on plastic.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the track or gives times. Reviewers describe its two voices and its mood, and that is what follows.

### The Voices

Wendy Roby of Drowned in Sound heard Reed singing deliberately off, half a beat behind Albarn, and wrote that Gorillaz had harnessed his doddery and made it charming. Songfacts calls his vocals semi-spoken.

### The Backing

Darren Lee of The Quietus wrote that Reed probably had not sounded so louche and vital in decades, over proto boogie-funk. No source names the instruments beyond the credits.

## Concepts in Focus

As a {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}, Reed shares the writing credit, not only the microphone. Albarn told Rolling Stone that Reed warned him: "Don't expect me to follow your verse-chorus thing. I'm just going to sing." {{ <concept_link name="verse-chorus form" /> }} is the pop habit of alternating verses with a returning chorus; Albarn said he valued that independence.

Reed's {{ <concept_link name="spoken word" /> }} manner is the heart of the track. Sean Highkin of Beats Per Minute found him impeccably nonchalant, and Alexander E. Traub of The Harvard Crimson called him just the right man for the song, his aloof monotone casually capturing the essence of repetitive, opaque lyrics.

## Artistic Context

Reed, a co-founder, singer and main songwriter of the Velvet Underground, died on 27 October 2013, aged 71. Plastic Beach, the third Gorillaz album, came out on 3 March 2010, and the video of {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-on-melancholy-hill.md" /> }} shows him among the guests crewing its submarine fleet.

## Impact and Reception

Robert Christgau listed it among the album's choice cuts, and Erik Thompson of The Line of Best Fit called Reed's contribution stellar and hilariously droll. Reed performed it with Gorillaz at Madison Square Garden on the Plastic Beach tour; Ryan Leas of Stereogum, who was there, recalled him spending most of it angrily gesturing to the side of the stage about his vocals. Listen for the half beat between the two voices.
