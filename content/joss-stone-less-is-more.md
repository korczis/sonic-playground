## Genre and Mood

**Joss Stone** sings **Less Is More** as classic soul with a modern R&B polish and an easy reggae lilt running through it. The mood is soulful, confident and relaxed, the sound of a singer who knows she does not need to push to be heard.

## Song Summary

The title doubles as a description of the performance. Instead of the big, belted runs she could deliver at will, Stone keeps much of the song in a warm, husky middle register and lets the groove do the swaying. A deep bass voice, a light, skanking rhythm and smooth background vocals give the track its laid-back, sunlit bounce, and the result feels effortless in the way only very good singing does.

## Musical Breakdown

The song moves at an unhurried pace, keeping its groove steady and letting the vocal shape the dynamics.

### Intro

A relaxed rhythm section sets up the lilting groove, warm bass and off-beat accents hinting at reggae before the voice arrives.

### Verse 1

Stone enters with a low, smoky tone, phrasing loosely around the beat, conversational and assured.

### Chorus

The hook opens out with layered background vocals, her voice lifting just enough to show the power held in reserve.

### Bridge

The arrangement thins for a moment and the deep backing voices come forward, leaning further into the song's Caribbean colours.

### Outro

The groove rolls on as Stone ad-libs over the final refrains, easing the song to a close without ever breaking its calm.

## Artistic Context

Less Is More appears on Mind Body & Soul, released in 2004, the album on which the teenage English singer moved from the covers of her debut The Soul Sessions to songs she co-wrote. Stone shares the writing credit with J. Shorten and Conner Reeves, the production is by Commissioner Gordon, and the soul singer Betty Wright, a mentor from her first record, produced the vocals. Delroy "Chris" Cooper adds the deep bass voice that gives the track its reggae flavour.

## Impact and Reception

Mind Body & Soul went to number one in the UK and confirmed that Stone was more than a gifted interpreter of old soul records. Less Is More has stayed in her live sets for years, often stretched out on stage into a long, loose jam. It earns its place here for how much it achieves with so little strain. Put it on a slow Sunday and let it sway.
