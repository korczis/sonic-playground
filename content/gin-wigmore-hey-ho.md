## Genre and Mood

**Gin Wigmore** arrived with a voice like gravel and honey, and **Hey Ho** shows it at its most playful. The song draws on retro soul, pop hooks and a bluesy swing, and the mood is bold and upbeat, cheeky enough to make a simple chant sound like a dare.

## Song Summary

"Hey Ho" is a high-spirited, rhythm-driven tune, with a vocal that struts, teases and dares you not to sing along. The arrangement leans on stomping beats and a vintage-flavoured groove, giving her raspy voice plenty of room to swing. Under the fun there is a streak of defiance, a singer clearly enjoying her own confidence. The memorable detail is the chant itself, instantly catchy and slightly mischievous, the kind of hook that works as well in a crowded room as on headphones. It is also a perfect showcase for her phrasing: she bends and clips the words with a singer's timing that makes even the simplest line sound like it has a wink attached.

## Musical Breakdown

The song is built for momentum, with very little standing still.

### Intro

A stomping rhythm sets a lively, toe-tapping pace from the first seconds.

### Verse 1

Wigmore's voice enters full of attitude, playful and sharp, riding the groove with obvious relish.

### Chorus

The "hey ho" hook turns into a group chant, simple, rousing and impossible to resist.

### Bridge

A brief shift in texture lets the band breathe and builds tension for the last round.

### Climax

The final chorus brings everything back at full energy, band and voice driving the song home.

### Outro

The track ends on a confident flourish rather than a fade, as if taking a bow.

## Artistic Context

Gin Wigmore is a singer-songwriter from Auckland, New Zealand. "Hey Ho" comes from her debut album Holy Smoke, released in 2009, the record that introduced her vintage soul and blues influences and her unmistakable rasp. It set the stage for the rougher rock of Gravel & Wine and the heavier sound of Blood to Bone, both of which also have songs on this playground.

## Impact and Reception

"Hey Ho" became one of the songs that made her name, helped along by its official video and its sing-along hook, and her debut earned her strong attention at home in New Zealand. It is the kind of chant that seems designed to be shouted back by a crowd. It belongs here as the cheerful entry point to her catalogue. Press play and try not to clap along.
