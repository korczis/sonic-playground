## Genre and Mood

**Robbie Williams**'s **Let Me Entertain You** is glam rock played by a pop star who had just left a boy band, and it sits where britpop met the 1970s. It is energetic, theatrical and playful, and it knows it: Williams himself called it more like camp rock opera, and not really heavy metal. The song asks for an audience in its title, and it got one.

## Song Summary

Williams and his writing partner Guy Chambers wrote it after watching the Rolling Stones' film Rock and Roll Circus, wanting a song in the manner of the Who; Williams has said the demo had a furious jungle beat underneath it. Chambers and Steve Power produced it. The lyric is mostly innuendo and double entendre: a man trying to persuade someone to cheat on their partner with him, with the other person's gender never stated. Songfacts adds that it also pokes fun at the singer's own camp, egotistic image.

### Main Section

Reviewing the single for Music Week in 1998, Alan Jones called it a fast and furious retro rocker with hints of Pinball Wizard and Sympathy for the Devil. Songfacts hears the Who in its piano part, although one of its own readers points out that Pinball Wizard has hardly any piano. On the album recording Williams also sings during the instrumental passage; the subtly remixed version on his 2004 Greatest Hits removes those vocals.

## Concepts in Focus

The song is a study in {{ <concept_link name="stage persona" /> }}. Its title is an offer, and Williams made it his concert opener for most of his shows, performing it more than 300 times, including the 2003 Knebworth concerts, the 1999 Brit Awards and Live 8 in 2005. Sung first, it introduces the entertainer rather than a story.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Vaughan Arnell, borrows another band's persona: the band is dressed as Kiss, Williams' make-up close to Gene Simmons' Demon and his outfit nearly identical to one of Paul Stanley's. Commentators called it a spoof of 1970s rock; Williams feared legal action, but Kiss took none.

As an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }}, the 2004 remix shows how a hit can be quietly changed for a compilation. This page reviews the 1997 album recording of Life Thru a Lens.

## Artistic Context

Life Thru a Lens, released in September 1997 by Chrysalis Records, was Williams' first solo album after Take That, recorded with Chambers from March 1997 in London. It entered the UK chart at number 11 and reached number 1 only months later, carried by Angels. Let Me Entertain You was its fifth and final single, released on 16 March 1998.

## Impact and Reception

The single reached number 3 in the UK and was later certified platinum there. Music Week expected it to lack the wide appeal of Angels, yet it became Williams' entrance music, and its 2003 Knebworth performance is recreated in the 2024 biopic Better Man. Compare it with {{ <track_link source="muse-uprising.md" /> }}, then play this one loud: it was built to open a show.
