## Genre and Mood

**Chet Faker** slows the night down on **Gold**, a track that blends electronic production, soul and R&B into something smoky and minimal. Its mood is soulful, hypnotic and intimate, the sound of late-night confessions over a groove that barely moves but never lets go.

## Song Summary

"Gold" is a slow-burning song about desire and devotion, built around a simple beat, finger snaps and a deep, steady bassline. Chet Faker's weathered, soulful voice carries everything, swinging between restraint and passion, while layers of keys and subtle electronic textures add warmth. The production is spacious and minimal, and that restraint is what makes the song feel so sensual and close. There is very little on the track that is not essential, and that economy means every small change, a new synth line, a doubled vocal, a little extra reverb, lands with real weight. It is soul music stripped to its bones and rebuilt with electronic tools.

## Musical Breakdown

The track builds slowly and subtly, adding warmth rather than volume as it goes.

### Intro

Finger snaps and a soft beat set a slow, swaying groove, with a deep bass beginning to pulse.

### Verse 1

His voice enters low and warm, almost whispered, carrying the melody over the minimal backing.

### Chorus

The hook opens up with a fuller vocal and richer keys, the groove locking in with hypnotic insistence.

### Bridge

A quieter moment strips the track down to beat and voice before the layers return.

### Outro

The groove rides out with his ad-libs and fading keys, ending as softly as it began.

## Artistic Context

Chet Faker is the alias of Australian musician Nick Murphy, who first broke through with a slow, soulful cover of Blackstreet's "No Diggity". "Gold" appears on *Built on Glass*, his 2014 debut album released on Future Classic, which mixed electronic production with soul and blues. Its video, directed by Hiro Murai, follows roller skaters gliding down a dark highway at night. The album's title nods to fragility, and much of the record lives in that tension between smooth surfaces and something more vulnerable underneath; "Gold" is where it sounds most seductive.

## Impact and Reception

*Built on Glass* was a major success in Australia, and "Gold" became one of its signature songs, with the video widely shared and admired for its simple, striking imagery. Murai's single-take feel and the hypnotic choreography of the skaters mirror the song perfectly: steady, graceful, slightly unreal. It is on this playground for its warmth and restraint. Listen late, with the lights low.
