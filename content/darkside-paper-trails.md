## Genre and Mood

**DARKSIDE**'s **Paper Trails** is electronic music that wants to be the blues, an experiment that never hurries. The Line of Best Fit heard a slow-stepping, sensual, sinuous beat with light, bluesy instrumentation, and lyrics that are dark and noirish, even a little macabre. The feelings follow: dark, sultry, and in the end cathartic.

## Song Summary

Paper Trails is the fourth track of Psychic, the 2013 debut album by Nicolás Jaar and Dave Harrington, written and produced by the two of them. Jaar sings and programs; Harrington plays guitar and bass. The detail that makes the track is the voice: The Edge, reviewing the single, described a contemplative croon, introspective and lugubrious, and pointed to a simple but fascinating trick, the main vocal layered with a downshifted baritone. The second detail is the shape: halfway through, the song opens up.

## Musical Breakdown

No source gives timings, so the parts below follow what critics heard in order.

### Intro

The track moves at a slow step. The Line of Best Fit heard a sinuous beat under light, bluesy instrumentation and reached for Chris Isaak meeting Matthew Dear.

### Main Section

Jaar's doubled voice sits low over Harrington's playing. The Edge heard production that seems ingenuous at first and proves meticulous on repeated listening, and a track that builds with a swagger.

### Second Half

Like Golden Arrow before it, the song opens up into dense, intricate, melancholic, misty-cool rock'n'roll, in The Line of Best Fit's words, and The Edge heard it culminate in an achingly beautiful catharsis.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="blues" /> }} is here as a sound rather than a form. Resident Advisor heard Psychic interwoven with bluesy six-string riffing that recalls Dire Straits and Pink Floyd, delivered without irony, and asked what Jaar would sound like with Eric Clapton.

{{ <concept_link name="pitch shifting" /> }} lowers a copy of the voice and lays it under the original. Here it gives Jaar a darker twin, a sultry quality, in The Edge's word, that no natural singer could produce alone.

The second half is a {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }} without a dance-music drop: tension accumulates through swagger and density until it breaks into what The Edge called catharsis.

## Artistic Context

Jaar and Harrington met through Jaar's live band; they began recording together on his 2011 tour for Space Is Only Noise, and Jaar called Darkside the closest thing to rock and roll he had done. Psychic, recorded over two years in New York, upstate New York and Paris, was released in October 2013 on Matador and Jaar's label Other People. The same year, as Daftside, they remixed Random Access Memories, including {{ <track_link source="daft-punk-give-life-back-to-music.md" /> }}. Compare Jaar's solo croon in {{ <track_link source="nicolas-jaar-time-for-us.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Psychic was acclaimed: Pitchfork gave it 9.0 and ranked it 11th among the albums of 2013, and it reached number six on Billboard's Top Dance Albums. Paper Trails, its second single, earned 9 out of 10 from The Edge. Give it the full 4:50 and wait for the turn.
