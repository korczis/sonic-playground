## Genre and Mood

**Ben Khan**'s **Savage** is a short, warped blend of R&B, electronic production and funk. The mood is hypnotic and gritty, with a seductive, slightly sinister cool: two and a half minutes that feel like a hot room at the end of a long night.

## Song Summary

Savage is all texture and attitude. A hip-hop-weighted beat lurches underneath wobbling, detuned synths, guitar stabs cut through the haze and something like brass sounds as if it were trapped inside a bubble. Over it, Khan's sultry, cynical vocal slides between a murmur and a falsetto, never quite settling. The track is tiny but dense, and it ends long before you are ready. Every element seems slightly bent out of shape, and that wobble is exactly what gives it its swagger and its strange, late-night glamour.

## Musical Breakdown

The song is compact and restless, packing its ideas into a structure that wastes nothing.

### Intro

A woozy synth figure and a heavy, swung beat start at once, the sound already bent and smeared, as if played from a warped tape.

### Verse 1

Khan's voice arrives low and close, half sung and half sighed, sliding over the beat with deliberate ease.

### Chorus

The hook lifts into his falsetto, with sharp guitar stabs and the muffled brass-like sounds pushing it forward.

### Bridge

The production twists for a moment, synths wobbling and textures shifting, a brief disorientation before the groove snaps back.

### Outro

The track wraps up quickly, leaving the warped funk hanging in the air and the urge to play it again.

## Artistic Context

Ben Khan is a London singer, songwriter and producer who mixes funk, R&B and electronic production into a sound that borrows from the past without ever sounding like a throwback. His early records fold vintage funk guitar and modern, glitchy electronics into the same frame. Savage comes from his debut 1992 EP, released in May 2014 on Dirty Hit, and was his third single. He followed it with the 1000 EP and later a self-titled album, developing the same hazy, synth-heavy style.

## Impact and Reception

Savage was one of the tracks that made blogs and critics take notice of Khan, praised for its warped, synth-heavy production and for how much character it packs into such a short running time. It belongs on this playground as a perfect example of mid-2010s bedroom funk at its most distinctive. Play it twice in a row; once is never quite enough.
