## Genre and Mood

**Ben Khan**'s **Savage** is a short, warped blend of R&B, electronic production and funk. The Globe and Mail called it a moody, wobbly track, and the mood is hypnotic and gritty, with a seductive, slightly sinister cool: two and a half minutes that feel like a hot room at the end of a long night.

## Song Summary

Savage is all texture and attitude. Threatening beats lurch underneath wobbling, detuned synths, sharp guitar stabs cut through the haze and horn-like sounds blurt as if trapped inside a bubble. Over it, Khan's sultry, cynical vocal sits high and never quite settles. The lyric is vague and intriguing rather than a story. The Line of Best Fit praised its warped, synth-heavy production and suspected Khan had slowed and pitched down the guitar melody of his earlier song Eden to build it. Every element seems slightly bent out of shape, and that wobble is exactly what gives it its swagger.

## Musical Breakdown

The song is compact and restless, packing its ideas into a structure that wastes nothing.

### Intro

A woozy synth figure and a heavy beat start at once, the sound already bent and smeared, as if played from a warped tape.

### Verse 1

Khan's voice arrives close and cool, half sung and half sighed, sliding over the beat with deliberate ease.

### Chorus

The hook lifts higher, with sharp guitar stabs and the muffled, horn-like sounds pushing it forward.

### Bridge

The production twists for a moment, synths wobbling and textures shifting, a brief disorientation before the groove snaps back.

### Outro

The track wraps up quickly, leaving the warped funk hanging in the air and the urge to play it again.

## Concepts in Focus

Savage shows a {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} used against its own perfection. Synths can hold a pitch exactly, but here the sounds wobble and drift as if the tape were stretching. That instability is the song's hook: the detuning makes something modern sound old, worn and a little menacing.

The voice uses a high, light register close to {{ <concept_link name="falsetto" /> }}, but not for the euphoria of disco. Khan sings up there coolly, almost cynically, and the thinness of the voice makes him sound detached from the threatening music around him. Listen to how the high voice and the low, heavy beat pull in opposite directions.

What holds the track together is the {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }}. There are hardly any sections to speak of; instead, guitar stabs, horn-like blurts and synth smears land in and around a heavy, lurching beat, so the rhythm keeps moving even when the harmony does not.

## Artistic Context

Ben Khan is a London singer, songwriter and producer who has cited J Dilla and B.B. King as influences, and who mixes funk, R&B and electronic production into a sound that borrows from the past without sounding like a throwback. Savage first appeared in December 2013 as his third single and then on his debut 1992 EP, released in May 2014 on his own Blessed Vice imprint via Dirty Hit. The writing credit is his alone. He followed it with the 1000 EP and later a self-titled album on Dirty Hit.

## Impact and Reception

Savage was one of the tracks that made blogs and critics take notice: The Line of Best Fit called it searing and glorious, and The Globe and Mail heard a debut full of dizzying grooves and curious funk, finding the song a bit unsettling in the best way. It belongs on this playground as an example of mid-2010s bedroom funk at its most distinctive. Play it twice in a row; once is never quite enough.
