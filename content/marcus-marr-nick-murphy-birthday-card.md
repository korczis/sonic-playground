## Genre and Mood

**Marcus Marr** and **Nick Murphy**, then still recording as Chet Faker, stretch **Birthday Card** out to nearly eight minutes of sleek, disco-tinged house. It is electronic music made for the long haul of a dancefloor, groovy and playful in its details, euphoric in its release.

## Song Summary

The song pairs two very different strengths: Marr's DJ-minded production and Murphy's smoky, soulful voice. The groove feels both retro and modern, a nod to disco and classic house, while Murphy's vocal adds warmth and a hint of melancholy. The Fader heard it unfurl methodically over nearly eight minutes with the build and pacing of a dancefloor cut. The length is part of the pleasure; the track keeps finding new angles on the same groove instead of rushing to a chorus.

## Musical Breakdown

As the opener of a club-minded EP, the track takes its time, unfolding in long movements rather than tight verses.

### Intro

A crisp beat and a snaking bassline start things off, with small percussive touches inviting a DJ to blend in.

### Build-up

Synth figures arrive layer by layer, and the groove gradually brightens and widens.

### Verse 1

Murphy's voice drifts in, low and relaxed, riding the groove rather than leading it, his husky tone a warm contrast to the shiny production.

### Main Section

The full arrangement locks together, bass, synths and voice bouncing off each other in a long stretch of groove.

### Bridge

The arrangement thins for a while, a moment of space and tension before everything returns.

### Climax

The groove returns with full force, the synths glittering at their brightest, the peak of the dancefloor journey.

### Outro

The layers ease away, leaving the beat to carry the track out.

## Concepts in Focus

Birthday Card is a study in the {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }} at full length. A pop song reaches its chorus in under a minute; this track spends its first minutes adding one element at a time, so that the arrival of the full groove feels earned. The pacing is the composition, which is why the track runs 7:46 and why cutting it short would remove its point.

Holding that length together is the {{ <concept_link name="house groove" /> }}: a steady pulse with bass and synths that repeat and shift slightly over time. The voice is one more layer in it rather than the leader. Compare Murphy's role in {{ <track_link source="chet-faker-talk-is-cheap.md" /> }}, where the arrangement exists to frame his voice.

## Artistic Context

"Birthday Card" opens Work, a four-track collaborative EP by the English DJ and producer Marcus Marr and the Australian singer-producer Nick Murphy, released on 4 December 2015 on Murphy's label Detail Records. The two began collaborating on Twitter, and the EP was recorded over four days at Marr's studio in South London; every track is credited to both of them. It came the year after Murphy's debut album, Built on Glass, and showed a looser, more dance-oriented side of him.

## Impact and Reception

The Fader premiered "Birthday Card" on 30 November 2015, a few days before the EP, after "The Trouble With Us" had been released as its first single in October. Work debuted at number 42 on the ARIA singles chart in Australia, and Music Feeds described it as a meeting of two usually distant sonic worlds, electro beats and soulful indie-pop vocals. It is here because it is pure, unhurried pleasure. Give it all eight minutes; it earns every one.
