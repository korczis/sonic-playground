## Genre and Mood

**Marcus Marr** and **Nick Murphy**, then still recording as Chet Faker, stretch **Birthday Card** out to nearly eight minutes of sleek, disco-tinged house. It is electronic music made for the long haul of a dancefloor, groovy and playful in its details, euphoric in its release.

## Song Summary

The song pairs two very different strengths: Marr's springy, DJ-minded production and Murphy's smoky, soulful voice. A bouncing, elastic bassline and bright synth figures build a groove that feels both retro and modern, a nod to Euro-house and disco, while Murphy's vocal adds warmth and a hint of melancholy. The length is part of the pleasure; the track keeps finding new angles on the same groove instead of rushing to a chorus.

## Musical Breakdown

As the opener of a club-minded EP, the track takes its time, unfolding in long movements rather than tight verses.

### Intro

A crisp beat and a snaking bassline start things off, with small percussive touches inviting a DJ to blend in.

### Build-up

Synth arpeggios and chord stabs arrive layer by layer, and the groove gradually brightens and widens.

### Verse 1

Murphy's voice drifts in, low and relaxed, riding the groove rather than leading it, his husky tone a warm contrast to the shiny production.

### Main Section

The full arrangement locks together, bass, synths and voice bouncing off each other in a long, irresistible stretch of groove.

### Bridge

A breakdown thins everything to a few elements, a moment of space and tension before the drop back in.

### Climax

The groove returns with full force, the synths glittering and the bassline bouncing at its most joyous, the peak of the dancefloor journey.

### Outro

The layers ease away, leaving the beat to carry the track out.

## Artistic Context

"Birthday Card" opens Work, a four-track collaborative EP by British producer Marcus Marr and Australian singer-producer Nick Murphy, released in December 2015 on Murphy's Detail Records together with Downtown Records. The two met online, and the EP was recorded in just four days at Marr's studio. It came the year after Murphy's acclaimed debut album, Built on Glass, and showed a looser, more dance-oriented side of him.

## Impact and Reception

The Fader premiered "Birthday Card" in late November 2015 ahead of the EP, and blogs welcomed it as a Euro-house opener with an irresistible build. The EP entered the Australian albums chart at number 42. It is here because it is pure, unhurried pleasure. Give it all eight minutes; it earns every one.
