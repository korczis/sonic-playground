## Genre and Mood

**Pharrell Williams** wrote **Happy** to be exactly what its title says, and it works. It is pop with a soul heart and a funk spine, a retro, Motown-flavoured groove dressed in modern production. The feelings are simple and total: joyful, uplifting, feel-good from the first handclap.

## Song Summary

Written for the soundtrack of the animated film Despicable Me 2, "Happy" is a song about a mood so strong nothing can dent it. The arrangement is deliberately light: a bouncing bassline, crisp handclaps, a few keyboard chords and Pharrell's airy falsetto, with a choir of backing voices answering him like a gospel congregation. Its genius is economy; there is nothing in it that does not make you want to move.

## Musical Breakdown

The song runs on a loop of call and response, building warmth rather than volume.

### Intro

A walking bassline and claps set a mid-tempo strut before a word is sung, and the groove is already infectious.

### Verse 1

Pharrell floats over the beat with easy, playful phrasing, describing a feeling too good to be brought down. Backing voices hum and coo around him.

### Chorus

"Because I'm happy" lands with a wall of harmonised voices and the instruction to clap along. It is impossible to resist, which is the whole point.

### Bridge

The band drops away and the song becomes almost a cappella, voices and claps trading the line about not being brought down, a gospel-style breakdown in miniature.

### Climax

The full groove returns and the final choruses stack the backing vocals higher, the room-without-a-roof feeling at its peak.

### Outro

The hook repeats until it fades, leaving the bassline bouncing in your head long after.

## Artistic Context

By 2013 Pharrell was already one of pop's most sought-after producers, half of The Neptunes and fresh from his parts in Daft Punk's "Get Lucky" and Robin Thicke's "Blurred Lines". "Happy" first appeared on the Despicable Me 2 soundtrack in 2013 and was then included on his second solo album, G I R L, in 2014. Its video was presented as the world's first 24-hour music video, with hundreds of people dancing through Los Angeles.

## Impact and Reception

"Happy" became the biggest song of 2014: it topped the US Billboard Hot 100 for ten weeks and reached number one in the UK and many other countries. It was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Original Song, won Grammys for Best Pop Solo Performance and Best Music Video, and inspired fan-made dance videos from cities all over the world. It is here because sometimes a song should simply make you smile. Press play, and clap along.
