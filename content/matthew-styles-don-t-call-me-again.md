## Genre and Mood

**Matthew Styles** opened his Sample & Hold EP with **Don't Call Me Again**, and his label, Running Back, introduced it in one line: straight up house that works itself up in a frenzy. That note is the only published account of how the track sounds. Discogs files the record under electronic music and house, and the two feelings here, intense and energetic, come from that word frenzy rather than from a review. Hold them loosely and let the track argue for itself.

## Song Summary

Don't Call Me Again runs 7:31, a length made for DJs, who need room to mix a record in and out. It is the first track on side A of a five-track 12-inch released by Running Back on 16 September 2011 under the catalogue number RB028, followed by Scale and a locked groove called Sample & Hold, with Polee and Galaxy 21 on side B. Styles is credited as writer and producer. The title suggests a voice or a story, but no lyrics are published and no source describes one, so this page leaves that open.

## Concepts in Focus

That phrase describes a {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }} on the scale of a whole track. In much club music the build-up is a short passage before a drop; in a long house record it can be the shape of the entire piece, with elements added and intensity raised over minutes so that dancers feel the climb without noticing each step.

The record's credits point to {{ <concept_link name="mastering" /> }}. Andreas Lubich mastered the EP and cut the lacquer at Dubplates & Mastering in Berlin. On vinyl the cut decides how loud and how deep a record can play, and the side A lock groove is a cutting trick of its own: a ring that repeats until the needle is lifted. Lubich also mastered the collection that carries {{ <track_link source="extrawelt-fernweh.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Running Back introduced Styles as an Englishman firmly schooled in the history of dance music as a DJ, and called this his first solo release in a long while. Discogs lists earlier records on BPitch Control and Horizontal and later ones on Stock5, No Fit State and Crosstown Rebels, and gives his full name as Matthew Styles-Harris, now a mixing and mastering engineer at Horizontal Studios. Running Back, Discogs notes, was founded by Gerd Janson and Thorsten Scheu and is run by Janson.
