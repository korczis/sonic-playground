## Genre and Mood

**Fischerspooner** open their second album with Just Let Go and follow it with **Cloud**, a synth-pop song that Treble filed with the album's electro tracks. It is energetic and theatrical, but the theatre here has a literary source: the Romantic artist, swallowed by his own creation.

## Song Summary

In the album's track-by-track press notes, Casey Spooner explained that the Romantics became a huge influence on Odyssey because he saw them as the roots of rock and roll, and that Cloud is the tale of a mad, obsessive genius caught up in his own creation. Metro Weekly quoted the same notes in shorter form: the Romantic hero led directly to the modern rock star. The song began as an idea by the New York artist Jon Wolfington, was written by Warren Fischer, Spooner, Wolfington and Eric Erlandson, the guitarist of Hole, and was co-produced by Mirwais. Its shape, the notes say, owes much to Fischer's love of the Cure's chorus and post-chorus structures.

## Concepts in Focus

Cloud is a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="verse-chorus form" /> }} and one of its extensions. Verses tell, choruses repeat; a post-chorus is a further section after the chorus that keeps the energy up, often with its own melody. The press notes credit Fischer's love of the Cure's songs built that way.

That would make Cloud a song of more than one {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }}. Scene Point Blank singled out its vocal melodies, and the sudden leap of Spooner's voice is the kind of detail a listener carries away.

The {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} matters too. Mirwais, the French producer heard on {{ <track_link source="mirwais-naiive-song.md" /> }}, co-produced the track and, by the album's notes, helped with Odyssey's final touches.

## Artistic Context

Fischerspooner, Warren Fischer and Casey Spooner, formed in New York in 1998 as an electroclash duo and performance troupe. Their second album, Odyssey, took over two years: Fischer worked in Brooklyn with engineer Nicolas Vernhes and session musicians and in Los Angeles with Tony Hoffer; guests included David Byrne, Linda Perry and Susan Sontag. EMI and Capitol released it in April 2005. Wikipedia describes the move as one from electroclash towards synth-pop; PopMatters heard the duo leaving the cold digital sound of their debut for the pop tradition of Erasure and the Pet Shop Boys.

## Impact and Reception

Cloud was not a single. Odyssey earned a Metacritic score of 70, and Treble called it simply a great pop record. Scene Point Blank found Cloud familiar territory and still smiled at Spooner's octave leaps. Listen for the moment after the chorus.
