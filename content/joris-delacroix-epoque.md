## Genre and Mood

**Joris Delacroix** blends deep house and melodic techno in **Epoque**, an atmospheric, emotive instrumental. Its reflective melodies and driving rhythm evoke a sense of nostalgia, the title itself French for an era, so it works both on a dance floor and on headphones.

## Song Summary

Epoque shows Delacroix's way of making electronic tracks that feel melodic first. Soft, harmonised synth lines sit over a solid, pulsing beat, and the balance between the two makes the track introspective and energetic at once. At almost seven minutes, it takes its time, letting each layer settle before the next arrives.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro: Gentle Melodies

The track begins with soft, atmospheric melodies that immediately set a nostalgic mood and establish the reflective tone.

### Build-up: Rhythmic Energy

A driving house beat emerges, adding an energetic pulse to the introspective melody and giving the track its forward momentum without overpowering it.

### Main Section: Melodic Bliss

The central part is where Delacroix's melodic writing comes forward. Layers of harmonised synths and subtle percussion blend into a wide, immersive sound.

### Climax: Emotional Peak

The track builds toward its peak, all elements together, the deep house groove intensifying as the melodic lines swirl.

### Outro: Gentle Fade

The elements fade away gradually, leaving a reflective mood that lingers after the music stops.

## Concepts in Focus

Like most melodic deep house, Epoque stands on a {{ <concept_link name="four-on-the-floor" /> }} kick: one beat on every quarter note, never changing. That steadiness is what lets the rest of the track be emotional. The body always knows where the next beat lands, so the ear can follow the melodies drifting above it.

Those melodies are built with the {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }}. Delacroix, who studied piano from the age of four, writes clear, singable lines and then gives them soft attacks, long releases and layered harmonies so they blur into atmosphere. In an instrumental track the timbre of the synth does the job a singer's voice would do elsewhere.

The form is one long {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }} and release. Instead of a short tension-and-drop, Epoque adds layers over minutes, raising intensity until its climax and then letting it ebb. Compare the more song-like melodic house of {{ <track_link source="the-avener-ane-brun-to-let-myself-go.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Joris Delacroix, born in Montpellier in 1987, took piano lessons from the age of four and discovered electronic music at sixteen. He released his first EP on Timid Records in 2008, then signed with the French label Way of House (WOH), which released his first album, Room With View, recorded at home, in 2011. After Going Back (2016) he moved to Universal Music: Epoque came out as a single in 2018 and closes his third album, Night Visions, released on 22 June 2018 by Mercury Music Group.

## Impact and Reception

Released both as a single and as the album's closing track, Epoque is a melodic deep house piece made as much for listening as for clubs; the version embedded here is the original mix. Its balance of reflective melody and steady energy is what makes it work on the dance floor and in quieter moments alike.
