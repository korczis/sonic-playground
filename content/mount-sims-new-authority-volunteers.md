## Genre and Mood

**Mount Sims** spent the early 2000s as one of electroclash's sleekest provocateurs, but **New Authority Volunteers** belongs to his darker, guitar-driven turn. It sits in post-punk and darkwave territory, alternative in spirit, and the mood is dark, rebellious and gritty, a short burst of unease rather than a dancefloor tease.

## Song Summary

The title alone reads like a poster for some sinister militia, and the song leans into that atmosphere of control and resistance. Instead of the glossy synths of his early records, the sound is built from tense guitars, insistent rhythm and a vocal that sits somewhere between croon and command. It is compact, just over three minutes, and wastes none of them. The memorable quality is its tension: it never fully releases.

## Musical Breakdown

The track is direct and economical, in the tradition of the post-punk singles it draws from.

### Intro

Guitars and drums start up with a cold, driving momentum, setting a mood that is closer to a rehearsal room than a club.

### Verse 1

The vocal enters with a detached, slightly menacing calm, delivering lines over a beat that never loosens its grip.

### Chorus

The hook tightens the screws rather than releasing them, the title chanted like a slogan.

### Outro

The song cuts off with the same abruptness it began with, leaving the unease hanging in the air.

## Concepts in Focus

New Authority Volunteers is late-period {{ <concept_link name="post-punk" /> }}, the tradition that followed punk with colder, more experimental music: prominent rhythm, tense guitars, detached vocals and dark themes. Mount Sims arrived there gradually; his 2005 album Wild Light already drew on dark wave and post-punk, with David J of Bauhaus playing bass on some songs, and the records that followed went further.

What makes the song interesting is what it is not. Mount Sims made his name in {{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }}, the scene that revived 1980s electro with a cool, ironic edge, and his debut UltraSex on International DeeJay Gigolo was one of its records. Here the cool voice is the same, but the gloss is gone; listen to how a delivery that sounded playful over synths sounds threatening over guitars.

## Artistic Context

Mount Sims is the project of Matthew Sims, an American musician from Milwaukee who first sang in the alternative rock band Citizen King, whose 1999 hit "Better Days (And the Bottom Drops Out)" reached number 25 in the US, then moved to Los Angeles and later Berlin. Sims handles almost every aspect of his music himself, vocals, instruments and production. His third album, Happily Ever After, released on Hungry Eye Records, was produced by Thomas Stern of Crime & the City Solution and featured Jessie Evans, Toby Dammit of Swans and Bryan Black of Motor, with themes of obsession, isolation, horror and madness. "New Authority Volunteers" comes from Happily Ever After... Again, its 2010 companion release.

## Impact and Reception

That darker phase was a world away from the club hits of his electroclash years, and in 2010 he also worked with The Knife and Planningtorock on the electronic opera Tomorrow, In a Year. "New Authority Volunteers" is on this playground because it shows the other side of a familiar name: the same cool detachment, now aimed at something more ominous. Put it next to the electroclash of UltraSex and you can hear an artist deliberately trading polish for grit. Play it loud on a grey afternoon and let it rattle you.
