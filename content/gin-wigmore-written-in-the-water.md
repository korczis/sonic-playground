## Genre and Mood

**Gin Wigmore** comes out swinging on **Written In The Water**, a track rooted in blues, rock and soul. Its mood is defiant, gritty and bold: a short, stomping song that sounds like it was made to be shouted along to with a drink raised high. It is the sound of a singer who would rather kick the door in than knock.

## Song Summary

The song is about fate and choice, about refusing to accept what seems already decided. Wigmore's raspy, powerful voice is the centrepiece, snarling and pleading by turns, while the arrangement keeps things raw and rhythmic, with stomping drums, handclaps and swampy guitar. At under three minutes, it does not waste a second, and its punchy, almost primal energy is what makes it stick. The production leaves rough edges in, a choice that suits her voice: it sounds best when there is something to push against. Lyrically it plays with the image of a destiny that is already written and the stubborn urge to rewrite it anyway.

## Musical Breakdown

Compact and direct, the track builds from a stomp to a full-blown roar in very little time.

### Intro

A heavy stomp and claps set a primal rhythm, with a bluesy guitar figure hinting at the attitude to come.

### Verse 1

Wigmore's voice enters rough and confident, her rasp cutting through the sparse arrangement.

### Chorus

The hook lands with gang-style backing vocals and a fuller band, her voice rising to a defiant shout.

### Bridge

A short stripped-back moment lets the rhythm and voice breathe before the final push.

### Outro

The last chorus hits hard and ends abruptly, leaving the stomp echoing.

## Artistic Context

Gin Wigmore is a singer-songwriter from New Zealand known for her distinctive husky voice and a sound that blends blues, soul, rock and pop. "Written In The Water" comes from *Blood To Bone*, her 2015 album, a record that pushed her sound toward darker, more rhythmic and more defiant territory. Her earlier records had leaned on a vintage, folk-tinged pop sound, and here the rhythms are heavier and the attitude sharper, a reinvention that suited the bigger, louder songs.

## Impact and Reception

The song became one of the standout tracks of the album, performed at New Zealand's music awards and in sessions for radio, and admired for its raw energy and Wigmore's voice. It also showed how her rasp, already her trademark, could carry a track built almost entirely on rhythm. It is on this playground for that grit and swagger. Turn it up, stamp along and feel a little braver.
