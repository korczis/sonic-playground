## Genre and Mood

**Felix Da Housecat** made **Silver Screen (Shower Scene)** at the meeting point of house and the cold electronics of early-1980s synth-pop, the sound soon called electroclash. It is sensual and mysterious rather than euphoric, energetic in its beat but cool in its voice, like a film star who will not quite look at the camera.

## Song Summary

The track's melody is sampled from "Passion" by The Flirts, an early-1980s dance record, in its Ben Liebrand remix. Over it the French artist Miss Kittin delivers a lyric written by Tommie Sunshine, half sung and half spoken, its imagery drawn from the film world the title names. Felix produced it with his collaborators Dave the Hustler, who arranged and mixed it and played the strings in the breakdown, and Tommie Sunshine; the three share the writing credit. The memorable detail is the distance in Kittin's voice: she sounds like she is narrating a scene she is also starring in.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro: Setting the Atmosphere

The borrowed synth melody opens the track, glossy and slightly sinister, and the electro beat falls in underneath.

### Verse 1: A Sensual Invitation

Miss Kittin enters in her deadpan, accented delivery. Her voice is not a sample but a performance made for the track, and it carries the narrative.

### Chorus: The Dancefloor Anthem

The hook is the melody itself, repeated until it sticks, with the beat driving underneath.

### Bridge: Cinematic Interlude

The sleeve credits a spoken "talk break" to Kittin and Felix and a strings breakdown to Dave the Hustler: the moment where the track turns into a little scene from the film its title promises.

### Verse 2 and Outro: Climax and Resolution

The melody and the voice return, and the track runs out on its groove rather than on a big finish.

## Concepts in Focus

Silver Screen is built on {{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }}. The melody that everyone remembers comes from The Flirts' "Passion", in the version remixed by the Dutch producer Ben Liebrand. Felix's album mixed early-1980s electronic music, in the spirit of ZE Records artists like Cristina, with house, and this sample is that idea in one gesture: an old synth-pop line placed inside a modern club frame.

The track also had a long life in {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }}. The original 12-inch on City Rockers in 2001 carried remixes by FC Kahuna and Laurent Garnier; later versions came from Adult. and Thin White Duke, whose mix Resident Advisor placed among the top 100 tracks of the 2000s. In 2022 David Guetta reworked it with Felix and Kittin, twenty-one years after the original.

And it travelled as a {{ <concept_link name="soundtrack" /> }}. It plays in the 2005 film Kiss Kiss Bang Bang and appears in the video games Midnight Club II, SSX 3 and The Getaway: Black Monday. For many listeners of that generation, a racing or snowboarding game was where they first heard it.

Above all, Silver Screen is a founding record of {{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }}: Miss Kittin's cool, accented narration, the early-1980s synth melody and the fascination with glamour and fame are the scene's whole vocabulary in four and a half minutes.

## Artistic Context

Felix Da Housecat, the Chicago producer Felix Stallings Jr., was a second-generation Chicago house artist by the time he made Kittenz and Thee Glitz. Silver Screen was the album's first single, released on the UK label City Rockers in 2001, and the album followed in the UK that year and in the US on Emperor Norton in 2002. It was made with Tommie Sunshine, Miss Kittin, Dave the Hustler and others, and it became one of the defining records of electroclash.

## Impact and Reception

The single reached number 39 on the UK Singles Chart and number 2 on the UK Dance chart. Miss Kittin put the Laurent Garnier remix on her own DJ mix album On the Road, and Afrika Bambaataa and the Soulsonic Force covered the song at the Big Day Out in 2004. Kittenz and Thee Glitz scored 78 on Metacritic and was placed at number 73 in Resident Advisor's top 100 albums of the 2000s.
