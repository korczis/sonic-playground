## Genre and Mood

**Amanda Blank**'s **Shame on Me** reaches this page in the hands of Toadally Krossed Out, and without Blank's voice: it is the instrumental of their remix, released on a dance EP. The words catchy and playful belong first to the song itself, which Exclaim! called a hooky, catchy chunk of electro ear candy, and to remixers The FADER described as ribbiting their way through warp-speed dance music. No published review describes this instrumental itself.

## Song Summary

Shame on Me is track six of Blank's debut album I Love You, released by Downtown Records on 4 August 2009. Its writers are Blank, Alex Epton (who produces as XXXChange) and Martin "Doc" McKinney, and MusicBrainz adds David Taylor, known as Switch; XXXChange and Switch produced it. On 7 December 2009 Downtown issued Shame on Me (Remixes), four tracks: Jacknife Lee's radio edit, Toadally Krossed Out's remix, a remix by Viking, and Shame On Me (Toadally Krossed Out Instrumental), which runs just over four minutes.

Play them one after the other and listen for what the beat has to carry once the voice is gone.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} is a new version of a recording made by reworking its parts, and a remix EP like this one gives a single several lives at once: a shorter edit for radio from Jacknife Lee, who had already produced on the album, and two longer club versions by outside producers.

An {{ <concept_link name="instrumental" /> }} takes the lead vocal away. Labels put instrumentals on singles and remix EPs for DJs, who can mix them under other records, and for rappers and producers who want the beat. Here it also removes the thing critics praised most in the original: Exclaim! heard Shame on Me as Blank straying from her dance and rap roots to test her skills as a vocalist.

## Artistic Context

Blank came up in Philadelphia with Spank Rock, Diplo and Santigold, and I Love You was produced by Diplo, Switch, XXXChange, Dave Sitek and Jacknife Lee. Hear the album's club side in {{ <track_link source="amanda-blank_dj.md" /> }} and its slower side in {{ <track_link source="amanda-blank-a-love-song.md" /> }}. Toadally Krossed Out appeared in The FADER in 2009 as a couple of superstar DJs dressed incognito; the magazine heard their Toads Theme as something like the competition tracks at vogue balls, and posted their remixes of Buraka Som Sistema and, in 2010, of Tiësto vs. Diplo.

## Impact and Reception

The original drew some of the warmest words on its album. PopMatters counted it among songs better than anyone could reasonably expect on a rap album, mxdwn heard it borrowing from Yaz, and it reached number 28 on Billboard's Dance/Club Play Songs chart. No source records how the remixes were received. Start with the album version, then put on the instrumental and hear the song without its singer.
