## Genre and Mood

**Doris Monteiro** sings **É Isso Ai** the way she sang almost everything: without strain. The song belongs to Brazilian pop at the turn of the 1970s, carries the bossa nova phrasing she had from the start, and later found listeners as samba rock. Its mood is playful and relaxed, with a singer who keeps something back.

## Song Summary

É Isso Ai, Portuguese for something like "that's it", was written by the Rio composer Sidney Miller and recorded for Monteiro's 1971 album Doris on Odeon, produced by Milton Miranda. On the same record she sang songs by Marcos Valle, Caetano Veloso, Noel Rosa, and Toquinho and Vinícius de Moraes. Slipcue, writing about the album as a whole, heard Odeon's horns-and-strings pop style made playful and up to date by inventive, tropicália-influenced arrangements, and a Monteiro who sounds a lot like Gal Costa at the time without the heavy-handed emoting. No source describes this song's lyric or structure, so this page stays with the voice and the record around it.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} at a major label in 1971 shaped a sound as much as a single song. YouTube's release credits Milton Miranda as producer; Slipcue credits the album to Miranda with Lindolfo Gaya, and hears it in a label style, horns and strings in the Odeon manner.

A {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }} can give a song a new name. In 2007 É Isso Ai was gathered on Samba Rock Vol.1, the compilation that also revived {{ <track_link source="cleo-galante-voce-vai-ver-que-eu-tenho-razao.md" /> }}. In 2004, for her seventieth year, Universal and EMI reissued twelve of her albums on CD.

## Artistic Context

Born in Rio de Janeiro in 1934, Monteiro began on radio in 1949, was elected Rainha do Rádio in 1956 and acted in eight films in the 1950s. Known as the Diva do Sambalanço, she counts among the notable singers of the passage from samba-canção to bossa nova; the Dicionário Cravo Albin places her phrasing with Johnny Alf, Dick Farney and Lúcio Alves. Sidney Miller (1945-1980) was compared early on with Chico Buarque for his careful, urban lyrics.

## Impact and Reception

Slipcue says the 1971 album catches Monteiro at her best. She died in Rio on 24 July 2023, aged 88. Listen as Slipcue advises, and meet her halfway.
