## Genre and Mood

**The Chemical Brothers**' **"Hey Boy Hey Girl"** is electronic dance music from the duo NME credited with big beat, made at the moment they turned towards rave. It is energetic from its first bars and built for one thing, a euphoric peak on a crowded floor.

## Song Summary

"Hey Boy Hey Girl" was the first single from Surrender, released on 26 May 1999 in Japan and on 31 May in the UK. Tom Rowlands and Ed Simons recorded it at Orinoco Studios in South London, with Steve Dub engineering. It samples "The Roof Is on Fire" by Rock Master Scott & the Dynamic Three, whose writers share the credit with the duo. The album version runs 4:50; a radio edit of 3:32 and an extended version of just over six minutes also exist. The player on this page is that album recording, as uploaded from the Brotherhood compilation; the official video uses a shorter edit.

## Musical Breakdown

No source we found gives times or describes the track's later sections and ending; this guide follows what NME heard.

### Intro

NME called it "perhaps the most famous dance intro of recent history": a "menacing, trance laden groove" that sets the tension before any voice arrives.

### The Vocal

Then comes "the first vocoded 'Hey boy'", filtering through the speakers, the moment NME says nobody over fifteen fails to recognise.

### Build-up

From there the track keeps climbing, "building to an absolute dance stomper" in NME's words.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} is written into the credits: five of the seven writers are the authors of "The Roof Is on Fire", the record the duo sampled. A sample can bring a whole song's writers onto a new one.

The {{ <concept_link name="vocoder" /> }} turns the title words into something between a voice and a synthesizer. One machine-coloured phrase is enough to identify the track, which says a lot about how a dance record can work with almost no lyric.

The {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }} is the shape of the whole track: tension first, release later. Compare the way the duo let a bassline carry {{ <track_link source="the-chemical-brothers-q-tip-go.md" /> }} sixteen years on.

## Artistic Context

NME's review of Surrender tells of an epiphany at Gatecrasher in Sheffield, where the duo watched trance work on a crowd; the album that followed "went raving", with guests such as Noel Gallagher, Bernard Sumner and Hope Sandoval. Surrender came out in June 1999 and became their second number-one album. The video, by Dom and Nic, follows a girl from a school trip to the Natural History Museum to the Ministry of Sound, where she sees every dancer as a skeleton.

## Impact and Reception

The single reached number 3 in the UK and the top ten in Canada, Ireland, New Zealand, Norway, Spain, Finland and Iceland. NME's 1999 review called it "a great moment in acid-bongo-pop fusion", and in 2011 the magazine ranked it 50th among the 150 best tracks of the previous fifteen years. In 2019 Resident Advisor called it Surrender's biggest single, and Dancing Astronaut placed the original at the duo's pinnacle. Turn it up and wait for the first hey boy.
