## Genre and Mood

**Fischerspooner** called it Fucker; on later pressings of their debut album it became **!@\*$%#**, a row of symbols where a word used to be. It is electroclash with the vocals taken out: electro machines with a dubby treatment. The feelings are hypnotic and playful, a groove that repeats and a title that grins.

## Song Summary

The track was the fourth of nine on the original #1, released on 26 November 2001 in a limited run on International DeeJay Gigolo Records. The album was re-pressed several times with a different running order, and on the reissue the track moved to ninth place under a censored title. It is one of the album's two instrumentals, written by Casey Spooner and Warren Fischer and produced by the duo with Nicolas Vernhes. Two reviews from 2002 heard it differently and both liked it: Alex Wisgard of Drowned in Sound called it the first and best of the instrumentals and said it sounds like Kraftwerk; Gullbuy's reviewer called it a nice, dubby instrumental.

## Musical Breakdown

### The Loop

Gullbuy's description is the closest thing to a map: the music does not really go anywhere, and that is the design. A pattern settles and stays.

### Every Four Bars

What moves is the treatment. Every four bars, the same review heard the music channelled through a different modern-sounding effect, and admitted that this was exactly the kind of thing he liked.

### The Other Instrumental

Its partner on the album is Ersatz, which Gullbuy heard switching patterns and styles almost as often as this track switches effects, a quick tour of electro past and present.

## Concepts in Focus

An {{ <concept_link name="instrumental" /> }} leaves the beat and the arrangement to carry everything. On an album whose singles depend on Casey Spooner's voice and stage persona, this track shows Warren Fischer's machines on their own.

The way it changes recalls {{ <concept_link name="dub" /> }}: the groove holds still and the effects reshape it, the studio itself becoming the instrument. Gullbuy heard the whole album as pure electro, cut sharp with digital editing and soaked in studio effects.

It belongs to {{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }}, the scene that revived early-1980s electro and synth-pop. PopMatters traced Fischer's minimal synthesizers to Kraftwerk, the Human League and New Order's Blue Monday.

## Artistic Context

Fischer, a classically trained musician, and Spooner, a video artist and experimental theatre performer, founded the group in New York in 1998; it grew into a troupe of more than twenty performers. #1 came out on International DeeJay Gigolo, Ministry of Sound and Capitol, carrying {{ <track_link source="fischerspooner-emerge.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

#1 holds 70 on Metacritic, while Q later put it on a list of the worst albums ever. The instrumentals got the least attention and some of the kindest words. Listen to it twice, the second time counting to four.
