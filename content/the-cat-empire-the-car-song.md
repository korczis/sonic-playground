## Genre and Mood

**The Cat Empire**'s **The Car Song** is jazz-trained party music with ska and rock in it, playful, humorous and energetic. PopMatters heard it chugging along with a sort of Latin rock and roll, and called it not the most universally appealing of the band's style experiments, but at least energetic.

## Song Summary

Harry James Angus, the band's trumpeter and one of its two lead singers, wrote and sings this story song. Its narrator is a struggling student whom everyone insists will become a lawyer one day, but who can think only of playing music, playing basketball and asking a childhood crush out, never of the exam. Late in the song comes the twist: an old high-school acquaintance who did become a lawyer is now going out with the same girl. PopMatters called it a catchy tale narrated by a kid struggling in school, and said the band's energy and wit make a hardly original topic seem fresh.

## Musical Breakdown

No source divides the song into sections or gives times, and the reviews describe it only briefly.

### The Band

mxdwn heard an uptempo blend of keys and Angus's trumpet. The recording also credits three guest horn players: Yaure Muniz on trumpet, Jesús "Aguaje" Ramos on trombone and Javier Zalba on baritone saxophone.

### The Story

The same review found the song's near-cliché narrations offered a bit of short-lived character. The story is told in order, from the classroom to the reunion, so the joke lands at the end.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Ben Quinn, turns the story into sport: the band plays a basketball match, the Guilt Resolution Cup, against people from Angus's "sordid past", with Sigmund Freud as coach. Angus misses a critical basket and The Cat Empire lose. It was filmed at Victoria University's Footscray Park campus with fans as extras, and was nominated for Best Video at the 2005 ARIA Music Awards.

A {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} matters even for a band known on stage. Two Shoes was recorded at Egrem Studios in Havana in late 2004, produced by the band, Felix Riebl and the UK-based producer Jerry Boys; MusicBrainz credits Boys with the mix alongside Rafe McKenna.

The Australian CD single carried three {{ <concept_link name="b-side" text="B-sides" /> }}: Riebl's "The Mother Place" and "The Return", and Angus's "Someday (Car Song Blues)".

## Artistic Context

The Cat Empire formed in Melbourne in 1999, and their sound fuses jazz, funk, ska and rock with heavy Latin influences. Two Shoes, released on 19 April 2005, was more Latin in flavour than their debut and had a higher share of songs by Angus.

## Impact and Reception

The Car Song, released in mid-2005, peaked at number 46 in Australia and was voted number 25 in the Triple J Hottest 100 of 2005. Two Shoes became the band's first number-one album and went platinum that year. Listen for the twist at the end.
