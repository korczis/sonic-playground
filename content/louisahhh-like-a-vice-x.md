### About the Artist

> *"A Trap I’ve Built is an intentionally brutal body of work. Wading through murky themes of rape culture, addiction, masochism, and deprivation, whatever isn't explicitly lyricized bleeds through in a sonic ambush. It is my intention that listening to these songs, or DJing them, should be an experience that leaves the audience a little shaken up—something they can feel in their teeth. I hope that *A Trap I’ve Built* is delighted in because (not in spite) of the fact that it is upsetting."*
>
> – Louisahhh

**Louisahhh** (Louisa Pillot), a New York-bred, Paris-based producer, DJ and vocalist, has carved a niche in the underground techno scene with her signature blend of raw intensity and poetic depth. She moved to Los Angeles in 2006, then found her way to Paris through Brodinski's Bromance label, where she became the go-to voice of its records.

Known for her collaborations with techno heavyweights and for **RAAR**, the label she runs with the techno and electro producer Maelstrom, Louisahhh’s music is unapologetically visceral, addressing themes of empowerment, rebellion, and vulnerability. *Like A Vice* exemplifies her ability to merge gritty, industrial production with deeply emotional storytelling.

### About the Song

*Like A Vice* unfolds like a living, breathing cathedral of sound, where minimalist rhythms evolve into towering walls of industrial techno. It begins with a sparse, almost cacophonic line of minimalist sounds—fractured, distant percussions that leave an unsettling silence in their wake. This intentionally disorienting introduction lingers, building a sense of tension that feels like something is about to break. Then, after what feels like an eternity, the voice emerges, its first words cutting through the chaos:

*"Feels dangerous?"*

This question is both an invitation and a declaration, a relief after the discomfort of the intro. It signals that the song is no longer a fragmented puzzle but a cohesive whole—its jagged edges now fitting together. The listener, once unsure, now feels grounded, as the track smoothly flows into its full form. What seemed like an erratic, disjointed beginning reveals itself as an integral part of the song’s larger plan, an intentional buildup to the moment of realization.

From this point on, Louisahhh’s haunting vocal delivery leads the listener through a journey of industrial energy and lyrical introspection. With pounding basslines, distorted textures, and a powerful sense of urgency, the track paints a sonic landscape that is at once foreboding and liberating. It captures the push and pull between control and surrender, reflecting an emotional journey that is both deeply personal and universally resonant.

> As the song progresses, its architecture becomes deliberately claustrophobic, enveloping the listener in a maze of sound that is both suffocating and freeing. The industrial walls of techno press inward, heightening the tension, yet paradoxically expand outward, offering fleeting glimpses of freedom within confinement. This interplay of constriction and release mirrors the emotional depth and intensity that defines the track. Each moment builds upon the last, creating a dynamic tension that propels the listener forward, as if there’s no turning back now. The song is not just heard; it is felt in the body, in the pulse, in the space between control and surrender.

As *Like A Vice* fades out, the song leaves the listener suspended, as if they’ve just crossed a threshold but are unsure whether they’ve emerged unscathed or transformed. The abruptness of the ending echoes the song's themes, creating an unresolved tension that lingers long after the music stops.

By the end, the listener is left suspended, utterly alone in the void. The abrupt conclusion—where pounding basslines and haunting vocals vanish in an instant—leaves the listener feeling exposed and introspective, as if the song has stripped away all pretense. It's a confrontation with vulnerability, creating an unresolved tension that lingers long after the music stops.

### About the Album: *A Trap I've Built*

The track *Like A Vice* closes Louisahhh’s five-track EP *A Trap I’ve Built*, released on RAAR on 1 December 2017, which Mixmag called her most visceral, unnerving and powerful release to date. Its tracks are Hey Trouble, To Me Right Now, Trap, Line and Like A Vice, and XLR8R described it as a bridge between her dancefloor work and a more live-sounding, post-alternative industrial pop. Available as a set on [SoundCloud](https://soundcloud.com/louisahhh/sets/a-trap-ive-built-raar008).

The EP moves through the shadowy edges of techno and industrial music. It is an exploration of extremes: raw, brutal and unflinchingly honest.

The EP's **voice-as-vice brutality** becomes its defining characteristic, with Louisahhh's vocals dominating, piercing, and commanding rather than merely complementing the instrumentation. Her voice carries raw yet deliberate power, echoing the struggle and triumph central to the record.

**Brutality as an Art Form**

*“A Trap I’ve Built”* isn’t just an EP—it’s an assault, both sonically and emotionally. The relentless pounding basslines, distorted synth layers, and frenetic percussive elements work in tandem with Louisahhh’s vocal grit to create a soundscape that feels like a constant push-and-pull between chaos and catharsis.

The brutality of the EP lies not just in its raw production but in the way it demands introspection from its listeners. Louisahhh’s voice often teeters on the edge of breaking, carrying the weight of her lyrical themes—power, addiction, rebellion, and the struggle to find freedom within self-imposed limitations. In tracks like *Like A Vice*, her voice twists like a blade, making you question whether the resolution will be liberation or surrender.

## Voice and Techno: A Dual Storytelling Mechanism

One of the most remarkable aspects of **"Like A Vice"** is how Louisahhh’s voice and the techno lines interplay to tell a story that feels alive and unpredictable. Her voice isn’t just a vessel for the lyrics; it’s a character in its own right, whispering, pleading, commanding, and at times, defying the relentless pulse of the music. The techno backdrop serves as an emotional mirror, evolving with the vocal performance, weaving an atmosphere that reflects the emotional stakes of the narrative.

> The opening verse introduces the central metaphor of the song—a vice—representing something that is both alluring and suffocating. Louisahhh's breathy, restrained delivery is matched by minimal percussion and a repetitive synth loop, creating a sense of quiet desperation. The subdued arrangement mirrors the internal struggle hinted at in the lyrics.

### The Voice: Telling a Story

Louisahhh’s vocal performance carries raw emotion, drawing listeners into a world of tension and desire. In the early lines her restrained delivery feels like a confession, quietly powerful. As the song builds, her voice transforms, embodying defiance and empowerment, and by the final moments her tone is triumphant, yet layered with complexity.

### The Techno Lines: A Living Atmosphere

The techno production mirrors and amplifies the emotional story. From the ominous, suffocating synths of the opening to the chaotic yet cathartic intensity of the chorus, the music evolves in step with the narrative. Its unpredictability—teetering between hope and despair—keeps listeners on edge. Will the music resolve in light or descend further into darkness? It’s a question the track refuses to answer clearly, keeping its emotional tension alive.

### The Ending: A Sudden Disappearance

As the track reaches its final moments, the relentless intensity gives way to an unexpected quiet. The pounding basslines and haunting vocals vanish almost suddenly, leaving the listener in a state of suspended reflection. The ending isn’t gradual—it’s abrupt, like stepping off a cliff into silence. This deliberate choice feels like a final commentary on the themes of the song: just as the story of control and surrender culminates, it dissipates, leaving an unresolved tension.

> **"Like A Vice"** is a song that defies easy categorization. It’s intense, raw, and deeply emotional, yet it leaves its ultimate message open to interpretation. Is the ending triumphant or tragic? Does the sudden silence symbolize liberation or a descent into nothingness?

The song’s unpredictability and emotional depth invite listeners to bring their own experiences to the narrative. It’s not just music—it’s an emotional mirror, asking you to reflect on your own relationship with control, desire, and surrender.

"Like A Vice" emerges as a living, breathing cathedral of sound—a masterpiece where minimalist heartbeats evolve into towering walls of industrial techno. The foundation is laid with subtle percussion, a rhythmic heartbeat that grows progressively intricate and intense. Each new layer of sound adds to the tension, creating an atmosphere that feels alive and ever-shifting.

## Concepts in Focus

Louisahhh's voice works like {{ <concept_link name="spoken word" /> }} as much as song. Mixmag has called hers one of the most instantly recognisable voices in dance music, built on emotion-laced spoken words that dominate the tracks they appear on. On Like A Vice she whispers, pleads and commands more than she sings melodies, so the voice behaves like a character in a story rather than a hook laid over a beat.

The track is a lesson in the long {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }}. Its opening is sparse and fractured, percussion scattered across silence, and it withholds a settled groove for a long time. When the voice arrives and the parts lock together, the relief is the payoff; tension built by absence rather than by the snare rolls of a festival track.

Its industrial weight comes from {{ <concept_link name="distortion" /> }}. Overdriven bass and textures add harmonics and grit, which makes the sound feel close and physical, the quality Louisahhh had in mind when she said the EP should be something listeners can feel in their teeth.
