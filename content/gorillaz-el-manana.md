## Genre and Mood

**Gorillaz**' **El Mañana** is the still point of Demon Days. Filed under trip-hop and dub, it is slow and soft-edged where the album around it is crowded and loud: Vice called it tranquil, Stereogum gorgeous. The feelings are melancholic and dark, and introspective, because the song turns inward rather than outward.

## Song Summary

El Mañana, Spanish for "the tomorrow" or "the future", was written by Damon Albarn. Albarn is credited with vocals, synthesizers, electric guitar and the string arrangement, Simon Tong with acoustic guitar, and Danger Mouse and James Dring with drum programming, for a string section of violins, violas, cello and double bass. Audio Snobbery hears in it a conversation between a hopeless man and his god, a mix of gospel, soul and blues with a siren in the background. The page plays the official animated video, which runs a few seconds longer than the 3:50 album track.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

According to the Gorillaz Song by Song blog, the instrumental builds up fully before Albarn's voice comes in. No source gives the parts a time.

### Main Section

The same writer hears a soft art-rock song centred on synthesizers, strings and acoustic guitar, with electric piano countermelodies, slide guitar and a drum-machine loop, and a whooshing siren that signals danger. Both reviewers also hear gospel backing vocals; the song's credits on Wikipedia name no choir.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }} is what makes the song so calm. The three singles before it each had a guest; El Mañana credits none, and is set for strings, guitars, synthesizers and programmed drums, with the siren as its one sign of threat.

Gorillaz are a virtual band, and the {{ <concept_link name="stage persona" /> }} is part of the song. Albarn sings as the cartoon 2-D, yet the Song by Song blog hears him writing in his own voice; the in-character notes it quotes, credited to Noodle, call the track internal dialogue over a staccato ballad, the calm at the centre of the storm.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, released on 11 March 2006 and directed by Jamie Hewlett and Pete Candeland at Passion Pictures, returns to Noodle's floating island from {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-de-la-soul-feel-good-inc.md" /> }}. Two helicopters attack it, set the windmill on fire and send the island crashing into a canyon; Noodle's apparent death there was, Vice notes, a false alarm.

## Artistic Context

Demon Days, released by Parlophone in 2005 and produced with Danger Mouse, is darker than the band's debut, a loose concept album that Albarn described as "the world in a state of night". El Mañana came out on 10 April 2006 as its fourth and final single, a double A-side with {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-neneh-cherry-kids-with-guns.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

The single reached number 27 in the UK and 17 in Scotland, and in 2025 the pairing was certified silver in the UK.
