## Genre and Mood

**Camille Yarbrough**'s **Take Yo' Praise** is soul with funk and jazz in its blood: Discogs files its album under soul, soul-jazz and jazz-funk, and Craft Recordings calls it an adventurous soul and R&B record. The feeling is soulful, romantic and heartfelt, a song that spends four minutes thanking someone, and AALBC's reviewer summed it up in two words: it exudes soul.

## Song Summary

The lyric speaks to a partner. It begins with a long road travelled together through hard times and good, and with the need to celebrate and praise that person as they deserve; a refrain returns to how rare and fine they are, and the title phrase arrives only in the closing lines. Yarbrough has said she wrote it for all the people who had come through the Black civil rights movement, because human beings need to praise and respect one another more than they do. The love song and the dedication sit in the same words.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

The song opens with its most famous lines, the passage later sampled for Praise You. No source describes the arrangement under them in detail, so this page does not.

### Main Section

The verses and the refrain alternate, the praise growing more insistent with each pass. The album's band, as credited, is guitar, clavinet, bass, drums and congas, and AllMusic's review hears the record's ambitions spilling out of minimal instrumentation.

### Outro

The last lines finally name the gift: take yo' praise.

## Concepts in Focus

Take Yo' Praise is a famous source of {{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }}. In 1998 Norman Cook, as Fatboy Slim, took a vocal passage from its opening and made it the voice of Praise You; released as a single in January 1999, it went to number one in the UK. In a 2021 interview with WhoSampled, Yarbrough said she liked it and felt Cook had kept the essence of her song.

What Cook found was a ready-made {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }}: a few lines that state the whole song and can be repeated without wearing out.

Yarbrough also came to the record from {{ <concept_link name="spoken word" /> }}. The Iron Pot Cooker grew out of her one-woman show Tales and Tunes of an African American Griot, and Billboard praised her own style of singing and recitation.

## Artistic Context

Yarbrough, born in 1934 and raised on Chicago's South Side, danced with the Katherine Dunham Company, and her show was staged at La MaMa in New York in 1973. The Iron Pot Cooker, her debut, came out on Vanguard in 1975: all songs her own, produced by Ed Bland, recorded at Vanguard's 23rd Street Studio. Hear another voice Fatboy Slim built a single around in {{ <track_link source="fatboy-slim-bird-of-pray.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Billboard heard traces of Nina Simone, whom you can hear in {{ <track_link source="nina-simone-sinnerman.md" /> }}, and Gil Scott-Heron; Spin called Yarbrough a hip-hop foremother. AllMusic named the album a landmark. MusicBrainz lists the song on compilations of samples and breaks from 1999 on, and on Fatboy Slim's own Back to Mine mix in 2020.
