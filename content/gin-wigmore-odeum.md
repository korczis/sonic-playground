## Genre and Mood

**Gin Wigmore** placed **Odeum** second on her fourth album, Ivory, and it is one of the record's quieter blows. Wikipedia describes the album as a heavier alternative rock sound with blue-eyed soul; Apple Music hears rock and soul with touches of funk and piano. Within that, BSides Badlands called Odeum one of her more somber reprieves, while Thomas Bleach heard it as off-beat driven and cinematic. Defiant, dark and atmospheric are this page's words for a song that is angry without being loud.

## Song Summary

Wigmore explained the song to PopCrush in March 2018: its story is to question and analyse the patriarchal layout of our system, an open letter of sorts to the misogynist who still believes it is fine to treat women so poorly. Her interviewer noted that the title refers to a live performance space; Wigmore said it evoked a sort of grand mystery for her. Apple Music's notes say the song bravely confronts uneasy realities. It runs 2:39 and is credited to Wigmore and Steve Rusch.

## Musical Breakdown

No source maps Odeum section by section, so this guide keeps to the three details reviewers described.

### Intro

Odeum grows out of the album's opener. Renowned for Sound wrote that Hallow Fate crossfades into it by slowing down its musical motifs, which creates a wonderful continuity.

### Main Section

BSides Badlands heard the vocal soaked in spacious distortion; Thomas Bleach called the song off-beat driven. Which instruments carry it, and how it ends, no source says.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="protest song" /> }} takes a stand against something in public life. Odeum does not name an event or a person; by Wigmore's own account it questions a whole system, which brings it close to the form, delivered as a letter rather than a slogan.

{{ <concept_link name="distortion" /> }} on a voice roughens it and pushes it forward. On a song addressed to someone, that grit can sound like the words are being forced out; listen for how the reviewer's spacious distortion sits around Wigmore's voice.

The {{ <concept_link name="off-beat" /> }} is the point halfway between the main beats. Thomas Bleach heard the song driven by it; listen for which part of the rhythm leans on those in-between points.

## Artistic Context

Wigmore, from New Zealand and based in Los Angeles, released Ivory on Island Records Australia on 6 April 2018, named after her son. She told PopCrush that she set out for the album to feel more empowered and positive after the miserable state of mind in which she wrote Blood to Bone, and that it collects not-so-pretty stories of her experience in the music industry. Compare {{ <track_link source="gin-wigmore-dirty-mercy.md" /> }}, the album's first single, also written with Rusch.

## Impact and Reception

Reviewers of Ivory singled Odeum out: Thomas Bleach called it an instant fan favourite, and BSides Badlands quoted it before any other song on the album. Ivory reached number 11 in New Zealand. Play it straight after Hallow Fate, the way the album runs.
