## Genre and Mood

**Rachel K Collier**'s **Ships** is electronic music with a pop song at its centre, filed under electro-pop by the blogs that covered it. Salacious Sound heard it as dreamy and eclectic, Podcart as beat-heavy and built for a party, and the page keeps both words, dreamy and energetic, because the reviews pull in those two directions at once.

## Song Summary

Ships came out as a digital single on 16 September 2016, the first of a run of solo releases on NIF Records, the label Collier set up to put out her own music. The lyric, as Salacious Sound summed it up, is about letting the bad ships sail on and focusing on what really matters: a song about letting go, sung by the person who also produced it. The official video's description credits it ℗ NIF Records 2016. No source names a tempo, a key or a writer besides Collier, so this page gives none.

## Musical Breakdown

No review walks through the song in order, so this breakdown keeps to the elements critics named and to what Collier herself showed in a live video.

### Main Section

The percussion leads: Salacious Sound singled out its tribal drums and a cheerful synth-pop mood above them, Podcart its weight, a "musical cannonball". Collier sings of the bad ships over that beat.

### Live Version

Six weeks after the release Collier posted a live performance on her channel, filmed in a London café. A laptop out of shot runs Ableton Live 9, controlled from an APC40 that triggers the clips, effects and vocal loops; an Arturia MiniBrute SE plays the bass and a King Korg the keys, and she sings into a Shure SM7.

## Concepts in Focus

The live video makes the {{ <concept_link name="loop" /> }} visible. Each part of the song sits in a clip that she starts and stops by hand, and her own voice is recorded and repeated while she sings over it: one person rebuilds the arrangement in real time.

Ships is also a lesson in the {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} as artist. Collier had sung on other producers' tracks, such as {{ <track_link source="ryan-blyth_once-twice-feat-rachel-k-collier.md" /> }} from the same year; here, as the PRS Foundation put it, she was "a one-girl production machine", writing, producing and releasing her own singles.

## Artistic Context

Collier comes from Mumbles in Swansea and studied music technology at the Royal Welsh College of Music & Drama before moving to London. Her voice first reached the charts through a 2012 version of Jimmy Cliff's Hard Road to Travel, used in an advert, and as the featured singer on Ray Foxx's Boom Boom (Heartbeat), which reached number 12 in the UK. Her 2015 EP Words You Never Heard preceded Ships, and Paper Tiger followed it in 2017, also on NIF Records, which the PRS Foundation says works with AWAL and showcases other electronic female producers. Ableton lists her as a certified trainer whose live rig is built around its Push controller.

## Impact and Reception

Ships did not chart, and the reviews it got were short blog posts. They agree on its energy and its good mood. Start with the official video, then watch one performer put it back together live.
