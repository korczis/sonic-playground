## Genre and Mood

**Doechii**'s **Anxiety** is pop rap built on somebody else's hit. It sits between hip-hop and pop: verses rapped with real force, a sung hook, and underneath them the instantly familiar instrumental of Gotye's Somebody That I Used to Know. The mood is introspective in its words and intense in its delivery, a song about nerves that keeps its own composure.

## Song Summary

Doechii recorded the song in 2019, in her bedroom, during the sessions for her mixtape Coven Music Sessions, and posted it on her YouTube channel that November. Six years later the old recording went viral on TikTok, and on 4 March 2025 Top Dawg Entertainment and Capitol released an official version with re-recorded vocals. Clash heard a song about being overwhelmed by expectation, about problems outside and inside, and about working towards self-forgiveness, with references running from Jacques Cousteau to Harriet Tubman. Doechii is credited as its producer.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

The Gotye instrumental is the bed of the whole track. One critic heard foreboding and a tentativeness in those plucks and strums that suits the subject; another found them chintzy.

### Verse 1

The first verse is where the energy crests, according to Claire Davidson of The Singles Jukebox, and where Clash's hard-hitting flows are clearest. Two critics thought the song has the feel of a freestyle at points.

### Chorus

Al Varela called the hook a merry-go-round chorus and singled out how Doechii layers her vocals over it.

### Outro

No source describes the ending in detail; one critic noted that the song's length helps it sound like its own thing rather than a loop.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} is the centre of the argument about this song. The credits name Gotye and Luiz Bonfá, whose 1967 guitar instrumental Seville was itself sampled in {{ <track_link source="gotye-kimbra-somebody-that-i-used-to-know.md" /> }}. Critics split on whether taking the beat almost whole is a bold risk or an obstacle in front of a sharper song.

{{ <concept_link name="rapping" /> }} and singing trade places throughout. Ian Mathers of The Singles Jukebox called both excellent, and the movement between them is how the song carries a restless subject over a steady bed.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by James Mackel and released on 18 April 2025, escalates from a kitchen fire to intruders to a flash mob, and nods to the body-painted video of the Gotye original. It won Best Music Video at the 2026 Grammy Awards.

## Artistic Context

The official release followed Doechii's biggest award so far: in February 2025 she won the Grammy for Best Rap Album for Alligator Bites Never Heal, only the third woman to do so after Lauryn Hill and Cardi B. In 2023 Sleepy Hallow had already released a song of the same name, featuring Doechii, whose chorus samples her demo.

## Impact and Reception

Anxiety became her first US top-ten hit, reached number three in the UK and topped charts in Australia, New Zealand and Switzerland. It was nominated for Record and Song of the Year, though reviews split: Billboard called it adventurous, Paste listed it among the year's worst.
