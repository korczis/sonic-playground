## Genre and Mood

**Hooverphonic**'s **2 Wicky** is mid-1990s trip-hop: a slow, sampled groove, a soft female voice and a mood that hangs in the air. Downtempo and electronic describe its means; dreamy and hypnotic are this page's reading of what Albumism called a shimmering slice of sonic heaven.

## Song Summary

2 Wicky is the second of eleven tracks on A New Stereophonic Sound Spectacular, the Belgian band's debut, which Columbia released on 29 July 1996, at first under the band's original name, Hoover. It runs 4:44 and was one of the album's four singles. The song rests on two borrowed records: Isaac Hayes's 1969 recording of Walk On By, the Burt Bacharach and Hal David song, and Le Voile d'Orphée by the French composer Pierre Henry. Hence Bacharach, David and Henry share the writing credit with Alex Callier and Raymond Geerts. Over the samples sings Liesje Sadonius, whose voice is heard on this album only; she left the band shortly after its release.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

According to Wikipedia, the main riff comes from Hayes's Walk On By, and it is the ground the song stands on from the start. No source describes the opening bars further; listen for how much of the song that borrowed figure already holds.

### Main Section

Sadonius sings softly over the groove. Albumism points out that her lines refer to the Prophet 600 and the Roland SH-101, two synthesizers, an example of what Callier said in 1996 about the album's lyrics: they evoke atmospheres instead of telling stories. The liner notes credit Callier with programming and keyboards, Frank Duchêne with keyboards and engineering and Geerts with guitars.

### Outro

No published account maps the ending or says where the Pierre Henry sample enters.

## Concepts in Focus

2 Wicky is a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} across worlds. Hayes had turned Walk On By into a twelve-minute funk vamp on Hot Buttered Soul; Hooverphonic set that soul record beside a piece by Pierre Henry and built a pop song on both.

The lyric's two names belong to the {{ <concept_link name="analog synthesizer" /> }}: the Roland SH-101 is an analog instrument made between 1982 and 1986 that became a staple of 1990s electronic music. Here the machines become words.

The song travelled through the {{ <concept_link name="soundtrack" /> }}. It heads the soundtrack list of Bernardo Bertolucci's 1996 film Stealing Beauty, beside Portishead's Glory Box, and later appeared in I Know What You Did Last Summer and Heights.

## Artistic Context

Hooverphonic formed in Belgium in 1995 around the bassist Alex Callier and the guitarist Raymond Geerts. Their first singer, Esther Lybeert, left on the day they were to sign with Sony Music, and Sadonius recorded the album in her place; Geike Arnaert became the permanent singer in 1997. Compare {{ <track_link source="hooverphonic-badaboum.md" /> }}, two decades later.

## Impact and Reception

Wikipedia says Stealing Beauty brought the band international recognition, and by June 1998 the album had sold over 140,000 copies worldwide. Albumism calls 2 Wicky the band's most universally familiar single. The upload here is the album recording, reissued on the compilation Collected; the shorter official video is on HooverphonicVEVO. Let the riff settle in.
