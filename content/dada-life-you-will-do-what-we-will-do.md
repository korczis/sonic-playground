## Genre and Mood

**Dada Life** gave **You Will Do What We Will Do** a title that sounds like an order, and the track behaves like one. It is big-room electro house, energetic and intense, which Your EDM counted among the hardest-hitting tracks on The Rules of Dada. What lifts it towards euphoria is a sung hook pushed through a vocoder.

## Song Summary

There is no story here, only instructions for a dance floor. The track runs on the duo's usual tools, growing bass and aggressive synths in Elektro Daily's summary, yet that review called it one of the album's more experimental tracks, and Dancing Astronaut praised the way its repeating melodies flow into and out of the drops. The detail that stays is the voice: robotic, melodic and, for two of the critics, Daft Punk-like.

## Musical Breakdown

No source gives times for this track, so the parts below follow the order in which the reviews describe it.

### Intro

Magnetic Magazine heard a heavily detuned riser building suspense towards a sub drop, the melody fading in slowly. Your EDM described a gigantic bassline and a rising lead, then a high lead with backing chords descending towards the drop.

### Drop

The drop is, in Your EDM's words, pure frenzy: a booming sawtooth bass drives the groove under the high lead. Elektro Daily singled out a smaller surprise, a few quick counts of a video game-like ping-pong sound placed exactly where the bass is expected to return.

### Breakdown

Later comes the signature element, a sung melody under a heavy vocoder; Magnetic Magazine heard it creep in from the background in the second drop. Then the main lead returns on the same notes and the same rhythm as the voice.

### Outro

After the drop the track goes round for one last sequence and ends; no review describes the outro itself.

## Concepts in Focus

The detuned riser at the start is a textbook {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }}: a sound that climbs in pitch or intensity tells the crowd that something is coming.

The {{ <concept_link name="drop" /> }} is the release, and here the bass does most of the work. That is why Elektro Daily's small surprise matters: holding the bass back for a few counts makes its return hit harder.

A {{ <concept_link name="vocoder" /> }} imposes a voice onto a synthesizer's sound, so the hook sings but sounds built rather than born. When the lead synth then doubles that line note for note, human and machine meet in the middle. Compare the pitched-up, chopped vocal of {{ <track_link source="dada-life-so-young-so-high.md" /> }}, the track just before it.

## Artistic Context

Dada Life are Olle Corneér and Stefan Engblom, a Stockholm duo formed in 2006 and named after the Dada art movement. The Rules of Dada, released on 16 October 2012 by So Much Dada and Universal, is their second album, and this is track five. It credits the song to the duo and Anthony Mills, a vocalist who had contributed to their debut album.

## Impact and Reception

It was never a single, yet Your EDM called it a refreshing spin on the duo's brand of electro and said the perfectly mixed vocals make the song. Put it on loud and wait for the voice: it arrives late, and it is worth the wait.
