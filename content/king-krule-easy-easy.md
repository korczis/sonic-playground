## Genre and Mood

**King Krule** opens his debut album with **Easy Easy**, and it barely sounds like an opening at all: one guitar, one voice, no fuss. The album is filed under indie rock and post-punk, and this is its plainest, most singer-songwriter moment. The mood is raw and defiant, less a performance than a statement, which is how Stereogum heard it in 2013: a song that hits with tremendous directness.

## Song Summary

Easy Easy is built mostly on a single guitar that leaves room for Archy Marshall's voice. The Line of Best Fit's Michael Palmer reported that Marshall apparently wrote it in 2007, when he would have been about thirteen, and found not much underneath other than a simple guitar pattern and a fairly basic vocal melody. That spareness is the point and the risk. What makes the song memorable is the voice: Marshall's drawl, which in Palmer's words sounds like nobody else around.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the song section by section, and at under three minutes it does not need much mapping; this follows what the reviews describe.

### Intro

Best New Bands heard the album begin with a pacing lone guitar riff, Marshall's first croons laid over it.

### Main Section

The voice arrives over it and takes the room. Palmer found the production of the whole record so focused on the vocals that they overpower everything else, and felt it hurt the first three tracks, this one included.

## Concepts in Focus

The song rests on a {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }} in its plainest form: one guitar figure, repeated, holding the song up while the voice moves. A riff that simple asks the listener to stop waiting for the arrangement to grow.

Its {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, shot by the Los Angeles directors Focus Creeps, follows Marshall around London: singing on rooftops, riding the tube, oversized suits, a cricket bat and lens flare. It's Nice That called it an angsty masterpiece.

A year later, Willow Smith released a {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }}, which Stereogum judged pretty damn good: proof that the song survives without the voice it was written for.

## Artistic Context

Archy Marshall, born in Southwark in 1994, recorded first as Zoo Kid and took the name King Krule from the Elvis Presley film King Creole. 6 Feet Beneath the Moon came out on 24 August 2013, his nineteenth birthday, on XL Recordings, and on True Panther Sounds in North America; he produced it with Rodaidh McDonald. Easy Easy was its first single, released on 15 July 2013.

## Impact and Reception

Easy Easy split critics: what Stereogum heard as directness, the Line of Best Fit heard as too bare. The album brought Marshall to prominence, especially in the United States, with sets on Conan and the Late Show with David Letterman. Play it loud and listen to the voice, not the arrangement.
