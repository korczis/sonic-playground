## Genre and Mood

**Billie Eilish** opens her debut EP with **COPYCAT**, a song that critics filed under electronica and pop with hip-hop-influenced instrumentation, and that sits squarely in the dark, quiet electro-pop the EP made her name with. Its mood is defiant and confident rather than loud: a W critic called it a "don't-eff-with-me opening statement", and Dancing Astronaut found it slightly mysterious, a little creepy, but catchy nonetheless.

## Song Summary

The song is a reply to someone who kept copying her. Eilish told Genius she wrote it with her brother Finneas O'Connell in his room after growing frustrated with the imitation, and she explained to Billboard that the more successful artists become, the more people start copying them. Finneas produced it alone, and the production is minimalist, built mainly on bass guitar and piano. Musicnotes' sheet music sets it at a slow 64 beats per minute in E minor. The memorable detail is the bass, the "weird, creepy bass sound" Eilish credits to Finneas.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

No source times the opening, but Billboard heard a "ground-shaking opener".

### Main Section

The verses keep the arrangement spare, Eilish's voice close and low over bass and piano. The lyric, printed under the official audio, is all threat and dare, down to the line about the trigger finger being hers.

### Bridge

Here the song turns into a piano ballad. Her vocal drops to near silence and builds into falsetto as she apologises and warns the subject that they have gone too far.

### Outro

Then it cuts straight back to the dark original sound, a "psych" twist that takes the apology back. The Irish Times heard a "menacing synth" in the ending.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} is the song's engine. At a slow 64 beats per minute the heavy bass, not the drums, sets the pace, and a Billboard writer compared the sound to later records by XXXTentacion and Tyler, the Creator.

The {{ <concept_link name="bridge" /> }} shows what a contrasting section can do. The softer piano and the rise into {{ <concept_link name="falsetto" /> }} make the threat sound like it is being withdrawn, so the return of the bass hits harder.

The song is in E minor, and the {{ <concept_link name="minor key" /> }} with that slow tempo gives the menace critics heard. Compare it with {{ <track_link source="billie-eilish-bury-a-friend.md" /> }}, where the same siblings push the darkness further.

## Artistic Context

COPYCAT was released on 14 July 2017 by Darkroom and Interscope as the fourth single from dont smile at me, which followed on 11 August 2017. Eilish and Finneas wrote all of the EP, Finneas produced it. The EP mixes bedroom pop and electropop with R&B and jazz and reached number 14 on the Billboard 200.

## Impact and Reception

NME called the song "impossibly swagger", and Insider later saw it as a precursor of sorts to Bad Guy. A Sofi Tukker remix arrived in January 2018. The song never entered the Hot 100, peaking at number 12 on the Bubbling Under chart, yet it has gone platinum in the US and four times platinum in Canada. Start the EP where she did, and listen for the bass.
