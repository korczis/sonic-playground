## Genre and Mood

**DJ Patife**'s remix of **Sambassim** is drum and bass with a Brazilian heart, a track where bossa nova softness and São Paulo club energy meet halfway. It is vibrant, groovy and openly joyful, the sound of a beach afternoon sped up to 170 beats per minute without losing any of its ease.

## Song Summary

Sambassim began as a song by the Brazilian singer and producer Fernanda Porto, and Patife's version turns her airy, sing-song melody into the centrepiece of a rolling breakbeat. The trick is contrast. The drums skip and rattle at full speed while the vocal floats on top as if nothing were rushing at all, and the bass rolls underneath like a slow tide. It is music for dancing that never forgets it is also a song.

## Musical Breakdown

The remix is built in the classic liquid drum and bass shape: a melodic introduction, a drop into the break, and long, flowing stretches where the groove and the voice trade places.

### Intro

Warm chords and a hint of Porto's melody set a relaxed, sunlit scene, the kind of opening that could just as easily lead into a bossa nova ballad.

### Build-up

Percussion starts to gather, light and crisp, with samba-flavoured hits hinting at the speed to come while the harmony stays soft.

### Main Section

The break drops and the track finds its stride: quick, shuffling drums, a deep, round bassline and the vocal gliding above them in Portuguese, never straining to keep up.

### Bridge

The drums thin out for a moment and the melody gets space to breathe, a short exhale before the rhythm section rolls back in.

### Climax

Everything returns together, the bass at its fullest and the percussion at its busiest, the samba swing and the breakbeat finally sounding like one idea.

### Outro

The track eases back towards its gentle opening, leaving the melody hanging in the air as the drums fall away.

## Artistic Context

DJ Patife is a drum and bass DJ and producer from São Paulo and one of the names, with his frequent collaborator DJ Marky, who carried Brazilian drum and bass to international dancefloors from the late 1990s onwards. His Sambassim remix, issued by the Brazilian label Trama and later collected on Cool Steps Drum 'N' Bass Grooves, became the record most associated with him and with Fernanda Porto's fusion of bossa nova and electronic music.

## Impact and Reception

The Patife remix is often cited as one of the best-selling drum and bass singles ever, and it remains a staple of liquid and Brazilian-flavoured sets decades later. It belongs on this playground because it shows how naturally samba and breakbeat fit together when someone gets the balance right. Put it on when the sun is out and let it carry you.
