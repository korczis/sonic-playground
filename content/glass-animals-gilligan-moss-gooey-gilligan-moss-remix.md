## Genre and Mood

**Glass Animals** handed **Gooey** to **Gilligan Moss**, and the **Gooey (Gilligan Moss Remix)** comes back as glistening indie electronic music rather than indie pop. As a remix it keeps the original's sticky intimacy but makes it dreamy and hypnotic, with a playful streak in its percussion that keeps the haze from ever getting heavy.

## Song Summary

The original is a slow, honeyed love song; this version stretches it out to nearly six minutes and lets its pieces float apart. Dave Bayley's vocal is cut into fragments and slivers, chiming percussion dances around a deep, patient groove, and the melody keeps reappearing from new angles. The detail that makes it memorable is that chiming top end, sweet on the surface with something faintly sinister underneath.

## Musical Breakdown

Rather than a verse-chorus song, the remix behaves like a slow tide.

### Intro

Bell-like tones and soft clicks flicker in the dark before any kick appears, as if the track is tuning itself.

### Build-up

A warm sub bass and a gentle, shuffling beat slide in, and the first pitched-up vocal fragments start to circle.

### Main Section

The groove settles into a hypnotic sway, layered percussion playing call and response with chopped pieces of the Gooey hook.

### Bridge

Midway the drums drop back and the chimes take over, a glittering breakdown that feels like the remix holding its breath.

### Climax

The beat returns fuller, the vocal fragments now stacked and the melody blooming into its most lush form.

### Outro

Layers fall away until only the chimes and a last echo of the voice remain, dissolving rather than ending.

## Artistic Context

Glass Animals, the Oxford band led by Dave Bayley, released Gooey as the lead single for their debut album ZABA in 2014, alongside an EP of remixes. Gilligan Moss was a young producer at the time, known for warped, twinkling takes on electronic music, and DIY premiered his version in April 2014 ahead of the album. At 5:39 it runs close to a minute longer than the album cut.

## Impact and Reception

The remix found an audience of its own on music blogs and became one of the best-loved alternate versions of Gooey, often described as lush and multi-layered and credited with putting Gilligan Moss on the map. It is on this playground because it shows how a great remix can find a new mood inside a familiar song. Put on headphones and let it melt.
