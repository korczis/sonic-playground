## Genre and Mood

**Glass Animals** handed **Gooey** to **Gilligan Moss**, and the **Gooey (Gilligan Moss Remix)** comes back as glistening indie electronic music rather than indie pop. It keeps the original's sticky intimacy but makes it dreamy and hypnotic, with a playful streak in its percussion that keeps the haze from ever getting heavy.

## Song Summary

The original is a slow, honeyed love song; this version stretches it out to more than five and a half minutes and lets its pieces float apart. DIY, which premiered the remix, described chiming percussion providing an oddly sinister backing and a song that keeps its loose shape while being stretched out to serious lengths. That chiming top end is what makes it memorable: sweet on the surface with something faintly unsettling underneath.

## Musical Breakdown

Rather than a verse-chorus song, the remix behaves like a slow tide.

### Intro

Bell-like tones and soft clicks flicker in the dark before the beat settles, as if the track is tuning itself.

### Build-up

A warm low end and a gentle beat slide in, and the first vocal fragments start to circle.

### Main Section

The groove settles into a hypnotic sway, layered percussion moving around pieces of the Gooey hook.

### Bridge

Midway the drums drop back and the chimes take over, a glittering passage that feels like the remix holding its breath.

### Climax

The beat returns fuller and the melody blooms into its most lush form.

### Outro

Layers fall away until only the chimes and a last echo of the voice remain, dissolving rather than ending.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} reworks the parts of a recording, and this one shows how far that can go while keeping a song recognisable. Gilligan Moss keeps Dave Bayley's vocal and the melody of {{ <track_link source="glass-animals-gooey.md" /> }}, but replaces its slow, sensual groove with twinkling, warped electronics. DIY compared his style at the time to producers such as Pantha du Prince and Four Tet, and you can hear that delicacy in the percussion.

The remix is also a slow {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }}. Instead of a single rise and drop, it adds layers patiently over several minutes, so that its fullest moment arrives late and feels earned. Listen for the point where the chimes, the beat and the voice finally sound together.

## Artistic Context

Glass Animals, the Oxford band led by Dave Bayley, released Gooey on 14 February 2014 as the lead single from their debut album Zaba, made with executive producer Paul Epworth; Bayley famously recorded its final chorus in eight different voices to imitate a choir. The Gooey EP that followed in April 2014 collected remixes and other extras, and Gilligan Moss's version was one of them. At 5:39 it runs close to a minute longer than the album cut.

## Impact and Reception

The remix found an audience of its own on music blogs: Complex later reported that it reached number one on Hype Machine, and it is often credited with putting Gilligan Moss on the map. The original went on to number 12 in Triple J's Hottest 100 of 2014, the band's best placing until Heat Waves. It is on this playground because it shows how a great remix can find a new mood inside a familiar song. Put on headphones and let it melt.
