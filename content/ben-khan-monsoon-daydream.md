## Genre and Mood

**Ben Khan** put **monsoon daydream** on his 2018 debut album, and it is the record's sound in under three minutes: electropop with funk in its hips, filed by Apple Music under alternative. The Guardian called the album singular coiled-lust electropop, full of loaded lines about honey and tempestuous weather, and singled this track out as magnificent. The mood is groovy, sultry and playful at once, a song that seems almost too excited to keep its footing.

## Song Summary

The Guardian's description is the best map: monsoon daydream gets so caught up in its own groove that it shudders and trips over itself. The arrangement is dense, full to the brim with raunchily screwball licks and a flutter of glockenspiel, and both the arrangement and Khan's voice reminded the critic of early Jamie Lidell. Khan wrote it alone, and it runs two minutes and forty-nine seconds.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through monsoon daydream section by section, so this breakdown follows the three things the reviews name rather than a verse-and-chorus map.

### The Groove

The groove is the engine and the joke: it runs so hard that it stumbles.

### The Arrangement

Around it the track is crowded with licks and small sounds, among them a glockenspiel that flutters rather than rings. The Skinny heard the whole album drenched in fluorescent synths and bubbling electronics.

### The Voice

Khan's voice sits inside that crowd. Across the album The Line of Best Fit heard morphed, contorted vocals amid phosphorescent wobbles and bursts of electronic sound.

## Concepts in Focus

The track is a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} as character rather than background. A groove is usually praised for being steady; here the praise is for one that shudders, as if the song danced faster than its own feet.

It also complicates the idea of {{ <concept_link name="lo-fi" /> }}. The Guardian heard an appealingly raw, bedroom-recorded quality on the album, yet it was made with big names: The Line of Best Fit reported that it was produced by Flood, known for Nine Inch Nails, PJ Harvey and Depeche Mode, mixed by Spike Stent and mastered by Dave Cooley. Rawness can be a choice that survives a professional studio.

That makes the {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} worth thinking about. Khan plays every instrument on the record himself; Flood's job was to shape the sound and the performances, not to supply the parts.

## Artistic Context

Ben Khan is a London producer and singer whose early singles made his name in the mid-2010s with warped funk such as {{ <track_link source="ben-khan-savage.md" /> }}. The self-titled album followed on Dirty Hit on 10 August 2018. DIY presented monsoon daydream, on 8 August, as the last track shared before release. Khan said he hoped the album would take listeners out of their reality to the start of a fantasy place.

## Impact and Reception

Reviews of the album were mixed to warm: The Line of Best Fit gave it 8.5 out of 10, The Skinny three stars, naming monsoon daydream among the tracks to hear, and The Guardian picked it out above all. Turn it up and listen for the moment the groove trips.
