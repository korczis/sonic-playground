## Genre and Mood

**Dr. Dre**'s **Murder Ink** is West Coast hip-hop from the second half of his 1999 album 2001, a short G-funk rap track that he shares with Hittman and Ms. Roq. It is filed as dark, gritty and eerie: dark and gritty because it is a story about killing and being killed, eerie because its beat borrows from the theme of a horror film.

## Song Summary

Murder Ink is the 15th track of 2001, over in 2:28. Edge Induced Cohesion summed it up as a song about the violence that comes to thugs who live by the gun, people who make enemies who watch them and catch them lacking. Wikipedia credits two samples: the Halloween theme by John Carpenter, who directed and scored the 1978 film, and Here Comes the Hotstepper, Ini Kamoze's 1994 dancehall hit. The label's credits name Andre R. Young, Brian Bailey and Raquel Weaver as writers, with Dre and Mel-Man producing and Dre and Richard "Segal" Huredia mixing.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes the beat bar by bar, so this breakdown follows the album's order and the reviews.

### The Skit Before It

The Car Bomb, a one-minute skit with Mel-Man and Charis Henry, runs straight into the track on the album.

### The Beat

The Halloween sample is the part critics remember: Okayplayer's Peter A. Berry gave it props. MusicBrainz credits Mike Elizondo on bass and Camara Kambon and Scott Storch on keyboards.

### The Hook and the Verses

Berry was less kind about the rest, hearing flavorless verses and a hook that sounds like it came together in exactly 15 seconds. He also felt that Dre's authoritative presence was sorely missed on the vocal end.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} lets a producer borrow a mood as well as a sound. A horror-film theme brings its associations with it, and here they frame a lyric about being watched by enemies. A dancehall chart-topper sits beside it, two borrowed worlds on one beat.

The Car Bomb is an {{ <concept_link name="interlude" /> }}, one of the skits that stitch 2001 together. It sets the scene, and Murder Ink tells what follows.

Both guests are a {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} in the full sense of the term. Wikipedia counts Hittman among the artists with several appearances on 2001, and Ms. Roq's smaller ones are this track, Let's Get High and the Bar One skit. Hear the two of them with Dre again on {{ <track_link source="dr-dre-let-s-get-high.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

2001 came out on 16 November 1999 on Aftermath and Interscope, recorded in 1998 and 1999 and produced mostly by Dr. Dre and Mel-Man. Wikipedia names Hittman among the ghostwriters behind some of the album's lyrics. It debuted at number two on the Billboard 200 and has been certified six times platinum.

## Impact and Reception

Murder Ink was not a single, and it sits near the bottom of most rankings. Berry placed it 17th in Okayplayer's 2024 ranking; in Soul In Stereo's retrospective Ronald Grant put it last of 17 and Edd Bowser 11th. Play The Car Bomb first, then this, and listen for what the borrowed theme does to the story.
