## Genre and Mood

**Asian Dub Foundation**'s "Flyover" is a fusion of dub, punk energy and electronic music, brimming with political urgency. It opens their 2005 album *Tank* at full speed: heavy synths, churning drum and bass rhythm and a confrontational vocal.

## Song Summary

"Flyover" carries the band's signature: relentless beats, rapid-fire vocals and a message that refuses to stay in the background. It opens an album written two years into the Iraq War, whose other songs take on the case for invasion and the oil behind it, and it sets that tone before the title track arrives. What keeps it from being only a slogan is its melodic chorus, a dub-friendly hook that opens space in the middle of the rush.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro: Setting the Stage for Rebellion

The track opens synth-heavy, with a deep bass underneath, setting a tone of intensity before the rhythm fully arrives.

### Build-up: Momentum in Motion

The drums kick in with the fast, syncopated churn of drum and bass, and the vocals enter with sharp precision, the beat and the bass locking into the song's backbone.

### Main Section: Unapologetic Confrontation

At the core of the track the energy is at full force: rapid breakbeats, heavy bass and a confrontational vocal, balanced against the melodic chorus.

### Bridge: Moment of Reflection

The arrangement eases toward a more dub-like space, bass and echo to the fore, before the rush returns.

### Climax: Peak Defiance

The final stretch is the densest, beats, bass and voice stacked as the track pushes to its end.

### Outro: The Aftershock

"Flyover" ends without a neat resolution, a fitting start for an album about a conflict that was still going on.

## Concepts in Focus

Asian Dub Foundation took their name from {{ <concept_link name="dub" /> }}, the Jamaican studio practice of stripping a record to drums and bass and treating everything else with echo and drop-outs. Flyover is not a reggae song, but you can hear dub's inheritance in the weight of its bass and in a chorus that a reviewer compared to Dreadzone, the British band that brought dub into dance music.

The rhythm underneath is drum and bass, a genre built on {{ <concept_link name="breakbeat" /> }}: syncopated, chopped funk-style drums played at high speed, while the bass moves at half that pace. That double sense of time, fast drums and slow bass, is what makes the track feel both frantic and heavy. Compare the Brazilian take on the same style in {{ <track_link source="dj-patife-sambassim-dj-patife-remix.md" /> }}.

On top sits the {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }}: the opener is synth-heavy, and the synths give the band's guitar-and-bass roots a harder, more electronic surface on this album.

## Artistic Context

Asian Dub Foundation formed in summer 1993 out of an education workshop in Farringdon, London, with Aniruddha "Dr Das" Das, John "Pandit G" Pandit and Steve "Chandrasonic" Savale among its founders. Their music fuses jungle, dub, ragga, rap rock and South Asian music with a punk spirit, and their 1998 album *Rafi's Revenge* was nominated for the Mercury Prize. "Flyover" opens *Tank*, their fifth studio album, released by EMI on 28 February 2005; for this record the band were joined by the On-U Sound vocalist Ghetto Priest. The track is credited to Das, Pandit and Savale.

## Impact and Reception

*Tank* was received as a sharp critique of the society that produced the war, with "Oil" and the title track as its most direct statements; reviewing its 2022 vinyl reissue, Sonic Abuse called Flyover a thrilling mix of churning drum and bass with a strong melodic edge. The year the album came out, the band won Best Underground at the UK Asian Music Awards.
