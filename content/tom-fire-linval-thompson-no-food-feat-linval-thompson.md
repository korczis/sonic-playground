## Genre and Mood

**Tom Fire** and **Linval Thompson** meet on **No Food**, reggae from a Paris studio with a Jamaican voice at its centre. The reviewer at Culture Dub heard a very catchy, melodic refrain that would stay in your head, and it is the hook that makes the track; the groove under it belongs to a producer whose first album was praised for its languid dub and whose second, Culture Dub found, runs on keyboards.

## Song Summary

No Food came out as a single on 2 April 2015 and became the fifth of eleven tracks on Low Fidelity, Tom Fire's second album, released on 4 September 2015 by Chapter Two Records with Wagram Music. It runs just over three minutes and a quarter. The guest is the point: Linval Thompson, a roots singer who began recording in the 1970s, sings over a production made forty years later. No source quotes or explains the lyric, so its subject is left to your ears.

## Musical Breakdown

### The Refrain

Culture Dub's one detailed remark about No Food concerns its refrain: catchy, melodic, made to return. Listen for how the verses lead back to it.

### The Production

The same review describes the album's common thread as the keyboard in every form. Under Thompson's voice, listen for the keys and for the dub roots that Longueur d'Ondes heard in Tom Fire's first album.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} is a guest credited on another artist's track. On a producer's album the guest is usually the voice, and Low Fidelity is built that way: Soom T, Mélissa Laveaux, Flavia Coelho and Winston McAnuff each take a track, and Linval Thompson takes this one. Mélissa Laveaux, whose own {{ <track_link source="melissa-laveaux-needle-in-the-hay.md" /> }} is reviewed here, sings Little Cake.

A {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is the part of a song built to stick, and the review's promise that this refrain will stay in your head is a description of exactly that.

{{ <concept_link name="dub" /> }} is the Jamaican art of remixing reggae in the studio, stripping it to drum and bass and adding echo. Thompson's own productions became the basis of some of Scientist's best-known dub albums, so the history runs through the singer as well as the producer.

## Artistic Context

Tom Fire is the name under which the French producer Camille Ballon releases his own music; Culture Dub calls him a musician, producer and beatmaker, and his first album, The Revenge, came out in 2011. Linval Thompson, born in Kingston in 1954, worked with Lee "Scratch" Perry and Bunny Lee, released his debut album Don't Cut Off Your Dreadlocks in 1976 and went on to produce Dennis Brown, Barrington Levy, Freddie McGregor and The Wailing Souls.

## Impact and Reception

Culture Dub judged Low Fidelity an album of great musical quality. No Food is here because it was among the owner's most played songs of 2022. Play it and see whether the refrain lets you go.
