## Genre and Mood

**Michal Prokop** and Framus Five come from rock, soul and the blues that shaped the band's first album in 1968, and **Ostraka** carries that history into a song about the present. Its mood comes from its words: tense, because every line names a fear, and reflective, because the fears are weighed rather than shouted. This is the concert version, from the live album Live 80.

## Song Summary

An ostrakon was a shard of a clay pot on which the free citizens of ancient Athens wrote the name of someone they wanted banished from the city for ten years. The notes to the studio video add that this old protection of democracy against tyranny turned, over time, into what we call ostracism. Michal Bulíř's lyric, set to music by the keyboard player Andy Čermák, is a catalogue of fears, and its refrain ends on the fear of one day finding the shard in the letterbox, "ve schránce najdu ostraku".

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

No source describes how the band opens the song at Forum Karlín, so this guide follows the published lyric.

### Verse 1

The first three stanzas alternate two openings, Mám strach (I am afraid) and Bojím se (I fear). The fears pile up: of the defeated and of false prophets, of wolves at one's back and of sheep all around, of head-hunters and of the anger of the humiliated.

### Chorus

The refrain gathers the list into a single image: a society that can turn on anyone, and the shard waiting in the letterbox.

### Verse 2

The second half widens the target: witnesses of the past whose memory fails, big words and the one holy truth, and, in a single balanced couplet, activists who cannot see the forest for the tree and, just as much, the alibists.

### Outro

The lyric ends on the refrain; no source describes how the band closes it here.

## Concepts in Focus

Ostraka is a {{ <concept_link name="list song" /> }}: it tells no story, it adds one item to the next. The list is even-handed on purpose; one couplet fears the activists and, in its own words, "stejně" (just as much) those who make excuses, so no side is left out.

This version is a {{ <concept_link name="live recording" /> }} from the band's Christmas concert at Forum Karlín in Prague on 20 December 2025. According to ČTK, the album came about almost by chance, after Supraphon asked whether the band recorded its concerts, and guests who had worked on the studio album Ostraka also played that night.

## Artistic Context

Framus Five was founded in Prague in 1963 and is led by Prokop; it paused in 1990 when Prokop went into politics. The studio album Ostraka came out on 25 April 2025 and was named Album of the Year at the Anděl awards, with Prokop also winning as solo performer. Live 80, fifteen songs and close to eighty minutes on 2LP and CD, marked his eightieth birthday in 2026, nearly two decades after Live 60.

## Impact and Reception

The studio album went gold and then platinum. No review of this live performance was found. Play it after the 2025 studio clip and listen to how a list of fears sounds sung in front of a crowd.
