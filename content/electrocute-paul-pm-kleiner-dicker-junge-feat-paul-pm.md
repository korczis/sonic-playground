## Genre and Mood

**Electrocute**'s **Kleiner Dicker Junge**, with **Paul PM**, is electroclash in miniature: under three minutes of electro and synth-pop, sung in German, from an album Discogs files under punk as well. Playful and irreverent is the brief, and the title, which the EP's English subtitle renders as Little Chubby Boy, sets the tone before a note is heard.

## Song Summary

No critic has written about this song on its own, so this page leans on its credits. The lyric is by Mia Dime, who sings the lead; Paul PM is credited for the voice of the little chubby boy; Nicole Morier made the beats and played guitar; Tico Zamora played bass, co-produced, recorded and mixed it. The writing is credited to the whole band. The recording first appeared in 2003 as Kleiner Dicker Junge (Little Chubby Boy) on the EP A Tribute to Your Taste, and returned in 2004, now crediting Paul PM as a featured artist, near the end of the album Troublesome Bubblegum.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }} is the scene the band came from. Nicole Morier, a singer from Albuquerque, founded Electrocute with Mia Dime in Berlin in 2002, drawn by the city's electropop and electroclash scene at the time; the German-language electroclash of {{ <track_link source="chicks-on-speed-kaltes-klares-wasser.md" /> }} comes from the same scene.

A {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} is usually a guest star. Here the credit names the character of the title: Paul PM's part is the little chubby boy, a role inside the song rather than a guest verse.

On a {{ <concept_link name="soundtrack" /> }} a song can find a second audience. Kleiner Dicker Junge was prominently featured in The Beat That My Heart Skipped, Jacques Audiard's award-winning 2005 French drama, far from the Berlin clubs, and appears on the film's soundtrack release.

## Artistic Context

Electrocute released the EP and the album on Emperor Norton; Troublesome Bubblegum was produced by Mickey Petralia with Morier. After those records Mia Dime left, and Morier carried on with Holly Doll, touring Europe, America and Australia. The first music video from the debut EP, directed by Danielle de Picciotto, was made for this song.

## Impact and Reception

mxdwn's review of the album found the music fun and the lyrics juvenile, comparing the band to Le Tigre and its words to Stereo Total. Of all the band's songs, this is the one Wikipedia names as prominently featured in a film. Listen for the two voices.
