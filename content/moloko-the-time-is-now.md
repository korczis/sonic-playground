## Genre and Mood

**Moloko**'s **The Time Is Now** is a disco song that Wikipedia counts among the duo's least electronic records: a dance track in shape, played on acoustic guitar, strings and piano. It is romantic and uplifting in its plea to seize the moment, and bittersweet in the way it ends, on a piano Freaky Trigger heard as cold and icy against the sunshine of most chart pop.

## Song Summary

Róisín Murphy and Mark Brydon wrote it, and Moloko produced it at home in Sheffield for their third album, Things to Make and Do. Brydon said they wanted a track with the structure of a dance record but kept acoustic. They could not afford full disco strings, so they hired a quartet, which he called street strings. The lyric is a plea to give up yourself unto the moment and to make it last. The album version runs 5:18; the single edit and the video are shorter.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

An acoustic guitar opens the song. Freaky Trigger called it shimmering and heard Steve Harley's Make Me Smile in it; AllMusic praised the flamenco-flavoured nylon-string guitar.

### Main Section

The quartet adds a flourish Freaky Trigger thought most pop does not understand; Murphy sings high, in a voice Wikipedia likens to the Bee Gees. Musicnotes' sheet music puts it in C major at 124 beats per minute.

### Chorus

The refrain returns to the title. Freaky Trigger wrote that its chord changes moved him right up to the fade.

### Outro

The song fades over a looping piano, chilling in Wikipedia's word, that seems as though it might stay on loop forever.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }} is the lesson here. Audrey Riley arranged the strings and plays cello, with Chris Tombling and Greg Warren-Wilson on violin and Susan Dench on viola: four players doing the work of a disco orchestra, chosen for the budget and kept for the sound.

Under the acoustic surface sits a {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} Mixmag put on its 2020 list of the best in dance music.

The single was also a set of {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} versions, from the Can 7 Soulfood mix to a nine-minute Francois K vocal mix. In 2005 Farolfi and Gambafreaks built a house record on a sample of it: {{ <track_link source="farolfi-gambafreaks-vs-moloko-a-style-suite-gambafreaks-vs-farolfi-mix.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Murphy and Brydon formed Moloko in Sheffield after meeting at a party in 1994. Things to Make and Do, released by Echo in April 2000, used fewer electronics and more live musicians than their earlier records. Boris Dlugosch's remix of their Sing It Back, a club hit before it, closes the album as a bonus track.

## Impact and Reception

Released on 20 March 2000, the song entered the UK chart at number 2, behind Melanie C and Lisa Lopes, and remains Moloko's highest-charting single. It reached 10 in Ireland and 3 in Greece, helped the album to number 3 in the UK, and was nominated at the 2001 BRIT Awards for Best British Single and Best British Video, losing both to Rock DJ. Sky Sports used it for Premier League coverage from 2004 to 2009. Start with the guitar; stay for the piano.
