## Genre and Mood

**Belleruche** placed **Reach for the Bottle** in the middle of Rollerchain, their darkest album, and it is one of its friendlier moments: trip-hop and soul with a light touch of funk, groovy and catchy, one of the tracks laut.de counted among the album's danceable, almost poppy material.

## Song Summary

laut.de heard the song as lightly funky and distantly reminiscent of Moloko, and quoted its refrain, which tells the listener to take a good look at themselves before they reach for the bottle. The French blog La Toile de Pandore heard something else: tribal rhythms, a story of bottles thrown into the sea, where it wondered whether Kathrin deBoer had slipped in the words of her songs, and a melody that would not leave. It named the track its second favourite on the album.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes Reach for the Bottle part by part, so this breakdown keeps to the elements the two reviews name.

### Rhythm

The track rests on what La Toile de Pandore called tribal rhythms, with the light funk laut.de heard in its groove.

### Vocal and Melody

Kathrin deBoer sings the song. The NZ Herald heard her voice across the album ranging from childlike to seductive, fragile to scornful; which of those she chooses here, no source says.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is the idea a song is built to stick with you, and La Toile de Pandore's word for this melody, entêtante, means exactly that: it gets into your head and stays. On an album that the NZ Herald found spacious, cold and dark, a hook like this is what keeps a listener close.

{{ <concept_link name="turntablism" /> }} is in the band's DNA. Belleruche are Kathrin deBoer on vocals, Ricky Fabulous on guitar and DJ Modest on turntables, and their debut album was called Turntable Soul Music. What the turntables play in this track, the sources do not describe, but Rollerchain was, according to Brighton Source, the record on which the trio drifted further from their earlier turntable soul into sparser, stranger songwriting.

## Artistic Context

Belleruche formed in North London in 2005 and signed in 2007 to Tru Thoughts, the Brighton label that also released {{ <track_link source="alice-russell-high-up-on-the-hook.md" /> }}; by 2012 Brighton Source called them the label's biggest-selling act. Rollerchain, their fourth album, came out on 7 May 2012, written and recorded by the three members, who are credited together as writers, producers and engineers. The label described it as darker, more bass-heavy and evocative, with the melodic, soulful touch kept.

## Impact and Reception

Reviews of Rollerchain were mixed: the NZ Herald gave it three stars and felt the band had not quite joined all the links together, while laut.de and La Toile de Pandore were warm, and both picked this track out. The band announced its end in October 2013 and new material in 2025. Pour something soft, and press play.
