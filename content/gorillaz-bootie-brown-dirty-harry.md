## Genre and Mood

**Gorillaz** and **Bootie Brown** made **Dirty Harry** sound like a party with something wrong at its centre. It sits between alternative and hip-hop: a funky beat, a children's choir carrying the tune, and a rap verse that arrives like a change of weather. It is groovy on the surface and provocative underneath; Drowned in Sound heard a strangely sinister edge in it.

## Song Summary

Damon Albarn has called it an anti-war song. The title points at the 1971 Clint Eastwood film about a gun-toting vigilante, though the words never appear in the lyric; Songfacts reads the song as aimed at George W. Bush and the Iraq War. The memorable choice is who sings: most of it belongs to a children's choir from California's San Fernando Valley, credited on the album as the San Fernandez Youth Chorus, sweet voices set against a bleak message about children in war.

## Musical Breakdown

No source maps the song section by section; this follows the parts the sources name.

### The Choir

The children open the song and carry its melody. Their first line is widely misheard as a plea for protection but describes 2-D finding refuge among the dispossessed. Beyond the Grooves calls the choir sun-drenched, which is what makes the words chilling.

### The Groove

Under the voices runs what Far Out calls a funky beat with a timeless edge, and the credits add the album's strings: violins, violas, cello and double bass.

### The Rap Verse

Then the track takes, in Beyond the Grooves' words, a sharp, aggressive turn. Bootie Brown of the Pharcyde raps, and his verse points straight at the 2003 Mission Accomplished speech. No source describes the ending.

## Concepts in Focus

Bootie Brown is a {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} who changes the song's register. On most Gorillaz tracks the second voice is Albarn as 2-D; here the choir and a rapper from South Central Los Angeles share it.

It is a {{ <concept_link name="protest song" /> }} built for the dancefloor, one of the most successful of its kind according to Beyond the Grooves. Danger Mouse told Q it began as a raw demo for which he imagined choirs, Gregorian chants and rapping.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} was the first Gorillaz video shot on location, in the Namib Desert: 2-D and an animated choir stranded after a helicopter crash, Bootie Brown leaping out of a dune in military fatigues.

## Artistic Context

Gorillaz are the virtual band of Damon Albarn and Jamie Hewlett; Demon Days, produced mainly by Danger Mouse, was Albarn's loose concept album about the world in a state of night. Dirty Harry is credited to Albarn, Hewlett, Brian Burton and Romye Robinson; an early version, I Need a Gun, appeared on Albarn's Democrazy. Compare the album's other song about children and guns, {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-neneh-cherry-kids-with-guns.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Released as the third single on 21 November 2005, it reached number six in the UK and fifteen in Australia, the band's third top-ten hit in a row, and was nominated for a Grammy for Best Urban/Alternative Performance. In 2020 it trended on TikTok. Listen to the choir first, then to what it is singing.
