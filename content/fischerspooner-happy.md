## Genre and Mood

**Fischerspooner**'s **Happy** is electro-pop and synth-pop from Odyssey, the 2005 album on which the New York duo moved from cold electroclash towards fuller songs. Its title promises one feeling and its subject delivers two: the mood is bittersweet under an energetic surface, driven by what one critic called battering percussion.

## Song Summary

Casey Spooner explained the song in the album's track-by-track notes: it is about his relationship with New York City, a place that had come to define him, a fickle beast that can take you to the heights of greatness or the depths of despair, both tragic and exhilarating. The band made several versions of it. The album cut was finished with help from the songwriter Linda Perry, who also makes a vocal cameo, and she is credited as a writer with Warren Fischer, Spooner and Nicolas Vernhes.

The {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} role on Happy is shared and overlaps with writing. The streaming credits name Fischerspooner and Vernhes as producers, and Vernhes is also a co-writer; Perry, a songwriter from outside the band, helped finish a song that already existed in several versions. Compare her role in {{ <track_link source="fischerspooner-all-we-are.md" /> }}, where she added melodies to an unfinished instrumental.

A {{ <concept_link name="soundtrack" /> }} gave the song a life beyond the album. Happy was never a single, but it was featured in the 2006 film Grandma's Boy and included on its soundtrack album, with Never Win.

## Artistic Context

Fischerspooner were formed in New York City in 1998 by Warren Fischer and Casey Spooner, who sings. Odyssey, their second full-length album, came out on Capitol on 5 April 2005 in the US and on 11 April elsewhere, made with Mirwais, Tony Hoffer, Vernhes and others, and with guests including David Byrne and Susan Sontag. Happy is track nine of twelve.

## Impact and Reception

Odyssey scored 70 on Metacritic and reached number two on Billboard's Top Electronic Albums. PopMatters' Patrick Schabe heard in Happy and All We Are a sort of transcendent electropop feel that moved Fischerspooner into the ranks of dance-pop royalty alongside New Order and Depeche Mode. The Honolulu Star-Bulletin treated Happy as one of the set-ups for the explosive finale. Play it loud enough to feel the percussion.
