## Genre and Mood

**The Roots**' **You Got Me - Live / 1999**, featuring Jill Scott, is hip-hop played by a band, with soul at its centre. Drowned in Sound heard it flit from gentle love poem to frenzied noise: hence romantic and intense, the two ends of the same performance.

## Song Summary

You Got Me was the single from the Roots' 1999 album Things Fall Apart. Jill Scott co-wrote it and recorded its chorus and bridge, but MCA wanted a higher-profile collaboration for the lead single, and Erykah Badu re-recorded her part; Eve rapped the second verse. Scott then joined the band on tour, and The Roots Come Alive, released on 2 November 1999 and recorded in Zürich, New York and elsewhere, captured her singing the song as its author. Drowned in Sound called the track the album's real delight, a take that morphs the Grammy-winning studio version into something truly mind-blowing.

## Musical Breakdown

No source times the performance, and this page does not guess its structure. The reviews describe its arc, from poem to noise.

### Intro

Drowned in Sound heard the song open as a gentle love poem. Scott had started her career as a spoken-word artist at Philadelphia's poetry readings; listen for how close the opening comes to that.

### Main Section

Scott sings the part she first wrote, with what the same review called gale-force hollers from a then-unknown singer. Listen to the room around her, and to the band, a hip-hop group known for playing on live instruments with Questlove on drums.

### Outro

The second half is where the review heard frenzied noise. Its ending had a life of its own: Okayplayer notes that The Roots (Interlude) on Scott's debut album borrows the outro of this live version.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="live recording" /> }} captures a performance in front of an audience rather than one built in a studio, and this one shows how far the two can drift apart. This version runs 8:50 in its digital release, and on the standard CD it shares one 17-minute track with an encore and a hidden track.

The song is also a lesson in the {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}. On record, the label chose the guest by fame: Badu was the bigger name, and Scott was barely known outside Philadelphia. On stage the credit went back to the woman who wrote the part, and the live release names her in the title.

## Artistic Context

The Roots were formed in Philadelphia in 1987 by the rapper Black Thought and the drummer Questlove, the man who discovered Scott. Things Fall Apart was their breakthrough, and You Got Me won the Grammy for Best Rap Performance by a Duo or Group. The Roots Come Alive reached number 50 on the Billboard 200 and number 12 on the R&B and hip-hop albums chart.

## Impact and Reception

Scott's debut, Who Is Jill Scott?, came out in 2000 and made her a leading voice of poetry-influenced R&B; Okayplayer argues that her version of You Got Me reigns supreme. This live take is where you can hear why. Give it the full nine minutes, loud.
