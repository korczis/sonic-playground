## Genre and Mood

**Kylie Minogue**'s **Can't Get Blue Monday out of My Head** joins two of the most recognisable records in dance-pop and synth-pop: her own 2001 hit and New Order's Blue Monday from 1983. It is a remix of a particular kind, a mashup, and its mood is the sum of its parents: the hypnotic pull of a cool, repeating hook and of a beat first released in 1983.

## Song Summary

The mashup was first heard on television. At the Brit Awards on 20 February 2002, Kylie Minogue performed a version of Can't Get You Out of My Head combined with Blue Monday, remixed by Stuart Crichton. The studio recording followed that year as a B-side of her single Love at First Sight, at 4:01, and opened the bonus disc of Fever's special edition. MusicBrainz credits all six writers of both songs: Cathy Dennis and Rob Davis on one side, Bernard Sumner, Peter Hook, Stephen Morris and Gillian Gilbert on the other.

### Main Section

Kylie Minogue's lead vocal carries the song she sang in 2001. Underneath, the arrangement draws on Blue Monday, a record built on an Oberheim DMX drum machine and a synth bassline sequenced on a machine Bernard Sumner built. The credits add electric guitar and keyboard by Rob Davis, who also programmed the drum machine, and programming by Crichton.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="mashup" /> }} lays parts of two existing recordings on top of each other, usually the vocal of one over the instrumental of another. Wikipedia counts this Brits performance among the first by a mainstream recording artist to use one, in the same years as Soulwax's 2 Many DJs album and Richard X's bootlegs.

As a {{ <concept_link name="b-side" /> }}, it was the extra track on the Australian, Benelux, French and British CD singles of Love at First Sight. Christopher Smith of Talk About Pop Music notes that it went on to become the version used on the Fever2002 world tour.

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} usually rebuilds one song; this one rebuilds two. Compare it with {{ <track_link source="flunk-blue-monday.md" /> }}, where Blue Monday is slowed into a ballad.

## Artistic Context

Fever, released by Parlophone on 1 October 2001, was her eighth studio album, and on the Brits night it won Best International Album while she won Best International Female Solo Artist. Her performance began, as HuffPost UK recalled twenty years later, with her being ejected from an oversized CD player. In 2008 the mashup opened Boombox, Parlophone's collection of her remixes.

## Impact and Reception

In 2011 The Guardian ranked the Brits performance number 40 in its series of the 50 key events in the history of dance music. HuffPost UK called it one of the most memorable Brits performances of all time. Play it next to both originals and listen to how little each song has to give up.
