## Genre and Mood

**Gorillaz** recorded **19-2000** for their 2001 debut album, and this is that original version, the mix the first music video was cut to. Wikipedia files it under trip-hop, dub and funk: alternative pop made from loops, synths and borrowed voices. The mood is playful and a little quirky, a cartoon band singing a song that never quite explains itself.

## Song Summary

Damon Albarn and Jamie Hewlett, the musician and the artist who invented Gorillaz as a virtual band of four animated characters, wrote it. Albarn sings the verses as the band's singer 2-D and plays synthesizers and guitar. The credits add a small, unlikely cast: Dan the Automator, the album's producer, with sampled loops and extra synths; Miho Hatori of Cibo Matto and Tina Weymouth of Talking Heads on backing vocals; Weymouth and her bandmate Chris Frantz on percussion. The single came with a completely new version, the Soulchild Remix, and comparing the two is the best way in.

## Concepts in Focus

The clearest lesson here is the {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }}. Damien Mendis and Stuart Bradbury, working as Soulchild, were given the album's demos and finished tracks and asked to turn one into a hit single. Play this page, then {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-19-2000-soulchild-remix.md" /> }}, and listen for what survived: the melody, the words, the voices.

The album version rests on a {{ <concept_link name="loop" /> }}, a passage repeated seamlessly. The credits give Dan the Automator the sampled loops; Albarn's synths, guitar and voice and the guests' percussion are laid over that floor.

Gorillaz were built around the {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}. Directed by Hewlett and Pete Candeland in hand-drawn and computer animation, it sends the band down a twisting highway in their jeep, through a loop-the-loop and past a UFO, until a giant moose sneezes Murdoc's missiles back at them. Road signs point to Amity, the Overlook Hotel and Camp Crystal, from Jaws, The Shining and Friday the 13th. Of the two cuts, one per mix, this is the original.

## Artistic Context

Albarn recorded the album between 1998 and 2000 at his Studio 13 in London and at Geejam Studios in Port Antonio, Jamaica. Parlophone released Gorillaz in the UK on 26 March 2001 and 19-2000 as its second single on 25 June. The Singles Collection 2001–2011 later carried both this version and the Soulchild Remix.

## Impact and Reception

The single reached number 6 in the UK, number 1 in New Zealand and number 23 on Billboard's modern rock chart, and went gold in both countries. Trouser Press counted it among the album's more agreeable synth-pop songs, and Stereogum, looking back in 2021, heard its bright pop as something that could not have lived inside Blur. Hear it before the remix, as the album first offered it.
