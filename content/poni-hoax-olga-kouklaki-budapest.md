## Genre and Mood

**Poni Hoax** and **Olga Kouklaki**'s **Budapest** is indie dance with disco in its spine and electro-pop on its surface: groovy enough for the clubs that made it a hit, dark and haunting enough to sound like a ghost story. We Love Budapest wrote that it felt retro then and feels retro now.

## Song Summary

Budapest is a song about the Hungarian capital from a Paris band, full of local detail, an iced game of chess and Tokaj wine running in the veins. Laurent Bardainne composed it and Guillaume Teyssier wrote the words, and the voice is not the band's singer Nicolas Ker but the Greek singer Olga Kouklaki, then on the same label, Tigersushi. The page plays the 2025 remaster of the version on the band's 2006 debut album.

## Musical Breakdown

The sources describe the song's parts rather than its structure, so this breakdown goes part by part.

### The Bassline: A Disco Beat

The foundation is the bassline, which Bardainne called a disco beat; in his telling, that line and the band's biography are why people call Poni Hoax Italo disco.

### The Band: Two Keyboards and a Violin

Bardainne and Arnaud Roulin play piano, Nicolas Villebrun guitar and Vincent Taeger drums, with a violin by Viviane Hélary; Joakim mixed it at Studio Juno.

### The Hook: A Keyboard Refrain

We Love Budapest singles out a catchy keyboard refrain that always gets people out on the dance floor.

### The Voice: A Ghost in the Room

Kouklaki's ghostly vocal carries the song; how its five and a half minutes end is not described in any source we found.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} decides the genre here. Bardainne's own account is that Budapest's bass line plays a disco beat, and that, with what the band's biography says, is why a band of conservatoire-trained musicians gets filed under Italo disco.

Kouklaki is a {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}, and an unusual one: she does not add a guest verse but is the song's lead singer. A 2006 blogger was sure there was a female voice on Budapest, yet knew Poni Hoax as a five-piece band of men.

The upload is the result of {{ <concept_link name="remastering" /> }}, done by Adrien Pallot at ChabMastering for the 2025 compilation Greatest Hits: Everything Is Real. A remaster leaves the parts and their balance alone; compare {{ <track_link source="poni-hoax-the-wild.md" /> }}, remastered for the same reissue.

## Artistic Context

Poni Hoax formed in Paris in 2001 around Bardainne, a saxophonist, keyboardist and composer; its instrumentalists had met at the Conservatoire and recruited Ker in a bar. Budapest first appeared on a 2005 EP, then on the 2006 debut album, produced by Joakim for his label Tigersushi, a record of electro-rock with what one listener heard as hints of Italo disco, garage, noise and murder ballads.

## Impact and Reception

Budapest made Pitchfork's Top 100 of 2006 and became a club hit across Europe, while the album itself sold poorly. The band has since been remixed many times by DJs; Budapest's own EP already carried Joakim's Italo Dub. Play it late and let the chess game go cold.
