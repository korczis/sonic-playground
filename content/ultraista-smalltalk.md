## Genre and Mood

**Ultraísta** make **electro-pop** with an experimental edge, and **Smalltalk** is their most direct song: playful, synth-driven and quietly insistent. Its words push against empty conversation, but the music invites you to move.

## Song Summary

"Smalltalk" was the first single from Ultraísta's self-titled debut, released on 9 May 2012 ahead of the album. Its refrain asks someone to stop the small talk, and the song builds that plea over layered synthesizers, bass and percussion. The video embedded here is Four Tet's remix, which Fact described as a garage shuffle with amped-up percussion that rearranges the original's synth layers into a build.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro: Setting the Scene

The original opens with a rhythmic pulse and synthesizer textures; in the remix, Four Tet's shuffling percussion sets the tone.

### Verse 1: Playful Lyrics

Laura Bettinson's light, rhythmic vocal enters, asking not to hear any more empty talk around the room.

### Chorus: Catchy and Uplifting

The title phrase returns as the hook, simple enough to stick after one listen.

### Bridge: Textural Exploration

The middle explores synthesizer textures, the layers that Four Tet later reorganised for his remix.

### Verse 2 and Outro: Continuation of Themes

The second verse turns to starting again, and the song ends on its refrain.

## Concepts in Focus

The version you hear is a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }}. Four Tet (Kieran Hebden) took the original's parts and rebuilt them as a garage-shuffle track that builds towards a climax of collision and juxtaposition, in the direction of his album Pink. The remix was first released as an iTunes bonus with the album and later appeared on its deluxe edition with versions by Matthew Dear, Crewdson, Justin Martin and Sasha. For another indie single remade by a dance producer, compare {{ <track_link source="metronomy-love-letters-soulwax-remix.md" /> }}.

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is the refrain itself, the request to stop the small talk. A remix can change everything around it, the tempo feel, the drums, the order of events, and still keep the song recognisable through that one phrase.

The colour of the song comes from the {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }}: Bettinson and Godrich both play synths in the band, and the original stacks them in layers over Joey Waronker's drums and percussion. Those layers are exactly what Four Tet pulled apart and rearranged.

## Artistic Context

Ultraísta began as a collaboration between Nigel Godrich, best known as Radiohead's producer, and the drummer Joey Waronker, both later of Atoms for Peace; the singer and synth player Laura Bettinson joined in 2010. The name comes from ultraism, a Spanish literary movement, and the band describe their music as conceived from a love of afrobeat, electronica and art. Their debut album *Ultraísta* came out on 2 October 2012 on Temporary Residence, produced by Godrich; Smalltalk is its seventh track.

## Impact and Reception

The album received generally favourable reviews, with a Metacritic score of 64. Smalltalk became the band's calling card largely through its remixes, which were collected again on a remix album in 2013 and on the 2019 deluxe edition.
