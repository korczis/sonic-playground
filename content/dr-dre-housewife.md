## Genre and Mood

**Dr. Dre**'s **Housewife** is West Coast hip-hop from the last stretch of his 1999 album 2001, a G-funk rap track with two guests. It is filed as provocative and gritty, and critics agree why: its subject, a warning about women and marriage, has aged worst on the album.

## Song Summary

Housewife is the 19th track of 2001, credited to Dr. Dre with Kurupt and Hittman, and runs 4:02. Edge Induced Cohesion summed it up as pessimistic advice about relationships or marriages with people who have no loyalty. The label's credits name five writers: Andre R. Young, Melvin Charles Bradford, Ricardo Brown Jr., Brian Bailey and Tracy Lynn Curry, the given name of The D.O.C., whom Wikipedia credits with lyrics for the album. Wikipedia also notes that the song interpolates Bitches Ain't Shit from The Chronic.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes the beat or gives times, so this breakdown stays with what the reviews say about each part.

### The Hook

Okayplayer's Peter A. Berry called it a serviceable hook.

### The Verses

Dre's own verse is the song's best-reviewed moment. Berry called it fine enough; Soul In Stereo's Edd Bowser went further, saying the song may have Dre's best flow on the album. Kurupt and Hittman share the rest, two of the voices that recur across 2001.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} can be a co-owner of a song. Kurupt is billed as a guest here, yet the same song also appears on his own album, Tha Streetz Iz a Mutha, released on the same day as 2001.

That makes it an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }} worth comparing. On Kurupt's record it is titled Ho's a Housewife, credited to Kurupt with Dr. Dre and Hittman, produced by Dre and listed at 4:44 against 4:02 here. The two releases are not one file pressed twice.

An {{ <concept_link name="interpolation" /> }} re-performs part of an older song instead of sampling its recording. Here the older song is Bitches Ain't Shit, the 1992 closing track of The Chronic, on which Kurupt rapped with Dre, Snoop Doggy Dogg and Daz. Seven years later, the borrowed material ties the new song to the old one and to the same attitude.

## Artistic Context

2001 came out on 16 November 1999 on Aftermath and Interscope, produced by Dr. Dre and Mel-Man and recorded in 1998 and 1999. Hittman appears on at least eight of its tracks, and Wikipedia names him among the ghostwriters behind some of Dre's lyrics. Hear the album's next track, {{ <track_link source="dr-dre-ackrite.md" /> }}, which comes at the same subject from another angle.

## Impact and Reception

Housewife was not a single, and critics are split on it. Okayplayer's 2024 ranking placed it 16th and found it mostly indistinct on an album that, as Berry put it, already deals with this topic, a very sexist one, way too much. In Soul In Stereo's retrospective, Bowser ranked it 7th of 17 and called it good from a technical standpoint but pretty cringe to sit through; Ronald Grant placed it 14th. Listen for Dre's flow, and decide what the words cost it.
