## Genre and Mood

**Mélissa Laveaux** wrote **My Boat** herself and recorded it with two accompanists: folk and singer-songwriter music at its most acoustic, a guitar, a voice, tabla and double bass. The mood is intimate and warm. Reviewing the album in 2009, Muzzart heard its first two songs as simple duets of guitar and voice, and praised a minimal production that lets the melodies weave a web of softness.

## Song Summary

My Boat is the second of twelve tracks on Camphor & Copper and, at 4:19, one of its longer songs. The credits are short: Laveaux on guitar and voice, Rob Reid on tabla, Martin Gamet on double bass. The same review said these opening songs express small things precisely and with restraint. No source discusses the lyric, so the words are left for you to hear; what the sources do describe is how little the song needs.

## Concepts in Focus

Wikipedia singles out Laveaux's percussive {{ <concept_link name="fingerstyle guitar" /> }}: plucking with the fingers rather than strumming with a pick, so one player can sound a bass note, a chord and a rhythmic accent at once. Muzzart compared her playing on the album to the early, stripped-down Keziah Jones. On My Boat, the guitar is the song's foundation.

The {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }} explains why a critic could hear a duet where the credits list a trio. Tabla, the North Indian pair of hand drums, and double bass are added around the guitar rather than over it. Reid also co-produced Laveaux's first, independent version of the album. Gamet plays the double bass on {{ <track_link source="melissa-laveaux-needle-in-the-hay.md" /> }} too, the album's Elliott Smith cover, where drums and claps push much harder.

## Artistic Context

Laveaux was born in Montreal in 1985, is of Haitian descent and studied ethics at the University of Ottawa. Camphor & Copper first came out on Malleable Records in 2006; a Lagardère Talent bursary in 2007 paid for a new recording, made with Lisa Patterson at Imaginit Music in Toronto, mixed by Bénédicte Schmitt at Labomatic in Paris and released by No Format! in November 2008. An edition with bonus tracks followed. Her later records moved towards producers' arrangements, as on {{ <track_link source="melissa-laveaux-generous-bones.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Muzzart found the album uneven but praised these opening songs, and Paperblog called the record original and impressive, mixing maturity and freshness. The song outlived the album's cycle: in 2012 Laveaux performed it in Cutlass, a performance art piece made with Andil Gosine, and No Format! put it on its YouTube channel in 2014. Listen once for the guitar, then again for the two players you may have missed.
