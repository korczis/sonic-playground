## Genre and Mood

**D12** recorded **40 Oz.** for their second album and used it to try on crunk, the shouted, club-oriented strand of Southern hip-hop. It is rap at its most gritty and aggressive, intense rather than quick: the Detroit News called it bombastic, and a Sputnikmusic reviewer heard a fight song built on a slow, oppressive beat.

## Song Summary

The title names the forty, the 40-ounce bottle of malt liquor that, as Wikipedia notes, rap songs have long name-checked. The beat comes from the Trackboyz, a production team from St. Louis, and the writing credits name four members of the group, Eminem, Kuniva, Bizarre and Proof, alongside two of the Trackboyz, Mark Williams and Joe Kent. Eminem co-mixed the track with Steve King. The album version runs 4:02.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the track bar by bar; this follows what its reviewers singled out.

### The Beat

Loud, looming bass drums form the backbone, in the Sputnikmusic review's words. The same reviewer set it against Get My Gun on the same album, which he heard as fast and hard-hitting where this one is slow and oppressive.

### The Verses

He heard the song as a coming-out party for the members other than Eminem, who all perform excellently in his account, while he found Eminem's own verses below his usual level.

### The Chorus

The chorus is the part he said really shines: heavy, oppressive, and made to be shouted back.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="rapping" /> }} in a six-man group works like a relay: each MC brings a different voice and flow to the same beat. On 40 Oz. the slow tempo leaves each of them space, which is how the review could hear the less famous members step forward. Compare one rapper carrying a whole track in {{ <track_link source="busta-rhymes-break-ya-neck.md" /> }}.

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} here is a chant more than a melody. Crunk, as Wikipedia describes it, favours shouted vocals, often in call and response, and the chorus is the part reviewers noticed first.

A {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} can define a track's sound before anyone raps on it. The Trackboyz, whose credits include J-Kwon's Tipsy and the St. Lunatics' Air Force Ones, built this beat for a Detroit group, and two of them share the writing credit.

## Artistic Context

D12 came out of Detroit with Eminem as their de facto leader, alongside Proof, Bizarre, Kuniva, Swift and Kon Artis (Denaun Porter). D12 World came out on Shady and Interscope on 27 April 2004, with producers ranging from Dr. Dre and Kanye West to the Trackboyz. The album also mourns Bugz, the member they lost before their debut, on Good Die Young; Proof was killed two years later, in 2006.

## Impact and Reception

D12 World entered the Billboard 200 at number one with about 544,000 copies in its first week and also topped the British and Australian charts, though reviews were mixed. 40 Oz. was not one of its two singles, but it was paired with My Band on a 2004 promotional record, and the deluxe edition's bonus DVD carried an uncensored music video. Play it loud and listen to the space between the drums.
