## Genre and Mood

**Gray** made **One Spliff** for his first EP, and it lives where drum and bass meets its own past: jungle, and the reggae and dancehall that jungle grew from. It is energetic by design. UKF called it a track full of energy, and Gray has said he leans towards the energetic side of the genre.

## Song Summary

One Spliff is the second of five tracks on the Rubadub EP, released by the Bristol label Born On Road on 13 December 2019 under the catalogue number BORN026, and it runs 4:27. The EP opens with Blood, featuring the Ragga Twins, and ends with Get to the Chopper. Gray told Goat Shed that One Spliff almost did not come out: Born On Road's founders Kelvin 373 and Aries loved his first version, he went away and made the tempo faster and more energetic, they liked the new take less, and he stood by it. The label backed him and released it, and it did well.

## Concepts in Focus

Drum and bass is built on the {{ <concept_link name="breakbeat" /> }}: syncopated drum patterns taken from, or written in the style of, the breaks of funk and soul records, played fast. UKF heard One Spliff and the whole Rubadub EP as a reminder of where the genre comes from, which in jungle means breakbeats meeting the bass weight and vocal style of reggae and dancehall.

It is also a product of {{ <concept_link name="club culture" /> }} in its rawest form. Gray was taken to a squat rave at fifteen and, before he turned eighteen, was booked to play them almost every weekend; later he found rollers and jungle nights, and Bristol's free parties. He told UKF that music always finds a way to bring people together.

## Artistic Context

Gray learned to produce in Fruity Loops from YouTube tutorials, studied at BIMM in London and then dBs in Bristol, which brought him to Born On Road. The label, a Bristol collective formed in late 2012 by Kelvin 373 and Aries, fuses jungle and rollers with MC culture and ragga. Gray made the EP in Bristol, where, he says, there is a big Jamaican population and you are exposed to a huge melting pot of music.

## Impact and Reception

Gray has said that One Spliff and Rubadub made him: their success let him pursue music full-time, and he spoke of having passed ten million streams. A Rubadub remix EP followed, with L-Side, Alcemist and DJ Limited. Turn it up and feel the tempo he fought for.
