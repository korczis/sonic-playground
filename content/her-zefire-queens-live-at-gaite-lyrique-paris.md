## Genre and Mood

**Queens - Live At Gaité Lyrique, Paris** is **Her** and **Zefire** playing Queens to a Paris audience, and the song becomes a long, soulful, groovy stretch of indie pop and R&B. This is not the studio track: it was recorded in front of a sold-out room at La Gaîté Lyrique and runs about seven minutes, nearly twice as long.

## Song Summary

Queens first appeared as a studio song with ZéFire in late 2016, with a video directed by Axel Morin, and belongs to Her Tape #2, the EP released in April 2017. A Milwaukee blog heard elements of "electronica, hip hop, jazz, and countless other influences" in it and "quality pop" from ZéFire. Her Live Tape #2, released on 30 June 2017, gathers seven songs from the Gaîté Lyrique concert; Queens closes it, after covers of "A Change Is Gonna Come" and "Let's Stay Together". The credits name Her as writers, singers and producers, Zefire as lyricist and vocalist, and Catherine Vigon and Anaelle Solet on background vocals; Michael Declerck mixed it.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="live recording" /> }} captures a band in front of people rather than building a track in the studio, and it often differs from the record. Queens shows the most measurable difference, its length. Her had already been changing the song on stage: in a live video from the Moth Club in London, premiered by Gigwise in March 2017, a "playful live version" opens with an a cappella intro before the full band comes in. The Paris recording is a separate performance, but the London video shows how freely the duo treated the song.

The song is also a case of the {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}. Queens is Her's song with Zefire's voice and words, credited to both. The venue's archive lists the evening itself as "her + ZéFIRE", so the guest is part of the concert's identity, not only the record's.

## Artistic Context

Her were Victor Solf and Simon Carpentier, a duo from Rennes formed in 2015 by two singers who had met in high school. The Gaîté Lyrique described them as mixing soul and pop "to claim their love for women", quoting their aim: "We're trying to achieve 1+1=3, that third person being Her". The concert, which the venue's archive dates to Tuesday 11 April 2017, came days after Her Tape #2; studio versions from the tapes include {{ <track_link source="her-jeanie-j.md" /> }} and {{ <track_link source="her-five-minutes.md" /> }}. Carpentier died of cancer in August 2017, and Solf ended the band after the tour.

## Impact and Reception

Her Live Tape #2 kept a moment the duo could not repeat: within months Carpentier had died. Listen to Queens as the sound of a band at full stretch in front of a sold-out Paris crowd.
