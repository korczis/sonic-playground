## Genre and Mood

**Orange Blossom**'s **Cheft El Khof** carries an Arabic title that the band glosses as I've Seen Fear. It sits between world music and electronic production, or, as the Moroccan weekly Finances News put it, somewhere between trip-hop and progressive rock. The feelings named here, haunting and intense, follow that title more than any review: no critic we found describes this track on its own.

## Song Summary

Cheft El Khof is the third track of Everything Must Change, the band's second album, released in France on 28 February 2005 by Bonsaï Music, with a British edition in May 2006. At 6:52 it is one of the album's long pieces. MusicBrainz credits the recording to a large cast: Leïla Bounous on lead vocals, Arnaud Fradin on slide guitar, Pasco on bass guitar, Fatoma Dembélé on percussion, Mathias Vaguenez as arranger, and Carlos Robles Arenas with Éric Chauvière as producers and mixers, Chauvière also as recording engineer. No source prints or translates the lyric, so this page does not paraphrase it.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }} is where a band like this is made or lost: deciding who plays what, and when, when the instruments come from different traditions. Here an arranger is credited separately, the percussionist Mathias Vaguenez, which tells you that the setting of the song for its players was a job in its own right.

The {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} is the other credit to notice. Carlos Robles Arenas, the band's drummer, produced and mixed the track with Éric Chauvière, so the person who shapes the final sound is also one of its players. ## Artistic Context

Orange Blossom formed in Nantes in 1993 around the violinist Pierre-Jean Chabot and the singer Jay C (Jean-Christophe Waechter), and named itself after the fiddle tune Orange Blossom Special. Carlos Robles Arenas, born in Mexico, joined on drums, djembé and samples in 1995, and the first album followed in 1997 on Prikosnovénie. Leïla Bounous, whom Finances News describes as an Algerian-Breton singer, arrived with Mathias Vaguenez, and left after the tour for Everything Must Change.

## Impact and Reception

Everything Must Change reached number 141 on the French album chart, and Songlines later recalled that it was released to international acclaim, took the band on tours of the Maghreb and Europe and onto support slots with Robert Plant. Then the band went quiet for about ten years, returning with a new singer, Hend Ahmed Hassan, on Under the Shade of Violets. The band posted Cheft El Khof on its own channel in 2016, eleven years on.
