## Genre and Mood

**Kelis**'s **"Trick Me"** is an R&B single cut with reggae and funk: critics heard a reggae romp, a skank and a funk ditty in the same three and a half minutes. The mood is defiant and playful, a warning delivered with a grin.

## Song Summary

Dallas Austin wrote and produced "Trick Me", the only name in its writing credit. It was recorded at DARP Studios in Atlanta, O'Henry in Burbank and Chalice in Hollywood, with Tony Reyes on guitar and background vocals, Greg "Ruckus" Andrews credited as DJ and Kevin Davis mixing. It followed "Milkshake" as the second single from Tasty, released on 17 February 2004; "In Public" with Nas had first been planned for that slot.

## Musical Breakdown

No source we found walks through the track section by section or describes how it opens and ends; what follows is what the critics heard.

### The Guitar

Scott Plagenhoef of Pitchfork describes "a skanking, slightly echoed guitar", the part that gives the track its reggae and ska feel.

### The Voice

Over that guitar, in Plagenhoef's account, Kelis advises the listener to call the police, because there is a madwoman in town. The Independent heard the whole thing as a "sprightly canter".

### The Production

Tony Naylor of NME called it "a wonderful computer-processed reggae romp", Blender's Joseph Patel a "crackling funk ditty" and Neil Drumming of Entertainment Weekly "a blistering sawtooth skank".

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} can shape an album as much as its singer. Most of the talk around Tasty was about the Neptunes, who made {{ <track_link source="kelis-milkshake.md" /> }}; "Trick Me" is Dallas Austin's, and Pitchfork wrote that he "punches above his weight" with it.

The skank that critics keep naming is a guitar chop on the {{ <concept_link name="off-beat" /> }}, the space between the main beats that ska and reggae fill. Heard inside an R&B single, it makes the track bounce rather than drive.

That guitar is also "slightly echoed": a {{ <concept_link name="delay" /> }} repeats each chop a moment later, so the part fills more rhythm than it plays.

## Artistic Context

Kelis arrived in Britain in 1999 and, as NME put it, took chances with producers such as Richard X and Timo Maas rather than chasing mainstream acceptance in America. Tasty, her third album, came out on Star Trak and Arista in December 2003. Arista then folded and she moved to Jive, so "Trick Me" was never released commercially in the United States, though it was sent to American radio; in Europe she stayed with Virgin and the singles continued.

## Impact and Reception

"Trick Me" entered the UK chart at number 2, behind Frankee's "F.U.R.B.", topped the charts in Hungary and Romania and reached the top five in Ireland, Australia and New Zealand, going platinum in Australia. It outpeaked "Milkshake" in Austria, Belgium, France, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands and Switzerland, and became her highest-peaking solo single in Germany, France, Italy and Switzerland. Put it on and follow the guitar.
