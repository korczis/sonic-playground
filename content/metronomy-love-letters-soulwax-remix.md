## Genre and Mood

**Metronomy**'s **Love Letters (Soulwax Remix)** takes a bittersweet, sixties-flavoured pop song and turns it into a long-form piece of electronic disco. In the hands of Soulwax it becomes groovy, hypnotic and, by the end, genuinely euphoric, a remix that respects the song while dragging it firmly onto the dancefloor.

## Song Summary

The original is a wistful tune about waiting for words from someone far away, all brass, organ and soft harmonies. Soulwax keep Joseph Mount's vocal and the melody but rebuild everything around it: a precise, machine-tight groove, sparkling arpeggios, punchy drums and their trademark sense of tension and release. The memorable detail is how long they make you wait for the hook, which turns its arrival into a small explosion.

## Musical Breakdown

At nearly six and a half minutes, the remix moves in long, carefully staged chapters.

### Intro

A crisp drum pattern and a pulsing synth bass lay down a clean, metronomic groove, with hints of the melody flickering in the background.

### Build-up

Arpeggiated synths begin to spiral and percussion fills in the gaps, the track slowly winding itself tighter.

### Verse 1

Mount's voice appears, cool and slightly detached against the glossy machinery, the song's melancholy still intact.

### Chorus

The Love Letters hook lands over the full groove, suddenly sounding less like a lament and more like a celebration.

### Bridge

Soulwax strip the arrangement back to bass and drums, a tense, minimal passage built purely for the floor.

### Climax

All the synth layers come rushing back with the vocal hook on top, the remix's big, euphoric peak.

### Outro

The arpeggios fade and the groove keeps ticking, a DJ-friendly tail that lets the energy settle gently.

## Artistic Context

Metronomy is the project of English songwriter and producer Joseph Mount. Love Letters is the title track of the band's 2014 album, a warmer, more analogue record than its predecessors, and its original video was directed by Michel Gondry. Soulwax, the Belgian outfit of brothers Stephen and David Dewaele, also known as 2manydjs, are among the most celebrated remixers in electronic music.

## Impact and Reception

The remix became a favourite in DJ sets and on playlists, proof that a guitar-band pop song can carry the weight of a club epic in the right hands. It sits on this playground as a masterclass in patience and payoff. Give it the full six minutes, ideally loud, and wait for the hook to hit.
