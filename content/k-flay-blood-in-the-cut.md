## Genre and Mood

**K.Flay** built **Blood In The Cut** as a breakup anthem, and it plays like one: intense, cathartic and defiant from the start. It sits in alternative rock, the format that took it to the radio, while her album around it is filed as electro and pop rock, and her own roots are in hip hop. Riff Magazine called the record genre-defying, at the intersection of metal, grunge and pop.

## Song Summary

K.Flay wrote the song with JT Daly and Lena Kwan Simon, and Daly produced it. The lyric describes a breakup and its violent effects. She wrote the words and the riff in about twenty minutes in her parents' basement over Christmas, and when it came to producing the chorus, she has said, she wanted to embrace a spirit of release. The credits are compact: K.Flay sings, Seth Cummings plays guitar, and Daly plays keyboards, bass and guitar. Riff Magazine heard it as a sonic recreation of an emotional response to a breakup, a song that changes pace like someone pacing back and forth.

## Musical Breakdown

The parts below follow what Riff Magazine describes; no source gives times, so none are given here.

### Verses

The song is driven by bass, clean in the verses, with K.Flay telling the story over it.

### Chorus

The bass turns distorted and the song shifts, in the review's words, from aggressive to paranoid.

### The Guitar

At one point an electric guitar comes abruptly into the mix and escalates the chaos, the review says, so the song gets wilder rather than resolving.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="distortion" /> }} means pushing a signal until it clips and gains harmonics, the grit you hear on a rock guitar. Here the review hears it on the bass, and the contrast is the point: the same instrument sounds clean while K.Flay is narrating and dirty when the chorus breaks open. The change of sound marks the change of feeling.

A {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }} is a short repeated figure that anchors a song. K.Flay wrote this one in the same twenty minutes as the lyric, so the music and the words arrived together.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by TJ Andrade and released on 7 September 2016, follows the song's arc: K.Flay walks unscathed from an overturned car into a diner bathroom, has a breakdown at the chorus and ends levitating at the end of a dark street.

## Artistic Context

K.Flay, born Kristine Flaherty in Wilmette, Illinois, in 1985, began making music at Stanford University, signed to RCA in 2012 and left a year later. In 2016 she became the first artist on Night Street Records, Dan Reynolds' imprint at Interscope. Blood in the Cut, released on 19 August 2016, first opened her Crush Me EP and then became the lead single of her second album, Every Where Is Some Where, released on 7 April 2017. Hear the album's lighter side in {{ <track_link source="k-flay-high-enough.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

The song reached number 4 on Billboard's Alternative Airplay chart, went gold in the United States and Canada, and earned K.Flay a Grammy nomination for Best Rock Song; the album was nominated for Best Engineered Album, Non-Classical. It reached the soundtrack of XXX: Return of Xander Cage and an episode of BoJack Horseman, and Interscope released three remixes, among them {{ <track_link source="k-flay-aire-atlantica-blood-in-the-cut-aire-atlantica-remix.md" /> }}.
