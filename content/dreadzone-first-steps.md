## Genre and Mood

**Dreadzone** end their 2005 album Once Upon a Time with **First Steps**, seven minutes without a singer. Discogs files the album under breakbeat, dub and breaks, and the Belgian reggae site Rebel Base heard this track as one of the album's two instrumentals that keep the ambient flavour and film samples of the band's earlier records. Atmospheric and hypnotic are the words that suit that description; no review walks through the track itself, so this page stays close to what is documented.

## Song Summary

First Steps is the twelfth and last track of Once Upon a Time, 7:07 long. The album also opens with it, in a sense: track one is First Steps (Intro), a 57-second piece, so the record begins and ends under the same title. The album credits production, arrangement and electronics to Greg Dread and Ben Balafonic, who also mixed it, and its vocals to Earl Sixteen and MC Spee; Rebel Base counts First Steps among the instrumentals, beside Ska Con Queso. Which films its samples come from, no source says, and this review does not guess.

### The Opening Intro

The album starts with First Steps (Intro), less than a minute long, a title the listener meets again only at the very end.

### The Closing Track

Eleven tracks later, after the vocal tracks with Earl Sixteen and MC Spee, the full First Steps closes the record. Rebel Base heard the ambient textures and film samples of earlier Dreadzone survive here, while the band's sound elsewhere had grown rougher with its members' work as DJs on the English club circuit.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="dub" /> }} is Dreadzone's foundation: the Jamaican way of remaking a record at the mixing desk, stripping it toward drums and bass and opening space with echo. Discogs lists dub beside breakbeat for this album, and the band's sound system ran the Dubwiser club night in London from 1998 to 2001.

An {{ <concept_link name="ambient texture" /> }} is sound that sets an atmosphere rather than carrying a tune. On an album built around two voices, an instrumental is where that layer can come forward; it is what Rebel Base heard surviving from the band's earlier work.

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} has been part of Dreadzone from the start: their 1996 hit Little Britain borrowed a line from the 1968 film If.... The review hears film samples kept in First Steps too; compare how sampling serves a sung refrain in {{ <track_link source="dreadzone-iron-shirt.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Dreadzone formed in London in 1993 around Greg Roberts, the former Big Audio Dynamite drummer billed as Greg Dread. Once Upon a Time came out in 2005 on Functional, the independent label run by Biff Mitchell, recorded by Greg Roberts, Balafonic, MC Spee, Earl Sixteen and the guitarist Steve Roberts; the band's own Dubwiser Records has since reissued it digitally.

## Impact and Reception

First Steps was never a single and has no recorded chart life. Its place is the album's last word, an instrumental that Rebel Base singled out for keeping the older Dreadzone audible. Play the 57-second intro, then let the closing seven minutes run.
