## Genre and Mood

**Macklemore & Ryan Lewis** made **Thrift Shop** a comedy record built on a serious beat. It is pop-rap with a punchline in most lines, playful and humorous on the surface and irreverent underneath, because its target is rap's own habit of boasting about what money buys.

## Song Summary

The song praises thrift shopping: saving money and finding pieces nobody else has instead of paying for a designer logo. Macklemore called it the opposite of rap songs about spending. Ryan Lewis produced it and wrote it with him, and the Seattle singer Wanz sings the hook.

## Musical Breakdown

### The Beat

The engine is a looping horn figure, which Grantland credited to Owuor Arunga and Stereogum's Tom Breihan called an energetic, genuinely funky, chopped-up horn loop; Lewis sampled no other record. Lewis sets it against handclaps, MPC hiccups and a bit of synth churn. Grantland's Molly Lambert heard a janky klezmer beat sharing DNA with A Tribe Called Quest's I Left My Wallet in El Segundo.

### Verses and Hook

Macklemore raps the verses as a list of finds and bargains. Wanz takes the hook in a voice Breihan called a huge rumble that adds gravitas, with DJ scratching by DV One on top.

### Bridge

Wanz returns for the bridge, where, for Breihan, he really shines. No source describes how the song opens or ends.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} came together quickly. Wanz told American Songwriter that an old contact called because Lewis wanted a singer who sounded like Nate Dogg. Macklemore already had the chorus and fed him the words in rhythm; Wanz's first improvised line stayed on the record. As a {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} he supplies a sung refrain against rapped verses; compare Gwen Stefani's part in {{ <track_link source="eve-gwen-stefani-let-me-blow-ya-mind.md" /> }}.

The single was a {{ <concept_link name="self-release" /> }}: The Heist was self-produced, self-recorded and self-released by the duo through Macklemore LLC, distributed by the Alternative Distribution Alliance. Breihan adds that independence still meant hiring a Warner subsidiary to promote the song to radio.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, co-directed by Jon Jon Augustavo with Macklemore and Lewis, was shot in Seattle thrift shops and released on 29 August 2012.

## Artistic Context

Thrift Shop came out on 27 August 2012 as the fourth single from The Heist, released that October. The album debuted at number two on the Billboard 200 and won Best Rap Album at the 2014 Grammys.

## Impact and Reception

Thrift Shop topped the Billboard Hot 100 for six non-consecutive weeks from February 2013, only the second independent number one after Lisa Loeb's Stay in 1994, and was Billboard's number one song of 2013. It also reached number one in the UK and won Grammys for Best Rap Performance and Best Rap Song. Reviews split: Digital Spy called it original, musically daring and genuinely funny, Grantland found it smart without being smug, and Breihan gave it 5 out of 10, hearing Macklemore oversell his lines. In August 2013 the rapper Le1f publicly claimed on Twitter that it borrowed its beat extensively from his earlier song Wut. Listen for how much of the comedy that horn carries.
