## Genre and Mood

**Glasser**'s **Tremel** is art pop made from electronic parts and a voice that does not behave like a pop vocal. It sits between electro-pop and experimental music: haunting rather than sad, hypnotic rather than danceable, circling its listener instead of marching towards a chorus.

## Song Summary

Tremel is one of the first songs Cameron Mesirow wrote as Glasser. In June 2009 LA Weekly already named it among the sister songs of her first release, Apply, and described it in four words: Kate Bush meets Cluster. Those songs were composed in Apple's GarageBand, her voice layered over synth sounds and ready-made percussive loops, with the closeness of a bedroom recording and hints of something grander. The version reviewed here is the single Young Turks released on 29 March 2010, about three minutes long; a longer version, three minutes and forty-six seconds, became the sixth track of her debut album Ring that September.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the single section by section, so this guide follows what critics heard in the song.

### The Beat

DIY's review of Ring picked out the thick bass and tribal percussion of Tremel. The low end and the drums are where to start listening.

### The Voice

Treble heard a sinister kind of ambience return with Tremel, and Mesirow's watery imagery growing more eerie. Across Ring, The Line of Best Fit called her voice the standout instrument and heard some of its textures as her own voice overlapped on itself.

### Single and Album

The single credits Mesirow with Ariel Rechtshaid, Daniel Hart, David Moyer and Garrett Ray as performers. On Ring the song runs longer, on an album produced by Glasser, Rechtshaid and the Swedish duo Van Rivers and the Subliminal Kid.

## Concepts in Focus

LA Weekly's portrait explains the song's starting point: {{ <concept_link name="lo-fi" /> }} not as hiss and tape noise but as the intimacy of music made alone at home, on software that came with a Mac. Their structure, the paper wrote, grew slowly out of the trance Mesirow cultivated in her headphones.

That trance rests on the {{ <concept_link name="loop" /> }}. GarageBand's prefab percussive loops repeat without a seam, and her songs grow on top of that repetition. Pitchfork, quoted on Wikipedia, called her songs circular, swooshing around without pausing at verse-chorus intersections.

The single also shows the {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} at work: beside the original it carries reworkings by Romantic Three, Jamie xx and Astronomer, the last over nine minutes long. Jamie xx's beat had already shaped {{ <track_link source="the-xx-crystalised.md" /> }} on the same label.

## Artistic Context

Mesirow was born in Boston and grew up in the San Francisco Bay Area; at San Francisco State University she met the singer Matt Popieluch, who helped draw out her songwriting. Her first EP, Apply, came out on True Panther Sounds in May 2009. Ring followed in September 2010, recorded in Los Angeles and Stockholm.

## Impact and Reception

Ring reached number nine on the US Dance/Electronic Albums chart and drew a Metacritic score of 78. Treble called its music immersive, embracing the listener with fluid warmth, and heard Tremel as its eerie turn. Start with the single, then the Jamie xx remix, and listen for what survives.
