## Genre and Mood

**Future Forward** and **Mount Sims** team up on **Time for Livin**, a long electro track from the tail end of the electroclash years. It is electronic music with a strut: hypnotic, carefree and groovy, a song that treats the dancefloor as the only place worth being.

## Song Summary

The title is the whole manifesto. Over six and a half minutes, a steady machine pulse and chilly analogue-style synths lay down a groove that barely changes and never needs to, while Mount Sims delivers the vocal in the cool, half-detached manner that made his name. The song is less about a story than about a state of mind, the moment when a party stops being an event and becomes a way of life.

## Musical Breakdown

Like much electro of its era, the track is built for DJs, with long sections that shift by adding and removing layers.

### Intro

A bare drum machine pattern and a simple bassline set the tempo, mechanical and steady, leaving plenty of room for a mix.

### Build-up

Synth lines slide in one by one, a riff here, a pad there, until the groove has a body and a swagger.

### Verse 1

Mount Sims enters with his deadpan, slightly sneering delivery, closer to talking than singing, riding on top of the beat.

### Chorus

The title phrase becomes the hook, repeated like a slogan over a fuller synth arrangement.

### Main Section

The longest stretch is pure groove: the beat, the bass and the synth riffs locked together in a hypnotic loop that rewards long listening.

### Outro

The layers drop away until the drum machine stands alone again, ready to hand over to the next record.

## Artistic Context

Mount Sims is the stage name of Matthew Sims, an American DJ, producer and performance artist based in Berlin, whose 2002 album UltraSex on DJ Hell's International DeeJay Gigolo label was one of the defining records of electroclash. "Time for Livin" came out on Kompute Musik, carrying a 2006 copyright, and little else is documented about Future Forward beyond this release. The track was later remixed by Italo-disco pioneer Alexander Robotnick, a fitting link to the older electronic dance music that electroclash loved to quote.

## Impact and Reception

This is not a chart record; it is a club record made for DJ sets, long nights and sound systems rather than radio. Its remixes, including Robotnick's versions and a Kompute mix, kept it circulating. It is here for its cool, unhurried swagger. Give it the full six minutes and let the loop work on you.
