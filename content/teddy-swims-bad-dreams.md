## Genre and Mood

**Teddy Swims** sings **Bad Dreams** like a man who has not slept in days. Wikipedia files it under pop-soul and pop rock: a soul voice with grit in it over a band that keeps moving. The feeling is longing and heartfelt, but the song is built to sway rather than to break down.

## Song Summary

The song is about what heartbreak does at night: the feelings that follow a break-up and leave the singer disoriented and sleepless, pleading with someone who has gone. Songfacts traces it to a writing camp in Palm Springs where Swims, in tears, admitted he was afraid to sleep because of the nightmares he has had for much of his life; he has also told People that the partner who helped him through restless nights inspired it. Producers Julian Bunetta, Matt Zara and John Ryan built it around a groove, by Swims's own choice.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} does the song's first job: it sets the mood. Starting on bass rather than on a big vocal tells you this is a dance with sorrow, not a power ballad.

It is also a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }}. A voice this large could have peaked in a shout; Swims has said he wanted a groove instead, because yelling every night on tour cannot last. The intensity sits in the rhythm rather than in volume.

Bad Dreams also lives in more than one form: an acoustic version followed in November 2024, running 2:56 against the original's 3:04, and a Hugel remix in April 2025. Each {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }} reads the same song differently.

## Artistic Context

Teddy Swims is the stage name of Jaten Dimsdale from Conyers, Georgia, whose father introduced him to Marvin Gaye, Stevie Wonder and Al Green. He built an audience with YouTube covers from 2019 and signed to Warner Records that December. Bad Dreams, released on 13 September 2024, followed his hits Lose Control and The Door and became the first single and fourth track of I've Tried Everything but Therapy (Part 2), released in January 2025.

## Impact and Reception

The song reached number six in the UK, number 30 on the Billboard Hot 100 and number two in Flanders and on the Dutch Top 40, and was certified diamond in France and triple platinum in the US. laut.de's reviewer called it sleek and super-catchy, with a timeless elegance comparable to Seal's Kiss From A Rose. Swims has sung it at the Billboard Music Awards and the 2025 Brit Awards. Start with the bassline and let it pull you in.
