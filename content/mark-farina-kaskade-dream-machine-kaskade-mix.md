## Genre and Mood

**Mark Farina** wrote Dream Machine as a quiet song, and **Kaskade** remade it for the house scene they shared as **Dream Machine (Kaskade Mix)**. The genres here are the mix's world, house and deep house, electronic music for clubs. The feelings, dreamy and melancholic, come from the song: a critic called the original "a tender, melancholy peach of a tune". No review we could find describes the Kaskade Mix itself.

## Song Summary

Dream Machine began on Air Farina, Farina's first full-length album, released by Om Records in October 2003. Sean Hayes sang it; Westword heard "Hayes's delicate tenor" on "a banjo-and-wah-guitar-complemented number", and PopMatters heard a "quirky pop-folk-rock song" by a singer who "sounds uncannily like Michael Stipe", "more suited to college radio than clubs." Om's own history adds a rumour: it was recorded in a closet at Farina's house when Hayes lived next door. The Kaskade Mix took that song to the clubs.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} can move a song between worlds. Dream Machine was the album's tender interlude in a set of grooves; in the Kaskade Mix the same song is reworked by a house producer for a house single. Compare it with the downtempo original and you hear what a remixer decides a song is really about.

The To Do single is built for the {{ <concept_link name="dj mix" /> }}: the original extended mix of To Do, Kaskade's Carry On Mix, Derrick Carter's Farina Experiment and this Dream Machine mix, each between six and seven minutes, long enough to blend.

## Artistic Context

Farina, originally from Chicago, is of the same generation as Derrick Carter and Cajmere and had a large hand in building San Francisco's house music scene; he is best known for the Mushroom Jazz series and a down-tempo but dancefloor-friendly mix of jazz, house and hip-hop. Kaskade, born Ryan Raddon in Chicago in 1971, moved to San Francisco in 2000 to work in A&R at Om Records and released his own house debut, It's You, It's Me, in 2003. PopMatters noted the support Farina got from his stablemate on the album's track To Do.

## Impact and Reception

The song travelled. Its downtempo mix appeared on Hôtel Costes 8, the series that put {{ <track_link source="the-limp-twins-sunday-driver.md" /> }} on volume 7, and Om chose Dream Machine for its 25-year retrospective in 2020: "What a tune!" Play the original and the Kaskade Mix back to back.
