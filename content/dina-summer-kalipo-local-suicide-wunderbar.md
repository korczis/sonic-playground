## Genre and Mood

**Dina Summer** with **Kalipo** and **Local Suicide** made **Wunderbar** as a homage to electroclash, the early-2000s collision of electro, synths and cool attitude, with disco underneath. The band's own album notes call it groovy and glamorous; asked by The Ransom Note for a colour, they chose glittery black, and for a season, winter. That is the mood: glamour with a dark finish.

## Song Summary

The band told The Ransom Note that Wunderbar was heavily inspired by the electroclash era, above all Miss Kittin & The Hacker's Frank Sinatra, and that it is about the superficiality of stars who want to be the centre of attention with the lights on them. Its setting, they said, is the back seat of a limousine driving through Hollywood. EVL, reviewing the album, heard a heavy, off-balance stomper with German vocals and warped synths.

## Musical Breakdown

No source we found walks through the track section by section, so this part keeps to what the reviewers and the band describe.

### The Beat

EVL's word is stomper: a heavy dance beat, and an off-balance one, that leans rather than glides.

### The Synths

Warped synths carry the colour, part of the retro palette of 80s disco, 90s electro and new wave that the album notes describe.

### The Voice

Over the top, Dina P sings in German, according to EVL, the voice of a song about being looked at.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }} is the reference point. Around 2000 it paired cold electro and synth-pop with deadpan, often spoken vocals that treated fame and fashion with irony. Wunderbar takes its model from {{ <track_link source="miss-kittin-frank-sinatra.md" /> }}, a cool portrait of celebrity excess, and brings it to a Berlin band of the 2020s.

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} sounds EVL heard are warped rather than clean, bent out of shape instead of polished, which suits a song about surfaces that do not hold up.

A {{ <concept_link name="stage persona" /> }} is the character a performer plays in front of an audience. Dina P also goes by Vamperella, and in a song about stars who need the lights on them, the line between performer and subject is part of the joke.

## Artistic Context

Dina Summer is the Berlin project of Kalipo, the Bavarian-born producer Jakob Häglsperger, once of the electro-punk band Frittenbude, and Local Suicide, the DJ couple Dina P and Brax Moody; Dina P, from Greece, was one of the first women DJs in Thessaloniki. Wunderbar first came out on 9 April 2020 on Hold Your Ground, on a single with Zig Zag, and both songs returned on the debut album Rimini, released by Audiolith on 15 July 2022, where Wunderbar runs the same 3:33.

## Impact and Reception

Wunderbar had two lives, first as a 2020 single and then as one of ten tracks on Rimini, which The Electricity Club later remembered as an Italo-flavoured debut. The band followed it with a second album, Girls Gang, in 2025. Put it on loud and picture the limousine.
