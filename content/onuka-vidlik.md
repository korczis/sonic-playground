## Genre and Mood

**ONUKA** made **Vidlik** sound like an emergency. It is electronic music with folk and world roots, built by a Ukrainian band that pairs traditional instruments with programmed beats, but there is nothing pastoral about it. The mood is intense, dark and haunting: a hard, repeating figure, warning sounds and a voice that gives orders rather than confides.

## Song Summary

Vidlik is the title track of ONUKA's 2016 EP, and the word means countdown. The EP is dedicated to the Chornobyl disaster of 26 April 1986 and its effects on Ukraine and the world; its last track, 1986, even uses a real recording of the control room. Nata Zhyzhchenko has a personal reason: her father fought the disaster, so she grew up with its stories. For the EP the band added the cimbalom, the lyre, the buhay and the theremin to their usual folk palette. On the title track The Singles Jukebox heard an aggressive electro-brass minimalism.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

The opening swarms of sound, which one critic likened to Justice, set the alarm before the main figure lands; this EP version runs about four and a half minutes, a minute longer than the music video's edit.

### Main Section

The core is a blunt, repeating synth-brass riff, which critics compared to tuba blasts, trading places with alarm-like sounds. Zhyzhchenko recites over it with what one reviewer called terrifying militance.

### Interlude

A short, vaguely operatic passage, a choral voice among them, breaks the pattern and briefly lifts the track.

### Outro

The figure returns and the track does not resolve so much as stop, abruptly, as if the count has run out.

## Concepts in Focus

Vidlik is driven by an {{ <concept_link name="ostinato" /> }}, a pattern repeated persistently while other things change around it. Here the repetition is the message: a countdown is a loop with a deadline. Listen to how little the main figure varies, and how the voice and the alarms, not the pattern, carry the drama. Compare the same nerve-wracking principle in {{ <track_link source="mount-sims-restless.md" /> }}.

That figure is also a {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }}, and an unusual one. Instead of a guitar, a rounded brass sound played on synthesizer delivers it, which ties the track to the band's trombones and French horns while keeping it electronic. Brad Shoup of The Singles Jukebox heard the synth-brass riffs alternating with alarm sounds, a call and answer between music and warning.

## Artistic Context

ONUKA was founded in 2013 by Yevhen Filatov and the singer Nata Zhyzhchenko. The name means granddaughter in Ukrainian and honours her grandfather Oleksandr Shlionchyk, a maker of folk instruments; the band's sound mixes bandura, sopilka and trembita with electronic drums and brass. Vidlik, their second EP, came out in February 2016, about two months before the disaster's thirtieth anniversary.

## Impact and Reception

The Singles Jukebox gave the track an average of 7.29 out of 10 in May 2016, with scores from 5 to 10: praise for its menace and its voice, doubts about its repetition. A year later ONUKA performed with the NAONI folk orchestra as an interval act at the Eurovision Song Contest final. Listen with the volume up and count along.
