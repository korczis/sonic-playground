## Genre and Mood

**Feist** recorded **When I Was a Young Girl** for her 2004 album Let It Die: a traditional ballad, credited on the sleeve as inspired by the Virginia singer Texas Gladden. It is folk at heart and indie pop in its company, a cover of a song nobody wrote. The mood is melancholic, as a young woman's deathbed song should be, and intense, because the arrangement leaves almost nothing between the voice and the listener.

## Song Summary

The story is old and grim. In his notes to Gladden's recordings, John Cohen places the song in the family best known as The Streets of Laredo, where a cowboy dies of a gunshot; in Gladden's version a young woman is dying, of syphilis. An Irish relative was printed as The Unfortunate Rake, and the frame later became St. James Infirmary. Feist keeps the woman's voice and the first line that gives the song its title. Gonzales arranged it, as he did most of the album.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the song part by part, so this follows what reviewers heard in it as a whole.

### The Rhythm Section

Treble's reviewer heard it as rhythmic and powerful, with little more than bass and percussion behind her; a Three Imaginary Girls review picked out the bongos. Listen for how a groove, not a guitar or a piano, keeps a ballad about dying moving.

### The Voice

With so little underneath, the voice does the work. Treble called it mighty; SPIN heard one of the album's most wistful moments, Feist describing a raucous youth.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="traditional song" /> }} has no author: it travels from singer to singer and changes on the way. The sleeve names not a writer but the singer whose version shaped this one. Alan and Elizabeth Lomax recorded Gladden at Salem, Virginia, in 1941 for the Library of Congress; she sang unaccompanied.

That makes this an unusual {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }}, one that follows a performance rather than a composition. Cohen describes Gladden's singing as restrained but intense, with blues-like flattened notes. Nina Simone had already taken the song to Carnegie Hall in 1963, on Folksy Nina.

The {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }} is where Feist and Gonzales make it theirs: where Gladden sang alone, they add a rhythm section and almost nothing else, so the old tune rides on a pulse. Compare how {{ <track_link source="nina-simone-sinnerman.md" /> }} drives a traditional-style spiritual with piano and handclaps.

## Artistic Context

Leslie Feist, born in Amherst, Nova Scotia, in 1976, sang with Broken Social Scene and toured Europe for more than two years with Gonzales. Let It Die grew from home demos she re-recorded with him; Renaud Letang engineered and mixed it at Studios Ferber in Paris in 2002 and 2003, and Arts & Crafts released it in Canada on 18 May 2004.

## Impact and Reception

Let It Die won two Juno Awards in 2005, Best Alternative Album and Best New Artist, and sold about 500,000 copies worldwide; several reviewers singled this track out among the covers. Three years later Feist and Gonzales wrote {{ <track_link source="feist-my-man-my-mood.md" /> }} together. Start here, to hear how little a song needs.
