## Genre and Mood

**Dr. Dre**'s **Big Ego's** is West Coast hip-hop from early in his 1999 album 2001, a rap track built on a beat HotNewHipHop counted among the most haunting Dre ever made. The mood is dark and confident at once: two rappers boasting, over music that sounds more like a threat than a celebration.

## Song Summary

Big Ego's is the fifth of 23 tracks on 2001, credited to Dr. Dre featuring Hittman and running 3:58. Its writers are Andre Young, Brian Bailey, Melvin Bradford, Scott Storch, Tracy Curry and Richard Bembery, and it was produced by Dr. Dre and Mel-Man, with Dre and Richard "Segal" Huredia credited for the mix. The beat samples John Barry's theme for the television series The Persuaders! and Rose Royce's Love Don't Live Here Anymore. The subject is in the title: Okayplayer summed it up as two big egos showing they have a reason to be that way.

## Musical Breakdown

No source gives times or describes the beat bar by bar, so this follows the reviews.

### Intro

On the album, Still D.R.E. runs straight into Big Ego's, so the track arrives without a gap, in the middle of 2001's opening run.

### The Beat

HotNewHipHop's Mitch Findlay called it a chilling, piano-driven classic. No source says where the two samples sit: listen for them under the piano.

### The Verses

Findlay heard a contrast between the two voices: Dre's quiet intensity against what he called Hittman's more exuberant brand of psychopathy, with Hittman's lines underlined by the sounds of a violent massacre.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} is how the beat borrows its atmosphere. John Barry's theme for The Persuaders! is a synth-laden piece of television music; Rose Royce's song is a soul record. Cut into a rap track, neither keeps its first setting: a sample here is a colour, not a quotation.

A {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} usually visits once. On 2001, Hittman is a regular, and he is also credited among the writers of the album's lyrics. Compare {{ <track_link source="dr-dre-ackrite.md" /> }}, where he carries the track.

{{ <concept_link name="rapping" /> }} is delivery as much as words. Here the same boast sounds different in two mouths, quiet in one, exuberant in the other.

## Artistic Context

2001, Dr. Dre's second solo album, came out on 16 November 1999 on Aftermath and Interscope, debuted at number two on the Billboard 200 and went six times platinum. It began Dre's work with the keyboardist Scott Storch, a writer here. NME heard tectonic funk beats and mournful atmospherics; AllMusic's Stephen Thomas Erlewine heard Dre adding ominous strings, soulful vocals and reggae to his formula.

## Impact and Reception

Big Ego's was never a single. Okayplayer ranked it ninth of the album's songs in 2024, and in Soul In Stereo's 1999 Rewind the two writers put it eighth and twelfth of 17. Edge Induced Cohesion called it "another reminder to haters that he is still armed and ready to defend himself if necessary." Play it after Still D.R.E., the way the album does.
