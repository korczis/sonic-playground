## Genre and Mood

**Gin Wigmore** slinks into a new era with **Dirty Mercy**. It is alternative rock soaked in blues and sharpened with pop, swaggering and swampy rather than loud, and the feelings it trades in are defiant, gritty and provocative, a song with a smirk and a dare in it.

## Song Summary

"Dirty Mercy" is about the push and pull of a dangerous attraction, the kind of mercy that comes with conditions. Wigmore's voice, raspy and sultry, coils around a stomping, mid-tempo groove of heavy drums, bluesy guitar and dark, modern production. She sounds in control and slightly menacing, teasing out each line as if she knows exactly how much trouble she is causing. The hook is sticky without being sweet, which suits the title perfectly.

## Musical Breakdown

The song works by tension and release, keeping its groove tight and its menace simmering.

### Intro

A heavy, stomping beat and a gritty guitar tone set a swampy, sinister mood.

### Verse 1

Wigmore enters low and close, her rasp almost a purr, drawing the listener in with a sly, knowing delivery.

### Build-up

Layers of vocals and instrumentation tighten around the beat, the tension rising towards the hook.

### Chorus

The title hook lands with a defiant snap, catchy and confrontational, her voice opening up to full power.

### Bridge

The arrangement drops back to beat and voice, a moment of swagger and suspense before the last round.

### Climax

The final chorus hits with everything at once, the drums stomping and her voice at its most ferocious.

### Outro

The groove cuts out sharply, leaving the attitude hanging in the air.

## Artistic Context

The New Zealand singer-songwriter released "Dirty Mercy" in October 2016 as the first single from her fourth album, Ivory, which followed in 2018. Written by Wigmore with Steve Rusch and Marc Orrell, and written and recorded in Los Angeles, it pointed to a sleeker, more modern sound while keeping the blues grit and growl that made her name on records like Gravel & Wine and Blood to Bone.

## Impact and Reception

The single, backed by an official video, opened a new chapter for Wigmore and set the tone for the album that followed more than a year later. It quickly became a fixture of her live sets, played on her US club tours from late 2016 through the Ivory shows, including a night at New York's Bowery Ballroom in 2018. It is here because it has more attitude in three and a half minutes than most songs manage in an album. Play it loud, preferably after dark.
