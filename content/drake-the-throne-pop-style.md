## Genre and Mood

**Drake** and **The Throne**, Kanye West and Jay-Z, meet on **Pop Style**, a hip-hop track that is all cool understatement. It is rap at its most minimal and most self-assured: confident, bold and almost hypnotic, a flex delivered in a murmur rather than a shout.

## Song Summary

"Pop Style" is about success, fame and the strange distance they create, but it never sounds excited about any of it. The beat is dark and sparse, built from deep sub-bass, dry drums and an eerie, looping synth figure, leaving huge pockets of space for the voices. Drake slides through his lines in a half-sung, half-rapped drawl, and the hook is less a chorus than a mantra. On the single version, Kanye and Jay-Z each add a verse, turning the song into a quiet summit of three of the biggest names in rap.

## Musical Breakdown

The structure is a relay between the three rappers over a beat that barely changes, and the tension comes from that stillness.

### Intro

A low, ominous synth loop and a rumble of bass set the mood before the drums kick in.

### Verse 1

Drake opens in an easy, conversational flow, trading boasts with a detached cool that makes them sound like simple facts.

### Chorus

The hook repeats a few clipped phrases over the same loop, catchy precisely because it refuses to rise.

### Main Section

Kanye's short verse brings a jolt of eccentric energy, his delivery more animated and playful against the deadpan beat.

### Bridge

Jay-Z follows with a measured, veteran verse, his calm, deliberate flow underlining the song's theme of power that does not need to shout.

### Outro

The beat runs on and fades, the loop circling until it simply stops.

## Artistic Context

"Pop Style" was released in April 2016 together with "One Dance" as the first singles from Drake's album Views, issued by Young Money and Cash Money. Its production is credited to Frank Dukes and Sevn Thomas, with additional work from Boi-1da and Noah "40" Shebib. The version with The Throne is the single; on Views itself, the song appears with a new Drake verse in place of the guests.

## Impact and Reception

Arriving on a wave of anticipation for Views, the single peaked at number 16 on the Billboard Hot 100 and reached number 33 in the UK. The guest list made headlines on its own, as a rare meeting of Drake and the duo behind Watch the Throne. It is here for its mood, the menace of a song that wins by holding back. Play it loud enough to feel the bass.
