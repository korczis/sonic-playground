## Genre and Mood

**The Prodigy**'s **Rok-Weiler** is the album at its bluntest: electronic music with rock guitars, aggressive, gritty and intense. Sonicabuse called it a massive, guitar-fuelled track dedicated to flattening the skulls of the opposition, and DIY praised the attack-dog vibe of the album's faster tracks. The pun in the title points the same way.

## Song Summary

Rok-Weiler was not released as a single, and what is known about it comes from the album's credits and its reviews. Liam Howlett wrote it with Keith Flint and with Stuart Henshall, Vincent Welch and Paul Frazer, who work as Black Futures and co-produced it. Flint is the only credited vocalist, and Rob Holliday plays guitar. No source describes its lyrics, so this page does not either.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the track in order or gives times. The reviews describe three layers.

### The Beats

Sonicabuse heard beats hewn from concrete; DIY found them crude, harsh and refreshingly powerful; The New Zealand Herald put the track among those that are brutal in their determination to attack all your senses.

### The Guitars

Sonicabuse heard the guitars set to stun, and thought the track could easily sneak onto Korn's album The Path of Totality.

### The Synthesizer

Talkhouse picked Rok-Weiler as a prime example of the album's filter sweeps on the synth.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="filter sweep" /> }} opens or closes a filter on a sound so that it moves between muffled and bright. Talkhouse heard the sweeps on this album's synths stay gloriously gooey, pledging allegiance to the sound of pre-laptop acid house, and Rok-Weiler is where it told readers to hear them. Listen for that squelch under the guitars and beats the other reviews describe.

A {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} shapes how a track sounds, and Howlett usually produces The Prodigy alone. Here Black Futures share the writing and the co-production; the same three writers are also credited on Rebel Radio, the album's third track. No source says which parts of Rok-Weiler are theirs.

## Artistic Context

The Day Is My Enemy, The Prodigy's sixth album, was written and recorded largely on tour and at the band's Tileyard Studios and released on 30 March 2015 by Take Me to the Hospital and Cooking Vinyl. Flint said the band knew they had to bust out the most "band" album they could create. Rok-Weiler is its seventh track, between {{ <track_link source="the-prodigy-wild-frontier.md" /> }} and the instrumental Beyond the Deathray. Flint died in 2019.

## Impact and Reception

The album debuted at number one in the UK. This track divided the critics who mentioned it: The List found it as lumpen as its title suggests, while DIY and Sonicabuse heard power in exactly that bluntness. Play it straight into {{ <track_link source="the-prodigy-beyond-the-deathray.md" /> }} and feel the drop in pressure.
