## Genre and Mood

**Romare** made **Down the Line (It Takes a Number)** out of other people's records, unhurriedly. It sits between downtempo and hip-hop: electronic music built from samples at a walking pace. Resident Advisor heard a sticky-slow hip-hop jam: soulful, relaxed and groovy, a track that leans back rather than pushing forward.

## Song Summary

Down the Line opens the B-side of Meditations on Afrocentrism, the twelve-inch with which the London producer Archie Fairhurst made his debut as Romare on the UK label Black Acre in March 2012. He wrote and produced it alone. The EP was conceived as a collage of African-American culture, assembled from samples of songs, speeches, voices, African music and film, and Romare described it as musical conversations between the past, present and future. This is its slow track: 88 BPM by Resident Advisor's count, with a funk guitar that Andrew Ryce called more languid than lashing. Stereofox heard a slow-dub take on experimental juke and footwork, with soul and R&B coming through the samples. No source names the records it samples, so this page does not guess them.

### Main Section

Two things carry it: a tempo slow enough to feel sticky, and the funk guitar riding over it at its own relaxed pace. Around them sit the samples in which Stereofox heard soul and R&B. Listen for how little the track needs to hurry.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} is the core of Romare's method. Ninja Tune says he began by mixing his own music with samples from second-hand records; his Bandcamp page calls his practice rooted in collage. A sampled past is set inside a new track, so the soul and R&B that Stereofox hears are quoted rather than imitated.

The {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} comes from the tempo. At 88 BPM the track sits closer to hip-hop than to the footwork around it on the EP, and the slowness gives the guitar room to stay languid.

A {{ <concept_link name="dj mix" /> }} gave the track a second life. In November 2013 Bonobo put it on his Late Night Tales mix as the ninth track; compare his own downtempo in {{ <track_link source="bonobo-bajka-days-to-come.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Fairhurst played drums and guitar through school and university and, according to Ninja Tune, studied African American visual culture. His alias comes from Romare Bearden, the African-American artist known for his collages, and the borrowing describes the music as well. Black Acre signed him in 2012 and Meditations on Afrocentrism was his first release; his debut album, Projections, followed on Ninja Tune in 2015.

## Impact and Reception

Andrew Ryce reviewed the EP for Resident Advisor on its release and called it high-intensity percussive music that did not sound like much else, though he heard its peak in The Blues (It Began in Africa) rather than here. Down the Line travelled further than that verdict suggests: Bonobo's mix carried it in 2013, and Stereofox chose it in 2014 as a throwback worth hearing again. Put it on slowly and let the guitar take its time.
