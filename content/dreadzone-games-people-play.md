## Genre and Mood

**Dreadzone** recorded **Games People Play** for their 2005 album Once Upon a Time, an American song of the late 1960s that had already been a reggae hit. Soulful and reflective fit a lyric about hypocrisy sung by a reggae veteran, but no review walks through this recording, so this page stays close to what is documented.

## Song Summary

Joe South wrote and recorded Games People Play in 1968. Wikipedia describes it as a protest song against hatred, hypocrisy and inhumanity, aimed at the way people treat each other rather than at a single war or government. It won two Grammys in 1970, including Song of the Year. Dreadzone's version is the seventh of the album's twelve tracks, 5:10 long. MusicBrainz lists it as a cover and credits the vocal to Earl Sixteen; Discogs credits Steve Roberts with guitar, bass and keyboards on it.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes this recording section by section, so this breakdown follows what the credits and the album's reviews document.

### The Song Underneath

The melody and the words are Joe South's. Inner Circle had turned them into a reggae single in 1994, a hit across Europe, so by 2005 the song already had a Jamaican accent.

### The Voice

Earl Sixteen leads it. Rebel Base, reviewing the album, singled out his velvet reggae voice on the tracks he sings.

### The Band

Around him are Steve Roberts's guitar, bass and keyboards and the electronics of Greg Dread and Ben Balafonic, who produced the album. The same review heard the band's sound grown rougher from their work as DJs on the English club scene.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} is a new recording of a song first recorded by someone else. This one carries the song from Joe South's American pop through Inner Circle's reggae to a London band whose singer grew up in Kingston.

The song is also a {{ <concept_link name="protest song" /> }} of an unusual kind. It names no enemy; it points at everyday cruelty and pretence, which is why it can be sung decades later without sounding dated. Compare the anti-war message of {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-bootie-brown-dirty-harry.md" /> }}, released the same year.

{{ <concept_link name="dub" /> }} is the sound world Dreadzone took their name from: reggae taken apart in the studio, with space and echo around the bass and drums. Discogs files the whole album under it.

## Artistic Context

Dreadzone formed in London in 1993 around Greg Roberts, formerly the drummer of Big Audio Dynamite, and Tim Bran. Once Upon a Time, their fifth studio album, came out in May 2005 on the independent Functional label. Earl Sixteen, born Earl John Daley in 1958 and raised in Kingston, had recorded for Lee "Scratch" Perry and Augustus Pablo before moving to England in 1985, and has sung with Dreadzone since 1995.

## Impact and Reception

Games People Play was not a single, and no critic wrote about it on its own; Rebel Base called the album a record for every reggae lover's collection. It was released again in 2020 on Dubwiser Records. Put on Joe South's original first, then this one, and listen for what a Kingston voice does to the same accusation.
