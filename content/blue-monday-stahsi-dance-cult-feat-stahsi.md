## Genre and Mood

**Blue Monday** and **Stahsi** are the names the streaming services give for **Dance Cult (feat. Stahsi)**, a six-minute house single released in January 2016 on the Australian label Onelove. Read the credits closely and the names trade places. Either way it is electronic house at a steady 120 beats per minute, hypnotic by design, with the nostalgia that any echo of 1983 carries.

## Song Summary

This is a cover version hidden inside its own metadata. The distributor's credits on YouTube name Blue Monday as vocalist, Stahsi as featured vocalist and New Order as lyricist and writer. The Discogs entry and an upload by another channel from the week of release name the act Dance Cult and the song Blue Monday. The likeliest reading is a club version of New Order's song by a project called Dance Cult, with Stahsi singing, whose artist and title were swapped somewhere between label and shop. The format is not in doubt: just over six minutes, made for DJs rather than radio.

At 120 beats per minute the groove sits a little below peak-time house. The original's signature is a sequenced synth bassline; a cover quotes it, bends it or replaces it, and that choice decides how familiar it feels.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} is a new recording of a song by an artist other than the original, and here the strongest evidence for one is a writing credit. Blue Monday has a long history of them: Orgy took theirs to number 56 on the Billboard Hot 100 in 1998, and Flunk, 808 State and Health recorded their own.

The {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} credit puts Stahsi in the title, so a search for the singer finds the record. Here it also shows how fragile credits are: the same names appear as artist, guest and title depending on where you look.

The {{ <concept_link name="house groove" /> }} is a fitting home for this song. New Order built Blue Monday from a drum machine and a sequencer, and its 1988 remix topped the US dance chart. A house version brings it back to the floor it was made for, as {{ <track_link source="jan-blomqvist-big-jet-plane.md" /> }} does for a folk song.

## Artistic Context

New Order released Blue Monday as a 12-inch single on Factory Records on 7 March 1983, built on an Oberheim DMX and a Moog Source driven by a sequencer Bernard Sumner made himself; it is often called the best-selling 12-inch single of all time. That machine pop fed later scenes, such as the electroclash of {{ <track_link source="fischerspooner-emerge.md" /> }}. Dance Cult came out on 28 January 2016 as a digital single on Onelove, catalogued OMG660.

## Impact and Reception

The single left almost no press trail; it lives on streaming services, its swapped credits still in place. It is here for the puzzle as much as the groove: metadata is part of how music travels. Put the 1983 original on first, then this.
