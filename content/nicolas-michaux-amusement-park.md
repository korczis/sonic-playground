## Genre and Mood

**Nicolas Michaux** wrote **Amusement Park** as a singer-songwriter's song with the blues underneath. One review heard a gentler, more melancholy blues in the manner of the Black Keys; a German critic called it a kind of poppy, Belgian kaputnik blues; Apple Music files the single under folk-rock. The feelings are melancholic and warm, the words EARMILK used for its sound, and nostalgic, as the lyric is.

## Song Summary

The lyric sets a memory of holidays in Norway, when there was time to stare at the sun for a while, against a present in which the world has become one big amusement park and the singer would rather stay in the dark. Michaux told EARMILK he began it at an upright piano in the apartment of his friend Grégoire Maus, founder of Capitane Records, singing very simple words around a few minor chords after noticing photographs of a Norwegian holiday. Highclouds read it as a song about over-urbanisation, in which even early memories are not safe from being paved over.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

By Highclouds' account the song opens on a slowly pulsating combination of guitar, bass and Wurlitzer, and that pulse anchors it to the end.

### Verse 1

Michaux sings softly over it; EARMILK hears tender vocals that drift in and out of your consciousness. Morgan Vigilante plays the drums, everything else is Michaux.

### Chorus

The refrain turns to the countryside: a tocsin ringing on the summer breeze, an orchard singing, someone kneeling beneath the trees.

### Verse 2

The second verse remembers chairs moved aside so the band could play, then a four-lane highway that splits the village in two.

### Outro

The first verse returns, holidays and amusement park, and the song ends where it began.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="guitar solo" /> }} matters because so little else moves. Highclouds describes a scornfully lamenting solo that breaks the song's tidal flow before the guitar returns to lockstep with the band; no source times it, so listen for the moment the pattern breaks and how quickly it closes again.

Amusement Park also opens a {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }}. Les Chutes, released by Capitane Records on 1 October 2021, gathers outtakes, variations and live versions from the sessions of his 2020 album Amour Colère, and Gaesteliste called this one new track brilliant.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Kevin Antoine with photography by François Dubois, was filmed in Copenhagen; EARMILK describes wandering through vacant spaces while nature pushes back.

## Artistic Context

Michaux was born in Liège in 1984 and grew up nearby in Tilff, where he joined the band Été 67 as a teenager and wrote most of its lyrics until it split in 2012. He divides his time between Brussels and the Danish island of Samsø, co-founded the cooperative label Capitane Records, and leads The Soldiers of Love, who play on the live tracks of Les Chutes. Compare {{ <track_link source="charles-pasi-up-to-us.md" /> }}, another European songwriter working from the blues.

## Impact and Reception

Amusement Park came out as a single on 23 September 2021, ahead of the album. Gaesteliste recommended Les Chutes to anyone who liked Amour Colère. Play it on a grey day and wait for the solo.
