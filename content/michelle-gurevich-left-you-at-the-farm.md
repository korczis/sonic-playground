## Genre and Mood

**Michelle Gurevich** recorded **Left You At The Farm** under her first stage name, Chinawoman, and her own Bandcamp tags the album as art pop, chanson and singer-songwriter music, among others; Discogs files it under acoustic and ballad, and Wikipedia notes that her music has been called lo-fi pop. No official video or upload of the track was found, so this page has no player. Melancholic and bittersweet are this page's reading of the lyric, not a reviewer's words.

## Song Summary

The song is the fifth track of Party Girl, released on 1 April 2007, and runs 4:56. Bandcamp says it was written and recorded at home in Montreal in the summer of 2006, with Gurevich on voice and keys; John Hornak mastered the album, which she produced, performed and mixed herself. The lyric, published with the track, is addressed to a you who cried in the car while the singer promised to stay until the end, and it turns on a confession: someone new walked into the singer's room, and the promise was broken. Who you is, the lyric never says.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes the recording section by section, so this page follows the published lyric and the credits rather than inventing an arrangement. It opens in the car, with the promise, and the first verse already breaks it with the title line. A second verse brings in the newcomer; a third admits a change of mind and holds the starkest line, about not being there at the end. Then the title line is repeated to close. All of it is carried by a voice and keys, as credited.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="lo-fi" /> }} recording means accepting the sound of a small setup. Gurevich made Party Girl at home, in apartments between 2005 and 2007; Wikipedia says her stage name came from a joke typed when GarageBand asked for a band name.

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} here is not a chorus melody that the sources describe but a sentence: I left you at the farm instead. It closes each promise and returns at the end, so the song is remembered by its admission.

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} came years later: MusicBrainz lists Stefan Baghiu's edit, 8:07 long, on the Palimpsests Vol. II EP in 2021.

## Artistic Context

Gurevich was born in Toronto to Russian-Jewish immigrants and worked ten years in the film industry before turning to songs. Wikipedia lists Leonard Cohen, Charles Aznavour and Alla Pugacheva among her influences and notes her contralto range. The title song of the same album, {{ <track_link source="michelle-gurevich-party-girl.md" /> }}, later inspired a French film.

## Impact and Reception

No review of the song was found. Its album travelled: Discogs lists a 2008 edition by Exotica Frontier for sale only in Russia and the CIS, and vinyl and CD reissues from 2016 on, and Wikipedia places her largest audience in Eastern Europe. Find it on her Bandcamp and listen to the last refrain.
