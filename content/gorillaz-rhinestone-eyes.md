## Genre and Mood

**Gorillaz**' **Rhinestone Eyes** is electro-funk by Billboard's label, synth-pop and electronic by its means, and it moves between melancholic and relaxed until the chorus turns groovy. Reviewers heard both moods in it: a sad ballad to one, future-funk to another.

## Song Summary

It is the fourth of Plastic Beach's sixteen tracks and runs 3:20, credited to Damon Albarn and Jamie Hewlett and produced by Gorillaz. It was recorded in 2009 at Studio 13 in London and The Shrine in Lagos; Albarn is credited with vocals, synthesizers, guitar, sampled loops and drum programming, and Gabriel Manuals Wallace with additional drums. Parts of an unreleased demo, Electric Shock, went into it.

## Musical Breakdown

No source maps the track or gives exact times; the reviews below place a few moments roughly, and the parts follow them.

### The Verse

Erik Thompson of The Line of Best Fit heard Albarn take the lead on a gorgeously relaxed song. Wendy Roby of Drowned in Sound heard it shuffle along bleeping, like a sad, synth-led ballad.

### The Synth and the Chorus

Sean Highkin of Beats Per Minute pointed to the impact of the synth that drops about a minute in. Thompson heard the song give way to the Clint Eastwood-like future-funk of a synth-laden chorus, and Darren Lee of The Quietus heard an analogue synth riff with MIA-esque chanting in the background.

### The Choir

About two-thirds of the way through, Roby heard an echoing kiddie choir appear.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="demo" /> }} can feed a song without surviving whole. Electric Shock was one of three demos Albarn aired on BBC Radio 1 in January 2009, alongside {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-broken.md" /> }}; it was never released, and elements of it were used here.

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} is what reviewers keep returning to: the riff, the drop, the chorus. A PAN M 360 review heard in it some of Albarn's best orchestrations, with dreamlike synths shared with {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-on-melancholy-hill.md" /> }}.

Its {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} was never finished. On 4 October 2010 Gorillaz posted a storyboard version of a possible video: the band reunites on Plastic Beach, the Boogieman attacks, and the album's guests help defend the island.

## Artistic Context

Plastic Beach came out on 3 March 2010 on Parlophone, and on Virgin in the United States. Lee called Rhinestone Eyes a quintessential Gorillaz track in the mould of {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-del-the-funky-homosapien-clint-eastwood.md" /> }}, the band's 2001 debut single.

## Impact and Reception

It was planned as the album's fourth single, and promo CDs went to US radio, but days later the band chose the non-album single Doncamatic instead. The song still reached Billboard's US alternative airplay chart and has since been certified platinum in Canada and New Zealand and gold in the UK. It appeared in FIFA 11 and was played at every show of the Escape to Plastic Beach tour. Ryan Leas of Stereogum heard it, with Broken, as one of the album's more world-weary tracks.
