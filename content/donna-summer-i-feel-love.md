## Genre and Mood

**Donna Summer**'s **I Feel Love** is disco that left disco's band behind: an electronic track in which nearly every sound comes from a synthesizer, a blueprint for hi-NRG and much that followed. It is hypnotic, a pulse that never lets go, and euphoric, a voice floating high above it. This page is about the 1977 seven-inch edit.

## Song Summary

Summer, Giorgio Moroder and Pete Bellotte, who wrote the lyric, made it at Musicland Studios in Munich for I Remember Yesterday, an album on which each track evokes a decade; this one was meant to sound like the future. Moroder borrowed a Moog from the composer Eberhard Schoener, whose assistant Robby Wedel showed him how to synchronise its parts with a click track. Everything you hear is electronic, Moroder said, the bass, the hi-hat, the snare; the one exception is a kick drum played by Keith Forsey.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

The track fades in. On the seven-inch edit the fade reaches full volume sooner than on the 5:53 album version.

### Main Section

The bassline came first, before the melody, which was unusual in disco. Each of its notes is doubled by a delay, the dry note in one channel and the echo in the other, a flickering, strobe-like motion. Over it Summer sings in head voice rather than disco's usual chest voice, in a single take; the musicologist Eric Weisbard has said she pushes so hard at the top of her voice that listeners are unsure what they are hearing.

### Outro

The edit fades out before the third verse and the final choruses, the pulse still running.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="sequencer" /> }} plays programmed notes in exact time, and that is the engine here: the parts were programmed onto 16-track tape, locked together by the click track Moroder called a revelation.

The {{ <concept_link name="analog synthesizer" /> }} behaved like one: the Moog drifted out of tune so quickly that it was recorded in bursts of twenty or thirty seconds, and the hi-hat was built from white noise shaped by an envelope.

The {{ <concept_link name="delay" /> }} on the bass is what makes the line move; Moroder told Mojo the delay was added by the engineer, by accident, at the tempo of the beat. The seven-inch is in effect a {{ <concept_link name="radio edit" /> }} of its day: a quicker start and fewer repeats.

## Artistic Context

Moroder and Bellotte produced it. Moroder tells his own story of that sound in {{ <track_link source="daft-punk-giorgio-by-moroder.md" /> }}, and Bronski Beat later folded the song into a medley ({{ <track_link source="bronski-beat-i-feel-love.md" /> }}).

## Impact and Reception

Released by Casablanca in July 1977, I Feel Love spent four weeks at number one in the UK and reached number six in the US. David Bowie recalled Brian Eno running in to say he had heard the sound of the future. In 1995 remixes by Rollo & Sister Bliss and Masters at Work took it back to number eight in the UK. It is in the Library of Congress's National Recording Registry, and Rolling Stone ranked it the greatest dance song of all time in 2022.
