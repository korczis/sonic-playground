## Genre and Mood

**Dan Kanvis** came up as a rapper, and **Skeleton** is the song where he stepped sideways into electro-pop. Hip-hop is still there in its tags and in one of its verses, but most of it is sung. The mood is introspective and full of longing: a song about wanting to be close to people and not quite being able to bear them.

## Song Summary

The lyric, published on Kanvis's Bandcamp page, circles social unease and solitude: a preference for being alone, a feeling of exposure in company, a wish for connection that keeps sabotaging itself. Its central picture is dancing while being watched, paired with the skeleton of the title, a body stripped bare and looked at. Later it turns to restless driving with nowhere to go. Kanvis wrote and produced it with a collaborator credited only as Kirk; Robert Szmurlo mixed it, with additional mixing by Kirk. It came out on 18 October 2019 as the first single from what became Understanding Dead Plants.

### Sung Sections

CMU's Andy Malt heard the song dive headlong into electro-pop, and the clearest sign is the voice: Kanvis sings most of the track instead of rapping it, which his earlier, more purely hip-hop records did not do.

### Rap Verse

In one verse he goes back to rapping. Malt noted that even there the delivery has moved on, tighter than the looser flow of his older material.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="rapping" /> }} is the thread that ties Skeleton to its author's past. Placed in a single verse, it changes its job: instead of carrying the song, the rapped lines become a contrast, speech rhythm cutting into a sung melody.

The credits are a small lesson in how records are made. Kanvis and Kirk wrote the song and each acted as {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }}, shaping it from idea to finished recording; Robert Szmurlo did the {{ <concept_link name="mixing" /> }}, balancing the recorded parts into one track, with Kirk adding more.

## Artistic Context

Kanvis grew up in suburban Metro Detroit and studied at DePaul University in Chicago, where in 2015 the student paper described him as a one-man operation, paying for studio time, booking shows and promoting his debut album Windows. When Skeleton arrived, CMU counted four years since his EP Parallel and five since Windows. Understanding Dead Plants, released on 13 November 2020 and recorded at home in Chicago and Los Angeles, is in his words a portrait of his adolescence near Detroit and his young adult years in Chicago; its title points both to tending plants and to Detroit's empty car plants.

## Impact and Reception

Skeleton was a quiet independent release, not a chart record. CMU chose it for its Approved column, heard Kanvis emerging like a butterfly from a cocoon, and said the song left you eager for what came next. Listen for the moment the singing gives way to the rap verse, and back again.
