## Genre and Mood

**Gabriella Cilmi** turns **Cry Me A River (Live Version)** into a soul song that sounds decades older than it is. It is a cover with a pop pedigree, but the delivery is pure soul: raw, bittersweet and full-throated, with the slightly ragged edges only a live take can give.

## Song Summary

Justin Timberlake's 2002 hit was a sleek, beat-driven kiss-off, layered with Timbaland's stuttering production. Cilmi strips all that away. Recorded live, her version leans on a small band and lets her husky, surprisingly mature voice carry the story of betrayal and cold satisfaction. Where the original sounds triumphant, this one sounds wounded, as if the singer is not quite sure she has won.

## Musical Breakdown

The arrangement is kept loose and sparse, with the voice always in front.

### Intro

A few soft chords from the band set a smoky, late-club atmosphere before the voice comes in almost immediately.

### Verse 1

Cilmi sings the opening lines low and measured, holding back, the grain in her voice doing as much work as the words.

### Chorus

The famous refrain opens up into a bigger, bluesier wail. It is here that you hear the old-school soul tradition she draws on, and the hurt behind the bravado.

### Bridge

The band thins out and her voice drops back to a near-whisper, making the next swell feel even larger.

### Climax

The last choruses are the most fiery, with runs and ad-libs that push the song from reproach to catharsis.

### Outro

The band settles and she lets the final line hang, a quiet ending that feels more like a sigh than a slammed door.

## Artistic Context

Gabriella Cilmi is a singer from Melbourne who released her debut album, Lessons To Be Learned, on Island Records in 2008, at sixteen. Her breakout single "Sweet About Me" showed the same taste for retro soul. This live recording of the Timberlake song is included on the album, credited to its original writers, Timberlake, Timbaland and Scott Storch, and it captures what her concerts of the time were about: a big, old-fashioned voice in a very young singer.

## Impact and Reception

The song stayed in her live sets around the album, including a V Festival performance in 2008, and the recorded take gave album buyers a taste of what those shows sounded like. It belongs here because it shows how a great vocalist can change a song's meaning without touching a word. Listen with the original nearby and hear two completely different break-ups.
