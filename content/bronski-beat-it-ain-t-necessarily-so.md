## Genre and Mood

**Bronski Beat**'s **It Ain't Necessarily So** is a cover, an 80s synth-pop record made from a song George and Ira Gershwin wrote for Porgy and Bess in 1935. Its mood is provocative and defiant under a playful surface. This page is the full album version, the one the band placed second on their debut album.

## Song Summary

In the opera the song belongs to Sportin' Life, a drug dealer who casts doubt on stories from the Bible. Bronski Beat, an openly gay trio, recorded it for The Age of Consent, and Pitchfork read the implication plainly: doubt about the Bible's statements on homosexuality, sung as one of the album's songs of desire "delivered in a distinct feminine mode". The sound does not shout. Pitchfork heard "a slice of sophisticated percolation more akin to fellow 1984 breakout star Sade than gritty British synth-pop", with a clarinet solo and the Pink Singers, an openly gay male choir from London, around Jimmy Somerville's voice.

## Musical Breakdown

The sources name the parts of the song but not their order or length, so this breakdown follows the parts.

### Verses

Story by story, from David and Goliath to Jonah in the whale, the 1935 lyric tells a tale from the Bible and then shrugs it off with the same line of doubt.

### Solo

A clarinet steps forward for a solo. Who plays it is unclear: Wikipedia names Arno Hecht of the Uptown Horns in its account of the singles and Richard Coles in the personnel.

### The Choir

The Pink Singers are credited on this track, a choir behind the lead.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} can keep every word and change the meaning. Sung by Sportin' Life in 1935, the song mocks piety; sung in 1984 by a band whose album sleeve listed the age of consent for gay men across Europe, the same doubt points at those who quote the Bible against gay people.

This recording is an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }} of the band's own single. Wikipedia gives the album track 4:40, MusicBrainz 4:43, against about four minutes for the seven-inch; the single is reviewed as {{ <track_link source="bronski-beat-ain-t-necessarily-so.md" /> }}. What the edit removed, no source says.

{{ <concept_link name="vocal harmony" /> }} is several voices singing together. Here a gay male choir joins Somerville, so the doubt is no longer one voice's.

## Artistic Context

The Age of Consent came out on London Records on 15 October 1984, produced by Mike Thorne, and this track was recorded at The Garden in London and Skyline Studios in New York. The inner sleeve listed the legal age for relationships between men in each European country; MCA removed the table from the US release, citing the "past sensitivities of several record store chains".

## Impact and Reception

The album reached number four in the UK and number 36 on the Billboard 200, and the single reached number 16 in the UK. Later reissues added a 12-inch version and a BBC session of the song. Play the album version and hear the arrangement given its full length.
