## Genre and Mood

**Potochkine** open their self-titled 2018 EP with **Dans Ta Face**, French for "in your face". The record's tags call it EBM, darkwave, industrial and techno, and the duo themselves describe their music as a mix of EBM, techno, dark ambient, a bit of industrial and experimental. The feelings are dark, intense and theatrical, the last one earned: the two met in drama conservatories in Paris.

## Song Summary

No source sets out the lyric, so this page does not paraphrase it; the title says enough about its attitude. The EP's own text speaks of the dismal chant of a powerful voice over the tense rhythm of dance and techno, and Aaron Vehling, reviewing it for Vehlinggo, heard a minimal record of thick synth arpeggiations, clean and simple drum machine parts and raw vocals. Those are descriptions of the whole EP; no source describes Dans Ta Face on its own.

## Concepts in Focus

An {{ <concept_link name="arpeggio" /> }} plays the notes of a chord one after another instead of together. Vehling heard thick synth arpeggiations across the EP: repeating synth figures that give a track its motion before any melody arrives.

The {{ <concept_link name="drum machine" /> }} is the other half of that sound. The review heard its parts as clean and simple, leaving the space to the synthesizers and the voice.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} followed in April 2019 on Data Airlines' channel, directed by Romuald Sintes and produced by Transfuges. No source describes its images.

## Artistic Context

Potochkine are Pauline, who sings and writes all the lyrics, and Hugo, the producer. Their founding idea was to melt theatre, dance and live electronic music, and the name is a word Hugo's goddaughter invented when she refused to say Pauline. After a 2016 EP, Libérez votre imagination, the self-titled record was their first release on a label, Data Airlines. Mythes, written over three years for the stage, and Sortilèges, made during the pandemic, followed.

## Impact and Reception

Vehling called the EP dark and visceral, though he singled out other tracks. Years later Dans Ta Face still stood for the first record: in 2026 an interviewer picked it as the record's punk influence made audible. Pauline disagreed; punk, she said, has been part of her life since childhood, and she found its violence and urgency again in EBM and techno. Listen and decide which of them is right.
