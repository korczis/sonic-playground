## Genre and Mood

**Dr. Dre**'s **Bang Bang** is West Coast hip-hop from the last stretch of his 1999 album 2001, a rap track with two guests and a heavy subject. Its title sounds like a boast; the critics who have written about it hear something closer to the opposite, a dark, melancholic look at the gunfire the title names.

## Song Summary

Bang Bang is the 21st of 23 tracks on 2001, credited to Dr. Dre featuring Knoc-turn'al and Hittman, and runs 3:42. Wikipedia credits its writing to Andre Young, Brian Bailey, Marshall Mathers and Royal Harbor, Knoc-turn'al's own name, and lists no samples. Okayplayer's Peter A. Berry reads it as a song about the gun violence Los Angeles is known for, on which Dre "takes a step back from glorifying the action" and simply names it. Edge Induced Cohesion called it a melancholy track about increased violence within the Black community.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes the beat in detail or gives times; this part keeps to what the reviews say.

### The Beat

Berry wrote that the beat fits Dre's conflicted view of the subject. Time's Josh Tyrangiel, quoted on Wikipedia, wrote that every Dre track begins with Dre behind a drum machine in a room full of trusted musicians.

### The Verses

Dre shares the track with Knoc-turn'al, a rapper and singer from Long Beach, and Hittman, whom Edge Induced Cohesion counts on at least eight of the album's tracks. Soul In Stereo's Edd Bowser wrote that the three have so much chemistry.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} can change what a song is. Here a guest is also an author: Knoc-turn'al is credited as a writer under his own name, Royal Harbor, alongside Marshall Mathers, better known as Eminem.

{{ <concept_link name="rapping" /> }} about violence can celebrate it or mourn it with the same words; delivery and setting decide. Compare the track before it on the album, {{ <track_link source="dr-dre-ackrite.md" /> }}, which Okayplayer heard as playful and threatening.

The {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} credit is less settled than it looks. Wikipedia credits 2001's production to Dr. Dre and Mel-Man and gives Lord Finesse only The Message, yet the Universal metadata on the official upload names Lord Finesse as producer of Bang Bang.

The track also travelled as a {{ <concept_link name="b-side" /> }}: MusicBrainz lists a British 12-inch of The Next Episode with Bang Bang alone on its second side.

## Artistic Context

2001 came out on 16 November 1999 on Aftermath and Interscope, Dre's second solo album, produced mainly by Dre and Mel-Man. Knoc-turn'al went on to Bad Intentions with Dre for The Wash soundtrack in 2001 and his own L.A. Confidential EP in 2002.

## Impact and Reception

Bang Bang was not one of the album's singles, but it has its defenders. Okayplayer's 2024 ranking put it 10th among the album's songs. In Soul In Stereo's retrospective, Bowser ranked it 10th of 17 and chose it as his underrated pick, saying its commentary on California's gun violence cements it; Ronald Grant placed it 15th. Hear it after Ackrite, as the album runs.
