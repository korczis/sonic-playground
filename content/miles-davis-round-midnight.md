## Genre and Mood

**Miles Davis** recorded **'Round Midnight** as a hard bop quintet playing a ballad, and the result is melancholic and intimate in the way late-night jazz is meant to be. The Audio Beat hears a late-night atmosphere from the first notes; the trumpet speaks quietly, close to the ear, until it suddenly does not.

## Song Summary

The tune is Thelonious Monk's, probably written in 1940 or 1941, with co-credits for Cootie Williams, who recorded it first in 1944, and Bernie Hanighen, who wrote lyrics. JazzStandards.com describes it as primarily minor. Davis's quintet recorded it on 10 September 1956 at Columbia's 30th Street Studio in New York, a former church, in a version based on Dizzy Gillespie's arrangement, and it opens the album 'Round About Midnight.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

Davis plays Gillespie's introduction on Harmon-muted trumpet, stripped down to very little. The mute blocks the air from the bell and gives what Sound On Sound calls a warm, high-pitched buzz.

### Theme

The theme follows in the same voice, snail-paced and spare, with fewer notes than Monk wrote.

### Interlude

At about 2:40, at the end of the theme, Davis adds a passage of his own: a shout chorus that jumps into major, played on open trumpet. Ethan Hein's analysis says it sets up the solo that follows.

### Tenor Solo

John Coltrane enters with an assertive, energetic tenor solo, over Red Garland's piano, Paul Chambers's bass and Philly Joe Jones's drums.

### Outro

Davis takes up the mute again for a version of Gillespie's ending, and the mood falls back to where it began.

## Concepts in Focus

The track is a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }}. Monk wrote the melody, Gillespie the introduction and the ending, and Davis the shout chorus, and each layer became part of how the standard is heard. The official site calls the interlude famous and the solo it leads into charged.

The {{ <concept_link name="minor key" /> }} of the theme is what makes the major passage hit so hard: for a few bars the ballad opens up, then closes again.

{{ <concept_link name="improvisation" /> }} lives inside that frame. Coltrane, then relatively unknown, solos inside it; compare how {{ <track_link source="art-blakey-the-jazz-messengers-moanin.md" /> }} hands its theme to its soloists.

## Artistic Context

At the 1955 Newport Jazz Festival Davis played 'Round Midnight with Monk in an all-star group, and Columbia's George Avakian, in the audience, signed him while he was still under contract to Prestige. 'Round About Midnight, produced by Avakian, was his first Columbia album, released in March 1957, with Coltrane, Garland, Chambers and Jones.

## Impact and Reception

Ralph J. Gleason gave the album five stars in DownBeat; The Penguin Guide to Jazz found it a footnote to the Prestige sessions. Hein calls this the definitive version and notes it was a jukebox hit in black neighbourhoods. Davis kept the song in his repertoire for about fifteen years, and the album entered the Grammy Hall of Fame in 2019. Listen for the moment the mute comes off.
