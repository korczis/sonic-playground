## Genre and Mood

**Corinne Bailey Rae** sings **Put Your Records On** with the ease of a summer afternoon. The track blends soul, pop and R&B into something light and warm, and its mood is carefree, uplifting and unmistakably feel-good: a song that seems to lower your shoulders by a few centimetres the moment it starts. Nothing about it is flashy, and that modesty is exactly where its warmth comes from.

## Song Summary

The song is a gentle pep talk. Bailey Rae encourages a girl, and by extension the listener, to relax, put on her favourite records, let her hair down and grow into herself without worrying about what anyone thinks. The arrangement is deliberately unfussy, with a bright guitar, soft drums and a warm bass, leaving her voice, sweet with a slight husk, to carry the song. The memorable detail is the lyric "girl, put your records on", sung like advice from an older sister. The production, organic and lightly polished, lets the small details shine: a guitar lick here, a soft harmony there, the brush of a cymbal.

## Musical Breakdown

The structure is classic and compact, every section designed to keep the breezy mood intact.

### Intro

A sunny, lightly strummed guitar and a relaxed backbeat set an easy tempo, inviting rather than insistent.

### Verse 1

Bailey Rae's voice enters close and conversational, painting small, everyday images of slowing down and enjoying the moment.

### Chorus

The hook lifts gently rather than exploding, a warm, singalong melody that feels instantly familiar.

### Bridge

The bridge turns more reflective, with lines about growing up and finding yourself, before the music opens back into the chorus.

### Outro

The final choruses drift out on her ad-libs and the gentle groove, ending as softly as the song began.

## Artistic Context

Corinne Bailey Rae is a singer-songwriter from Leeds who drew on soul, jazz and folk for her self-titled debut album. "Put Your Records On" was released as a single from that album in 2006, and it became her signature song, introducing a voice that sat somewhere between neo-soul and classic pop. Before her solo career she had played in an indie band and worked in a jazz club, and both experiences show: the song has a songwriter's economy and a jazz singer's ease with phrasing.

## Impact and Reception

The single was a major hit in the UK and travelled widely abroad, and it earned Grammy nominations, including Song of the Year. Its gentle message has kept it on radio, playlists and soundtracks ever since. It is on this playground because sometimes the best thing a song can do is help you breathe out. Put it on and let it do exactly that.
