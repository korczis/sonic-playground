## Genre and Mood

**Corinne Bailey Rae** sings **Put Your Records On** with the ease of a summer afternoon. The track blends soul, pop and R&B into something light and warm, and its mood is carefree, uplifting and unmistakably feel-good. Nothing about it is flashy, and that modesty is exactly where its warmth comes from.

## Song Summary

The song is a gentle pep talk. Bailey Rae encourages a girl, and by extension the listener, to relax, put on her favourite records, let her hair down and grow into herself without worrying about what anyone thinks. The arrangement is deliberately unfussy: acoustic and Spanish guitars, a warm bass, a Hammond organ and a small horn section in the background, leaving her voice, sweet with a slight husk, to carry the song. The memorable detail is the title line, sung like advice from an older sister.

## Musical Breakdown

The structure is classic and compact, every section designed to keep the breezy mood intact.

### Intro

A sunny, lightly strummed guitar and a relaxed backbeat set an easy tempo, inviting rather than insistent.

### Verse 1

Bailey Rae's voice enters close and conversational, painting small, everyday images of slowing down; the opening verse pays homage to Bob Marley's "Three Little Birds".

### Chorus

The hook lifts gently rather than exploding, a warm, singalong melody that feels instantly familiar.

### Bridge

The bridge turns more reflective, with lines about growing up and finding yourself, before the music opens back into the chorus.

### Outro

The final choruses drift out on her ad-libs and the gentle groove, ending as softly as the song began.

## Concepts in Focus

Put Your Records On is a model of {{ <concept_link name="verse-chorus form" /> }}. The verses carry new images each time over similar music; the chorus brings back the same words and melody; a single bridge offers a change of view before the last choruses. In three and a half minutes the listener always knows where they are, and that predictability is part of the song's calm.

Its {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is the title phrase. It does not rely on volume or a big vocal leap; it works because it is short, conversational and repeated, an instruction the listener can sing back to themselves. ## Artistic Context

Corinne Bailey Rae is a singer-songwriter from Leeds who drew on soul, jazz and folk for her self-titled debut album, released by EMI in February 2006 (Capitol in North America), which entered the UK albums chart at number one. "Put Your Records On", written with John Beck and producer Steve Chrisanthou, came out on 20 February 2006 as the album's second UK single and its first in North America. The liner notes credit Bailey Rae on acoustic guitar and vocals, Chrisanthou on electric and Spanish guitars and programming, Beck on keyboards, Sam Dixon on bass, Joe Tatton on Hammond organ and a three-piece horn section. Before her solo career she had played in an indie band and worked in a jazz club, and the song has a songwriter's economy and a jazz singer's ease with phrasing.

## Impact and Reception

The single reached number 2 in the UK, topped the UK R&B chart and reached number 64 on the Billboard Hot 100, and it is certified triple platinum in both the UK and the US. At the 2007 Grammy Awards it was nominated for Record of the Year and Song of the Year. It was used in the film *Venus* the same year, and in 2020 a cover by the American project Ritt Momney went viral on TikTok and reached number 30 in the US, giving the song a second life. Put it on and let it help you breathe out.
