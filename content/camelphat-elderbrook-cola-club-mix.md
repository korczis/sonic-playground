## Genre and Mood

**CamelPhat** and **Elderbrook** made **Cola**, and this is **Cola (Club Mix)**, its full-length version: house with the weight of tech house, built for DJs rather than radio. It is hypnotic in the voice, groovy in the bass and dark in the pads; Mixmag warned that it is not your typical sugary summer anthem, which is part of why it became one.

## Song Summary

Defected released the single in June 2017. Wikipedia puts it at 122 BPM with a debt to Chicago house; Defected's own note promised lush, saturated beats under Elderbrook's crystalline voice. The lyric has been read two ways. Some critics heard the chorus as a reference to spiking drinks; Alexander Kotz, who sings as Elderbrook, told the Los Angeles Times it is about a woman too drunk to get into a club who is handed a Coca-Cola to sober up and thinks it is rum and Coke.

### Intro

At 6:56 the club mix gives a DJ room to blend in and out; no source describes what the opening bars contain.

### Main Section

Mixmag heard Elderbrook's vocals as hypnotic and pensive rather than lush, the pads as moody rather than ethereal, and on a big rig the bouncing bass line coming to the fore. Will Adams of the Singles Jukebox heard a polyrhythmic pulse spanning the crisp beats and an eerie chord progression.

### Breakdown

Kat Stevens, also of the Singles Jukebox, singled out a lull in the music designed to build suspense, the pause a dance floor waits through for the beat's return.

## Concepts in Focus

This version is the long one. Like an {{ <concept_link name="extended mix" /> }}, a club mix keeps the intro, the instrumental passages and the ending at DJ length. The {{ <concept_link name="radio edit" /> }} runs 3:44 and the album edit on CamelPhat's Dark Matter 4:03, against 6:56 here; compare the short form of {{ <track_link source="format-b-chunky-radio-edit.md" /> }}.

The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} is what Mixmag heard rise to the front on a big sound system, and the lull Kat Stevens described is a classic {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }}: take the beat away, let the tension gather, bring it back.

## Artistic Context

CamelPhat are the Liverpool duo Dave Whelan and Mike Di Scala; Elderbrook, the singer and producer Alexander Kotz, co-wrote the track and, as EARMILK put it, brought his indie roots to CamelPhat's bigger underground beats. Solomun, MK and Radio 1's Danny Howard all supported it early.

## Impact and Reception

Cola became, in Mixmag's words, one of the defining tunes of Ibiza 2017, played at Pacha, Ushuaïa, Amnesia and beyond. It reached number one on Billboard's Dance Club Songs chart, number 18 in the UK, and was nominated for Best Dance Recording at the 2018 Grammy Awards. Play the club mix loud and wait for the lull.
