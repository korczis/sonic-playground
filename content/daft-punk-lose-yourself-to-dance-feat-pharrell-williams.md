## Genre and Mood

**Daft Punk** and **Pharrell Williams** made **Lose Yourself to Dance** a disco and funk song that takes its time. Groovy, hypnotic and finally euphoric, it runs close to six minutes on the album, at a tempo Wikipedia gives as 100 beats per minute, slow enough to stomp to rather than race.

## Song Summary

The sixth track of Random Access Memories came, the duo said, from a wish to make dance music with live drummers. Thomas Bangalter spoke of redefining dance music as something lighter or more primal, and of the feeling of being unified and connected on a dance floor. Pharrell sings the lead; Daft Punk add vocoder voices; Nile Rodgers plays guitar, Nathan East bass and John "J.R." Robinson drums.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

Mixmag's Nick DeCosemo heard heavy beats like the Billy Squier drums at the start of Dizzee Rascal's Fix Up, Look Sharp, with more claps thrown in. Another Mixmag writer, Jeremy Abbott, credited big multi-layered claps and thrashing cymbals with driving the song forward.

### Verses

Pharrell's trademark falsetto, in DeCosemo's words, urges the listener to lose themselves, while Rodgers does the business on guitar.

### Chorus

A rising vocoder line sings come on, come on in counterpoint to Pharrell's chorus. DeCosemo called it the album's closest thing yet to a Harder, Better, Faster, Stronger moment.

### Outro

No source this page found describes the ending; listen for how long the groove holds once the vocals have made their point.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="vocoder" /> }} imposes a voice onto a synthesizer, and here it lets the robots answer the man. The chanted come on rises against Pharrell's line, so the chorus becomes a dialogue between human and machine, the duo's signature since {{ <track_link source="daft-punk-harder-better-faster-stronger.md" /> }}.

Pharrell's {{ <concept_link name="falsetto" /> }} keeps the vocal light and airy above heavy drums. He said the song made him feel like he was walking through London in the middle of the night in 1984 or 1985, and that he did not hear the 1970s in it at all.

The {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} is the point. Random Access Memories used session musicians and kept electronics to drum machines, a modular synthesizer and vintage vocoders, so the pulse here is played, not programmed.

## Artistic Context

Random Access Memories, released by Columbia in May 2013, was Daft Punk's fourth and final studio album, a tribute to late-1970s and early-1980s American music. It followed {{ <track_link source="daft-punk-pharrell-williams-nile-rodgers-get-lucky-radio-edit.md" /> }}, the album's lead single, with the same singer and guitarist. In a 2023 Memory Tapes episode, Pharrell said he had not expected his vocals to end up on the finished versions of either song.

## Impact and Reception

Sent to radio as the second single on 13 August 2013, in a four-minute edit, it reached number 49 in the UK and number one on Billboard's Dance Club Songs, and is certified platinum in the US. Its video, shot on 70mm film, premiered on 16 September 2013. Give it the full album length.
