## Genre and Mood

**Rachel K Collier**'s **Microfreaking Out** is electro-pop made by an electronic producer, a three-minute song with a sharp tongue. Its lyric pushes back at someone who stays up glued to a phone, picking fights online, and refuses to be set off by them: defiant in what it says, playful in how it says it, starting with a title that puns on the synthesizer it was built around.

## Song Summary

The track was released on 18 November 2020 through NiF Records. Three days later the producer and YouTuber Andrew Huang published 4 Producers 1 Synth: Arturia Microfreak, an episode sponsored by Arturia in which Collier, Red Means Recording, Cuckoo and Huang each made a track around the same synth. Collier posted her own production walkthrough the same day, with the lyrics printed underneath. In 2023 the song became the third track on her album Songs In The Key Of Internet.

### Verses

No review describes how the track sounds, so this page follows the lyric. The verses picture a person always holding a telephone that never rings, up late and hooked on dissing others, while the singer insists you cannot arm her because she is not an explosive.

The chorus turns on bringing somebody digitally down, shrugs off whatever gets shared, and lands on the title line. How the synth parts move under it is for your ears to judge.

## Concepts in Focus

Arturia describes the MicroFreak as a compact hybrid {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }}: one digital oscillator with many modes feeds an analog state-variable filter with low-pass, band-pass and high-pass settings, and it is played from a flat, touch-sensitive 25-key keybed with polyphonic aftertouch, up to four voices at a time. The digital side supplies a wide range of raw tones; the analog filter shapes them.

An episode built around one instrument makes {{ <concept_link name="sound design" /> }} the real subject. Four producers start from the same box, so what tells their tracks apart is largely the sounds each one builds from it: the bass, the leads, the textures. Collier's walkthrough video takes you through how she built hers for this song.

## Artistic Context

Rachel K Collier is a Welsh electronic producer and performer from Swansea, who first made her name as a featured singer and topline writer on other producers' records before writing, performing and producing her own. Ableton notes that her first educational video was made for Andrew Huang's 4 Producers series; she now teaches online and in person as an Ableton Certified Trainer. Hear the track before this one on the album in {{ <track_link source="rachel-k-collier-i-wish-you-well.md" /> }}.

No review or chart run for the single turned up. It reached listeners first through Huang's episode and Collier's walkthrough, as much a lesson as a release, and later through an album whose title, Songs In The Key Of Internet, points the same way as its lyric.
