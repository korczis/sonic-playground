## Genre and Mood

**Oum Shatt** play guitar rock with Eastern melodies, as their Europavox profile puts it, and **Power to the Women of the Morning Shift** is where their debut album starts: indie rock with a psychedelic drift and a world-music accent in its harmonies. The feelings follow the sources: hypnotic rhythms, laid-back passion, and a critic's word for this song, a hit.

## Song Summary

The title is a salute to working women, and Kaput's Christina Mohr read the album as an homage to women with this title as her example. Her colleague Mario Lasar found the song quite the hit, thanks to a pointed guitar motif and its lyric, and was reminded of Jonathan Richman's Egyptian Reggae; he also regretted that the song never makes clear what trade the women of the morning shift work in. It opens the album Oum Shatt, released on 27 May 2016, and runs 3:09.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the song part by part or gives times, so the breakdown keeps to what the critics named.

### The Guitar Motif

The pointed guitar figure is what Lasar credits for the song's pull. On the album as a whole he heard guitars tracing winding paths, almost like arabesques set to music, in a sound that stays mostly transparent.

### The Rhythm

Mohr singled out the drums of Chris Imler, which she heard as ready to erupt at any moment, and the use of Arabic harmonies and rhythms as the band's most striking feature.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }} is a short instrumental figure repeated until it becomes the song's signature. Here it is a guitar motif, half of why the song works for its critic; compare the rhythmical guitar that carries {{ <track_link source="melissa-laveaux-kouzen.md" /> }}.

A {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is whatever catches the ear and stays. Lasar names two: the motif and the words.

The song also exists as an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }}. MusicBrainz lists a separate Arabic version, released on the Gold to Straw single two weeks before the album, and the band had first released the song as the title track of an EP in August 2013, which credits Jonas Poppe as writer, producer and mixer. MusicBrainz lists the 2013 and 2016 tracks as separate recordings; no source says how they differ.

## Artistic Context

Oum Shatt formed in Berlin in 2012 around the singer and guitarist Jonas Poppe, formerly of the electropop trio Kissogram, with the drummer Chris Imler; the name honours the Egyptian singer Umm Kulthum and, according to Politis, the Tunisian salt flat Chott el Djerid. The self-titled debut came out on Snowhite; Musicboard Berlin later wrote that it cemented the band's distinctive sound of distilled rock 'n' roll, discreetly woven Arabic harmonies and hypnotic rhythms. For another artist who names Umm Kulthum among her influences, hear {{ <track_link source="hindi-zahra-beautiful-tango-remastered-version.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Mohr called the album the most laid-back and most passionate music she had heard in a long time. HHV Mag, announcing the second album Opt Out in January 2024, called the 2016 debut celebrated and the band critics' favourites. Start the album where the band does, with the guitar motif.
