## Genre and Mood

**Mindchatter** released **Trippy** as his first single, and the first review it got filed it under bedroom pop: Indie Shuffle wrote on the day of release that he had absolutely nailed that sound, and tagged the song indie pop, with Still Woozy and Andrew Applepie as points of comparison. Apple Music files it under dance. The feelings chosen here, dreamy and quirky, follow the blog's second post a few weeks later, which heard his music as dreamy and unusual; read them as a pointer, not a description of every bar.

## Song Summary

Trippy came out on 10 January 2019 and runs 2:52. No source sets out its lyric or describes its arrangement in any detail. What the sources agree on is its place: it was the first song Bryce Connolly put on streaming services as Mindchatter, and the start of the project. Indie Shuffle's first post did not describe the music so much as the mood around it, a list of small domestic distractions of the kind a bedroom pop producer might know, ending with the question of why you have not fed the cats yet.

### Main Section

The one sound on record is the title's: in February 2019 Indie Shuffle recalled that it was indeed a trippy song, and that the name was no fluke. Listen for what earns the word; the rest is yours to describe.

## Concepts in Focus

Mindchatter is a {{ <concept_link name="stage persona" /> }} of the plainest kind, a name rather than a costume. Connolly has explained it as the struggle to stay present and free of mind chatter, something he calls a big part of his life. The alias names a state of mind before a note is heard.

He is also his own {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }}. He produces and records his music, and by his own account in a 2022 interview he learned to sing as an adult and built that skill at ICON Collective in Los Angeles, where he also learned to finish tracks instead of leaving them half done. Trippy, he told Atwood Magazine in 2020, was the song without which he could not have made what followed.

## Artistic Context

Connolly is a New York native who grew up on hip-hop, techno and folk and DJed around the city from high school on. About six months after he began releasing music, Mind of a Genius Records approached him, and the label flew in to see him open for Polo & Pan in New York; compare {{ <track_link source="polo-pan-canopee.md" /> }}. Current listings of Trippy credit the label in their copyright line. Later releases moved on, among them the EP Spiritual Puberty on Foreign Family Collective.

## Impact and Reception

His label's biography says Trippy and his single Tough As Nails both topped the Hype Machine chart, and that Pete Tong praised his music on BBC Radio 1; no source records a conventional chart. Play the song with its title in mind and decide what makes it trippy.
