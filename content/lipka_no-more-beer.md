## Genre and Mood

Lipka make **No More Beer** a party song about the end of the party. The Geneva duo tag their music as electronic, electro and indie, and this track is playful and humorous first, with a bittersweet edge for anyone who has watched a good night run dry.

## Song Summary

The premise is simple and very funny: a voice addresses the party people, apologises for ruining the fun and asks the DJ to cut the music, because there is an announcement to make. The beer is running out. What follows is a refrain that repeats that news and a quick exchange about where more might be found, each suggestion shot down. It is a wry take on an everyday disaster, exactly the kind of lyric the band describe as their specialty.

## Lyrics Breakdown

### Intro: A Toast to the Night

The track opens with the music already playing, as if we have walked into the party, before the first voice interrupts it.

### Verse 1: Reflecting on Good Times

The voice greets the party people, admits they are having fun and asks the DJ to stop the music for a moment. The comedy is in the politeness.

### Chorus: The Bittersweet Goodbye

The announcement arrives and becomes the hook: the beer is running out, repeated until the line is both refrain and punchline.

### Verse 2: Embracing New Beginnings

Two voices trade questions and answers about where the beer might still be, a little comic scene in the middle of the song.

### Bridge: A Moment of Reflection

The opening address returns, the party interrupted once again, as if the bad news needs repeating for those who did not hear it.

### Final Chorus: Celebration of Life

The refrain closes the song, the party still going and the beer still running out.

## Concepts in Focus

The middle of the song is a small play built on {{ <concept_link name="call and response" /> }}. One voice asks where to look, another answers, and each answer is a dead end. The form comes from African and African-American traditions, work songs, gospel, blues, and here it is used for comedy, but it works the same way: one line sets up the next.

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is the announcement itself. A good hook is short, repeated and memorable; this one is also a punchline, so every repetition gets the joke again. Listen to how the song gets more mileage out of one sentence than many songs get out of a whole chorus.

## Artistic Context

Lipka formed in Geneva in 2009 when two childhood friends, Felix Landis and Etienne Maître, combined backgrounds in punk, drum and bass, dancehall, folk, electronica and rock; Felix's voice sits over Etienne's production, and live Etienne also acts as MC, with projections, smoke and light by the visual artist Florian Pittet of SigmaSix. Their debut album LIPKA, written, composed and produced by the two, was produced by Evidence Music, the Geneva company Etienne Maître co-founded in 2013; it was first released in 2013 and appeared on Bandcamp in September 2015, the year the band posted this video.

## Impact and Reception

Lipka come out of the Swiss Romand scene: their song "Peanuts" was played on the Couleur 3 show Repérages, and they appeared on the 2013 Demo Tape Clinic compilation of the M4music festival. No More Beer is a good introduction to their humour and to the sound of a duo that refuses to stay in one genre. Put it on near the end of a party and watch who laughs first.
