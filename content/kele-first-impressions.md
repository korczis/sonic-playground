## Genre and Mood

**Kele** opens his album Trick with **First Impressions**, a track in the space between electronic music, house and R&B. The mood is intimate and hypnotic, with a romantic glow that feels like the first, nervous minutes of meeting someone new.

## Song Summary

The song is about love at first sight, or at least that electric moment when it feels possible. Kele Okereke sings in a cool, collected near-whisper, close to the microphone, while a sleek, late-night house groove rolls underneath. The singer and DJ Yasmin (Yasmin Shahmir) adds a second voice that blends with his, softening the edges and giving the track a dreamy, duet-like quality. The production is smooth and restrained, more after-hours lounge than peak-time club, setting the tone for the record it opens. Nothing is loud and nothing is hurried; the beat ticks along patiently, the bass hums low and the vocals stay soft, so the track works by drawing you closer rather than reaching out to grab you. It is a confident way to start an album, trusting atmosphere over spectacle.

## Musical Breakdown

The track unfolds slowly, letting its groove and atmosphere carry the story.

### Intro

Soft synth pads and a muted beat open the song, creating a hazy, nocturnal atmosphere.

### Verse 1

Kele's hushed vocal enters, intimate and measured, describing the first spark of attraction.

### Chorus

Shahmir's voice weaves around his, the melody lifting gently as the groove becomes a little fuller and warmer.

### Bridge

The arrangement thins out, leaving the voices and a pulsing bass to hold the mood.

### Outro

The beat and pads fade slowly, like the end of a long, quiet conversation.

## Concepts in Focus

First Impressions credits Yasmin as a {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}, and her part shows what a feature can do. She does not take a verse of her own so much as shadow and answer Kele, so the song becomes a conversation between two people at the start of something. Consequence of Sound called her contribution angelic temptation next to his soulful whispers. A feature also connects scenes: Yasmin came to the record from UK dance music, where she had sung Gorgon City's first single, Real.

Under the voices runs a restrained {{ <concept_link name="house groove" /> }}. The kick and hi-hats keep the familiar house pattern, but everything is muted and spacious, more after-hours lounge than peak-time club. Consequence of Sound described the album moving through 2-step, garage, deep house and post-Burial house, and tied Kele's club turn to the Crosstown Rebels scene of labels like the one behind {{ <track_link source="infinity-ink-infinity.md" /> }}.

The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} is what holds the track together. With the vocals kept soft and close, the bass hums low and broad under every bar, and when the arrangement thins in the bridge it is the bass, not the beat, that keeps the mood alive.

## Artistic Context

Kele Okereke is best known as the frontman of the British indie band Bloc Party, but his solo records have explored club music and electronic production. First Impressions is the opening track of Trick, his second solo album, released on 13 October 2014 on Lilac Records and produced by Kele with XXXChange and Tom Belton, who also mixed this track. Trick is a nocturnal record that follows the course of a night and a new romance, and it leans, as Consequence of Sound noted, on broad basslines and club structures rather than guitars, reflecting years in which Kele had been making weekly house mixes.

## Impact and Reception

Trick entered the UK Albums Chart at number 99 and scored 67 on Metacritic, with reviews from Clash and NME at 7 out of 10 and the Guardian calling it confident, carnal disco. Critics noted how far it moved from Bloc Party's guitar-driven energy toward something softer and more seductive, and Consequence of Sound singled out First Impressions as a sensual after-hours opener. It is here for that understated intimacy: a frontman known for urgency choosing to whisper instead. Play it late at night and let it draw you in.
