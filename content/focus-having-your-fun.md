## Genre and Mood

**Focus** was one of Phil Asher's names, and **Having Your Fun** is the song that, in Qobuz's account, became a favourite at CoOp, the West London club night he co-founded. It belongs to broken beat, a dance style rooted in jazz-funk, hip-hop and drum and bass; Discogs adds future jazz, deep house and disco. The feelings named here, groovy and carefree, come from its title and its place on a late-night compilation, not from a review: none we found describes its sound in detail, so this page leans on who played what.

## Song Summary

The album credits are specific. Phil Asher wrote, produced and arranged the song; Maiya James sings it; Kaidi Tatham plays keyboards, credited as two Yamaha instruments, and an upright acoustic piano; Felix Hopkins edited the vocal and mixed the album. It first came out in 2002 as a 12-inch on Versatile, DJ Gilb'R's label, and that November it appeared at three minutes and forty-four seconds on the Focus album Sweet And Sour. The version on the streaming release of Late Night Tales runs almost exactly as long.

### The Players

A singer, a keyboard player on synthesizer and acoustic piano, and a producer arranging around them: a small cast for a dance record.

### The 12-Inch

The Versatile 12-inch put an extended version of 5:39 on one side and three versions by 4hero on the other, reworked by Dego: a full rework, a short 2000 Black Interlude and a bonus Bop Step. Its notes give the extended version 110 beats per minute and the rework 122.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }} can give a club track a second life. Each Late Night Tales is one artist's selection, and in 2003 George Evelyn of Nightmares On Wax put Having Your Fun near the end of his, fourteenth of sixteen tracks, after an opening by {{ <track_link source="dj-fitchie-joe-dukie-midnight-marauders.md" /> }}.

The 12-inch notes say its extended version contains a sample of Fonda Rae's Over Like a Fat Rat, an example of {{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} tying a 2002 record to an older dance record.

The {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} here came at once, not years later: 4hero's rework sat on the same record as the original and went on to compilations such as FabricLive 12.

## Artistic Context

Phil Asher grew up in West London and was best known as the driving force of Restless Soul, a name known above all for remixes. Focus was the alias he used for this 2002 release. He died in 2021; The Vinyl Factory called him a house and broken beat maestro when Benji B played a tribute mix that included this song in 4hero's version.

## Impact and Reception

Having Your Fun travelled from a club night to a 12-inch, an album, a 4hero rework and a Late Night Tales selection. Put it on late, as Nightmares On Wax did, and listen for the voice, the keys and the piano the credits name.
