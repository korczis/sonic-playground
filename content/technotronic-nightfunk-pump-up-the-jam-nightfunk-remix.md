## Genre and Mood

**Technotronic** made **Pump Up The Jam** in 1989 as a fusion of rap and deep house, filed under hip house and Eurodance; in **Pump Up The Jam - NightFunk Remix**, the Belgian producer **NightFunk** brings it into tech house, the style Insomniac lists as his. Energetic and groovy are the original's own qualities and the reason a club producer would want it. No review describes the remix itself, so this page says what is known and leaves the rest to your ears.

## Song Summary

The original was written by Manuela Kamosi, known as Ya Kid K, and Jo Bogaert under his alias Thomas de Quincey, who produced it. The remix keeps their credit; the release names NightFunk as mixer and Sound Products as producer. Universal Music Belgium released it as a single on 12 June 2020, running 6:04, two weeks after a 3:33 NightFunk Radio Edit. Insomniac describes NightFunk's signature as raw drums, punchy basslines, catchy vocals and a solid groove, a fair list of what to listen for.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} of a classic works between memory and change: the hook has to be recognisable within seconds, and everything around it can be new. Compare {{ <track_link source="kideko-the-jam.md" /> }}, where another club producer rebuilt the song from its a cappella in 2015.

The {{ <concept_link name="radio edit" /> }} here came first, on 29 May 2020, and the full remix followed on 12 June. The short version is for radio and streaming; the long one, almost twice its length, is for DJs.

Both versions point back to {{ <concept_link name="club culture" /> }}. Bogaert said he expected Pump Up the Jam to be a club hit, and it became a pop hit as well; NightFunk returned it to the place it was made for.

## Artistic Context

Technotronic was formed in 1987 in Aalst, Belgium, by Bogaert. Ya Kid K sang Pump Up the Jam, released on 18 August 1989, but the Congolese model Felly Kilingi appeared on the cover and mimed in the video, because the record company wanted a face and Ya Kid K did not want to promote it. It reached number two on both the Billboard Hot 100 and the UK Singles Chart, and Wikipedia calls it the first Eurodance song to become a hit in the United States. NightFunk has released on labels such as Trick, Solid Grooves Raw and Spinnin Deep.

## Impact and Reception

According to Insomniac, this remix gave NightFunk a number one single on Beatport, the download store for DJs, in 2020. The song had been remixed before, and D.O.N.S.'s version topped the British dance chart in 2005. Play the 1989 single first, then this, and listen for what thirty years changed in the drums.
