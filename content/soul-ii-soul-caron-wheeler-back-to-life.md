## Genre and Mood

**Soul II Soul** and **Caron Wheeler** made **Back To Life** a soul record that moves like a club track. uDiscover Music hears soul, reggae, dub, house, new jack swing and hip-hop in it at once. The voice is soulful, the lift is real, and the groove still fills a floor.

## Song Summary

Wheeler has said the song came out of a near-death experience: she "literally came back to life". On the 1989 debut album, Club Classics Vol. One, it was a mostly a cappella piece near the end of the record. For the single, Jazzie B and Nellee Hooper re-recorded it in two new versions; the second reworked the chorus, added However Do You Want Me to the title, and became the one the world knows. That album version is the one on Keep On Movin', the album's North American title; the official video carries the single.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the single bar by bar; these parts follow what its makers said about how it was built.

### Intro

It starts with Wheeler's voice. Engineer Arabella Rodriquez, Wheeler recalled, suggested bringing the beats in "almost at the start when it's just me singing".

### Verse 1

The beat underneath is borrowed: the drum track is sampled from Graham Central Station's The Jam. Jazzie B described the shuffle as reggae mixed with what would become known as hip-hop, breakbeats and electronic sounds.

### Chorus

The title phrase arrives here, the line the second single version added. The Reggae Philharmonic Orchestra also appears on the track, though no source says where its strings come in.

## Concepts in Focus

Back To Life is a lesson in the {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }}. The same song exists as an album a cappella, as two single versions, as 12-inch club mixes and, in 1993, as a jazz-tinged house remix by Masters At Work.

The single rests on {{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }}: a drum track lifted from a 1970s funk band, working as a {{ <concept_link name="breakbeat" /> }}, a recorded groove looped into a new one. It is why the song swings rather than marches.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Monty Whitebloom and Andy Delaney of Big TV!, was shot in Epping Forest. Hooper later co-produced Björk's {{ <track_link source="bjork-it-s-oh-so-quiet.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Soul II Soul began as a sound system run by Jazzie B, whose club nights at the Africa Centre in London often overflowed; their motto was "A happy face, a thumpin' bass, for a lovin' race!" Wheeler sang lead on the earlier single Keep On Movin' and on this song, and left at the end of 1989. Virgin released the single on 30 May 1989; Wheeler, Jazzie B, Hooper and Simon Law wrote it.

## Impact and Reception

Back To Life spent four weeks at number one in the UK, reached number four on the Billboard Hot 100, the group's only US top ten, and topped the US R&B and dance club charts. It won the Grammy for Best R&B Performance by a Duo or Group with Vocal. uDiscover calls it the moment London's underground went mainstream. Start it from the first word and wait for the beat to arrive.
