## Genre and Mood

**Guti**'s **No Promises** is tech house cut close to minimal, hypnotic and groovy: one long groove meant to move a room rather than tell a story. The album's own notes call it a truly driving dancefloor groove.

## Song Summary

No Promises is the fifth track of Patio De Juegos, Guti's first album, released by Desolat on 28 March 2011, and the digital original mix runs six minutes. The album is credited as written and produced by Guti; the track carries a featuring credit for Ryan Crosson, whom those notes introduce as Visionquest's, and Crosson's own channel posts it as Guti & Ryan Crosson. On the vinyl edition it sits on side A, right after the title track.

### The Groove

The album's notes credit Crosson's hand with the track's driving dancefloor character.

### The Sound Around It

Discogs files the record under minimal and tech house. Across the album, PopMatters heard sly keyboards over what it called exasperatingly constant house rhythms, while Beats Per Minute found a label like tribal minimal tech house inadequate for a record it heard as whimsical as its name.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} in club music is often another producer rather than a singer. Ryan Crosson was one of Visionquest; compare a record that crew put into a mix in {{ <track_link source="aquarius-heaven-can-t-buy-love.md" /> }}.

{{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} is the whole point of a six-minute track like this: the parts locking together so a DJ can ride them. Original Mix in the title marks the producer's own version, the one any later remixes are measured against.

{{ <concept_link name="mastering" /> }} is the last step before release, balancing the finished tracks for loudness and tone. Patio De Juegos was mastered by Enrico Mercaldi at Time Tools Mastering.

## Artistic Context

Guti is Gabriel Gutiérrez from Argentina, a pianist from a young age who, as he told Miami New Times in 2011, was playing in a big rock band when he became a Desolat artist and quit everything to start again. Loco Dice, the Düsseldorf DJ whose own imprint is Desolat, signed him in 2009. The album's title translates as Playground; Guti called it a heartfelt, honest and intimate record that starts and ends with solo piano.

## Impact and Reception

Reviews of the album were mixed: Beats Per Minute gave it 81 percent and called it a confident, unique and inviting debut, while PopMatters, at five out of ten, found it hardly unpleasant but missing what makes jazz interesting, such as improvisation. Guti said he was surprised that some songs went big. Let it run the full six minutes.
