## Genre and Mood

**Gin Wigmore** drives **Man Like That** with the stomp of blues, the warmth of soul and the swagger of rock. It is bold, confident and gritty, a short, fierce song that knows exactly what it wants.

## Song Summary

The song is a declaration of desire, a list of qualities in the kind of man the singer is after, delivered with a wink and a growl. Wigmore's voice is the main event: raspy, powerful and full of attitude, it sounds like it was cured in smoke and whisky. The arrangement is built for stomping, with hand claps, heavy drums and twangy guitars giving it a vintage, juke-joint feel while the production stays punchy and modern. At under three minutes it hits hard and leaves quickly. There is humour in it too: the lyric is a playful shopping list of manly virtues, and Wigmore sings it with the relish of someone who knows she holds all the cards.

## Musical Breakdown

The track wastes no time, building quickly from a lean groove to a full-throated finish.

### Intro

A stomping beat and hand claps kick things off, raw and rhythmic, with a guitar line snarling on top.

### Verse 1

Wigmore enters with a playful, teasing delivery, setting out her terms with a grin you can hear.

### Chorus

The hook bursts open, her voice soaring and cracking with energy as the band hits harder behind her.

### Bridge

The arrangement drops back to the beat and voice for a moment, building tension before the last chorus.

### Outro

The song ends with a final punch, as sudden and confident as everything before it.

## Artistic Context

Gin Wigmore is a singer-songwriter from New Zealand whose distinctive, husky voice set her apart early in her career. "Man Like That" comes from her second album, Gravel & Wine, released in 2011, a record that leaned into blues, soul and rockabilly influences with a darker, rougher edge than her debut. The song reached a large international audience when it was used in a Heineken commercial.

## Impact and Reception

"Man Like That" became one of Wigmore's best-known songs, its retro energy and instantly recognisable vocal making it a natural fit for screens and playlists alike. It showed how a contemporary artist could channel old blues and soul swagger without sounding like a pastiche. It is here for its sheer attitude, under three minutes of grit and charisma. Play it loud and stomp along.
