## Genre and Mood

**Platinum Doug** delivers a straight-up house record with **Move On (Original Club Mix)**, a dance track that is all about the club. There is nothing ornamental about it: the mood is energetic and groovy, quietly uplifting, the sound of a floor that has found its rhythm and has no intention of slowing down.

## Song Summary

As the title suggests, the track is about letting go and pushing forward, and the arrangement turns that idea into motion. A punchy kick, a bouncing, rubbery bassline and crisp percussion form the backbone, while a short vocal hook repeats the title like a chant. The release offered only this version, the original club mix of 5:07, with long intros and outros that give DJs room to work. The memorable detail is the way the vocal and bass lock together, each phrase landing right on the groove so the whole track feels like it is leaning forward.

## Musical Breakdown

Move On follows the classic architecture of a club mix, built for blending and for long, steady energy.

### Intro

A tight kick and hi-hats open the track, stripped back and ready for mixing, with the bass waiting in the wings.

### Build-up

The bassline drops in, bouncing and insistent, as extra percussion and small synth details stack on top.

### Main Section

The vocal hook enters, repeated over the groove, the track settling into its driving, feel-good rhythm.

### Bridge

A breakdown pulls back the kick, leaving the vocal and a few synth textures to build anticipation.

### Climax

The beat slams back in with every element running at once, the energy at its peak.

### Outro

The layers fall away until only drums remain, giving the next DJ a clean exit into their next record.

## Concepts in Focus

The title's "Original Club Mix" marks this as an {{ <concept_link name="extended mix" /> }}: the full-length version made for clubs and DJs rather than radio. Its drum-only intro and outro are not filler; they are where a DJ beat-matches it with the previous and next record. A radio edit would cut straight to the hook and stop after three minutes.

What carries it is a {{ <concept_link name="house groove" /> }}: a kick on every beat, a bassline that bounces against it and hi-hats on the off-beats, a pattern that has driven house music since Chicago in the 1980s. Listen to how little else is needed once those three parts lock together.

On top sits a {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }}, the title repeated as a chant. In a club track the hook works as much as rhythm as melody, a phrase the crowd can catch after one listen. ## Artistic Context

Platinum Doug is the Swiss producer Christian Hirt, an artist of the Swiss house label Enormous Tunes, where he released singles such as Serious Moods, Californian Knight and Cream before Move On, and later on No Definition. Move On came out on 24 February 2017 as a single-track release, catalogue ETR360, with Hirt credited as composer and lyricist. His tracks have appeared on club compilations such as Kontor's Top of the Clubs series and the label's own yearbooks, the natural habitat of functional house music.

## Impact and Reception

Move On is the kind of track that lives in DJ sets and club playlists rather than on charts, and it earns its place through dance-floor function. It is on this playground as a reminder that not every great house record needs a story; sometimes a bassline, a vocal and four on the floor are enough. Play it loud, preferably on a proper sound system, and move with it.
