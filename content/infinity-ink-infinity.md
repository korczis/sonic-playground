## Genre and Mood

**Infinity Ink** made a modern classic with **Infinity**, a deep house track that crosses over into big-room house without losing its electronic cool. The mood is hypnotic and groovy, quietly euphoric, the kind of record that sounds best when the sun is coming up and nobody wants the night to end.

## Song Summary

The track is built around a single, mesmerising idea: a low, half-whispered vocal repeating the title over a rubbery, rolling bassline, as if trying to stretch one moment into forever. Around it, crisp drums, minimal percussion and sparse synth details keep the groove lean and driving. The memorable detail is that bassline, warm and elastic, which locks the whole room into one long, unbroken movement.

## Musical Breakdown

Infinity relies on repetition and subtle variation, slowly drawing the listener deeper into its loop.

### Intro

Crisp drums and a muted bass pulse set the stage, with hints of percussion flickering at the edges.

### Build-up

The bassline grows fuller and more insistent as hi-hats and small synth details stack up.

### Main Section

The vocal hook enters, low and hypnotic, riding the elastic bassline as the groove settles into its full shape.

### Bridge

A brief breakdown pulls back the drums, leaving the vocal and bass suspended for a moment of tension.

### Climax

The full groove returns, the bassline driving harder and the vocal more insistent, the track at its most euphoric.

### Outro

The elements slip away one by one, leaving the drums and bass to carry the loop into the next record.

## Artistic Context

Infinity Ink are the London-based duo of Italian producer Luca Cazal and British singer and producer Ali Love, who is also known as a member of Hot Natured. After early releases on Hot Creations, they released Infinity in 2012 on Damian Lazarus's Crosstown Rebels label. The track arrived at the height of the UK deep house revival and became one of the label's most anticipated records of the period. The original EP was followed by a remix package, including a version by Claude VonStroke that went straight to the top end of the download charts.

## Impact and Reception

Infinity became a summer anthem, one of the most played records in Ibiza clubs that season, and it has remained a staple of DJ sets ever since. Few deep house records of its era have aged so gracefully, and its hook still gets an instant cheer when it drops. It is here because it is simply one of those tracks that never seems to get old. Let it loop, and let it last.
