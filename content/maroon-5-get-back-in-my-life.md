## Genre and Mood

**Maroon 5**'s **Get Back In My Life** is pop rock from the band's third album, Hands All Over, and one of its brisker moments: upbeat and energetic on a record that reviewers otherwise heard as smoother and more mellow than what came before. It was never a single, and very little has been written about it, so this page sticks to what is documented.

## Song Summary

The song is credited to three members of the band, Adam Levine, Jesse Carmichael and James Valentine, both as composers and as lyricists, and it was produced by Robert John "Mutt" Lange. It runs 3:37 and sits ninth on the standard twelve-track album. No source discusses its lyric or its arrangement in any detail.

## Musical Breakdown

### Main Section

The only description of the music itself comes from Tamlyn Stewart's review in the Northern Advocate: "Get Back In My Life picks up the pace and will set feet tapping and heads nodding." The same review found the album as a whole moving "away from up-tempo rock into pop with a far more mellow sound", which is the background against which this track stands out. No source describes its intro, sections or ending, so they are not described here.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} can change how a band sounds without changing who writes the songs. Lange called the band himself after hearing it was writing a new album, and Maroon 5 spent two months writing and recording with him at his studio by Lake Geneva in Switzerland. Ashley Rhoades of The Stanford Daily wrote that the record's "glossy finish is largely thanks to producer Robert "Mutt" Lange", whose earlier work was with rock and country artists. Levine said of him: "He worked me harder than anyone ever has."

Get Back In My Life is a {{ <concept_link name="deep cut" /> }}: never a single, known mostly to those who play the album through. Hands All Over's singles were Misery, Give a Little More and Never Gonna Leave This Bed, and the 2011 reissue added Moves like Jagger, co-produced by Shellback, who also worked on {{ <track_link source="taylor-swift-actually-romantic.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Maroon 5 began in Los Angeles in 1994 as Kara's Flowers and took its current name in 2001 before Songs About Jane. The band's press release called Hands All Over "a killer hybrid of rock, pop, funk and R&B", and Levine summed up the album as "just great pop". It was released in September 2010 by A&M Octone and debuted at number two on the Billboard 200.

## Impact and Reception

The album drew generally favourable reviews, a Metacritic score of 63, but none of the reviews Wikipedia quotes mentions this track. Eight years later the band reached number one with the softer {{ <track_link source="maroon-5-cardi-b-girls-like-you-feat-cardi-b-cardi-b-version.md" /> }}.
