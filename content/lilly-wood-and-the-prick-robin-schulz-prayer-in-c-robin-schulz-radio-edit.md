## Genre and Mood

**Lilly Wood and The Prick** wrote the song, and **Robin Schulz** turned it into a global hit with **Prayer in C (Robin Schulz Radio Edit)**. The track fuses deep house, dance and folk roots, and its mood is melancholic, energetic and deeply bittersweet: a sad song you dance to with your eyes closed. It is a clear example of how a remix can change a song's meaning without changing a word.

## Song Summary

The lyric is bleak, a prayer about a world in trouble, sung by Nili Hadida in a cool, almost resigned voice. Robin Schulz keeps that voice and the looping acoustic guitar riff, then sets them over a bouncing deep house beat and a warm bassline. The contrast between the dark lyric and the bright, propulsive groove is what makes the remix so addictive. Schulz's touch is light: he adds space, a gentle swing and a warm low end, and resists the temptation to overload the arrangement. The guitar riff, simple and instantly memorable, does the rest.

## Musical Breakdown

The radio edit is concise and focused, building around the guitar riff and the vocal hook.

### Intro

The plucked guitar riff opens the track, quickly joined by a soft kick and a rounded bass that set the bouncing tempo.

### Chorus

### Outro

The riff and beat fade together, leaving a lingering sense of melancholy.

## Concepts in Focus

This is the {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} as transformation. The 2010 original is a stripped-back folk-pop song; Schulz kept Hadida's vocal and the guitar figure and replaced everything around them with a house beat and bassline. The words are the same, but the song now lives on dance floors and summer radio, and for most listeners the remix simply is the song. Compare {{ <track_link source="the-avener-ane-brun-to-let-myself-go.md" /> }}, part of the same mid-2010s wave of folk voices on deep house beats.

The track's identity is its {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }}: a short, plucked acoustic guitar phrase repeated almost throughout. Because it is simple and slightly melancholic, it can loop for minutes without tiring, and it gives the track a hook before any word is sung. Listen to how the beat and bass are arranged to leave that riff in front.

This version is the {{ <concept_link name="radio edit" /> }}, about three minutes long: a quick start, fewer repeats and the hook early, the shape radio and streaming playlists prefer. A longer club mix would spend more time on drums-only intros and outros for DJs; the edit gets straight to the riff and the voice.

## Artistic Context

Lilly Wood and The Prick is a French duo of singer Nili Hadida and multi-instrumentalist Benjamin Cotto, who wrote the song together. The original "Prayer in C" appeared on their 2010 album *Invincible Friends*. The German DJ and producer Robin Schulz first shared his remix as a free download, which upset the song's copyright owner, Wagram Music, before it was officially released on 6 June 2014 on Tonspiel and Warner; it later opened his debut album *Prayer*.

## Impact and Reception

The remix became one of the biggest dance hits of 2014. It reached number one in France, Germany, the UK, Austria, Belgium and Denmark among others, topped the US Hot Dance/Electronic Songs chart and reached number 23 on the Billboard Hot 100, and was certified diamond in Germany and triple platinum in the UK. It made Robin Schulz an international name and became a template for a wave of similar summer remixes. It is here for that balance of sadness and sunlight. Let the guitar riff pull you in.
