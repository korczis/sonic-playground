## Genre and Mood

**Gin Wigmore** sings **Black Sheep** with her chin up and her boots on. It is rock with blues grit and a soul singer's swagger, a stomping anthem that is defiant, rebellious and bold from its first chant to its last shout.

## Song Summary

The song is a proud declaration of not fitting in, a celebration of the outsider who stopped apologising for it. Wigmore's voice, raspy and powerful with a rough, smoky edge, sits on top of a stomping rhythm, guitars and gang vocals that make the whole thing feel like a crowd marching behind her. AllMusic described the album it opened as all big, stomping drums and kinetic high vocals, and this is its clearest example. The production borrows from vintage rock and roll and blues stompers, but the attitude is entirely her own.

## Musical Breakdown

The track is short, direct and built for impact, with each section raising the stakes.

### Intro

Wordless backing vocals open the song, a gang chant that sets the mood before the first verse.

### Verse 1

Wigmore enters with a low, gravelly growl, laying out her case as the family outcast with a wink rather than a complaint.

### Chorus

A one-line pre-chorus, sung twice, leads into the hook: stretched, stuttering syllables, the title worn like a badge, and backing voices that make it sound as if the whole flock of misfits has joined in.

### Bridge

There is no separate bridge. After the second chorus the first verse comes back, a moment of swagger before the final charge.

### Climax

The last chorus hits hardest, her voice at full rasp and the band stomping behind her.

### Outro

The song ends on its tag line, repeated, as blunt as its message.

## Concepts in Focus

Black Sheep is a compact lesson in {{ <concept_link name="verse-chorus form" /> }} with one twist. Its sections are verse, pre-chorus and chorus, twice; then, where many pop songs would put a bridge, it simply repeats the first verse and goes back to the chorus. That keeps the song barely over three minutes and never lets the energy dip.

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is built for crowds. Wigmore breaks words in the chorus into stretched, stammering syllables and closes on a short tag line repeated four times in the outro, the kind of phrase people can shout back after one listen. The chanted backing vocals, sung by producer Butch Walker and his band, make that crowd part of the record itself.

Few songs show the power of the {{ <concept_link name="soundtrack" /> }} placement better. Black Sheep turned up in Grey's Anatomy, Teen Wolf and The Good Wife, in trailers for Orange Is the New Black and The Other Woman, in a Nissan commercial in 2017 and in the 2018 video game Far Cry 5, reaching listeners who never saw it on a chart.

## Artistic Context

Gin Wigmore is a singer-songwriter from Auckland, New Zealand. "Black Sheep", which she wrote with Butch Walker, was the lead single from her second album, Gravel & Wine, recorded in Santa Monica in 2011 with Walker producing and his backing band, The Black Widows, playing. Before the sessions she spent two months travelling through Mississippi and Alabama for inspiration, and the album leaned into vintage soul, blues and rock and roll. On the recording Walker plays guitar and percussion, Jake Sinclair bass and organ and Fran Capitanelli acoustic guitar.

## Impact and Reception

Released on 26 September 2011, "Black Sheep" debuted and peaked at number 13 on the New Zealand chart, and Gravel & Wine went straight to number one there. Its afterlife on screen has been bigger still, and Wigmore performed it on The Ellen DeGeneres Show when the album reached the US in 2013.
