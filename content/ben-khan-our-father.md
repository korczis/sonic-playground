## Genre and Mood

**Ben Khan**'s **our father** is the darkest corner of an album that mixes electro-pop, funk and electronic production. Written in Music heard it as experimental funk in the manner of Prince; The Music, reviewing the album, heard menace, a thump more likely to unsettle a listener than to excite one. Gritty and dark, it sits inside Khan's sound and against its sunnier moments.

## Song Summary

"our father" is the ninth of fifteen tracks on Ben Khan, released by Dirty Hit on 10 August 2018, and at 2:22 one of its shorter songs. Its title borrows the opening words of the Lord's Prayer. The National Student, which found Khan's words often overpowered by his production, named it as one of the songs that show he does not neglect his lyrics: our father, it wrote, tackles religion. What the reviews agree on is the beat. The Indiependent liked the soulful vocal but felt it was lost in a distorted, muffled kick drum.

## Musical Breakdown

The reviews describe layers of sound rather than a structure, so the parts below follow what they heard, not the order of the song.

### The Beat

A kick drum that is distorted and muffled at once, in The Indiependent's account, and the demented thump The Music described. Each review mentions it.

### The Groove

Under that thump, Written in Music heard experimental funk with Prince's name on it. Listen for the funk that moves beneath the noise.

### The Voice

Soulful, by The Indiependent's account, and partly buried. Listen for how much of it reaches you through the beat.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="kick drum" /> }} is the lowest drum of the kit, the one that marks the pulse. Here, by the reviews' account, it is also the main event: not a steady background thud but a muffled, heavy blow that shapes the whole track.

That blow is rough because of {{ <concept_link name="distortion" /> }}: driving a sound past what it can cleanly hold adds grit and harmonics. On {{ <track_link source="ben-khan-love-faded.md" /> }}, the next track, the distortion is on a guitar; here it is on the drum.

{{ <concept_link name="mixing" /> }} decides how loud each part is and where it sits. The Indiependent felt the vocal lost the battle with the kick, and The National Student found Khan's words overpowered by his production across the album; whether that is a fault or a mood is the listener's call.

## Artistic Context

Ben Khan is a London producer, singer and songwriter whose EPs 1992 and 1000 preceded this self-titled debut, produced by Flood, known for his work with PJ Harvey, Depeche Mode and Nine Inch Nails. Written in Music notes that as a teenager Khan listened to old blues, seventies rock and nineties grunge. The album moves between funk guitar and electronics; our father is where it turns heaviest, a contrast with {{ <track_link source="ben-khan-do-it-right.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

The track drew only passing mentions, and divided ones: experimental funk to Written in Music, unsettling menace to The Music, a good vocal lost in the mix to The Indiependent. Listen once for the kick, then once for the voice.
