## Genre and Mood

**Marcus Marr** and **Nick Murphy**, then performing as Chet Faker, made **The Trouble With Us** together, and it lands squarely between disco, funk, soul and modern electronic production. The mood is groovy on the surface and longing underneath, a bittersweet song about a relationship going wrong that insists on making you dance anyway.

## Song Summary

The lyric circles the familiar problem of two people who cannot quite make it work, but the music refuses to sulk. A funky bassline, crisp drums and a chopping guitar riff lock into a loose, live-sounding jam, while Murphy's smoky, soulful voice pleads and teases over the top. Music Feeds described it as "fuelled by catchy hooks and a funk-flecked guitar riff", and Inthemix heard an "intergalactic funk-tinged track". The memorable detail is the feel: it sounds like two musicians having a great time in a small room, which is more or less exactly what happened.

## Musical Breakdown

The song plays like a studio jam shaped into a pop single of under four minutes.

### Intro

Drums and bass kick in straight away with a confident, disco-funk strut, guitar stabs flickering at the edges.

### Verse 1

Murphy's voice slides in, relaxed and husky, telling his side of the story over the rolling groove.

### Build-up

Keys and extra guitar layers stack up and the rhythm tightens, the tension rising towards the hook.

### Chorus

The title line hits with a sing-along lift, frustration turned into a groove you cannot stand still to.

### Bridge

The arrangement thins to bass, drums and voice, a brief, intimate moment before the band surges back.

### Climax

The final choruses pile on guitar licks, vocal ad-libs and percussion, the jam at full heat.

### Outro

The groove rides out with the band still playing, as if nobody wanted the session to end.

## Concepts in Focus

The song is built on a guitar {{ <concept_link name="riff" /> }}: a short, funky figure, played by Marr, that repeats under the verses and choruses and gives the track its identity. In disco and funk the rhythm guitar is often as important as the vocal, chopping chords on and around the beat so the band feels lighter on its feet. Listen for how the riff never changes much while the voice moves around it.

That riff is one part of a {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} made by two people playing most of the instruments themselves. The sleeve credits Marr with production, mixing, keyboards, guitar, bass and backing vocals and Faker with lead vocals, synth bass and keyboards. Recorded over four days, 6 to 9 July 2015, the parts sit together with the small looseness of musicians reacting to each other, which is what makes a groove feel human.

## Artistic Context

Chet Faker is the Australian musician Nick Murphy, born in 1988, whose name was a tribute to Chet Baker; he dropped it in 2016 to release music under his own name and revived it in 2020. Marcus Marr is a London DJ, producer and guitarist. The two met online after Faker posted one of Marr's songs on Twitter; Marr thanked him, they exchanged ideas and then recorded the four-track Work EP together at Marr's studio in Brixton, South London. The Trouble With Us premiered on Triple J's Good Nights with Linda Marigliano on 15 October 2015 and was released the next day as the lead single; Work followed on 4 December 2015 through Detail Records and Downtown, with every track credited to both.

## Impact and Reception

Triple J made it a pick of the week, and its video, directed by Kinopravda and shot at Korda Studios, followed on 10 December 2015. The single gave Chet Faker fans a looser, more danceable side of him and introduced Marr to a much wider audience. It is on this playground because it turns heartbreak into a party without losing its soul. Turn it up and let the bassline argue the case.
