## Genre and Mood

**Marcus Marr** and **Nick Murphy**, then still performing as Chet Faker, made **The Trouble With Us** together, and it lands squarely between disco, funk, soul and modern electronic production. The mood is groovy on the surface and longing underneath, a bittersweet song about a relationship going wrong that insists on making you dance anyway.

## Song Summary

The lyric circles the familiar problem of two people who cannot quite make it work, but the music refuses to sulk. A tight, funky bassline, crisp drums and chopping guitar lock into a loose, live-sounding jam, while Murphy's smoky, soulful voice pleads and teases over the top. The memorable detail is the feel: it sounds like two musicians having a great time in a small room, which is more or less exactly what happened.

## Musical Breakdown

The song plays like a studio jam shaped into a pop single.

### Intro

Drums and bass kick in straight away with a confident, disco-funk strut, guitar stabs flickering at the edges.

### Verse 1

Murphy's voice slides in, relaxed and husky, telling his side of the story over the rolling groove.

### Build-up

Keys and extra guitar layers stack up and the rhythm tightens, the tension rising towards the hook.

### Chorus

The title line hits with a sing-along lift, frustration turned into a groove you cannot stand still to.

### Bridge

The arrangement thins to bass, drums and voice, a brief, intimate moment before the band surges back.

### Climax

The final choruses pile on guitar licks, vocal ad-libs and percussion, the jam at full heat.

### Outro

The groove rides out with the band still playing, as if nobody wanted the session to end.

## Artistic Context

Chet Faker is the Australian musician Nick Murphy, who later returned to recording under his own name; Marcus Marr is a London producer and guitarist. The two connected on Twitter and recorded the Work EP over four days in July 2015 at Marr's studio in Brixton. The Trouble With Us premiered on Triple J in October 2015 as the lead single, and the four-track EP followed on 4 December 2015 through Detail Records and Downtown Records, with every track credited to Marr and Murphy.

## Impact and Reception

The single gave Chet Faker fans a looser, more danceable side of him and introduced Marr to a much wider audience, becoming a favourite at indie-disco nights. It is on this playground because it turns heartbreak into a party without losing its soul. Turn it up and let the bassline argue the case.
