## Genre and Mood

**Florence + The Machine** open How Big, How Blue, How Beautiful with **Ship To Wreck**, and they open it at a gallop. It is folk-pop with an alternative-rock spine, energetic and bright on the surface, while the lyric is about tearing down what you have built. That gap is the song: defiant in its sound, bittersweet in its words.

## Song Summary

Florence Welch has said she wrote it with her self-destructive side in mind, about the way making something can turn into wrecking it. The lyric describes a night of confusion in nautical images, a damaged ship drifting ashore as a picture of a relationship gone wrong. The music refuses to sink with it: Rolling Stone heard it as upbeat and bouncy, and The Fader argued that Welch's voice and the big arrangement turn the doubt into something close to defiant ownership of one's own choices.

### Intro

There is no slow build. Spin compared the immediate intensity of the opening to the band's first single, Kiss with a Fist.

### Verse

Acoustic guitar strums over a steady backbeat with a lively bass line, while Hammond organ and glockenspiel colour the edges.

### Chorus

The title question arrives over a quickened pulse, and the band rides it as a refrain built for a crowd.

### Outro

The song is short, under four minutes; no review describes how it ends.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }} does the arguing. Acoustic guitar by Leo Abrahams, electric guitar by Rob Ackroyd, drums by Chris Hayden, organ by James Hallawell and three backing singers make a full, warm band sound, and that warmth is what makes a song about self-sabotage feel like a celebration. The Line of Best Fit called it Welch's most conventional song, yet found the gap between lyric and sound remarkably subversive.

The {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} here is two people. Markus Dravs produced the album and played the glockenspiel; Kid Harpoon co-wrote the song, co-produced it and played bass, guitar, drums and synth. Welch has said Dravs banned songs about water, which makes a song full of ships a small act of mischief.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, directed by Vincent Haycock with choreography by Ryan Heffington, shows Welch struggling with an alter ego who wrecks a dinner party and her relationship. It later became chapter four of the short film The Odyssey and was nominated for Best Rock Video at the 2015 MTV Video Music Awards.

## Artistic Context

The song was recorded in London in 2014 and released by Island on 9 April 2015 as the album's second single. Its lyric is referred to again later on the record, in {{ <track_link source="florence-the-machine-delilah.md" /> }} and St. Jude.

## Impact and Reception

It reached number 27 in the UK, topped the US Adult Alternative Airplay chart and was nominated for Best Pop Duo/Group Performance at the 58th Grammy Awards. Ticketmaster later called it some of Welch's poppiest work. Turn it up and listen to how cheerful a shipwreck can sound.
