## Genre and Mood

**Ian Brown**'s **Time Is My Everything** is alternative rock with an indie singer's ease, but its colour comes from somewhere else entirely: a brass part that critics heard as mariachi. The mood is uplifting and confident; Hot Press's John Walshe counted it among the album's self-empowerment songs and heard uplifting brass in it.

## Song Summary

Brown wrote the song with Tim Hutton, and the streaming credits name Brown, Dave McCracken and Tim Wills as its co-producers. Its signature is the horns. Exclaim!'s Cam Lindsay heard Brown use a mariachi band, or a sample of one, to lead it; PopMatters heard a little mariachi-style brass thrown into the mix with another strong melody. No source discusses the lyric in detail, but the Providence Phoenix heard what seemed to be a tribute to a newborn child.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }} is what critics wrote about. The same horns became mariachi for three of them, spaghetti-western soundtracks for Phil Udell of Hot Press, who heard a subtle brass section, and Love's Forever Changes for Treble. Compare the brass Brown used five years later in {{ <track_link source="ian-brown-stellify.md" /> }}.

A {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }} usually gathers older hits. The Greatest, released by Polydor in September 2005, covers material from Brown's first four solo albums and took in this single only eight months after its release; its limited edition added a Stateside Hombres Overeasy mix and a Them Lads remix.

## Artistic Context

Brown sang with the Stone Roses until their split in 1996 and began his solo career in 1998. Solarized, his fourth solo album, came out on Fiction on 13 September 2004, produced by Brown and McCracken, and reached number seven in the UK. Time Is My Everything followed as its second single on 17 January 2005, with a music video now on Brown's VEVO channel.

## Impact and Reception

The single entered the UK chart on 29 January 2005 and peaked at number 15, spending four weeks on the chart. Udell found Brown upbeat and making really interesting records again; Treble thought the rest of the song stood up to the Love comparison. Let the horns lead you in.
