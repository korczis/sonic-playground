## Genre and Mood

**Niklas Ibach** takes Dotan's **Hungry** and recasts it as deep house, and the **Remix** is a lesson in keeping a song's soul while changing its body. The electronic pulse is gentle and hypnotic, the mood still melancholic, and the longing at the heart of the original survives every bar.

## Song Summary

Dotan's original is a folk-pop plea, a song of longing and need sung with raw, unguarded directness. Ibach keeps the voice and its emotional weight front and centre, then sets it on a soft four-on-the-floor beat with warm bass and airy pads. The result works as a dance record and as a quiet listen. The memorable detail is how the steady kick makes the ache feel less like despair and more like motion.

## Musical Breakdown

The remix follows a classic deep-house arc, built for a gradual lift rather than a big drop.

### Intro

A soft kick and muted percussion establish the groove, with hints of melody drifting in at the edges.

### Verse 1

Dotan's voice enters over the rolling beat, intimate and slightly worn, carrying the song's plea.

### Build-up

Pads and bass thicken beneath the vocal, raising the energy without crowding the space.

### Chorus

The hook arrives with a fuller groove, the vocal lifted by the beat into something almost hopeful.

### Outro

The production thins back to its pulse and fades, leaving a lingering, bittersweet glow and the echo of that plea still hanging in the air.

## Concepts in Focus

This is a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} in the classic mid-2010s sense: a young producer takes an acoustic, singer-songwriter recording, keeps the vocal and its emotional weight, and rebuilds everything underneath for the club. Niklas Ibach first shared it in September 2014, when he was about twenty, as a free download for anyone who liked his Facebook page; a blog that picked it up called it a vibey, addictive rework. The label release followed in 2015. A remix like this can widen a song's audience without flattening it.

The change of genre comes from the {{ <concept_link name="four-on-the-floor" /> }} kick. Dotan's original moves like a folk-pop song; Ibach puts a soft kick on every beat underneath it, and suddenly the same plea becomes something you can move to. The steady pulse turns the ache into motion rather than despair.

The arrangement follows a gentle {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }} rather than a festival drop: pads and bass thicken gradually under the vocal until the chorus arrives on a fuller groove. Compare the way another singer's fragile voice is carried by a deep house beat in {{ <track_link source="the-avener-ane-brun-to-let-myself-go.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Dotan (Dotan Harpenau), born in Jerusalem and raised in Amsterdam, released his self-produced album 7 Layers in January 2014; it reached number 2 in the Netherlands and went double platinum, carried by the triple-platinum single Home. Hungry, one of its best-known songs, reached number 2 on Studio Brussel's list of 2015 in Belgium. Niklas Ibach is a young German producer from the melodic deep house scene, part of the wave of remixers who turned acoustic and indie-pop vocals into gentle club music in those years. His remix, first shared online in 2014, was released in 2015 through Virgin and Universal Music in Germany and appeared that November on the compilation Bravo Dance 2015. The video on this page is the remix as uploaded on Dotan's own channel.

## Impact and Reception

The remix became a staple of deep house playlists and YouTube promotion channels. It belongs here as a bridge between songwriting and the dance floor: listeners who came for the beat stay for the voice. Play it as the evening turns, when you want to move a little but still feel something.
