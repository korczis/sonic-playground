## Genre and Mood

**Niklas Ibach** takes Dotan's **Hungry** and recasts it as deep house, and the **Remix** is a lesson in keeping a song's soul while changing its body. The electronic pulse is gentle and hypnotic, the mood still melancholic, and the longing at the heart of the original survives every bar.

## Song Summary

Dotan's original is a folk-pop plea, a song of longing and need sung with raw, unguarded directness. Ibach keeps the voice and its emotional weight front and centre, then sets it on a soft four-to-the-floor beat with warm bass and airy pads. The result works as a dance record and as a quiet listen. The memorable detail is how the steady kick makes the ache feel less like despair and more like motion.

## Musical Breakdown

The remix follows a classic deep-house arc, built for a gradual lift rather than a big drop.

### Intro

A soft kick and muted percussion establish the groove, with hints of melody drifting in at the edges.

### Verse 1

Dotan's voice enters over the rolling beat, intimate and slightly worn, carrying the song's plea.

### Build-up

Pads and bass thicken beneath the vocal, raising the energy without crowding the space.

### Chorus

The hook arrives with a fuller groove, the vocal lifted by the beat into something almost hopeful.

### Outro

The production thins back to its pulse and fades, leaving a lingering, bittersweet glow and the echo of that plea still hanging in the air.

## Artistic Context

Dotan is a Dutch singer-songwriter whose album 7 Layers made him one of the biggest domestic acts in the Netherlands in the mid-2010s, and "Hungry" was among its best-known songs. Niklas Ibach is a German producer from the melodic deep-house scene, part of the wave of remixers who turned acoustic and indie-pop vocals into gentle club music during that period. The video on this page is the remix as uploaded on Dotan's own channel.

## Impact and Reception

The remix became a staple of deep-house playlists and YouTube promotion channels, the kind of track that travels quietly from one sunset set to the next. It belongs here as a perfect bridge between songwriting and the dancefloor. It also shows how a good remix can widen a song's audience without flattening it: listeners who came for the beat stay for the voice. Play it as the evening turns, when you want to move a little but still feel something.
