## Genre and Mood

**Electrocute**'s **Tiger Toy** pairs a band from Berlin's electroclash years with **The Lady Tigra**, a voice from 1980s Miami hip-hop. Discogs files the album it lives on under synth-pop, electro and punk, and the playful, bold tags here are a reading of that mix and of its title rather than of any review: no critic has written about the song on its own.

## Song Summary

Because no review describes Tiger Toy, this page rests on its credits and releases. The song first appeared on the On the Beat EP, released by Sargent Records in April 2008, where Nicole Morier is credited for the vocals, the production and the guitar on this track, and The Lady Tigra for vocals. In July 2009 it became track six of Double Diamond, an album released only in Japan, on Pony Up Records, now crediting her as the featured artist. It runs 3:25.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} brings another audience and another style into a song. Here the guest comes from a different decade and scene: The Lady Tigra was one half of L'Trimm, the Miami bass duo whose Cars with the Boom, an ode to subwoofers, reached number 54 on the Billboard Hot 100 in 1988. In 2008 she moved to Los Angeles and released her first solo project, Please Mr. Boombox.

{{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }} is where Electrocute began. Morier, a singer from Albuquerque, founded the band with Mia Dime in Berlin in 2002, drawn by the city's electropop and electroclash scene; compare the German-language {{ <track_link source="electrocute-paul-pm-kleiner-dicker-junge-feat-paul-pm.md" /> }} from those first years.

## Artistic Context

After an EP and an album on Emperor Norton, Dime left; Morier toured with Holly Doll and met the Sydney DJ Legs Le Brock in Australia in 2005. Morier later moved to Los Angeles and put the band on hold to pursue her songwriting career, then revived it as a duo with Le Brock. Double Diamond, Wikipedia notes, gathers every track of On the Beat plus six new songs and three instrumentals.

## Impact and Reception

Tiger Toy is not among the singles Wikipedia lists for the band, and Double Diamond stayed a Japan-only album until Gummizelle Records reissued it digitally in November 2016, the version YouTube carries. No chart or review records its reception. Listen for the two voices.
