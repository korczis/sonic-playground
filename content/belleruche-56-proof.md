## Genre and Mood

**Belleruche** open The Liberty EP with **56% Proof**, and it belongs to the sound the London trio built from a singer, a guitarist and a turntablist: trip-hop and soul at a downtempo pace, filed by Discogs under downtempo, future jazz and trip hop and tagged by the band itself as blues, electronic, soul and turntable. Groovy and soulful are the words that sound suggests; beyond those tags, no review we found describes this song, and this page says so rather than invent.

## Song Summary

Tru Thoughts released the EP on 1 March 2010 (30 March in the United States) on 12-inch vinyl with a CD inside and as a download. 56% Proof runs about four and a half minutes and is side A, track one. It is one of only two new songs on the record, beside Gold Rush; the rest are acoustic versions and remixes. The sleeve credits all three members with writing, producing and engineering, and notes that this was the last record made in Flat 2. The title reads like a spirits label; the lyric is not printed on the band's pages, and no source explains it.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="turntablism" /> }} is the record player played as an instrument, and it is a third of this band, whose debut album was called Turntable Soul Music. Listen for what the decks add beside the guitar, here and on {{ <track_link source="belleruche-clockwatching.md" /> }} later the same year.

{{ <concept_link name="mastering" /> }} is the last polish before a record is made. Discogs credits Jason Howes with mastering the two new songs at The Premises Studios, and Tim Rowkins with mastering the 12-inch at Hell Yeah Mastering: one song, prepared twice for two formats.

A {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }} gave the song its afterlife. In June 2010 the Los Angeles label Label Love offered it as a free download on its sampler Label Love 001, and in October 2013 it returned on Belleruche's Best Of.

## Artistic Context

Ricky Fabulous and DJ Modest were playing guitar-and-turntable sets in London bars when they met Kathrin deBoer at a market, Tru Thoughts recounts. They pressed their first 7-inches on their own Hippoflex label, then signed to Tru Thoughts, which released their first albums and {{ <track_link source="belleruche-anything-you-want-not-that.md" /> }}, remixed four times on the EP's CD. The Liberty EP previewed the third album, 270 Stories, released that October; Gold Rush made it, 56% Proof did not.
