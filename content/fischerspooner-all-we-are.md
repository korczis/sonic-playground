## Genre and Mood

**Fischerspooner** made **All We Are** the quietest kind of statement on Odyssey, their 2005 second album. It is art pop and electro-pop by an act known for electroclash, but the band itself called it the most acoustic track on the record. The mood is atmospheric and dreamy, with a spiritual pull in the lyric: critics heard space, lounge and something close to transcendence in it.

## Song Summary

The song began with Warren Fischer, who wanted a slow-burning, timeless rock anthem built on repeated minimal patterns in the manner of Philip Glass. He travelled to Los Angeles to record musicians with the producer Tony Hoffer and the engineer Nicolas Vernhes at Sunset Sound. All We Are was also where the album's collaboration with the songwriter Linda Perry began: she took the unfinished instrumental and added melodic ideas, and Casey Spooner then shaped those melodies further and wrote the words, drawing on images of light, creation and the collective unconscious.

## Concepts in Focus

An {{ <concept_link name="ostinato" /> }} is a pattern repeated persistently while other parts change above it. Fischer asked for repeated minimal patterns after Philip Glass, so the song's slow burn comes from repetition rather than from a big chorus.

The credits show how the {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} role was shared on Odyssey. Fischerspooner produced the album with several collaborators, including Tony Hoffer, Nicolas Vernhes and the French producer Mirwais; on this track Vernhes is also a co-writer, beside Fischer, Spooner and Perry.

Compare it with {{ <track_link source="fischerspooner-a-kick-in-the-teeth.md" /> }}, the other Perry co-write from the album, built instead from a chorus of stacked voices.

## Artistic Context

Fischerspooner, the New York duo of Warren Fischer and Casey Spooner, came out of the electroclash scene with their debut #1. Odyssey, released by Capitol on 5 April 2005 in the US and on 11 April elsewhere, took more than two years. PopMatters called it a warm, organic rejection of the cool, digital synth-pop of the debut. All We Are is the eleventh of twelve tracks, just before the closing Boredoms cover.

## Impact and Reception

Odyssey drew a Metacritic score of 70, and in November 2005 All We Are came out on a single paired with A Kick in the Teeth. Reviews of the song itself were few and split: PopMatters and the Daily Cardinal praised its atmosphere, while Scene Point Blank counted it among the album's lower points. Give it time; it is built to grow by repetition.
