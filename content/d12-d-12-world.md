## Genre and Mood

**D12**'s **D-12 World** is hip-hop as a crew roll call: five rappers, one beat, one hook. It is irreverent like the whole album and defiant in its framing. Plugged In heard the title cut welcome the listener to a world of sex, Ecstasy and guns while waving off the real one, and called it the umbrella theme of the record.

## Song Summary

The track runs three minutes and ten seconds and is the only one on D12 World that Kanye West produced. The writing credits name West and the five members other than Eminem: Denaun Porter (Kon Artis), Von Carlisle (Kuniva), Ondre Moore (Swifty McVay), Rufus Johnson (Bizarre) and DeShaun Holton (Proof). Marshall Mathers is not among them; the Detroit News counted it among the album's Em-free tracks.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes the beat in detail or gives times; this guide follows the song sections of the Genius transcription.

### Intro

Bizarre opens the track before the first chorus.

### Chorus

Kon Artis takes the hook, the welcome into D12's world. It returns twice more, the one fixed point in a track that belongs to whoever holds the microphone.

### First Round

Five verses in a row: Bizarre, Swifty McVay, Kuniva, Kon Artis and Proof, one voice after another on the same beat.

### Second Round and Outro

After the second chorus the turns shrink: Kuniva again, Bizarre and Swifty together, Swifty trading with Kuniva and Kon Artis, then Kon Artis alone, before a last chorus and an outro.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="posse cut" /> }} is a showcase of difference, and D-12 World is one in its plainest form, its second half cutting the verses shorter until members share them. Compare the more crafted relay of {{ <track_link source="d12-6-in-the-morning.md" /> }} on the same album.

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} carries a lot here. With five rappers and no Eminem, Kon Artis's chorus is what makes the track a song rather than a cypher.

A {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} from outside the group was the selling point. West's debut album, The College Dropout, had come out that February, and the Indiana Daily Student called him Mr. Hot Commodity. The verdicts were harsh: HipHopDX wrote that D12 managed to butcher his beat on an uninspired title track, the Indiana Daily Student that they lacked the soul and groove it demanded, while the Chicago Maroon blamed the beat as forgettable, short of his usual energy and soul.

## Artistic Context

D12 came together in Detroit in 1996 around Proof; Eminem joined after Bugz was killed in 1999 and became the group's de facto leader. D12 World, released on Shady and Interscope on 27 April 2004, was executive-produced by Eminem, with beats from Dr. Dre, Hi-Tek, the Trackboyz and West among others. It was the last D12 album with Proof, who died in 2006.

## Impact and Reception

The album entered the Billboard 200 at number one with about 544,000 copies in its first week, but the title track was never a single, and the Detroit News filed it with the throwaways. Listen to it as the group's answer to the question every review asked, what D12 sound like on their own, and judge that answer yourself.
