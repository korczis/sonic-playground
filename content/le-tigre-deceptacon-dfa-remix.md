## Genre and Mood

**Le Tigre**'s **Deceptacon**, as the DFA remixed it, carries a dance-punk rallying cry over to electro-funk. The original is fast, scrappy and defiant; this version keeps the defiance and loosens everything else, groovy where the original is urgent, and long enough for a groove to settle in.

## Song Summary

The original "Deceptacon" opened Le Tigre's self-titled debut in 1999. Kathleen Hanna and Johanna Fateman wrote it, and the trio, completed by Sadie Benning, built it from found pieces: sampled handclaps, a sped-up guitar riff, a vocal nod to Barry Mann's "Who Put the Bomp", all over a four-note bassline that never moves, with no chorus at all. Red Bull Music Academy called it a rallying cry. The DFA, the New York production duo of James Murphy and Tim Goldsworthy, took it apart, and the streaming title still credits them by name.

## Musical Breakdown

No review walks through the remix in order and no source gives its timings, so this follows the parts critics singled out.

### The Keyboard Vamp

Andy Battaglia of The A.V. Club heard a mix "so in love with its paunchy electric-piano vamp" that the vamp nearly becomes the song. Zeth Lundy of PopMatters heard the keyboard as a clavinet, a sound with "the undeceiving insinuation of physical touch".

### The Bassline

Under the keys runs what Battaglia calls a "lazy bassline", far from the original's tight figure and the main reason the remix sounds relaxed rather than furious.

### The Drums

Battaglia's warning is the best tip: keys and bass are so seductive that the drums are easy to miss, yet they are what make this a dance record.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} can trim a song for radio or rebuild it for a club; the DFA did the second. Alex Burden of The Skinny wrote that they reshape tracks in their own image instead of chopping them, citing "the strutting electro-funk bestowed on Le Tigre's Deceptacon". Battaglia described the method as isolating parts and letting them play out for long stretches.

That method serves the {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }}: PopMatters heard the DFA keep a groove intact while stretching it to near-unrecognisable lengths. Hear the same patience in Murphy's own {{ <track_link source="lcd-soundsystem-dance-yrself-clean.md" /> }}.

The original belongs to the story of {{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }}, the movement Red Bull Music Academy ties it to, which also produced {{ <track_link source="fischerspooner-emerge.md" /> }}, another single the DFA remixed.

## Artistic Context

The remix first appeared on Remix, Le Tigre's remix album on Mr. Lady Records; MusicBrainz dates it to 2001, the Wikipedia infobox to 2003. A 2004 reissue added a long vocal mix as a bonus. In 2006 it opened The DFA Remixes – Chapter One on DFA Records, next to the duo's work for the Chemical Brothers, Gorillaz and Hot Chip.

## Impact and Reception

Remix itself was received coolly: Pitchfork called it "just about as standard as remix albums can get", putting "the disco before the discussion", and Out wrote that "Le Tigre deserves better". The DFA track outlived that verdict: Pitchfork placed it 226th among the best songs of the 2000s. Play it twice, once for the keys and once for the drums.
