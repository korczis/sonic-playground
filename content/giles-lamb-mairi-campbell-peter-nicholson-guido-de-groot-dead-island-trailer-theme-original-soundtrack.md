## Genre and Mood

**Giles Lamb** wrote the **Dead Island Trailer Theme** for a zombie game, and it sounds like nothing of the kind. It is soundtrack music in a neoclassical idiom, piano first and strings after, and its feelings are melancholic and elegant: Axis Animation asked for something beautiful and mournful, not metal or horror stings, and Wikipedia describes the result as melancholic and refined, set against the gore on screen.

## Song Summary

The announcement trailer for Dead Island, published on 16 February 2011, tells its story backwards and in slow motion: a family on holiday on the island of Banoi, a daughter bitten and turned, a father forced to throw her from a hotel window, and at the end the home footage of their arrival. Lamb, who worked for the sound company Savalas, began at the piano, improvising a simple theme over a rough animatic of the reverse-motion sequence. Five days later, on 21 February, Deep Silver released the music as a single, credited to Lamb with Mairi Campbell, Peter Nicholson and Guido de Groot.

## Musical Breakdown

No source times the piece, and the single credits do not say who plays what, so this breakdown follows Lamb's own account of how it was built.

### The Piano Theme

The theme is spare and melancholy, made as much of space as of notes. Lamb's principle was juxtaposition: where the image goes dark, the music stays luminous.

### The Strings

Strings came in later, layered to deepen the emotion without pushing too hard. The theme was also slowed slightly, and the trailer's edit was tightened around it.

### The 10th Anniversary Version

The original mixed real and virtual instruments out of necessity. In 2021 Lamb re-recorded it with the Scottish Session Orchestra, arranged with Peter Nicholson and Atzi Muramatsu.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="film score" /> }} is music written to fit picture, and here the picture was a short film with no gameplay at all. In a 2011 interview with T3, Lamb said the music would be at least half of the experience and would drive the film's emotion.

{{ <concept_link name="improvisation" /> }} is where the theme began: Lamb playing at the piano over a rough cut of the film. The strings, the slower tempo and the tightened edit came after.

The 2021 recording is an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }} by the composer himself, and a chance to hear what the piece sounds like without the compromises of 2011.

## Artistic Context

The trailer was produced by Axis Animation, directed by Stuart Aitken, and released by Deep Silver, the publisher of Techland's game. The team took inspiration from Philips' Carousel advertisement and wanted a short film rather than a showcase of violence.

## Impact and Reception

Nearly a million people watched the trailer within days, and by 2018 it had passed 16 million YouTube views. It won gold at Cannes Lions in the Internet Film category, and Wired called it a masterpiece. In 2016 Deep Silver released a musical tribute to it. Watch the trailer once, then listen to the theme alone.
