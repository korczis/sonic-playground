## Genre and Mood

**Sie** and **Courtney Gray** take **"Riders On The Storm"** out of the Doors' rain-soaked jazz-rock and into an orchestral, electronic cover. The label that released it calls it panoramic, with vocals that swoop over heavy strings to create a moody and electronic vibe. The feelings are atmospheric and longing: the label pictures you staring out of a car window, yearning for love lost.

## Song Summary

The song was the last the Doors recorded with Jim Morrison, credited to all four members, with lyrics by Morrison that partly refer to a hitchhiking killer. Sie, the British producer and mix engineer Sie Medway-Smith, recorded, mixed and produced this version at The Garden Studios in London, and the label says it was sixteen years in the making. It runs 5:57, between the original's 7:14 album version and its 4:35 single. No source describes its sections or how it ends.

## Musical Breakdown

No review we found walks through this cover part by part; here is what its credits and label describe.

### The Strings

James Banbury plays the strings, and the label calls them heavy: the orchestral bed under everything else.

### The Voice

Courtney Gray sings lead. The label's word for her vocals is earth-shattering; they swoop on top of the strings.

### The Band and the Electronics

Sie plays bass, Scott Addison keyboards, Jeremy Shaw guitars and Tim Weller drums, while Alberto di Angeli adds Moog and a Sherman Filter Bank.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} is a new recording of a song someone else made famous. The Doors' original is known for the sound of rain and storm, Ray Manzarek's Rhodes piano and Robby Krieger's tremolo guitar, and Morrison's whispered vocal overdubbed as an echo. This one, on its credits, replaces the weather with an orchestra.

That decision is an {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }}: who plays which part. A rock band's lineup is still here, with strings and analogue synthesis layered on top.

## Artistic Context

Sie Medway-Smith started as a tape operator at Milo Studios in London's East End and has worked as a producer, mix engineer, musical director and programmer with Depeche Mode, Björk, Massive Attack and Gang of Four. His cover came out as a single on 6 July 2018 and then on The Shape Of Cats To Come, a four-track EP released on 14 September 2018, which marked the return of Pussyfoot Records after a gap of fifteen years. Pussyfoot is the London label founded in the 1990s by the Glasgow-born producer Howie B, who engineered Björk's Post and co-produced U2's Pop; his own new track appears on the same EP.
