## Genre and Mood

**Ladytron**'s **He Took Her to a Movie - Bertrand Burgalat Remix** comes from the band's synth-pop and new wave beginnings, when their first EPs were praised for vintage synthesizers and a retro-futurist cool. Treat them as a frame, not a listener's report.

## Song Summary

He Took Her to a Movie was Ladytron's debut single, released in 1999. The history of their first album says it was recorded for £50 with the guest vocalist Lisa Eriksson, that NME made it Single of the Week, and that it was inspired by Kraftwerk's 1978 song The Model. Burgalat's version runs 3:46 on 604 (Remixed & Rare), more than half a minute longer than the 3:10 album version.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} is a new version of a recording made from its parts, often by someone outside the band. This one first appeared in 2000 as the Bertrand Mix on the 10-inch edition of the Commodore Rock EP released by the French label Tricatel, where it and the instrumental Olivetti Jerk took the place of two tracks of the British CD.

The remixer matters because he is a {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} with a long record: born in 1963, Burgalat co-produced Laibach's 1988 cover of the Beatles' Let It Be, and his label's biography says he has worked on more than 200 records, arranged for Supergrass and remixed Depeche Mode.

The version reached a wider audience through a {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }}. 604 (Remixed & Rare), released digitally on 1 September 2009 and on CD in 2011, gathers remixes, B-sides and rarities from the 604 era, including this remix and the Surreal Madrid remix of the same song.

## Artistic Context

Ladytron formed in Liverpool in 1999, when the producers and DJs Daniel Hunt and Reuben Wu were joined by Helen Marnie and Mira Aroyo; Hunt had founded the label Invicta Hi-Fi. Their debut album 604 followed in 2001. Compare {{ <track_link source="ladytron_seventeen.md" /> }}, the band's 2002 single from their second album, Light & Magic.
