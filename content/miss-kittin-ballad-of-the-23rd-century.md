## Genre and Mood

**Miss Kittin** released **Ballad of the 23rd Century** in 2013 on Calling from the Stars, and its two feelings are borrowed from the one critic who described it. Chi Ming Lai of The Electricity Club called it a potential science fiction series theme, quite pretty in places: futuristic by its title and by that ear, atmospheric by its place on the album's quieter half. It is filed under downtempo because the reviews hear that half as warmer and less made for dancing.

## Song Summary

The track runs 5:14 and is the sixth of ten on the second disc of a double album that holds 23 new songs. YouTube's art track credits Caroline Hervé, Miss Kittin's own name, as composer and lyricist, and Wikipedia credits her as the album's producer, with outside co-producers named only on other tracks. Beyond Lai's sentence, no review describes what happens inside it.

## Musical Breakdown

No published review or timed analysis maps the track's parts; what the critics describe is the disc around it. Sean Thomas of Drowned in Sound heard the first disc as more vocal and tune laden and the second as more instrumental and abstract. Resident Advisor's reviewer heard the second disc as mostly instrumental lullabies that radiate a warm, pulsating glow, with a downtempo feel that borders on IDM at times. Lai found that half too lively and percussive to count as ambient, yet not especially danceable. Listen for which of those descriptions this track fits.

## Concepts in Focus

Ballad of the 23rd Century is a {{ <concept_link name="deep cut" /> }}. None of the album's four singles, from Life Is My Teacher in December 2012 to Maneki Neko in November 2013, came from this part of the record, so the track reaches listeners who play the whole double album. That is exactly where Thomas found it: his review says disc two has flashes of invention, with this track, Sortie des Artistes and Sunset Mission in particular impressing.

It is also a lesson in the {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} as author. Miss Kittin produced Calling from the Stars herself, bringing in Pascal Gabriel and Gesaffelstein for three other tracks only, and released it on Nobody's Bizzness, the label she founded in 2004, together with wSphere. Wikipedia quotes her saying she created the label to keep the maximum freedom with her music.

## Artistic Context

Caroline Hervé was born in Grenoble in 1973 and became known through electroclash, above all the tracks she made with The Hacker, such as {{ <track_link source="miss-kittin-frank-sinatra.md" /> }}. Calling from the Stars, released on 19 April 2013, was her third solo studio album after I Com in 2004 and BatBox in 2008. Thomas heard its newer tracks move away from the humour of her earlier work towards a more confident, crafted space.

## Impact and Reception

The album holds a Metacritic score of 75 and reached the album charts in Belgium and France. Reviews of its length were mixed: Thomas called it a decent record in need of editing, with a very good hour-long album inside it, and Resident Advisor thought the second disc's mood too homogeneous for its length. Start here, and judge whether it sounds like the 23rd century.
