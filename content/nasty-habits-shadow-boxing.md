## Genre and Mood

**Nasty Habits** is the drum and bass producer Doc Scott under another name, and **Shadow Boxing** is the tune that the DnB 365 blog files under techstep, in the style the label No U-Turn helped create. Its feelings are the ones every source reaches for: dark and hypnotic. Complex's khrisd wrote that it does not need much but a sinister vibe, some deep bass and hypnotic drums.

## Song Summary

Doc Scott first released Shadow Boxing in 1996 on his own label, 31 Records, paired with Prototyped. This is the original mix as remastered for the label's 2014 single, catalogue 31RS004, whose other side is Om Unit's remix; it runs just under eight minutes. The blog describes an original built from three things: two-step drums, a bassline that fades in gradually, and fight sounds sampled from the kung fu film Shaolin & Wu Tang, and nothing else.

## Musical Breakdown

No timed analysis maps the track, so this follows the elements the sources name.

### The Drums

Two-step drums carry the tune, drums that Complex heard as hypnotic.

### The Bass

The bassline fades in rather than dropping in, so the low end arrives as a slow pressure. Complex's other phrase for the tune fits it: no need to clutter up the mood.

### The Samples

The only samples are fight sounds from the film. There is no vocal in the original; a vocal sample from the Wu-Tang Clan's Bring da Ruckus arrived only with the 1997 remix.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} here takes atmosphere rather than melody from its source. Shaolin & Wu Tang was also sampled extensively on the Wu-Tang Clan's Enter the Wu-Tang (36 Chambers), and the blog quotes Simon Reynolds comparing techstep to Wu-Tang-style horror-core rap, both seemingly based on the pursuit of phobia and psychosis as entertainment.

The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} shows how a drum and bass track can build tension without a drop. Compare Doc Scott's own 1997 remix: there, by the blog's account, the bass enters suddenly after a vocal sample, the drums become a militant stomp and an amen break closes each eight-bar section. The blog's author still prefers the original.

This version is a {{ <concept_link name="remastering" /> }}, not a remix: Complex describes the flipside of the 2014 release as a re-mastered version of the '96 original, the same arrangement with a new overall sound.

## Artistic Context

Doc Scott, born Scott McIlroy in Coventry, began DJing around 1989 or 1990, played the Metalheadz nights at the Blue Note in the mid-1990s and was called the King of the Rollers by Goldie. His labels include Reinforced, Metalheadz and his own 31 Records, and the blog notes that he revived the Nasty Habits name for this tune three years after Bludclot Artattack. Compare a later, equally uneasy record, {{ <track_link source="gridlok-insecticide-vip.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Complex called the original a seminal release that helped establish the darker side of the scene. Its afterlife confirms it: the 1997 remix, Om Unit's 2014 version and a remastered original eighteen years on. Play it loud enough to feel the bass arrive.
