## Genre and Mood

**Cortex** open their first album with **La Rue**, "the street", and the genres are announced at once: jazz in the playing, funk in the rhythm. The mood is groovy and warm. A record shop's note on the album speaks of an electric groove that is fresh, warm and funky in all the right parts, and La Rue is where that groove first starts.

## Song Summary

La Rue is the first of thirteen tracks on Troupeau Bleu and the only one written jointly by the band's two Alains: the drummer Alain Gandolfi and the pianist and leader Alain Mion, who wrote nearly everything else. It is credited with lyrics as well as music. A review on Jazz Music Archives describes its shape: the electric piano opens alone, very funky, in the spirit of Herbie Hancock, and then the band comes in with real funk, above all from the bassist Jean Grevet. No source examined describes the lyric, so this page does not interpret it.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

Electric piano, unaccompanied, before anyone else plays; the reviewer heard Herbie Hancock in its funky touch.

### Main Section

The band enters: drums by Gandolfi, bass by Grevet. The groove that follows is what the reviewer singles out, a bass part that carries the funk under the piano.

### Outro

No source describes how La Rue ends; listen for whether the groove fades or the band closes it together.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} is the feel a rhythm section creates when it locks together, and La Rue is a good place to hear one forming: first the piano alone, then the moment drums and bass join and the music starts to move. The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }} matters most here. Listen to how much of the funk the reviewer heard rests on Grevet's part.

Troupeau Bleu is also a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }}. DJ Cam took the title track for Bronx Theme in 1997; Madlib used Huit Octobre 1971 for Jaylib's No Games and for MF DOOM's One Beer, and WhoSampled counted 142 samples of the album by 2022. The same second life came to {{ <track_link source="galt-macdermot-coffee-cold.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Alain Mion founded Cortex in 1974. Troupeau Bleu was recorded in two days, 15 and 16 July 1975, at Studio Damiens in Boulogne-Billancourt, with Mireille Dalbray as singer, Alain Labib on alto saxophone, Gandolfi on drums and Grevet on bass, and released by Disques Espérance. Wax Poetics credits Dalbray's ghostly vocals with much of the album's cult status; she died not long after its release.

## Impact and Reception

The album was long an obscure favourite of funk collectors and became, in Wikipedia's account, a secret weapon for rare groove DJs and then hip-hop producers. Rolling Stone placed Cortex among the key influences on generations of rap acts. In 2022 Mion was still leading a new line-up. Start the album here and stay for the rest.
