## Genre and Mood

**Dreadzone** named their 2005 album after **Once Upon a Time (In Jamaica)**, its third track and one of its two singles. Discogs files the album under breakbeat, dub and breaks. Groovy and nostalgic are the feelings here: a dance rhythm from London, and a lyric that, as one review heard it, looks back over the history of Jamaican music.

## Song Summary

The album version runs 5:31. Earl Sixteen sings it; the album credits production, arrangement and electronics to Greg Dread and Ben Balafonic, and Steve Roberts plays guitar, bass and keyboards on this track. The Belgian reggae site Rebel Base called it simply a killer tune: Sixteen, it wrote, runs through a large part of Jamaican music history over a riddim whose horn section it singled out. Functional issued it as a single in 2005, on CD with a radio edit and on 12-inch with remixes.

## Musical Breakdown

No source gives times; this guide follows what Resident Advisor heard in the single's original version and Rebel Base on the album.

### The Rhythm

Resident Advisor heard a percussive breakbeat over a deep, dubby bassline: the drums of breakbeat electronica set inside the low end of dub.

### The Keys

Around the beat the review heard echoing keys and a reggae-style off-beat piano.

### The Second Verse

Here, by the same account, the beat switches to a two-step dancehall rhythm under the singer.

### The Riddim and the Voice

Rebel Base praised the riddim, above all its horns, and heard Sixteen's velvet voice as the album's most striking side.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="dub" /> }} is Jamaica's art of rebuilding a record at the mixing desk around drums and bass, with echo opening space for everything else. The deep, dubby bass and echoing keys Resident Advisor heard are that inheritance; for the band's instrumental side of it, listen to {{ <track_link source="dreadzone-king-dub-rock.md" /> }}, the track just before.

{{ <concept_link name="breakbeat" /> }} replaces reggae's drum patterns with broken, syncopated drums from funk and dance music. Discogs gives the album exactly that style, and it is what makes this reggae song a dance track.

The {{ <concept_link name="off-beat" /> }} is where reggae keeps its piano: short chords between the main beats, answering the drums.

## Artistic Context

Dreadzone formed in London in 1993 around the former Big Audio Dynamite drummer Greg Roberts. Earl Sixteen joined as a vocalist in 1995; he came to England in 1987 and voiced tunes for Mad Professor, he told Rebel Base in 2005. Once Upon a Time, the band's fifth studio album, came out in 2005 on the independent Functional label; Roberts called it a much more vocal album than its predecessors, shaped by the band's deejaying. Sixteen also sings {{ <track_link source="dreadzone-games-people-play.md" /> }} there.

## Impact and Reception

Resident Advisor called the original a winner for summer and heard three remixes beside it: a darker King At The Controls mix, a faster Brothers Bud mix with a harpsichord solo, and a two-step Mafiatone mix with new ragga lyrics. Rebel Base put the album in every reggae lover's collection. Start with the album version, then hear what each remix kept.
