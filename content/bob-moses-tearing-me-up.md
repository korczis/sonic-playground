## Genre and Mood

**Bob Moses** built their name on music that lives between the club and the bedroom, and **Tearing Me Up** is the purest example. It is deep house with an indie songwriter's heart, electronic in its patience and pulse but sung like a confession. The mood is hypnotic and melancholic, full of a longing that the groove keeps circling without ever resolving.

## Song Summary

The song is about a relationship that hurts more than it helps, and the arrangement mirrors that slow ache. A steady four-on-the-floor kick, a rolling bassline and moody, chiming guitar figures create a hypnotic bed, while Tom Howie's low, smoky vocal sits right on top of it. The album version stretches the idea to nearly eight minutes, letting the tension simmer instead of chasing a drop. What makes it memorable is that restraint: it feels like a song about to boil over that chooses, again and again, to stay at a simmer.

## Musical Breakdown

The track grows by accumulation, one layer at a time, as good deep house does.

### Intro

A soft kick and a muffled bass pulse arrive first, joined by shimmering textures that sound like streetlights through a rainy window.

### Verse 1

Howie's voice enters close and weary, phrasing the lyric with a singer-songwriter's ease. The guitar picks out small, melancholy figures around him.

### Chorus

The refrain lands with quiet force, the vocal doubling up as the bass deepens. It is less a release than a deeper dive into the same feeling.

### Main Section

Long instrumental stretches let the groove do the talking: percussion shuffles, filtered synths swell and recede, and the guitar loops into a trance-like pattern.

### Outro

The layers fall away gradually, leaving the kick and a few ghostly echoes of the hook, a slow fade that feels like walking home at dawn.

## Artistic Context

Bob Moses are Tom Howie and Jimmy Vallance, two musicians from Vancouver who reconnected in New York and began making records that fused live guitar and vocals with deep house production. Tearing Me Up appears on their 2015 debut album Days Gone By, released on Domino, and it became their breakthrough. The official video, shot in Paris and directed by Zack Spiger, turns the song into a dark, intimate love story.

## Impact and Reception

The track carried Bob Moses from underground clubs to festival stages, and its RAC remix went on to win the GRAMMY for Best Remixed Recording. Reviewers singled out the video as one of the more memorable clips of its year. It is here because few tracks capture heartbreak on a dancefloor so precisely. Give it the full eight minutes, ideally late at night, and let it get under your skin.
