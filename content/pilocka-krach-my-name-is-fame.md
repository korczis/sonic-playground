## Genre and Mood

**Pilocka Krach** speaks rather than sings on **My Name Is Fame**, and that choice sets the tone: this is techno and electronic music with a straight face that is clearly not entirely straight. The feelings are playful and quirky, which is how her reviewers keep describing her: a sense of humour, delivered over a club beat.

## Song Summary

Discogs and her label give the bare facts: she wrote and produced the track herself in 2012, released it in February 2013 as the A-side of Fame, a picture-disc 7-inch limited to 100 copies on her own label Greatest Hits International, with David Bowie Will Never Die on the flip side. Two years later it became the seventh track of Best Of. No source sets out its lyric, so this page does not paraphrase it. What DMC World's Ben Hogwood heard is a brilliant, weighty kick drum under a deadpan spoken word vocal.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="spoken word" text="Spoken word" /> }} is speech instead of song, and Pilocka Krach uses it here in its deadpan form: no melody to hide behind, so the words and their timing carry the character. Compare how {{ <track_link source="miss-kittin-frank-sinatra.md" /> }} turns a flat voice into irony about celebrity.

The {{ <concept_link name="kick drum" /> }} is the other half. Hogwood's word for it is weighty, and under a spoken vocal the weight and length of each kick do much of the work of moving the body. Listen to how much space it leaves between hits for the voice to land.

## Artistic Context

Pilocka Krach is a Berlin artist, born in Köpenick, a veteran of the club Bar25 who, according to her label's press text, first emerged from Vienna's noise avant-garde scene and has released tracks continuously since 2007. Best Of came out on 22 May 2015 on Monika Enterprise, with sleeve design by The Designers Republic; the label's press text calls it funky, demanding, dirty, funny, live and direct, more Freddie Mercury than Robert Hood.

## Impact and Reception

Groove's Christin Bolte heard these songs as live hits now gathered on one album, from a rousing solo act whose sing-along techno all of Berlin joins in with. HHV Mag's Kristoffer Cornils was cooler: her over-the-top charm, he wrote, works better in short formats. Hogwood gave the album four out of five. At three and a half minutes, My Name Is Fame is exactly that short format. Turn it up and let the kick do the talking.
