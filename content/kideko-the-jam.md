## Genre and Mood

**Kideko**'s **The Jam** is house music built from a memory. It is energetic and groovy, a club track with a UK garage swing in its rhythm, and it is nostalgic: its voice comes from a hit of 1989, and later writers heard the whole record as a nod to early-1990s rave that still fitted the dance floors of 2015.

## Song Summary

The Jam was Kideko's debut single, released on 14 August 2015. Its vocal comes from the a cappella of Technotronic's Pump Up the Jam, the hip-house record by Ya Kid K and Jo Bogaert that reached number two in the UK and the US. Kideko told Insomniac that there was no sketch: he started with drums, went looking for a line or two of vocal, came across Technotronic's a cappella and thought it would be fun to build a track from it. Once he had chosen the parts he wanted, he played with basslines, and it came together quickly.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through The Jam section by section or gives times, so this guide follows the layers the producer and his biographers describe.

### The Beat

The drums came first. Insomniac heard the result as giving the old sample a modern, garage-tinged makeup.

### The Vocal

Short inserts of the Pump Up the Jam vocal sit on top of the groove, the hook everyone already knows, cut down to a few lines.

### The Bassline

The Amsterdam Dance Event biography heard deep-set basslines, a bubbling rhythm and colourful percussive flourishes, a groove aimed at the house and bass scene.

## Concepts in Focus

The Jam is a lesson in {{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }}. Kideko chose Technotronic because, as he put it, the original is such a classic and so well known that it would translate well in the club. A famous voice brings its memories with it; the producer decides how much to keep. Compare the opposite approach in {{ <track_link source="the-lost-fingers-pump-up-the-jam.md" /> }}, where the words stay and every machine goes.

What makes the old vocal sound new is the {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }}. Kideko wrote it only after choosing his vocal lines, so the bass answers the sample rather than copying the original's groove.

The single also travelled through the {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }}: official versions by Dale Howard, Brookes Brothers, Rainer + Grimm and DubRocca appeared on Kideko's own channel, each taking the vocal somewhere else.

## Artistic Context

Kideko is the Brighton-based DJ and producer Ryan Hurley, who, in the words of his Qobuz biography, sprang onto the UK house scene in 2015 with this single. The Amsterdam Dance Event biography credits him with official remixes for Sigma and Shift K3y; since then he has released on Ministry of Sound, Spinnin' Deep and Toolroom, with later records such as Crank It (Woah!) with George Kwali and Dum Dum featuring Tinie Tempah and Becky G.

## Impact and Reception

The Jam peaked at number 41 on the UK Shazam chart in June 2015, and BBC Radio 1 DJs including Annie Mac, MistaJam, Danny Howard and Pete Tong supported it. Magnetic Magazine later called it his breakout single, a shot of adrenalized energy. Play it after Technotronic's original and listen for what stayed.
