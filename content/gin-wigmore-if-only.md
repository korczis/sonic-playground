## Genre and Mood

**Gin Wigmore**'s **If Only** is the slow, string-lit song in the middle of Gravel & Wine: a ballad with soul in its bones and pop in its polish. Reviewers in New Zealand heard it as lovelorn and called it brilliant, and a later webzine review described a sweet, slow song with an old-fashioned feeling. Heartbroken and longing are this page's names for that mood.

## Song Summary

If Only is the sixth of eleven tracks on Gravel & Wine, released in New Zealand on 7 November 2011, and at 4:45 it is the longest song on the record. Wigmore wrote it with Dan Wilson, and Butch Walker produced it in Santa Monica. As the reviews describe it, it rests on piano, voice, strings and bass. No source sets out the lyric line by line, so this page leaves the story of the title's wish to the song.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro

The song opens on the piano. The New Zealand Herald's TimeOut reviewer heard rolling broken-chord piano parts there and the ghost of Elvis in them.

### Main Section

Around voice and piano the same review heard lush strings and a subtle bass. MusicBrainz credits Jake Sinclair with piano and bass guitar, Walker and Fran Capitanelli with guitars, and all three with background vocals. The Waikato Herald heard a gorgeous piano-based ballad with an almost Adele-like sound.

### Outro

No source describes how the song ends; listen for whether the strings or the piano have the last word.

## Concepts in Focus

An {{ <concept_link name="arpeggio" /> }} is a chord played one note at a time. The broken chords the TimeOut reviewer heard at the start are exactly that: the piano rolls through each harmony instead of striking it, which gives a slow song a steady, rocking pulse without a drum.

The {{ <concept_link name="arrangement" /> }} does the rest. Strings and a quiet bass gather around piano and voice the way they did on pop ballads half a century earlier, which is why one blog called it the sort of 60s-styled ballad that would have sounded nostalgic even in the sixties.

The song also got a {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}: Wikipedia lists one for If Only in 2012, although the album's singles were Black Sheep and Man Like That.

## Artistic Context

Wigmore, born in Auckland in 1986, spent two months in Mississippi and Alabama before recording Gravel & Wine with Walker in California. The album debuted at number one in New Zealand and reached the US on 2 April 2013. Her co-writer, Dan Wilson, fronted Semisonic and co-wrote Adele's Someone Like You, which won a Grammy in 2012, a link worth knowing when a reviewer reaches for Adele. Hear the album's louder side in {{ <track_link source="gin-wigmore-black-sheep.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

If Only was never one of the album's singles, yet it is the track the reviews kept stopping at: TimeOut named it among the album's stunning ballads, and the Waikato Herald simply wrote "Brilliant." Let the piano roll in and give the album's longest song its full four and three-quarter minutes.
