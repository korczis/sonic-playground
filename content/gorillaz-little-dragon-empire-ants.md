## Genre and Mood

**Gorillaz** and **Little Dragon** made **Empire Ants** a song with two moods: synth-pop and electronic, it begins melancholic and dreamy and ends somewhere near euphoric. Reviewers agree less on its label than on its turn, the moment a slow lament becomes something to dance to.

## Song Summary

Empire Ants is the seventh of Plastic Beach's sixteen tracks and runs 4:43. Damon Albarn sings the first half; Yukimi Nagano, the voice of the Gothenburg band Little Dragon, takes over the second. The writing is credited to Albarn, Nagano and three Little Dragon members, Erik Bodin, Fredrik Wallin and Håkan Wirenstrand. Gorillaz produced it, with Simon Tong on guitar and Wallin and Wirenstrand on keyboards.

Nagano's vocals were recorded over several days at Studio 13, Albarn's own studio in London, in the same sessions as To Binge, the album's other Little Dragon song. Albarn played several instrumental tracks and Nagano chose which one to use.

## Musical Breakdown

No source gives times for Empire Ants or maps it bar by bar. Four reviews describe the same two-part shape, and the parts below follow them.

### The Lament

Erik Thompson of The Line of Best Fit heard Albarn singing leisurely over a lilting, fractured piano line. Darren Lee of The Quietus called the opening a mournful synth-lament, Ryan Leas of Stereogum a pleasant, floaty poolside lament, and Wendy Roby of Drowned in Sound heard here the shimmeriest, wateriest synths on the record.

### The Turn

Then, in Leas's words, throbbing, luminous synths bubble up and Little Dragon take over. Lee heard the song segue into a woozy Balearic wig-out, Thompson a luxurious Balearic house party, and Roby a song bursting out of its despondent chrysalis into a wonderfully pulsing, throbbing thing.

## Concepts in Focus

As a {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }}, Little Dragon do more than add a verse: the second half belongs to Nagano, and members of the band share the writing credit. Albarn was introduced to the band by his partner, Suzi Winstanley, a fan; Nagano had not known Gorillaz before and was at first ambivalent about the invitation.

The song's {{ <concept_link name="build-up" /> }} is slow rather than loud. Thompson called it a slow-building dance track that conveys both emotion and depth, and wrote that under less skilled hands the transition would sound jarring.

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} carries both halves: the watery synths Roby heard at the start and the luminous ones Leas heard bubbling up later.

## Artistic Context

Plastic Beach, the third Gorillaz album, came out on 3 March 2010 on Parlophone, and on Virgin in the United States. Albarn recorded it from June 2008 to November 2009. Four tracks after Empire Ants, Albarn sings alone on {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-broken.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Mark Beaumont of BBC Music called the song a trickling ballad to rank alongside Blur's best, and Leas called it Albarn's stunning team-up with Little Dragon. Roby named it the standout among the album's gloomier songs. It was played throughout the Escape to Plastic Beach tour, and Nagano sang it with Gorillaz again at Outside Lands in San Francisco on 11 August 2017, the first time in nearly seven years.
