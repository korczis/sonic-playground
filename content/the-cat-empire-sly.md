## Genre and Mood

**The Cat Empire**'s **Sly** is energetic and playful, and hard to file under one genre. PopMatters heard a comparatively straight-ahead rocker, mxdwn a jagged, jaunty R&B and rock hybrid, and a Sputnikmusic user review a fast-paced reggae tune; Wikipedia calls the band ska-jazz. All three descriptions are of the same three minutes and forty-seven seconds.

## Song Summary

Felix Riebl, the band's lead singer and percussionist, wrote the words and music. It opens Two Shoes, which the band recorded at Egrem Studios in Havana in late 2004 with Riebl and the UK-based producer Jerry Boys; Boys mixed it with Rafe McKenna. Three guest horn players are credited: Yaure Muniz on trumpet, Jesús "Aguaje" Ramos on trombone and Javier Zalba on baritone saxophone. No source explains the lyric, so this page does not.

## Musical Breakdown

No source gives times or a section-by-section analysis; the parts below are the ones reviewers describe.

### Opening

mxdwn's Matthew Kiel heard the album open with a salvo of drums, keyboards and horns.

### The Shift

From there, in the same review, the song turns from an arena-sized blast into its jaunty R&B and rock hybrid, with Riebl interjecting sing-speak breaks into his hip-pop delivery. The Sputnikmusic reviewer found his voice gave it an almost pub-like, homely feel.

### The Horns

Dave Hoffman of PopMatters singled out some nice trumpet work by Harry James Angus.

## Concepts in Focus

The Australian CD single lists the song as a {{ <concept_link name="radio edit" /> }} of 3:47, the same length MusicBrainz gives the album recording; an edit label does not always mean a shorter song.

Its three {{ <concept_link name="b-side" text="B-sides" /> }} are long: "1001", written by Ryan Monro, Riebl and Oliver McGill, which also hides on the Australian album after "The Night That Never End", "No Sense" at 6:46 and "Swing Sly" at 4:39.

{{ <concept_link name="sync licensing" text="Sync licensing" /> }} took the song further: it appeared on the soundtrack of EA Sports' FIFA 08. Compare {{ <track_link source="vitalic-my-friend-dario.md" /> }}, which reached players through FIFA 06.

## Artistic Context

The Cat Empire formed in Melbourne in 1999. Sly was issued as the lead single on 28 March 2005, ahead of the album, on EMI. Two Shoes followed on 19 April, more Latin in flavour than the debut and with more songs by Angus, among them the second single, {{ <track_link source="the-cat-empire-the-car-song.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

Sly peaked at number 23 in Australia and was voted number 38 in the Triple J Hottest 100 of 2005; in the United States it reached number 18 on Billboard's Adult Alternative Songs chart. Two Shoes became the band's first number-one album. Hoffman still found the opener a little ordinary next to the band's earlier recordings. Put it on and decide which genre you hear first.
