## Genre and Mood

**Gorillaz** recorded **Spitting out the Demons** in the Demon Days era and then tucked it away behind a single. It belongs to the band's alternative, trip-hop side rather than to its hits, and critics who met it on a later compilation heard it as dark and groovy: funky, with what one called a neurotic edge.

## Song Summary

The song first came out in 2005 as a B-side of Feel Good Inc., and two years later it became track eleven of D-Sides, at five minutes and ten seconds. MusicBrainz credits the writing to Gorillaz and the production to Gorillaz with Jason Cox and James Dring, Cox also engineering and mixing it and Dring programming. No review we found describes its lyric or arrangement, so this page keeps to its feel and its place in the band's story.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes the track part by part; this breakdown follows what reviewers heard in it.

### Groove

Sputnikmusic's reviewer cometuesday heard a funky groove, considerably less distorted than what precedes it, and listed it among the compilation's recommended tracks.

### Setting

On disc one of D-Sides it follows Murdoc Is God, which the same reviewer called an unforgiving snippet of ultra-distorted bass pummelling that precedes and complements this song. The two work as a pair: noise first, then the groove.

### Mood

On the Italian site Debaser, the reviewer babymurdoc singled out the neurotic Spitting out the Demons, and Debaser's listening guide recommends it, together with Rockit, for the darker edges of the album.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="b-side" /> }} is the track a single carries behind its lead song. This one rode behind Feel Good Inc., one of the band's biggest songs ({{ <track_link source="gorillaz-de-la-soul-feel-good-inc.md" /> }}), together with 68 State and Bill Murray. D-Sides then gathered B-sides, demos and unreleased songs of the Demon Days era for the first time, which is how most listeners met it.

A {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} is the feel a rhythm creates over time, and it is the word reviewers reached for here. Hear it straight after {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-murdoc-is-god.md" /> }} and the funk lands as relief.

Gorillaz are a virtual band, formed in 1998 by Damon Albarn and the artist Jamie Hewlett, with four cartoon members as their {{ <concept_link name="stage persona" /> }}. On 16 November 2014, at the Royal Albert Hall in London, Albarn opened a concert under his own name with this song, in a set that also included {{ <track_link source="gorillaz-neneh-cherry-kids-with-guns.md" /> }} and Feel Good Inc. with De La Soul.

## Artistic Context

D-Sides followed the band's first B-sides collection, G-Sides, and came out on Parlophone on 19 November 2007. Its producers are Gorillaz, Jason Cox, James Dring and Danger Mouse, and its material was recorded in 2004 and 2005 at Studio 13 in London and in Hong Kong. It reached number 63 in the UK and 71 on the Billboard 200.

## Impact and Reception

Spitting out the Demons was never a single in its own right. D-Sides drew generally favourable reviews, a Metacritic average of 69. The band's own channel published an official visualiser for it in 2010, and Albarn's choice of it as an opener nine years after its release suggests how much he still trusted it. Play Murdoc Is God first, then let this one in.
