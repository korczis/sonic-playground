## Genre and Mood

**Fischerspooner** made their name with Emerge, a pounding electroclash anthem, but **Horizon** is the long one. At 5:33 it is the longest track on the original edition of their debut album #1, and the one review that singles it out heard scale in it: Eamon Sweeney of Hot Press called it a towering epic where Daft Punk meets Pink Floyd. It belongs to the duo's electronic, electro-pop and theatrical world, and by that account to its most intense corner.

## Song Summary

Casey Spooner and Warren Fischer are credited with its music and its words, and they produced it with Nicolas Vernhes. Beyond Hot Press's single phrase, no source describes how it sounds, what its lyric is about or how it is built, so this page describes less and leaves the rest to your ears. The comparison itself is a clue: Daft Punk's French house on one side, Pink Floyd's long-form rock on the other.

### Main Section

What can be said is its weight on the album: five and a half minutes against tracks mostly between four and five, placed eighth of nine on the 2001 edition, just before Natural Disaster, and sixth of thirteen on the later reissue.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="electroclash" /> }} is the scene #1 belongs to: 1980s electro, synth-pop and new wave brought back with punk attitude and a love of performance. Horizon asks whether the style can also carry an epic, and the Pink Floyd comparison suggests that Hot Press heard that ambition in it.

The {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} credit on #1 is shared between the duo and Nicolas Vernhes. ## Artistic Context

Fischer, a classically trained musician, and Spooner, a video artist and experimental theatre performer, formed Fischerspooner in New York in 1998, as a performance art piece about entertainment that, in Spooner's words, ultimately became legitimate entertainment. #1 first appeared on 26 November 2001 in a limited run on DJ Hell's International DeeJay Gigolo Records, then on Ministry of Sound and Capitol with a different track listing. The upload here comes from a 2026 digital edition, provided by Edsel and released under exclusive licence by Demon Music Group.

## Impact and Reception

#1 received mostly favourable reviews, with a Metacritic score of 70, though Pitchfork gave it 3.1 and Trouser Press wrote that, judged on its own merits, it does not hold up. PopMatters' Adrien Begrand called it shallow, pretentious, flamboyant, catchy and just plain freaky at times, and still heard it as fresh and human. Hot Press counted Horizon among its three scorchers, beside Emerge and Natural Disaster. Give it its full length and decide for yourself where the horizon is.
