## Genre and Mood

**Blue Harlem** recorded **Nobody but Me** for their 2005 album Talk to Me, which Discogs files under jazz and blues, style rhythm and blues. The band calls its sound a high-energy blend of big band swing and R&B, and that sets the mood here: energetic and confident, a song whose title is a claim. No review describes this recording itself, so the sound is told through the band, its players and the song.

## Song Summary

Nobody but Me is a cover. SecondHandSongs lists Blue Harlem's recording as a version of the song written by Billy Myles and first released by Lou Rawls in 1965, and MusicBrainz links it to the same Myles work. The digital release on YouTube instead credits Doc Pomus and Mort Shuman, who wrote a different Nobody but Me, the Drifters' B-side to Save the Last Dance for Me. The two specialist databases agree on Myles, and this page follows them. The recording runs 3:34, the eighth of fifteen tracks.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through this recording part by part, so this breakdown follows the album's credits.

### Vocals

Imelda May is the credited singer, the voice the band says it launched before her solo career.

### Horns

Behind her, the album lists a horn section: Sid Gauld and Simon Da Silva on trumpet, Jeff Williams on trombone, and Al Nicholls, Pete Cook and Mick Foster on tenor, alto and baritone saxophones.

### Rhythm Section

Simon Wallace on piano, John Day on double bass and Sam Brown on drums complete the band.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} is a new recording of a song by another artist, and its credits can go wrong. Several different songs are called Nobody but Me, by different writers, and the metadata of this one mixed two of them up. Comparing the databases is how a careful listener finds the right original.

{{ <concept_link name="swing" /> }} is a rhythmic feel, pairs of notes played long-short rather than even, and it gave its name to the big-band music of the 1930s and 1940s that Blue Harlem play. The band's site quotes Ronnie Scott's praising them for combining jazz and rhythm and blues in the manner of America's West Coast in the 1940s and early 1950s.

The {{ <concept_link name="blues" /> }} runs underneath: jump blues and rhythm and blues, the style Discogs gives this album, grew out of it, and the album is filed under blues as well as jazz.

## Artistic Context

Blue Harlem are a London swing and jump-blues band led and managed by the tenor saxophonist Al Nicholls, for more than 25 years one of the leading acts of that scene in the city. Imelda May, born in Dublin in 1974, sang with them before she became known for her rockabilly revival. Talk to Me came out in 2005 on the band's own label.

## Impact and Reception

The band's site says Blue Harlem launched Imelda May's career, and this album keeps a record of it. Play the recording, then find the Lou Rawls original.
