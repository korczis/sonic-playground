## Genre and Mood

**Cléa Vincent** takes on **All that she wants**, the Ace of Base hit, and turns it into a homespun cover of breezy French synth-pop. It is playful and dreamy, steeped in nostalgia for early-nineties radio but delivered with an indie, bedroom-pop lightness that makes it feel brand new.

## Song Summary

The original, written by Jonas Berggren and Ulf Ekberg of Ace of Base, is a dark little pop song about a restless woman who wants another baby, whatever that word means, set to a reggae-pop shuffle that conquered charts worldwide. Vincent keeps the famous melody but softens its strut, replacing the glossy production with warm, slightly lo-fi keyboards, a relaxed beat and her own airy voice; she has said she approached it more like a hip-hop or R&B song than nineties reggae-pop from Sweden. The memorable detail is the charm of the handmade: it sounds like a song recorded in an apartment on a lazy Sunday, and, as it happens, Midnight Special's studio is in an apartment.

## Musical Breakdown

The cover follows the original's shape but trades punch for haze.

### Intro

Soft synth chords and a gentle drum-machine pattern establish a sunny, slightly woozy groove.

### Verse 1

Vincent's voice enters light and nonchalant, singing the familiar lines with a wink rather than a sneer.

### Chorus

The well-known hook arrives in a gentle wash of keys, instantly recognisable yet dreamier and more intimate than the original.

### Bridge

A short instrumental passage lets the synths drift, adding a little French-pop melancholy to the mix.

### Climax

The last choruses layer vocals and keyboards for a warm, swaying finale.

### Outro

The groove fades softly, leaving a lingering hum of synths and the urge to put the original on next.

## Concepts in Focus

All That She Wants is a {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} in the transforming sense. Vincent keeps Berggren and Ekberg's melody and words and changes almost everything else: the production, the tempo feel and the attitude. A faithful cover shows respect; a transforming one shows what the interpreter hears in a song. Here she hears a sweetness that the original's cool, sinister polish hid.

The main thing she changes is the {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }}. Ace of Base built the song on a reggae-inflected pop shuffle, tight and bouncing. Vincent told Bandcamp Daily she made it closer to hip-hop and R&B, which means a looser, laid-back beat that sits behind the voice rather than pushing it. Listen to how the same melody feels slower and more private on that bed.

What survives is the {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }}. Vincent said she loves the song's musical hooks, and the chorus melody is recognisable after two notes even in its new clothes. That is the test of a great pop hook: it does not depend on its original production. Compare the way AnnenMayKantereit and Parcels strip back a Kylie Minogue hit in {{ <track_link source="annen-may-kantereit-x-parcels-cant-get-you-out-of-my-head.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Cléa Vincent, born in Paris in 1985, trained on piano at a classical conservatory and then at the Paris jazz school Arpej, and built her following at open-mic nights. A first album for Polydor, Happée Coulée, was shelved; she later called its recordings cold and sterile. She found a home at Midnight Special Records, the Paris independent run by Victor Peynichou, whose small studio is in an apartment. There she re-recorded songs from the shelved album, added covers and remixes, and released the results as two EPs in 2014: Non mais oui on 10 March, with this Ace of Base cover, and Non mais oui 2 in October. The covers also include Seul sous la lune by Daniel Darc, sung as a duet with Baptiste W. Hamon. Her influences span Ariel Pink and Mac DeMarco, French new wave, early-nineties Europop and the yé-yé era.

## Impact and Reception

The Non mais oui EPs launched Vincent's career: the video for her single Château perdu became a YouTube success that M6 then broadcast, and her first album, Retiens mon désir, followed in 2016 to good reviews and a tour of France and Europe. Bandcamp Daily heard in the re-recordings a warm, organic lo-fi pop that the shelved versions lacked, and this cover is the clearest example: a pure Euro-pop hit from her teenage years remade as something tender. It is here because it shows a great cover can reveal the sweetness inside a familiar hit. Put it on and sing along softly.
