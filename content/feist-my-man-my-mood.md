## Genre and Mood

**Feist** opened the campaign for her third album with **My Moon My Man**, an indie pop song with folk roots and a singer-songwriter's eye for detail. It is introspective and romantic, with a melancholic undertow, but it is also playful: PopMatters heard a "wry sweetness" in it, and that mix of wit and longing is the song's character.

## Song Summary

"My Moon My Man" was written by Feist with Chilly Gonzales, the Canadian pianist and producer she met while touring with Peaches and who had worked with her on her 2004 album Let It Die, and the two produced it together with the French engineer Renaud Letang. The lyric circles around a lover addressed as both moon and man, closeness and distance in the same breath. The arrangement is small and specific: Gonzales on piano, Mocky on acoustic bass, drums and Farfisa organ, and Feist's voice in front, recorded largely in two weeks at La Frette Studio near Paris in March 2006.

## Musical Breakdown

### Intro: Gentle Beginning

The song opens with the band setting a light, slightly mischievous rhythm, the piano at its centre. It establishes a tone that is more knowing smile than tears.

### Verse 1: Lyrical Introspection

Feist's voice enters close and conversational. She plays with the words of the title as much as she sings them, so the yearning in the lyric comes with a raised eyebrow.

### Chorus: Emotional Climax

The chorus fills out the arrangement under the title phrase. It lifts the song without turning it into a big ballad, keeping the understatement that runs through The Reminder.

### Bridge: Reflective Interlude

Midway through, the arrangement steps back for a moment, giving the voice and the lyric room before the band returns.

### Verse 2 and Outro: Continuing the Journey

The second verse continues the conversation with the absent lover, and the song winds down rather than building to a climax, leaving the question it asks unresolved.

## Concepts in Focus

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} is how many people first met the song. Directed by Patrick Daughters, who made the videos for the whole album and earlier for "Mushaboom", it was shot in the middle of the night at Toronto Pearson International Airport. Feist steps onto a moving walkway, puts down her suitcase and starts to dance while travellers pass in and out of the frame and sometimes join in; the lighting turns a dramatic blue, and when the song ends the travellers vanish and the ordinary lights come back. The opening had to be reshot upstairs in a bar near the airport because of the lighting. The video turns a song about longing into a scene of solitude in a public place, which is exactly the song's mood.

The single also shows what a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} is for. Alongside the album version, it carried a remix by the German producer Boys Noize, who later included it on his debut album Oi Oi Oi. The remix keeps Feist's voice and moves the song into electro clubs, an audience the original was never aimed at. Comparing the two is a quick lesson in how much of a song's identity lives in its arrangement rather than in its melody and words.

## Artistic Context

Leslie Feist, born in Amherst, Nova Scotia, in 1976 and raised in Calgary, came up through Toronto's scene: she played in By Divine Right, worked the back of the stage at Peaches' shows, and joined Broken Social Scene before her solo breakthrough. "My Moon My Man" was released on 6 March 2007 as the first single from The Reminder, which came out in April and May 2007 on Arts & Crafts in Canada and Cherrytree/Interscope in the United States. The album debuted at number 2 in Canada and number 16 on the Billboard 200, was shortlisted for the Polaris Music Prize and won the Juno Awards for Album of the Year and Pop Album of the Year in 2008.

## Cultural Impact

"My Moon My Man" reached number 13 on the Canadian Hot 100 and number 8 on the US Adult Alternative chart, and was soon overshadowed by "1234", the next single, after Apple used it to advertise the iPod Nano. The song had its own afterlife in a Verizon Wireless commercial, an episode of Grey's Anatomy and, years later, Hulu's Tell Me Lies. Not every critic was won over; Drowned in Sound had hoped for a dance-floor stomper and found it slight. Watch the airport video first, then listen again with your eyes closed.
