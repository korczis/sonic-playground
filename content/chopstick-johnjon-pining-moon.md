## Genre and Mood

**Chopstick & Johnjon** made **Pining Moon** a song first and a house track second. It moves at an unhurried deep house pace, but it is built on a sung lyric and a melody, closer to pop than to a DJ tool. The mood is longing and melancholic, yet relaxed: the title's pining sits inside a groove warm enough to make it bearable.

## Song Summary

Pining Moon is the eighth of twelve tracks on Twelve, the duo's debut album, released on 4 April 2014 on their own Berlin label Suol. It came out first as a single on 7 March 2014, with a video about a week later. The composers are credited as Chopstick and Johnjon, the lyric to Chris James, the London-based singer who appears on eleven of the album's twelve tracks. Resident Advisor described the album as a warm, funk-laden take on house aimed between the dance floor and the living room, and Balance Music heard the single as a turn towards more organic, slower sounds.

## Musical Breakdown

### The Groove

The beat is steady and warm rather than driving, giving the song room instead of pushing it towards a peak.

### The Song

The lyric and melody sit at the centre, and the arrangement serves them, which is what the album's press release meant by favouring song over track.

### The Long Version

At five and a half minutes, the album version gives the groove time to settle around the song before and after the vocal.

## Concepts in Focus

Twelve closes with an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }} of its own single: track twelve is Pining Moon (Acoustic Version). Hearing the same song first with its house beat and then without it is the clearest way to understand what the production adds. If the song survives without the groove, it was a song all along; the beat was its setting.

The single also shows how a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} can keep a song in circulation. The 2014 release paired the original with mixes by Jay Shepheard and Death On The Balcony; Oliver Koletzki remixed it for his 2019 collection Remix Tales, and in 2020 Suol issued Pining Moon & Roots Revisited, with a remix by Daniel Bortz.

Underneath it all is a {{ <concept_link name="house groove" /> }} used gently, as in {{ <track_link source="marian-for-you.md" /> }}: the steady pulse is there for comfort rather than release.

## Artistic Context

Chopstick & Johnjon are Chi-Thien Nguyen and John Muder, who started working together in Hamburg in 2002 and later moved to Berlin to make music full-time, with releases from 2004. They run Suol, which has released music by Daniel Bortz, Till von Sein and Fritz Kalkbrenner. Twelve came a decade into their partnership, with Chris James, also of the band Stateless, as its main voice and Tanner Ross as a guest.

## Impact and Reception

Balance Music praised Twelve for craftsmanship over maximum floor impact, and Pining Moon became the song that kept coming back, in remixes and acoustic form, for years. Put on the original, then the acoustic version, and listen for what stays.
