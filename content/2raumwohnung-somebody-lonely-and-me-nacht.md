> "Somebody Lonely and Me (Nacht)" by **2raumwohnung** is the night half of a song the Berlin duo recorded twice.
> Inga Humpe's German verses and a short English refrain sit inside a darker synthesizer arrangement, and the title says what the lyric keeps returning to: somebody lonely, and me.

The track is slow, cool and unhurried. It is music for the late hours the album side is named after, with a vocal that stays close and quiet rather than reaching for a big release.

## Genre and Mood

**2raumwohnung's** "Somebody Lonely and Me (Nacht)" sits where **electronic** pop meets **chill**: a steady, restrained arrangement led by synthesizers and Humpe's light voice. The mood is **reflective** and **dreamy**, and the plainness of the English refrain makes it **poignant** rather than dramatic.

## About the Artists

**2raumwohnung** is the Berlin duo of Inga Humpe and Tommi Eckart, formed in 2000. Humpe had sung with the punk band Neonbabies until 1983, with DÖF, and in a duo with her sister Annette Humpe, and the pair release their music on their own label, It Sounds. Their records mix German and English lyrics with electronic pop; 36 Grad (2007) was their most commercially successful album, and Nacht und Tag reached number 23 in Germany.

## Song Summary

Nacht und Tag, released in June 2017, is a double album built on one idea: ten songs, each recorded twice, once as a night version and once as a day version. "Somebody Lonely and Me" was its first single. In its review, The Electricity Club heard the song harden up in daylight and turn, at night, to a primal synth sound it compared to Yazoo. The lyric is a search: German lines about longing and loss, questions about the eyes and the face the singer wants to see, and an English refrain that answers each question with the same picture of somebody lonely, and the singer herself. Humpe wrote the words; she and Eckart composed and produced the track, with trumpet credited to Sebastian Studnitzky.

## Sound Breakdown

### Intro: Inviting Serenity

The night version opens in synthesizers. Without guitars or a bright beat to lean on, the opening sets a cool, nocturnal tone, the version's whole reason to exist.

### First Verse: Emotional Exploration

Humpe enters in German, singing softly and close to the microphone. The first lines describe voices of longing heard in a space between the world and outer space, the people who have lost something calling out; it is a lonely image delivered without self-pity.

### Chorus: Embracing Connection

The refrain switches to English and to very few words. All the singer can see is somebody lonely, and herself. Repeated twice in a row, the line is simple enough to work as the song's hook, and the change of language makes it stand out from the verses.

### Bridge: Moment of Reflection

The lyric has no separate bridge; the second verse plays that role. Humpe sings of a bridge that burned for a long time, then two lines in English ask where the face she wants to see forever is, and where the wing without her feathers has gone. The languages now mix inside a single verse.

### Final Chorus: Unity and Resilience

The refrain returns and is repeated to the end. The song does not climb to a new section or a key change; it ends on the same picture it has been circling, which suits a lyric that finds no answer.

## Concepts in Focus

The idea of the whole album is the {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }}. A remix is usually another producer's reworking of a finished track; here the artists themselves recorded each song twice and released both versions side by side. The melody and the words stay the same, so whatever changes between the Nacht and Tag versions of this song is the effect of arrangement and sound alone.

On the night side that sound is the {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }}. The Electricity Club compared this version to Yazoo, the early-1980s synth-pop duo, and it is the synthesizers that make it cooler and darker than its daytime twin. Humpe sang in the German punk and new wave scene of those same years, so the reference is a return as much as a borrowing.

The English refrain is the {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }}. In a song whose verses are in German, a handful of short English words repeated twice is what a listener remembers, whichever language they speak.

## Overall Vibe

"Somebody Lonely and Me (Nacht)" shows **2raumwohnung** doing a lot with little: a quiet voice, a few synthesizers, a refrain of a handful of words. Its **reflective** and **dreamy** surface hides a careful design, and the album around it invites the listener to compare.

## Conclusion

In "Somebody Lonely and Me (Nacht)," **2raumwohnung** make loneliness sound calm rather than desperate. Listen to it next to the Tag version from the same album; hearing how much of the mood moves when only the arrangement changes is the best lesson this song has to teach.
