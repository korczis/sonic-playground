## Genre and Mood

**Ian Brown** opens his fifth solo album with its title track, **The World Is Yours**, alternative rock with an orchestra behind it and a funk groove underneath. The mood is defiant and bold, a statement rather than a confession, and it moves: the BBC's review heard it as funk-infused and called it, with Me and You Forever, a lovely track.

## Song Summary

The credits name Brown and the New York producer Emile Haynie as its writers, and on the album it runs 4:31. Its subject is the one alternative-zine's review called the album's theme song: one law for the rich and another for the poor. The review found the message still relevant, and also old enough to appear in writing about the French Revolution two centuries earlier. The album returns to the song at its very end, with a short reprise.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the title track section by section or gives times, so this guide follows what reviewers heard on it and on the album around it.

### The Groove

The BBC heard this track as funk-infused. Across the album, it noted, Paul Ryder of Happy Mondays and Andy Rourke of the Smiths add a smattering of funky sounds.

### The Strings

musicOMH heard a sweeping, cinematic feel and a lushly orchestrated string section on the album, and singled out this track as one of those that sound particularly good, like The Verve of the Urban Hymns era.

### The Voice

The BBC described Brown's vocals across the album as shimmery and languorous, delivered with conviction.

## Concepts in Focus

Much of the album works as {{ <concept_link name="protest song" /> }}, a vitriolic state of the world address in the BBC's words, and its title track states the argument before the others name targets. {{ <track_link source="ian-brown-save-us.md" /> }} turns the same anger into a list of pleas.

The {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} here is also a co-writer. NME wrote that Brown likes hip-hop, so he brought in Haynie to handle the beats; the album's producers were Haynie, Robert Maxfield and Tim Wills.

The song also exists as an {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }}. The deluxe edition adds a disc of orchestral mixes of all twelve tracks, and MusicBrainz lists this one's orchestral mix at about three minutes; the closing reprise is shorter still.

## Artistic Context

Brown sang with the Stone Roses until their split in 1996, then went solo. For The World Is Yours, released by Fiction and Polydor in 2007, he gathered Rourke, Ryder, Steve Jones and Paul Cook of the Sex Pistols and Sinéad O'Connor, with a 30-piece orchestra, as NME counted it.

## Impact and Reception

The album scored 58 on Metacritic, a mixed reception. The Guardian's Caroline Sullivan said it draws its strength from sounds rather than words; the Observer heard Brown's most arresting solo set, thanks largely to its John Barry-esque strings. Start with the title track, then skip to the reprise.
