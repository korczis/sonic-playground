## Genre and Mood

**The Prodigy**'s **Medicine** is electronic music where breakbeat meets hip-hop and ragga: gritty, intense and defiant. NME counted it among the album's breakbeat tracks, Metal Hammer heard a ragga-slam groove, and The New Zealand Herald a Middle-Eastern hip-hop cacophony.

## Song Summary

Liam Howlett wrote it with Maxim and Mark Hull, the Ipswich reggae and dancehall MC known as YT, and the two share the vocals. The album credits also name a sampled source: "Se Makri Sofra / At a Long Table", performed by Babaka & His Ensemble on the album Music of the Balkans, Vol. 2. The List quoted a fragment of the lyric, "a spoonful of sugar … to keep you in your place", and heard a chant that hopes to be political. The embed is the album recording, uploaded by the band.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through Medicine in order or gives times; the reviews describe three things.

### The Colour

Sonicabuse heard a swirling eastern menace, The New Zealand Herald something Middle-Eastern. The credited sample comes from an album of Balkan music; no source says which sound in Medicine is taken from it.

### The Groove

Metal Hammer's word was ragga-slam. The New Zealand Herald held the track up as an example of Howlett's pinpoint production perfectionism.

### The Vocals

The List was reminded of the band's classic "Poison", but heard Medicine as a football chant that swirls around, directionless and incoherent.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} builds a track from pieces of other recordings, and the credits are where those pieces are named. Medicine's credits name the original performers, Babaka & His Ensemble, and Mark Summers as sample replay producer at SCORCCiO Sample Replays; no source says what was replayed here. Compare {{ <track_link source="the-prodigy-the-day-is-my-enemy.md" /> }}, which borrows the words of a Cole Porter song rather than a recording.

A {{ <concept_link name="groove" /> }} is the feel that makes a rhythm move the body. Ragga belongs to the reggae and dancehall world that YT comes from; he told United Reggae he started chanting on sound systems in 1987.

A {{ <concept_link name="deep cut" /> }} is an album track that never became a single, known to those who play the whole record. Medicine is one, and its place moves between editions: twelfth of fourteen on the CD, it closes the 2LP edition Sonicabuse reviewed, drawing a line, in that review's words, under the most expansive Prodigy album since The Fat of the Land.

## Artistic Context

The Day Is My Enemy, the band's sixth album, was released on 30 March 2015 by Take Me to the Hospital and Cooking Vinyl. On the CD, Medicine comes just before {{ <track_link source="the-prodigy-invisible-sun.md" /> }}. The expanded edition added a South Central remix. Another British breakbeat track built on a borrowed record and reggae voices is {{ <track_link source="dreadzone-iron-shirt.md" /> }}.

## Impact and Reception

The album debuted at number one in the UK. Medicine split the critics who mentioned it: Metal Hammer counted it among the album's real high points and The New Zealand Herald among Howlett's best tricks, while The List found the vocals disappointingly lazy. Listen to the voices against the swirl and judge for yourself.
