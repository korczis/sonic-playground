## Genre and Mood

**Depeche Mode** take **Wrong (Live in Barcelona)** through synth-pop, electronic music and the muscle of alternative rock. It is intense, dark and defiant, a catalogue of failures delivered with the swagger of a band that knows exactly how to own an arena.

## Song Summary

"Wrong" is a breathless list of everything that can go wrong in a life: wrong place, wrong time, wrong choices, recited with grim relentlessness. On record it is a jagged, minimal piece of electronic menace; live it becomes heavier and more physical, with real drums under the synths and Dave Gahan carrying the litany with theatrical gusto. The audience turns its bleakness into something like a celebration, and the rhythm section gives the song a pounding forward motion that the studio cut deliberately withheld. It is a fine example of how Depeche Mode's electronic songs grow teeth in front of a crowd.

## Musical Breakdown

The live version keeps the song's tight structure but adds weight and crowd energy.

### Intro

A distorted, lurching synth figure cuts through the arena, instantly recognisable and greeted with a roar.

### Verse 1

Gahan launches into the rapid-fire lyric, each line hammered home over a stuttering, mechanical rhythm.

### Chorus

The title word lands like a slammed door, the full band behind it, drums and synths locking together.

### Bridge

The arrangement briefly opens up, letting the synth textures swirl before the litany returns.

### Outro

The song crashes to an abrupt stop, leaving the Barcelona crowd's cheers to fill the space.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="live recording" /> }} is never just the studio song played again. This one was captured at the Palau Sant Jordi on 20 and 21 November 2009, and you can hear the room: the cheer when the opening figure starts, the weight of a live kit under parts that were programmed on the record, the crowd at the end. The touring band, with drummer Christian Eigner and keyboardist Peter Gordeno alongside Gahan, Martin Gore and Andy Fletcher, turns a studio construction into a performance.

The song's identity still lives in the {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }}. Depeche Mode were one of the bands that proved synths could carry dark, adult rock music, and Wrong is built around a snarling, slightly off-kilter synth figure rather than a guitar. Listen to how the crowd reacts to that sound before a single word is sung.

## Artistic Context

"Wrong", written by Martin Gore, was the lead single from Sounds of the Universe, Depeche Mode's twelfth studio album, released to radio in February 2009 with a video by Patrick Daughters in which a driverless car rolls backwards through downtown Los Angeles. The world tour that followed was filmed and recorded over two nights in Barcelona and released by Mute on 5 November 2010 as Tour of the Universe: Barcelona 20/21.11.09, the band's first Blu-ray, with the concert also on two audio CDs.

## Impact and Reception

The studio single topped the Scottish chart, the band's only number one there, reached the top ten in nine countries including Germany, Italy and Sweden, and went to number one on the US Billboard Dance Club Songs chart; in the UK it peaked at 24 and remains their most recent top-40 hit. This live version captures the band three decades into their career still sounding abrasive and urgent, with a crowd that clearly knows the new song as well as the classics. Play it loud and imagine the arena lights.
