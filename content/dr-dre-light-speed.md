## Genre and Mood

**Dr. Dre**'s **Light Speed** is West Coast hip-hop from the middle of his 1999 album 2001, a G-funk rap track shared with Hittman. It is filed as confident and dark: confident because the lyric is a boast, dark because the critic who described it best heard a fog of nocturnal gangsta funk rather than anything as fast as its title.

## Song Summary

Light Speed is the ninth track of 2001, credited to Dr. Dre featuring Hittman, and it is over in 2:41. Edge Induced Cohesion sums it up as Dre, with Hittman, bragging about his smoking skills and his ghetto identity. The label's credits name Andre R. Young and Brian Bailey as writers, with Dre and Mel-Man producing and Dre and Richard "Segal" Huredia mixing; Wikipedia's track list adds a third surname, Brown. MusicBrainz credits Sean Cruse on guitar, Mike Elizondo on bass and Camara Kambon on keyboard.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes the beat bar by bar or gives times, so this breakdown keeps to what the critics and the credits say.

### The Opening

The song arrives straight after Bar One, a short skit, as Edge Induced Cohesion notes.

### The Hook

Okayplayer's Peter A. Berry called the hook deceptively infectious: it does not announce itself, and it sticks anyway.

### The Verses

Berry heard another set of charismatic flows from Dre. Hittman shares the boasting, one of the many appearances that make him a regular on the record.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} is usually a guest, but on 2001 Hittman is closer to a member of the cast. Wikipedia credits him on several of its tracks and names him among the ghostwriters behind some of the album's lyrics; here he is both a voice and a credited writer. Hear him in a darker setting on {{ <track_link source="dr-dre-big-ego-s.md" /> }}.

{{ <concept_link name="sampling" /> }} ties the song to an older record. Wikipedia's sample notes say Light Speed contains a sample of I'm Still #1 by Boogie Down Productions, the group led by KRS-One, from their 1988 album By All Means Necessary. No source says which part is borrowed, so listen for it rather than take a map on trust.

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} is the song's best argument. Berry's phrase, deceptively infectious, describes a hook that sounds modest and still comes back to you later.

## Artistic Context

2001, Dr. Dre's second solo album, came out on 16 November 1999 on Aftermath and Interscope, recorded in 1998 and 1999 and produced by Dre and Mel-Man. Light Speed sits between the skit Bar One and {{ <track_link source="dr-dre-forgot-about-dre.md" /> }}, the second single.

## Impact and Reception

Light Speed was not a single, and critics disagree about it more than most. Okayplayer's 2024 ranking placed it 12th of the album's songs. In Soul In Stereo's 1999 Rewind conversation, Ronald Grant ranked it 9th of 17, remembered being particularly taken with it on first hearing and said it oozes signature West Coast bravado; Edd Bowser ranked it 17th, last. Listen for the hook and the sample, and decide where you would rank it.
