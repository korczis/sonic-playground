## Genre and Mood

**D12**'s **Fight Music** is hip-hop and rap at its most physical: intense, gritty and openly aggressive, a song about violence and street fights with a chorus built to be shouted. Yet Rolling Stone heard it as the record that showed rap fans a side of the group beyond its shock-heavy horrorcore.

## Song Summary

Fight Music was the third single from Devil's Night, D12's 2001 debut, and one of the album's Dr. Dre productions. Most of its verses deal in violence and dangerous street fighting, with two exceptions that Wikipedia points out: Bizarre goes for graphic shock, and Eminem turns to the young listeners who relate to songs like this one. The credits list all six members as writers, alongside Dre and the bassist and guitarist Mike Elizondo, and the album version runs 4:21.

## Musical Breakdown

No source walks through the track section by section; this follows its credits and the people who made it.

### The Beat

Rolling Stone heard Dre's bass rhythm and Elizondo's ringing guitar lines giving the performers a raw, emphatic sound. The album's personnel list gives Elizondo guitars, bass and keyboards on the track and adds Scott Storch on keyboards. Denaun Porter, who was there, said its dark feel reminds you of old rock bands.

### The Hook

Eminem sings the chorus. Porter recalled that Eminem still calls the song his best vocal performance, from the verse to the hook; Traci Nelson is credited with backing vocals.

### The Verses

The crew passes the beat around on the theme of fighting, until Bizarre's and Eminem's verses step outside it.

## Concepts in Focus

Fight Music is a {{ <concept_link name="posse cut" /> }}: one beat, a whole crew, each MC with his own turn. Compare how the same group shares its title track in {{ <track_link source="d12-devils-night.md" /> }}.

The {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} holds such a track together: with several voices taking turns, Eminem's chorus is the fixed point the listener returns to.

A {{ <concept_link name="record producer" /> }} rarely works alone. Dre built the beat, but Elizondo, credited as a writer, plays its guitar and bass and Storch adds keyboards: the sound Rolling Stone praised belongs to a session band as much as to one producer.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }} borrows from the film The Warriors: Ice-T opens it, lines are quoted from the movie, Fat Joe and Obie Trice make cameos, and parts were shot at Coney Island and in Melrose in the Bronx. It exists in explicit and clean versions; the clean one changes or mutes words in the audio.

## Artistic Context

Devil's Night came out on 19 June 2001 on Shady Records and Interscope, produced mainly by Eminem and Dr. Dre. Porter told HotNewHipHop that Fight Music came out of a Los Angeles session with Dre while they were working on Ain't Nuttin' But Music, and that it shared a feel with Revelation, the song that closes the album.

## Impact and Reception

The single reached number 11 in the UK, number 5 on the UK R&B chart and number 27 in Australia. In 2022 Rolling Stone placed it 37th among Dr. Dre's 40 greatest productions, calling D12 truly underrated. Turn it up and listen for the guitar.
