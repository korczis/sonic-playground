## Genre and Mood

**Iggy Pop** sings **In The Death Car** here not as a film song but as part of a rock show. The page carries a live recording, made at a theatre in Évry, France, in April 1994, a dark and raw document of a song that began as a soundtrack.

## Song Summary

The song comes from Arizona Dream, Emir Kusturica's film, released in France in January 1993. Goran Bregović wrote the soundtrack, and Iggy Pop sang four of its songs, writing the lyrics for three of them; In the Deathcar, as the soundtrack spells it, opens the album. A year later he sang it on stage. This version comes from the Agora Theatre in Évry on 23 April 1994, released in 2011 on Roadkill Rising, a four-disc set of his bootlegs, where it is credited to Pop and Bregović.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="live recording" /> }} captures a single night. Roadkill Rising presents its tapes as an official bootleg series made with the artist's participation; the sources do not say who recorded this one, only where and when.

{{ <concept_link name="remastering" /> }} is what made it releasable: Randy Wine remastered the set at MoonWine Studios. A remaster can even out level and tone; it cannot replace what the tape never caught.

A {{ <concept_link name="soundtrack" /> }} usually stays with its film. This one found a second home in Iggy Pop's own concerts; compare {{ <track_link source="nancy-sinatra-bang-bang.md" /> }}, a song that a film later revived.

## Artistic Context

James Newell Osterberg Jr., born in 1947, led the Stooges and is regarded as the godfather of punk. In 1992 he worked with Bregović on Arizona Dream, and in 1993 released American Caesar; at Évry the song sat between Caesar and Fucking Alone, two songs he wrote with Eric Schermerhorn. The box set's third disc collects the 1990s, from Berlin in 1991 to the Phoenix Festival in July 1994.

## Impact and Reception

Roadkill Rising was reviewed as a whole rather than track by track: AllMusic gave it three stars out of five, the Austin Chronicle three stars, and Agit Reader found the 1994 versions of The Passenger and TV Eye spoiled by flat playing from his band. Neither of the two reviews we could read discusses this song. Play the soundtrack first, then this.
