## Genre and Mood

**Lukas Graham**'s **7 Years** is soul-pop with a hip-hop accent, a ballad that looks back on a life and then guesses at the rest of it. It is nostalgic and reflective by design, and bittersweet because the looking forward is shadowed by a loss: the singer measures his future against the age at which his father died.

## Song Summary

The song walks through Lukas Forchhammer's life by age. CBS New York's account begins at seven, when his mother told him to go and make friends, and follows that lesson through eleven and twenty to the thirty and sixty he hopes to reach. Forchhammer described it to the Official Charts Company as a song about his life so far and what he hopes to achieve, and about growing older; he said that being a father is what matters most. He wrote it with Stefan Forrest and Morten Ristorp, the production duo Future Animals, and Morten "Pilo" Pilegaard, who produced it with them.

A percussion backbeat holds the song in place as the same lesson travels on.

### Thirty and Sixty

The last part leaves memory for hope, with synthesized strings and the click of a slide projector worked into the arrangement.

## Concepts in Focus

According to the sheet music Musicnotes published, as Wikipedia reports it, the song is in G minor and turns on one {{ <concept_link name="chord progression" /> }}, G minor, B-flat, E-flat and F, at 120 beats per minute. Four chords repeated through a whole story keep attention on the words.

The {{ <concept_link name="minor key" /> }} gives that loop its shade. A song about friends, music and children could sound like a celebration; in G minor it sounds like someone counting.

The band said that 7 Years has no {{ <concept_link name="hook" /> }} and no fixed structure. The Danish critic Anders Houmøller Thomsen of Jyllands-Posten heard it otherwise, crediting Forchhammer with an instinct for choruses that stick. Listen and decide which of them is right.

## Artistic Context

Lukas Graham, formed in 2011, is rooted in Christiania, the self-governing community in Copenhagen where Forchhammer grew up. 7 Years appeared on the band's second album, Lukas Graham, the Blue Album, released by Copenhagen Records in Denmark in 2015 and reworked by Warner Bros. for the United States in April 2016. Forchhammer told the Official Charts Company that he hopes to reach sixty because his father died at sixty-one.

## Impact and Reception

The song topped the charts in Denmark, Britain, Australia, Canada and several other countries, reached number two on the Billboard Hot 100 in April 2016 and was nominated for three Grammys, among them Record and Song of the Year. It was the best-selling song of 2016 in Britain. Time put it on its list of the ten worst songs of that year, puzzled by its popularity. Play it and count the ages yourself.
