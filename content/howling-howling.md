## Genre and Mood

**Howling**'s **Howling** sits where folk meets deep house: an acoustic guitar and a soft, high voice over a restrained club beat. NBHAP called the duo's sound acoustic techno and heard on their album a hypnotic, trance-like pull; it is music for the end of a night, dreamy and a little nostalgic.

## Song Summary

The song began as a demo by the Australian singer **RY X** (Ry Cuming), who sent it to the Berlin producer **Frank Wiedemann**, one half of Âme. Wiedemann, by his own account to The Line of Best Fit, tried not to tamper with its delicate production. Resident Advisor described the result as essentially a Wiedemann edit of an existing RY X song, to which he added a bassline and a beat. The track then gave its name to the duo itself, and three years later it returned as the eleventh of twelve tracks on their debut album, Sacred Ground; that version runs just over seven minutes.

### The Song Underneath

Kaput described a gruffly played acoustic guitar meeting RY X's voice, velvety and fragile, with electronic parts worked in subtly around them.

### What the Producer Added

Resident Advisor names the additions: a bassline and a beat. It found the track tentative next to the duo's later work, but praised Wiedemann's restraint and self-discipline in it.

## Concepts in Focus

"Howling" shows how thin the line between a song and a {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} can be. The words, melody and guitar were RY X's; what made it a club record was a producer reworking its parts. Âme's own remix, which Wikipedia notes was used in the film Taken 3, pushed it further toward the dance floor.

It also shows how {{ <concept_link name="club culture" /> }} can launch a record before it exists. Wiedemann worked on the demo for two days and played it at Berlin's Watergate before it was mixed or mastered; Ninja Tune says the track spread in early 2012 through Dixon's Boiler Room set while still close to a demo.

The song now lives in more than one {{ <concept_link name="alternate version" /> }}: the duo's track, and RY X's own recording, released on his 2016 solo album Dawn.

## Artistic Context

Cuming and Wiedemann met over Skype through a mutual friend; the 2012 release of "Howling" came out on Innervisions, the label Wiedemann helped lead. Sacred Ground followed on 4 May 2015 as a co-release of Modeselektor's Monkeytown and Ninja Tune's Counter imprint, recorded in places from a ceremony space in Topanga to Wiedemann's farm by Lake Constance.

## Impact and Reception

For Kaput's Thomas Venker, the track became an anthem of 2012 for many listeners, though he admitted he did not share the enthusiasm. The Skinny found the album's subtle house competent but at times clinical; Resident Advisor heard it as highly sophisticated pop. Hear it near the end of the album, where the duo placed it, and give it all seven minutes.
