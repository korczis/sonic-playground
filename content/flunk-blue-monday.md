## Genre and Mood

**Flunk**'s **Blue Monday** is a cover that carries New Order's 1983 club record somewhere quiet. It sits between trip-hop and downtempo, and PopMatters heard it as a folktronica ballad of understated beauty, close to the Icelandic band Múm. The feelings follow from that: dreamy, hypnotic in the way the same reviewer heard the whole album as hazy downbeat, and melancholic once the words come forward.

## Song Summary

New Order released Blue Monday on 7 March 1983 as a 12-inch on Factory Records, and it became the best-selling 12-inch single in history. It was a record made by machines and built for dancing, with no standard verse and chorus. Flunk slowed it down and shifted the focus from the danceable beat to the lyric. Anja Øyen Vister sings it, and PopMatters credited her with taking a song familiar to millions and confidently making it her own.

### The Tempo

The decisive change is speed. Slowed down, the song stops being a dance track and becomes a ballad, and every line has room around it.

### The Voice

PopMatters described Vister on the album as a Björk-like chanteuse who croons; here her voice, not a drum pattern, is what holds the song together.

### The Band

Flunk began as a project of the producer Ulf Nygaard and the guitarist Jo Bakke, with Vister improvising vocals and Bakke layering guitars afterwards. The album credits its production to Flunk; no source lists who plays what on this track.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} can keep every word of a song and still change what it means. Compare {{ <track_link source="johnny-cash-hurt.md" /> }}, another 2002 cover that turned a song made among machines into something close to a confession.

The original is a landmark of the {{ <concept_link name="drum machine" /> }}: it opens with a sixteenth-note kick drum from an Oberheim DMX, the sound most people recognise within a second. Flunk's version is defined by what it does without that opening.

The original's synth bass was played on a Moog Source and driven by a {{ <concept_link name="sequencer" /> }} that Bernard Sumner built himself; before MIDI, an engineer had to design a circuit to keep the machines in step. Flunk keep the song and drop the machinery.

## Artistic Context

Flunk formed in Oslo in late 2000 and signed with Beatservice Records, which released For Sleepyheads Only in Norway on 29 April 2002; Guidance Recordings issued it in the United States that October. Blue Monday, the second track, was the band's first single. The debut brought a BBC Radio 1 session and a London live debut at the Notting Hill Arts Club.

## Impact and Reception

The single was well received in the UK, turned up on many compilations in Europe and North America, was remixed at least seven times and was used in the films Walking Tall (2004) and Nancy Drew (2007). Not everyone was won over: Stylus Magazine's Mallory O'Donnell thought it only showed how little melody the original has. Play both and judge for yourself.
