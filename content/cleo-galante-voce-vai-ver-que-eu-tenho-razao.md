## Genre and Mood

**Cléo Galante**'s **Você Vai Ver Que Eu Tenho Razão**, "you'll see that I'm right", is filed today under samba rock, the Brazilian blend of samba with rock, soul and funk, and in online stores under Brazilian music and MPB. The title is a confident promise, and groovy is the word for the dance music it is collected with. It is also one of the least documented records on this playground: no review, biography or liner note we could find describes it, and this page says so rather than invent.

## Song Summary

What the catalogues establish is short. Som Livre released the song on a single on 11 January 1972, with Momentos de Cada Dia as its second track, and credits Galante as lead vocalist and composer. The version collected on Samba Rock Vol.1 runs 2:40, is also dated 1972 and adds a credit to World, which no source explains. The Som Livre single as streamed today runs a few seconds longer; whether the two are the same take is not documented.

## Musical Breakdown

### Main Section

No source describes the arrangement, so this is a guide to listening rather than a description. Wikipedia describes samba rock as built on drum kit, bass guitar, keyboards and brass with a strong groove, with the electric guitar Jorge Ben brought to it. Listen for which of those this record uses, and for the samba rhythm under them. Like most singles of its time, it says what it has to say in under three minutes.

## Concepts in Focus

The 1972 release was a single, and Momentos de Cada Dia, listed second, was its {{ <concept_link name="b-side" /> }}: the reverse of the record, the track a buyer got along with the one they came for.

The song's second life came through the {{ <concept_link name="compilation album" /> }}. Samba Rock Vol.1, released by Atração on 1 October 2007, opens with Galante and runs through Doris Monteiro, Elizeth Cardoso, Erasmo Carlos, Toquinho and Noriel Vilela before closing with Jorge Ben Jor's Berenice; MusicBrainz lists the song on an earlier collection too, Humbatuque Apresenta Samba Rock, from 2003. Compilations like these put forgotten singles back in front of dancers. For the genre's founding voice, hear {{ <track_link source="jorge-ben-jr_take-it-easy-my-brother-charles.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Samba rock, Wikipedia explains, began at block parties in the predominantly Black neighbourhoods of São Paulo, where DJs such as Osvaldo Pereira mixed American rock and roll and soul with samba from the late 1950s. Jorge Ben is called its father, Trio Mocotó among its originators, and couples dance it with hips and feet loose and ballroom manners kept. About Galante the sources are silent; an official YouTube channel uploads the records, tagging them samba rock and jovem guarda, and a second spelling, Cleo Galanth, appears in the catalogues.

## Impact and Reception

We found no contemporary review and no chart record. The song's reception is visible only in where later compilers put it: first, on a record that ends with Jorge Ben Jor. In November 2016 São Paulo declared samba rock part of its cultural heritage. Put this on and hear what those compilers heard.
