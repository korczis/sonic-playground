## Genre and Mood

**Laurent Garnier**'s **The Man With The Red Face** is techno and house that leaves the door open for jazz. A loop that hardly changes holds the floor for nine minutes while a saxophone talks over it: hypnotic first, intense as the horn presses on. Garnier called the track "extremely simple"; what moves is the soloist.

## Song Summary

The track grew out of a gig. Booked for the Montreux Jazz Festival in 1998, Garnier worked it up in rehearsals in Ireland, prompted by The Deep's Dom Dom Jump to bring "the wind element to house music". For the record, Philippe Nadaud improvised over the loop in Garnier's Paris studio for twenty to twenty-five minutes while Garnier and the engineer Laurent Collat pushed him. When the take was over, Nadaud's face was red, and the track had its title.

## Musical Breakdown

No source maps the nine minutes, so this breakdown follows the parts Garnier described rather than a timeline.

### The Loop

A Reese bass line from a Roland Juno-106 and a funky keyboard figure from a Yamaha DX100, bought because Detroit producers used it. With no drum machine, Garnier took the percussion and hi-hats from his other tracks.

### The Saxophone

Nadaud plays two instruments: a saxophone and an EWI, an electronic wind instrument that turns breath into synthesised sound. Listen for which one is speaking.

### The Edit

Days later Garnier cut the take down and arranged the phrases so that they "answer each other": improvised, but shaped by the producer.

## Concepts in Focus

{{ <concept_link name="improvisation" /> }} usually happens once. Here it was recorded at length and composed afterwards: Nadaud played freely, Garnier chose and ordered the phrases. Nadaud said he could not play it that way again, but was not ashamed of the result.

The {{ <concept_link name="loop" /> }} makes that editing possible: because the bed does not change, a phrase can sit almost anywhere, and the ear follows the horn.

The {{ <concept_link name="bassline" /> }}, a Reese bass, belongs to the dance floor rather than the jazz club and keeps this a club record however far the saxophone wanders. Compare {{ <track_link source="marek-hemmann-gemini.md" /> }}, where a tenor saxophone rides a minimal house groove for just as long.

## Artistic Context

Garnier, born in 1966, began mixing at the Haçienda in Manchester as DJ Pedro and later set up the label F Communications with Eric Morand. Unreasonable Behaviour, his third studio album, came out there on 7 February 2000; this is its longest track, produced and mixed by Garnier with Collat. Nadaud stayed: Resident Advisor announced the 2007 live album Public Outburst, inspired by seventies improv jazz, with Philippe "Man with the Red Face" Nadaud in the band.

## Impact and Reception

The single reached number 65 in the UK in April 2000; a two-part 12-inch with Greed later that year reached number 36, Garnier's first Top 40 hit in Britain. The album scored 83 on Metacritic and Resident Advisor ranked it 76th among the albums of the 2000s; Mixmag MENA wrote in 2022 that the track belongs to a "boundless status" in electronic music. Give it all nine minutes and follow the horn.
