## Genre and Mood

**The Asteroids Galaxy Tour** announced themselves with **The Sun Ain't Shining No More**, a burst of indie pop soaked in sixties psychedelia and brassy funk. It is playful, vibrant and relentlessly upbeat, which is a delicious contradiction for a song whose title promises the opposite.

## Song Summary

On paper this is a breakup lament: the light has gone out and nothing feels warm any more. In practice it sounds like a marching band crashing a garden party. Mette Lindberg's bright, girlish voice bounces over punchy horns, a strutting rhythm section and fuzzy, retro keyboards, and the gap between lyric and mood is the whole charm. The memorable detail is that horn line, cheeky and triumphant.

## Musical Breakdown

The song moves quickly, packing a lot of colour into well under four minutes.

### Intro

Drums and bass kick off with a swaggering, slightly vintage groove, as if the needle has just dropped on a lost soul single.

### Verse 1

Lindberg's voice enters high and playful, sliding over the beat with a sing-song lilt that makes the sad words sound like a dare.

### Build-up

Keys and backing vocals thicken the texture, and the rhythm tightens into a stomping pulse that tells you the horns are close.

### Chorus

The brass explodes into the hook, a joyful, technicolour blast that turns heartbreak into a parade.

### Bridge

A brief, woozier passage lets the psychedelic side show, with swirling keys and echoing vocals before the band charges back in.

### Climax

The last chorus doubles down on horns, handclaps and harmonies, the most exuberant moment of the song.

### Outro

It ends on a final brassy flourish, leaving the room grinning.

## Artistic Context

The Asteroids Galaxy Tour are a Danish pop band formed in Copenhagen in 2007 around the singer Mette Lindberg and the songwriter and producer Lars Iversen, expanding to five musicians on stage. The Sun Ain't Shining No More was their debut single in 2008, and the song later appeared on their first album Fruit, released in Europe on 21 September 2009 on Small Giants. Its retro, horn-driven pop set the template for everything the band did next, including "Around the Bend", which Apple used in a worldwide iPod Touch advert from December 2008, and "The Golden Age", a top-ten hit in Austria and Switzerland in 2009.

## Impact and Reception

The single introduced the band beyond Denmark and remains one of their signature songs, a showcase for Lindberg's unmistakable voice and the band's knack for joyous, cinematic arrangements. It belongs here because it proves sadness can be danced away. Press play, and if the sun really is not shining, this will do the job instead.
