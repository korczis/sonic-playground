## Genre and Mood

**La Roux** made **Bulletproof** the brightest kind of synth-pop: Pitchfork called it a catchy-as-hell electro-pop smash, and Digital Spy heard bright, bouncy electropop. Under the bounce is a hard edge. Elly Jackson described it as a song about being fed up with treating herself badly and letting others do it, and refusing to repeat old mistakes; uDiscover Music calls it a defiant anthem, and empowering is this page's word for how it has been heard since.

## Song Summary

Jackson and Ben Langmaid wrote and produced it together at Elfin Studios in Teddington, London, and Serban Ghenea mixed it in Virginia Beach. Jackson remembered the writing as quick: a strong verse, several candidate choruses and one missing line, and when it came she knew they had the one and wanted to call their manager. Released by Polydor on 21 June 2009 as the third single from the duo's debut album, it runs 3:25, and it says what it means in its title.

The BBC Chart Blog's Fraser McAlpine gave it five stars and praised its verses; Jackson delivers the song, in uDiscover's word, defiantly.

### Chorus

Digital Spy's Nick Levine found the chorus as immediate as that of In for the Kill, the single before it.

### Breakdown

McAlpine also praised the vocoder breakdown, a passage built around that voice-shaping effect; where in the song it falls, no source says.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="vocoder" /> }} imposes the shape of a voice on a synthesizer's tone, so the words come out as machine-like chords. In a song called Bulletproof, a voice turned partly machine is a fitting image, though that reading is this page's, not the critics'.

The {{ <concept_link name="synthesizer" /> }} is the sound of the whole record. Plugged In heard a strong nod to Erasure, The Human League, Depeche Mode and {{ <track_link source="eurythmics-annie-lennox-dave-stewart-sweet-dreams-are-made-of-this-2005-remaster.md" /> }}; Common Sense Media heard a less creative imitation of the same decade.

A {{ <concept_link name="remix" /> }} gave the song further lives: The Gold EP in May 2010 added remixes and a live version, and in 2023 Jackson and Chromeo released a rework, Discoproof, first played at Coachella.

## Artistic Context

La Roux were Jackson and Langmaid, and their album La Roux, released in June 2009, went platinum in the UK, was nominated for the Mercury Prize and won the Grammy for Best Electronic/Dance Album. uDiscover lists David Bowie, Prince, The Knife, Nick Drake and Joni Mitchell among the duo's inspirations.

## Impact and Reception

Bulletproof went straight to number one in the UK and became a sleeper hit in the US, reaching number eight on the Billboard Hot 100 in June 2010 after a performance on The Ellen DeGeneres Show; Rolling Stone ranked it among the 25 best songs of 2009. Jackson later said its success fed her anxiety, that she wished she could erase it, and that it bought her a house. A TikTok challenge gave it a second life in 2020. Turn it up and listen for the vocoder.
