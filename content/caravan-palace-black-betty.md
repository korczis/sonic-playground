## Genre and Mood

**Caravan Palace** play electro swing, the Parisian blend of vintage jazz and club production, and **Black Betty** is their version of a song far older than either. It is energetic and playful, a song with one big refrain built for a crowd to shout back, and it has been the band's concert favourite since before it was ever released.

## Song Summary

Black Betty began as a traditional African-American work song. John and Alan Lomax recorded it in 1933 with two prisoners, Iron Head Baker and Clear Rock Platt, and Lead Belly made the first commercial recording in 1939. Who or what Betty was is still argued over: a bottle of whiskey, a prison whip, a penitentiary wagon, and in modern versions a fast car. Ram Jam's 1977 rock version, with altered lyrics, reached number 18 in the US and became the template most later covers follow. 303 Magazine heard Caravan Palace covering Ram Jam's take.

## Musical Breakdown

### Chorus

Everything turns on the refrain. Reviewing a 2016 Denver show, 303 Magazine described the band repeating the chorus over and over with gusto, and the crowd singing it back.

### Main Section

No published review describes how the studio recording is arranged, and this page does not guess. What critics say of the band in general is that they take traditional swing and turn it into bass-heavy dance beats while playing real instruments. On stage in 2025, Music Connection heard the song as a reimagined dance mix with a trombone and baritone saxophone soli section; whether the single uses those horns is for your ears to judge.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="cover version" /> }} can be faithful or transforming, and this one changes the song's world: a rock hit passes to a band that plays swing for the dance floor. The band recorded what their label called their own unique version in their Paris studio, between tours.

Underneath sits a {{ <concept_link name="work song" /> }}, music made to keep labour in time. Hearing the Lomax recordings beside this single shows how far a melody can travel from a prison farm.

{{ <concept_link name="swing" text="Swing" /> }} is the lilt that electro swing borrows from 1920s and 1930s jazz. The band name Django Reinhardt and Lionel Hampton among their influences, next to Daft Punk; compare their {{ <track_link source="caravan-palace-lone-digger.md" /> }}.

## Artistic Context

Caravan Palace formed in Paris in 2008, first as a trio hired to score a silent film; Zoé Colotis is its singer. By 2016 they were touring the world behind their third album, which is when Black Betty became a set staple. They played it, with Lone Digger, on BBC Two's Jools' Annual Hootenanny at the turn of 2016 and 2017, and released it as a non-album single on 26 May 2017.

## Impact and Reception

The label reported that the song reached number nine on YouTube's global viral chart. Live critics split: 303 Magazine called it a hit and a sing-along but the least exciting part of the show, while Music Connection named it the highlight of the night. Play the Lomax field recording first, then this, and hear more than eighty years of one song.
