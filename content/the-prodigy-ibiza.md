## Genre and Mood

**The Prodigy**'s **Ibiza**, with **Sleaford Mods**, is electronic music with a punk mouth: satirical, aggressive and gritty. Sonicabuse called it a sleazy slab of electro-punk aggro, and Metal Hammer a streets-on-acid romp. It is big beat turned against the dance world that made the band.

## Song Summary

The target is in the title, but Liam Howlett insisted it is not the island. The band had played a gig there, and he called the song an attack on DJs who arrive in their Learjets, plug a USB stick in and wave their hands in the air to a pre-programmed mix. Howlett wrote it with Jason Williamson of Sleaford Mods, who delivers the lead vocal; MusicBrainz also credits Keith Flint with vocals.

## Musical Breakdown

No source describes the track section by section or gives times, and none describes its beat or synthesizers in detail. What the reviews describe is its voice and its attitude.

### The Voice

The Music Times, announcing the collaboration, described Williamson's delivery as a more laid-back, spoken-word approach. Sonicabuse heard a typically foul-mouthed performance that takes the spoken-word side of Blur's "Parklife" and filters it through the mind of an addict in a broken-down estate.

### The Attitude

Consequence of Sound heard Sleaford Mods echoing the grimy hip-hop numbers in The Prodigy's catalogue on a satirical banger. Drowned in Sound granted the sharp satire and Williamson's gritty, acid-tongued lines but found part of the vocal hugely irritating.

## Concepts in Focus

A {{ <concept_link name="featured artist" /> }} is a guest credited beside the main act, and here the guest changes the band's voice. On most of the album, Talkhouse wrote, the vocals are as mantra-minded as the drums; Ibiza, give or take, is the exception, built around Williamson's words.

His style is closer to {{ <concept_link name="spoken word" /> }} than to singing: talking, ranting, accusing in rhythm. Hear how it works on his own band's bare loops in {{ <track_link source="sleaford-mods-tweet-tweet-tweet.md" /> }}, then over Howlett's production here.

The {{ <concept_link name="music video" /> }}, uploaded on 20 April 2015, moves the fight from the club to an office: the two bands intimidate a desk clerk, with Williamson at the head of a rowdy gang. The embed above is the audio upload; the video runs a few seconds longer.

## Artistic Context

The Prodigy announced the collaboration on Instagram in October 2014. The Day Is My Enemy, their sixth album, which Howlett promised would sound more violent, was released on 30 March 2015 by Take Me to the Hospital and Cooking Vinyl. Ibiza came out a week earlier, on 23 March, as its third official single, and as a glow-in-the-dark vinyl for Record Store Day 2015.

## Impact and Reception

The album debuted at number one in the UK, the band's sixth consecutive chart-topping record. Reviews of this track split: Drowned in Sound was irritated, while Sonicabuse called it one of the most menacing tracks of the year. Its opposite on the same record is {{ <track_link source="the-prodigy-beyond-the-deathray.md" /> }}. Play both.
